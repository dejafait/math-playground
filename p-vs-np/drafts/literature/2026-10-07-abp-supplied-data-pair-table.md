## Inspected primary coverage

**Raz–Shpilka.** *Deterministic Polynomial Identity Testing in Non Commutative Models*, [author-hosted 16-page manuscript](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~shpilka/publications/RazShpilka_PIT.pdf), no printed revision date; accessed 2026-10-07. Read Definition 1, Lemma 2 and its series/parallel Step 1, printed pp.4–5; reuse the previously read normalization cases. The lemma assumes a size-s formula and zero free term, producing homogeneous-component ABPs of O(s²) vertices each in O(s³) total time over a field F without a characteristic restriction. Step 1 treats constants and nonleveled graphs and wires the two operand graphs in parallel for addition or sequentially, in operand order, for multiplication. This supports local separate-graph composition, including over F₂. Its cost is in formula size. It supplies no bound for recursively copying a shared pair-table DAG. Affine inputs and constants are retained in the proposed receiving model; the homogeneous-only convention must not silently replace them.

**Limaye–Malod–Srinivasan.** *Lower Bounds for Non-Commutative Skew Circuits*, [Theory of Computing 12(12), 2016](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v012a012/v012a012.pdf), published 7 September 2016. Read §1.2, §§2.1–2.2 and Definition 3.1, printed pp.3–5,9. Skew multiplication has a variable/scalar operand on either side. The palindrome example has linear-size skew circuits and a full-rank middle coefficient matrix, while small homogeneous linear-edge ABPs factor through a cut. Thus skew and ABP models cannot be conflated. §1.2 also warns that switching multiplication sides can destroy cancellation; that warning is relevant to formal equality, particularly over F₂. The rank mechanism and affine-model/field specialization are already covered by L033's assessment and audit. Neither this example nor the stronger skew lower-bound theorems supplies a valid L025 instance. No new lower-bound theorem or model normalization is proved here.

**Arvind–Joglekar–Raja.** *Noncommutative Valiant's Classes: Structure and Complete Problems*, [ECCC TR15-124 revision 1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2015/124/revision/1/download), posted 1 May 2016, 30-page PDF. Read Proposition 4 with its complete proof and Remarks 5–6, printed pp.6–7. The ABP case assumes the source g already has polynomial-size ABPs and replaces its variable-labeled transitions by the matrices of the abp-reduction. The circuit and skew cases instead preserve those respective representations. The name “abp-reduction” does not turn its arbitrary source into an ABP. Remark 6 separately distinguishes noncommutative weakly-skew and skew models. Also inspected Theorem 19's statement and initial left/right/scalar transformation cases, pp.14–15; the original version calls it Theorem 14. Its palindrome completeness is not an ABP conversion theorem. The rest of that completeness proof was not inspected and is not an input. Its stated circuit syntactic-degree bound is stronger than L025's semantic root-degree promises, so its size accounting is not imported for this target.
