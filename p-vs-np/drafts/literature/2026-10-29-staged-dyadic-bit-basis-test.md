## Proposed recipe, to be constructed and justified only after approval

For each endpoint H=A,B independently, start from its literal raw C,D
and full vertex list. Preserve s,t,h_0,...,h_N,f_N and change only the
ordered f_0,...,f_(N-1) coordinate block. At stage u=0,...,k-1 use the
upper bit factor from the tensor circuit: on the k-bit binary coordinates
it acts by U(2)=[[1,1],[0,1]] on bit u and identity on every other bit.
Here bit 0 is least significant; the matrix is extended by identity on
all other graph coordinates. This specifies which imported factor is
intended, without computing an instance or claiming a scalar arrow.
Do not include the polar-code bit-reversal permutation implicitly.

The candidate next raw pair is (E_u^(-1) C E_u,E_u^(-1) D E_u),
using the previous stage's pair. Proposed raw roots are still s,t.
For each nonzero entry place one literal 1 or x occurrence, retaining
both labels when both occur. This is a proposal to verify, not permission
to treat any matrix as an acyclic graph. Keep the original endpoints'
full occurrence records as endpoints; do not replace them by a parity
compression or by their accepted polynomial.

Compute every stage's inverse and candidate transfer matrix explicitly;
derive and then check its orientation against all three scalar equations.
No raw or closed equation is assumed merely from the arithmetic circuit.
The potential zigzag consists of the k stages on each side and a central
ordinary scalar arrow between the two terminal stage graphs, with the B
stages traversed in the reverse chain order. Either orientation of the
central arrow is allowed, and it may be singular. Solve its ENTIRE
initial/transition/final linear system in original stage coordinates.
For a bounded deterministic test, use RREF with free variables zero to
select a solution in each consistent orientation, keeping variable order
lexicographic in the saved vertex orders. Charge the selected actual map;
this is not a sparse-solution optimizer or a support lower bound.
