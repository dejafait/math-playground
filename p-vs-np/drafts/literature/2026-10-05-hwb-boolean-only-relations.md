**Forbes–Shpilka–Tzameret–Wigderson (FSTW).** [*Proof Complexity Lower Bounds from Algebraic Circuit Complexity*, Theory of Computing 17(10), 1–88, published 1 November 2021, DOI 10.4086/toc.2021.v017a010](https://toc.cs.uchicago.edu/articles/v017a010/v017a010.pdf). Read Fact 3.12, printed p. 21, and Propositions 4.5–4.6 with the complete matrix-telescoping and final IPS-identity proofs, pp. 30–32. Fact 3.12 is uniqueness for **commutative multilinear** polynomials on the Boolean cube; it does not identify arbitrary free-ring polynomials by evaluations. Proposition 4.5 assumes a supplied width-r roABP in the order x_1<...<x_n with individual degree at most d. It gives poly(r,n,d)-explicit width-r roABPs for ml(f) and coefficients h_j satisfying f=ml(f)+sum_j h_j(x)(x_j²−x_j), with individual degree of h_j at most that of f. There is no characteristic or field-size restriction here, so F₂ is allowed.

The proof divides each univariate transition matrix A_j−ml(A_j) by x_j²−x_j, then telescopes the ordered matrix product. Its last factor placement uses commutativity of the **polynomial entries**, despite the ordered matrix products. Proposition 4.6 further assumes unsatisfiable f_j and supplied same-order width-r coefficients g_j with sum_j g_j f_j≡1 modulo Boolean axioms, and same-order width-s axiom roABPs. It multilinearizes g_j, applies the width-rs product construction and Proposition 4.5 to each product, then explicitly verifies zero at zero placeholders and one after substitution. The resulting width and explicitness depend polynomially on the supplied widths, number of variables/axioms and individual degrees. This is not a local witness theorem for a Boolean-zero polynomial in the free ring and is not a license to commute scalar entries without corrections.

Also read Lemma 4.9 with its sparse and formula proof cases, pp. 34–35, and the statement/construction reduction of Corollary 4.10, p. 35. These correct a multilinear g times a monomial or low-degree sparse multilinear f. The formula case costs poly(t,2^d), where d is the multiplying monomial's degree; the sparse case has a polynomial-depth-three alternative. No polynomial sparsity or small unfolded multilinear formula is supplied for every fixed root here, and d can grow with n for a prefix multiplier. Thus these are supporting restricted alternatives, not an import covering all gates. Page numbers here are the printed journal pages; internal references sometimes call a Proposition a Theorem.
