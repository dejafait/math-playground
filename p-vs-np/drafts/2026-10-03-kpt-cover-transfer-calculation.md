**Calculation saved before the full write-up.** Write t(y)=V_N(code(F),a(F,y)) for an arbitrary unsatisfiable CNF F, and E_i(y) for equality of its second output to supplied tuple u_i. After discharging the first antichecker, the strategy gives ¬t(y) ∨ P(U_2(F,a(F,y));z_2). Its cover gives t(y) → OR_i E_i(y), because the first solver's answer on F checks to 0 by concrete evaluation. For each constant u_i, fix a challenge η_i making its antichecker false using an externally correct comparison circuit C_N. Substitute x_2=code(F), y_2=a(F,y), C_2=C_N, η_2=η_i. Its fixed solver also checks to 0 on F. Gate congruence under E_i identifies the complete second tuple, so this specialized strategy gives E_i(y) → ¬t(y). Combining all these implications with the supplied cover gives ¬t(y), hence ¬F(y) by L013's evaluator identity. Malformed tuples instead have a false format check; extra well-formed tuples get the same challenge treatment. For an empty list, the coverage proof itself gives ¬t.

The antichecker checks and solver checks are on concrete data. Their proof does not use a theorem asserting that C_N is correct on every input. Choosing positive counterexample witnesses uses external SAT correctness; verifying them uses only gate evaluation. The complete-list and coverage bounds charge all tuple bits and proofs. This is the difference from the failed first-round canonical substitution in ATTEMPTS/013: the variable a(F,y) remains free throughout.
