## Inspected primary passages

**Ésik–Maletti, Simulation vs. Equivalence**, four-page
[author manuscript](https://www.informatik.uni-leipzig.de/~maletti/pub/esimal10.pdf),
2010 bibliographic version, no additional revision date established.
Reused Definition 3.1 and Theorem 4.2; read their complete p.2–3 equations,
paired reachable-row construction, stabilization, generator-coordinate
transitions, initial coordinates, final equality, and finite-semiring
deterministic variant. This source already underlies the notebook.
It constructs a common source for equivalent automata over a Noetherian
semiring. F₂ satisfies the hypotheses. Its chosen space is generated by
paired reachable rows; it need not be the entire residual relation.
It bounds neither expanded generator support nor a sparse acyclic
singleton realization. The deterministic variant uses reachable vectors
as states; determinism is not a linear-size statement.

**Bhaduri, Coalgebras for Bisimulation of Weighted Automata over Semirings**,
[arXiv:2109.00732v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.00732v3), header
20-Dec-2022. Read Definition 3.1/Theorem 3.2, Definitions 3.3/3.5,
Lemma 3.6, both directions of Proposition 3.7 and Theorem 3.8/Corollary
3.9, printed pp.11–14. The semiring hypotheses include F₂. Language
equivalence is the kernel relation of the final semantic map and is the
largest linear bisimulation. The proofs use output/transition preservation,
quotients and finality; they impose no sparsity or occurrence budget.
For comparison with E_N, the two endpoint spaces can be placed in a direct
sum; the cross-space relation is not literally an equivalence relation on
either endpoint separately. No quantitative direct-sum specialization is
derived here. The preprint's quotient-transition formula suppresses a
class bracket, and its finality proof prints `ker` where a homomorphism
is intended; these typing slips are not imported as equations.

**Gottlieb–Neylon, Matrix sparsification and the sparse null space problem**,
[arXiv:1008.1498v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1498v1). The fetched PDF
has an arXiv 9-Aug-2010 header and a separate 30-May-2018 title date;
citations designate this retrieved text, not an inferred revision history.
Read §2.2's explicit rational domain, §2.3 Lemmas 2–3 with both reductions,
§3.1 Theorem 4 and proof through Corollary 7, and §3.2 Lemma 8's
positive-real construction. These distinguish sparse null bases from
arbitrary bases and give an exact same-span sparsification formulation.
They do not constrain an induced shift or accepting boundaries. The
all-square-minors-nonsingular hypothesis and characteristic-zero
construction are not verified for our binary family. No hardness,
unsparsifiability or approximation assertion is transferred to F₂.
PDF screenshots of pp.4–5 timed out; the full relevant text and proof
passages were accessible, so this is not an essential source blocker.

**Coleman–Pothen, The Null Space Problem I. Complexity**, SIAM J. Algebraic
Discrete Methods 7(4) (1986), 527–537, DOI
[10.1137/0607059](https://doi.org/10.1137/0607059),
[full primary text](https://cedric.cnam.fr/~bentzc/INITREC/Files/DW10.pdf).
Read Algorithm 2.1/Theorem 2.1, Lemmas 2.2–2.4, Theorems 2.5–2.6 and
the complete greedy/matroid proof, pp.529–531, plus the real-field
qualification p.532. The greedy characterization concerns a sparsest null
basis; its proof selects minimum-weight independent circuit vectors.
The conformal-decomposition argument uses real signs. No such argument,
polynomial search, finite-field hardness statement, or shift-compatible
optimum is imported. This is a comparison, not an obstruction to our
explicit dyadic family. Theorem 3.1's proof is omitted in this source;
it is not used as a mathematical premise.

**Boyar–Find, Cancellation-Free Circuits in Unbounded and Bounded Depth**,
[arXiv:1305.3041v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.3041v1), 14-May-2013,
§6, Theorem 10 and Proposition 3, printed pp.10–14. Read the recursive
matrix definition, full C1/C2/C3 gate-elimination and cut-rank proof,
matching upper construction, and the following cancellation discussion.
The bound is (n/2)log₂n XOR gates for cancellation-free Sierpiński circuits;
the proof uses the forbidden cross-half paths and discusses why allowing
cancellation requires care. This does not lower-bound arbitrary binary
relation bases, nor equate gate count with expanded scalar support.
No present-day general lower-bound status is inferred from this old text.
The earlier [arXiv:1207.5321v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.5321v1),
23-Jul-2012, was opened as a precursor; its separate numbering is not used.
