# Decider proof systems and the extended-resolution simulation obligation

Saved 2026-09-25 before the detailed argument; completed in [L011](../lemmas/L011-decider-proof-systems-and-simulation.md). The text below preserves the initial plan. This is not a candidate resolution or the current checkpoint.

**Gap and target.** A superpolynomial lower bound for one proof system excludes arbitrary polynomial-time SAT decision only after a valid computation-to-proof transfer. Construct the refutation system R_A whose certificates are rejecting computation traces of a fixed correct SAT decider A. Test the transfer to a fixed polynomial-time-checkable refutation system Q, in particular extended resolution (ER), without postulating efficient formal proofs of A's correctness.

**Prior evidence and test.** L010 already refutes the instancewise transfer to ordinary resolution. No earlier lemma constructs the general decider-induced proof system or fixes the quantifiers for a global polynomial-time decider. If a polynomial simulation follows from trace checking alone, identify its actual inference rules and overhead. If it instead needs short Q proofs of the conclusions or a separate soundness/reflection argument, expose that obligation and stop the automatic-transfer inference. A counterexample system can refute a system-independent assertion but cannot by itself refute ER-specific simulation.

**Working argument.** Local transitions give a polynomial-time trace verifier in the full certificate length. External correctness of A makes it sound, and totality makes it complete for well-formed UNSAT. If A has one polynomial running-time bound, every UNSAT formula has a polynomial R_A certificate. For any fixed Q, a polynomial size simulation of R_A implies polynomial boundedness of Q. Conversely, polynomial boundedness of Q gives such a size simulation; under the assumption A is polynomial, SAT is in P, so NP witness search should also give a polynomial-time translation. This conditional equivalence needs a complete prefix-search argument with the output bound charged. It does not prove the Q bound.

**Discriminator and remaining obligations.** A full truth-table refutation system is polynomial-time checkable in its proof length yet has exponential shortest proofs on syntactically large contradictions. It tests the proposed system-independent inference even if P=NP; it does not establish an ER lower bound. The standard Cook–Reckhow equivalence quantifies over some proof system, not a specified one. The intended result is an assessment of the simulation gap, with any ER-specific theorem still explicitly unproved.
