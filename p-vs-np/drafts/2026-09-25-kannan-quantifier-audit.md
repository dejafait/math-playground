# 2026-09-25 — Kannan quantifiers and the joined-language test

Saved before completing the proof and checkpoint update. The current checkpoint remains in PROGRESS.md.

**Gap and target.** The circuit route needs one fixed NP language outside every polynomial circuit-size bound. The intermediate test is whether Kannan's fixed-exponent lower bounds, combined with the temporary assumption P=NP, supply such a language. A successful uniform NP construction combining the witnesses would give the missing membership input; a theorem with only exponent-dependent witnesses and no common verifier bound stops that direct inference. This does not require abandoning other circuit mechanisms.

**Redundancy.** L006 already supplies the needed counting and DNF estimates, but neither it nor the earlier attempts addresses Kannan's language/exponent order or a tagged join of lower-bound languages. L003 concerns explicit computation transcripts and a different same-input padding obstruction. L007's security premise is not used.

**Sources inspected.** Fortnow–Santhanam–Williams, [Fixed-Polynomial Size Circuit Bounds, §II.A and Theorem 8](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/circuit.pdf), defines SIZE using O bounds and states the named Kannan theorem. Cai–Watanabe, [report C-256, §1 and Theorem 1](https://www.is.c.titech.ac.jp/report-c/C-256.pdf), distinguishes exponent-dependent languages from a single language and gives a constructive variant. The original Kannan publisher page was not retrievable; no original theorem number is inferred. Cook's [official statement, §1](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/pvsnp.pdf) was rechecked and agrees with the existing target note.

**Proof sketch to complete.** Write c_L(n) for minimum circuit size. Kannan supplies, for each k, a language K_k in Sigma_2^P outside SIZE(n^k). Under P=NP, complement closure and two bounded quantifier eliminations put each K_k in P; the polynomial exponent may depend on k. For a concrete quantifier countermodel, take r=(k+1)ceil(log_2 n), and when 8<=r<=n let H_k on n bits be the first r-variable truth table from L006 requiring more than 2^r/(32r) gates, evaluated on the first r input bits. Restricting unused inputs of any circuit costs at most three constant-producing gates. Thus c_H_k(n)>2^r/(32r)-3, while the DNF bound gives c_H_k(n)<=2r2^r. The ratio of the lower bound to n^k diverges, but the upper bound is O_k(n^(k+2)). This gives actual decidable languages in P/poly witnessing the quantifier distinction, without claiming they lie in NP.

For any family A_k outside SIZE(n^k), the tagged join J={1^k 0 x : k>=1, x in A_k} is outside P/poly: fix k above a proposed circuit exponent and hardwire its fixed prefix. That argument does not provide a uniform NP verifier for J. Even a computable family of individual polynomial algorithms need not have one polynomial bound when k is input. Check all positive input lengths, fixed-k constants, finite exceptional lengths, and the no-free-constants convention before finalizing.
