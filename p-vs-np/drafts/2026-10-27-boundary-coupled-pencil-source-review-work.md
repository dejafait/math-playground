## Inspected primary statements and essential cases

**Schrempf, _Linearizing the Word Problem in (some) Free Fields_,
arXiv:1701.03378v2, submitted revision 3-Aug-2018; PDF title date
6-Aug-2018; IJAC 28(7) (2018), 1209–1230.**
[Primary v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.03378v2), printed pp.3–5,
Definitions 1.4, 1.10–1.11, Remark 1.12, Proposition 1.13 and its full
product verification; pp.9–10, Theorem 2.2, Lemma 2.3 with its complete
block-shear proof, Theorem 2.4 with both directions and Example 2.5.
The base field K is any commutative field and X a finite alphabet.
An ALS represents uA⁻¹v with u=e₁ and full affine-linear A. Scalar
invertible P,Q act as (uQ, PAQ, Pv); admissibility fixes the first row
of Q. The product has diagonal A_f,A_g, upper-right block −v_fu_g,
row (u_f,0), column (0,v_g)ᵀ and solution (s_fg,s_g)ᵀ.
Lemma 2.3's scalar T,U satisfy

    u_f U=0,  T A_g−A_f U=A_f u_fᵀu_g,  T v_g=v_f.

They suffice for equality without minimality. Theorem 2.4's converse
assumes both systems minimal of the same dimension. Its proof uses the
minimal-representation isomorphism and independent solution families;
Example 2.5 and the following discussion warn against the nonminimal
converse. No support bound is stated. This distinguishes a two-sided ALS
change from a single weighted-automaton intertwiner.

**Schrempf, _A Standard Form in (some) Free Fields: How to construct
Minimal Linear Representations_, arXiv:1803.10627v3.**
[Primary v3 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.10627v3), arXiv header
7-Dec-2020, rendered title date 27-Nov-2024 (both dates retained; no claim
that the arXiv version was submitted in 2024). Journal reference:
_Open Mathematics_ 18(1) (2020), 1365–1386,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/math-2020-0076).
Read printed pp.5–6 Definition 1.11, pp.8–9 Propositions 2.6/2.7,
Lemmas 2.8/2.9 and Proposition 2.10 statement. Polynomial ALS here means
a nonzero polynomial, upper unitriangular system matrix and scalar
multiple of the final unit column. Proposition 2.10 assumes nonzero
p,q given by minimal polynomial ALS of dimensions at least two; its
product has dimension n_p+n_q−1. It cites the proof in Schrempf 2019,
Proposition 26, which was not read and is not imported. The statement
does not bound transformations from arbitrary nonminimal endpoints or
their support. Zero/constant G and arbitrary fixed roots therefore cannot
be silently placed under that minimal multiplication theorem.

**Garg–Gurvits–Oliveira–Wigderson, _Operator Scaling: Theory and
Applications_, FoCM 20 (2020), 223–290.**
[Primary author-hosted journal PDF](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/GargGOW20.pdf),
Appendix A.1 pp.281–284, Definition A.1 and Proposition A.2, including
the example and complete induction/base case. As stated the theorem is
over Q⟨x⟩ for an m-by-n matrix with division-free formulas, per-entry
size at most s and input bit complexity at most b. The added identity
size k is the **total** number of multiplication gates over all entries;
polynomial invertible P,Q linearize A⊕I_k in polynomial time. The
induction replaces one entry a+bc by a, b, −c and a new diagonal 1,
reducing that total by one. This is polynomial-ring equivalence preserving
coranks, with no initial/final vector conditions. We do not specialize its
Q statement to F₂ or confuse total formula size with a per-entry bound.
The finite-field source below supplies the relevant separate comparison.

**Arvind–Joglekar, _On Efficient Noncommutative Polynomial Factorization
via Higman Linearization_, ECCC TR22-022 revision 4, 23-May-2025.**
[Primary revision PDF](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/022/revision/4/download),
§2 Theorems 2.7–2.9 (printed pp.7–8; PDF pages 9–10), and Appendix A.1
Theorem A.1 with its full block induction and final matrix (printed
pp.28–30; PDF pages 30–32). Also inspected the earlier
[CCC 2022 version](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol234-ccc2022/LIPIcs.CCC.2022.12/LIPIcs.CCC.2022.12.pdf),
Theorems 7–8, pp.12:6–7. The later revision explicitly extends formula
linearization to ABPs. Appendix A.1 starts with a layered ABP, a product
of compatible linear matrices. Its block operation sends diag(AB,I) to
[[0,A],[-B,I]], using polynomial upper/lower unitriangular multipliers;
induction linearizes the remaining nonlinear block. The resulting
transformation entries have polynomial-size ABPs (O(s²) in that proof).
Neither the statement nor this construction supplies designated scalar
simulation boundaries or total support across all scalar arrows. For
variable-sized input matrices we adopt no dimension-free reading of
§2's per-entry s notation. The proof's layered presentation and charges
must not be substituted for the original arbitrary DAG for free.
