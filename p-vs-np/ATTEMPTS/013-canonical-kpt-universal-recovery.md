# Canonical assignments in direct two-round KPT recovery

Tested 2026-09-27. The proposal substitutes a canonical satisfying-assignment circuit from a hypothetical polynomial-time SAT decider into the first response of a supplied two-round KPT solver-or-antichecker strategy, then restores a universal solver guarantee.

**WHY IT FAILS.** [L014](../lemmas/L014-canonical-kpt-recovery-guard.md) identifies the exact guard for recovering the first universal solver term independently of the second term: the selector must succeed whenever the first solver fails on a satisfiable input. This condition is weaker than correctness of the selector alone, but, for the explicitly checked evaluator, a polynomial proof bound for it is equivalent to polynomial boundedness of EF under external selector correctness. On an arbitrary unsatisfiable F it reduces to a refutation of F; the substituted first term itself is already true. The entire fixed first antichecker tuple can be handled with concrete comparison and assignment data, so losing those data is not the explanation. Polynomial substitution and concrete witness checking do not supply the missing guard proofs. This stops the direct substitution-plus-recovery argument. It does not prove that no other use of the adaptive strategy works, that the guard has long proofs, or that P differs from NP. The existential arithmetic premise and a suitable EF lower bound remain unresolved.
