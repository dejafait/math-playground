# L040 — Nonuniform class equality supplies aggregate faithful affine-ABP values

## Hypotheses

Use precisely L039's signed-AND ER convention, original-variable domain, explicit affine-F₂ ABP model and ordinary arbitrary-advice nonuniform classes. In particular T=2+|F|+|pi| includes the full binary original width-at-most-three CNF and supplied ER refutation, with all written identifiers and premise references. Extension values are recursively evaluated on **every** original Boolean assignment, whether or not it satisfies F. All graph, label, dictionary and root records are charged; circuit-valued edges and implicit graphs are excluded.

Assume the hypothetical equality P/poly=oplusL/poly. No uniform generation of advice or representatives is assumed.

## Conclusion

There is one fixed polynomial q, independent of F and pi, such that every such supplied ER refutation admits an explicitly framed collection of x-only affine-F₂ ABPs of aggregate binary length at most q(T) computing all positive and negative original-variable and extension values, and constants zero and one, on every original assignment. Thus L039's representative-only premise U holds, even without its used-ancestry restriction. Any subset of these required signed/base roots can be selected with no larger aggregate bound.

Together with L039, this gives the conditional characterization

    U if and only if P/poly=oplusL/poly.

This is a specialization of known circuit-value membership and nonuniform parity-program characterization with notebook-specific aggregate accounting; it is not a claimed discovery beyond the checked sources. Neither side is established unconditionally. No efficient representative constructor, formal local H identities, formal extension-polynomial equality, or SAT transfer follows. The separate informal critical audit of 2026-10-08 found no substantive error; no formal verification or certified originality is claimed.

**One fixed evaluator language.** Fix the following binary format once, independently of all ER instances. A word consists of a self-delimiting structural prefix followed by exactly n assignment bits. The prefix writes n, v, a selected output index r, and v topologically ordered vertex records. Integers are encoded as gamma(a+1) for nonnegative a; finite gate tags have fixed-size codes. Require v>=n+2. Vertices 1,...,n are input vertices in that order; vertices n+1,n+2 are constants 0,1. Each remaining vertex is tagged AND, OR or NOT and has the appropriate one or two predecessor indices strictly smaller than its own. Require 1<=r<=v and exactly n remaining bits after all records. Invalid encodings are rejected. Thus for any fixed legal prefix d and any x in {0,1}^n, d x is legal and has the same length; all variable coordinates lie in the suffix.

Call the resulting selected-output language Eval. Polynomial parsing first checks the integer lengths, vertex types, bounds and input length. A malformed count larger than the word length cannot force more iterations: a legal vertex/assignment count is at most the word length, and larger claimed counts can be rejected immediately. Dense indices have at most the word length in bits. On a legal word, its circuit and assignment can be converted to the book's explicit CVP representation in polynomial time and length, with the same selected output. This conversion merely rewrites records and input/output lists; it does not evaluate a circuit or unfold paths. The imported CVP membership theorem then decides Eval in polynomial time. A prefix with n=0 is permitted and gives the unique empty assignment to its constant/input-free circuit. This format change is the necessary specialization of the cited input, rather than a new circuit-evaluation theorem.

Since Eval is this single fixed language in P, foundations/02 and the hypothetical equality give Eval in oplusL/poly. Apply the imported characterization once to Eval. Choose a parity-program family (B_m) for all word lengths m, computing Eval on every m-bit word. By explicit configuration expansion where needed, these are finite acyclic multigraphs with edge labels 0,1,y_j,NOT y_j and designated endpoints. Their full binary descriptions have one nondecreasing polynomial majorant

    b(m)=A(m+2)^k,

for fixed integers A>=1 and k>=1 depending on Eval and the chosen family, not on an ER instance, root or description. Include dense vertex records, edge occurrences, literal coordinate indices and endpoints in this bound. The usual polynomial graph-size bound yields such a full-bit polynomial after dense renumbering and writing indices of O(log(m+V+E+2)) bits; increasing A,k absorbs the framing and all finite small lengths. No uniform algorithm for selecting B_m is asserted. This step does not introduce a separate language, exponent or advice family for each circuit.

**Shared circuit descriptions for all required roots.** For a supplied F,pi let n be the number of distinct original variables and e the number of extension definitions. There are n<=T and e<=T; moreover the total length of the distinct original identifier strings is at most T, since each occurs in F and every occurrence is charged there. Sort these strings by a fixed order and retain the resulting original-variable dictionary. Identify its i-th variable with x_i. Read extension definitions in their supplied introduction order. Their freshness and earlier-operand rule give a topological signed-AND circuit directly; no literal ancestry is unfolded and no allocation up to the largest numerical identifier is made.

Construct one ordinary Boolean circuit containing the n input vertices and constants 0,1. For each extension z iff (a AND b), represent a positive operand by its earlier positive vertex and a negative operand by a NOT vertex of that earlier positive vertex, then add one AND vertex for z. At most two new NOT vertices and one AND vertex are required per extension. After all positive extension vertices, append one NOT vertex for each positive original-variable or extension vertex to supply every negative root. Existing NOT vertices need not be shared to obtain the bound. The resulting vertex count satisfies

    v <= n+2+3e+(n+e) = 2n+4e+2 <= 6T+2.

Repeated operands, complementary operands, ignored inputs and arbitrarily shared extension ancestry are all represented by ordinary predecessor references. By induction over the definitions, the positive extension vertex has exactly its recursively defined Boolean value on every original assignment, and each appended negative root has its complementary value. This induction uses no clause satisfaction and no correctness/complexity property of the refutation. It is only the necessary signed-AND-to-CVP applicability check.

The root set consists of the two constants, all n positive and n negative input values, and all e positive and e negative extension values, hence

    K=2(n+e)+2 <= 4T+2.

It is a fixed finite set, not an uncharged extra list of arbitrary demanded outputs or repeated copies. Root aliases suffice for repeated uses. These roots cover L039's positive extensions and any required signed/base values. Use the same circuit records for each root and change only the selected output index. Let d_r be the evaluator prefix for root r and m_r=|d_r|+n. All integers in it are O(T+1), every record has a fixed number of fields, and there are O(T+1) records. Therefore, for one absolute encoding constant c>=1,

    m_r <= c(T+2)log₂(T+2) <= M(T):=c(T+2)^2

after increasing c if necessary. Take c integral so M(T) is integral. This bound is common to every supplied proof and every selected root. Binary identifiers of arbitrary magnitude are confined to the retained dictionary; they never become an implicit dense array or a larger evaluator coordinate. The evaluator prefix uses dense ranks only.

**Literal specialization and fidelity.** Choose B_(m_r). Fix all prefix coordinates to the actual bits of d_r and identify its j-th suffix coordinate with x_j. Replace a positive prefix literal by its fixed bit, a negative prefix literal by the opposite fixed bit, a positive suffix literal by x_j and a negative suffix literal by the explicit affine label 1+x_j. The labels 0,1 stay constant. Leave every vertex and edge occurrence in place. This is an explicit x-only affine-F₂ ABP A_r on the same acyclic graph, with the same endpoints; no circuit-valued label is used.

For every x, the value of each specialized affine edge on x equals the enabled-bit value of the corresponding edge of B_(m_r) on d_r x. L039's finite bit-path interface therefore gives

    A_r(x) = enabled-path parity of B_(m_r)(d_r x)
           = Eval(d_r x)
           = the specified root value on x.

This statement concerns Boolean evaluation. It does not assert equality to any particular free noncommutative polynomial for the extension. Constants obtained by substitution may produce zero edges, cancellation or unused vertices, which may simply be retained. Unequal path lengths, parallel occurrences, dead vertices and nonextremal/coincident endpoints retain L039's conventions; in particular substitution does not alter the empty-path contribution. Thus every original assignment is covered, including assignments violating F, and no satisfaction promise or reduction-closure theorem is used.

**Full aggregate encoding.** Each B_(m_r) has at most b(m_r)<=b(M(T)) vertex/edge records in a full encoding, up to an absolute fixed record-count constant absorbed into b. Retain only dense vertex identifiers and give each specialized graph an explicitly written copy of the n-entry original dictionary, even for variables absent from its labels. A label has at most a constant term and one dense original-variable entry, so no affine expansion creates a long label. Store each original identifier once in that dictionary, with self-delimiting string lengths. Its string payload is at most T; the count and n length fields cost O((T+2)log(T+2)) bits, bounded by O((T+2)^2).

The dense graph indices cost O(log(b(M(T))+2)) bits; variable indices cost O(log(T+2)). Write all edges separately, including parallel occurrences and zero labels, and write all vertex and endpoint records. Consequently a single specialized, separately framed graph costs at most

    C [ b(M(T)) log₂(b(M(T))+T+2) + (T+2)^2 ]
    <= C' [ b(M(T))+T+2 ]^2

bits for absolute encoding constants. This coarse square charges complete record descriptions, not just node count. Identifiers with long binary names have already been charged in the copied dictionary.

Write K such graph descriptions separately; no sharing or compression is required. Add a self-delimiting collection header and a root list recording each root's sign, original/extension identifier or constant tag, and graph index. K<=4T+2 and each written identifier has at most T bits, so even independently copying each root name and writing each graph index costs O((T+2)^2+(T+2)log(b(M(T))+T+2)). This fits the same aggregate square bound. Thus, increasing one constant C_0, the complete collection has at most

    q(T)=C_0(T+2) [ A(c(T+2)^2+2)^k + T+2 ]^2

bits. This is a single polynomial in T with fixed constants A,k,c,C_0. It covers every required root, every graph copy, labels, original dictionary, output names and framing. Root-specific word lengths need not be equal: the one nondecreasing b evaluated at the common M(T) bounds them all. No instance-dependent exponent is selected.

When n=0 the suffix is empty; the evaluator and substitution remain valid. All specialized labels are constants, and all original dictionaries are empty. One may alternatively represent each constant value by two distinct endpoints and one constant-labelled edge. Under the strict signed-earlier-literal ER convention, a proof with no original variables may have no extension at all because there is no first operand; then the two constant roots still meet the bound. Our evaluator also covers input-free constant circuits, so neither case needs a missing x_1 anchor. Small T and finite exceptional evaluator lengths are absorbed in the fixed majorants, not in proof-dependent constants. Empty extension collections and ignored input variables cause no additional case.

This establishes the claimed representative collection for all supplied proofs in L039's scope. Restricting to its genuinely used proofs yields U. The already established implication in L039 gives the final equivalence. No ancestry construction from L027, no H witness and no free-ring certificate identity is needed for the reverse direction.

**Separate critical audit — 2026-10-08.** The prior SPECIALIZE assessment `drafts/literature/2026-10-08-faithful-affine-abp-class-converse-critical-audit.md` authorized this audit. No substantive error was found, and no hypothesis, conclusion, polynomial bound or direct mathematical input is changed. The published inputs above are reused with their stated source/version and unread-proof qualifications. The following checks are of the local specialization, not a new evaluation or complexity-class theorem.

For a word of length N, a gamma field can be scanned and decoded using at most N bits of storage and polynomial time; an incomplete code is rejected. Before iterating through v records or reserving n assignment positions, compare each count to N and reject a larger count. Legal encodings have n<=N and v<=N because the assignment bits and explicit vertex records are written. This rejection therefore loses no legal word, even when a malformed field denotes an exponentially large integer. The fixed input/constant records and remaining gate tags have bounded descriptions; the two predecessor indices of a binary gate, the one index of a unary gate and the selected output are checked by binary comparisons. Counts, index validity, strict earlier references and the exact remaining suffix length can all be checked in polynomial time in N. In particular parsing does not evaluate the circuit, follow exponentially many paths or allocate up to an encoded integer. For a legal fixed d_r, every n-bit suffix yields the same valid parse and the same selected circuit, so the CVP format conversion is applicable to every such assignment. At n=0 the suffix is empty, and the two constant vertices still make a legal evaluator description possible.

The equality assumption is applied to this one language Eval only. Thus A and k in b(m) are fixed for all m before F, pi or r is selected. A polynomial-size explicit parity-program family admits polynomial binary framing: the finite configuration expansion used by the imported interface has polynomially many states and transition occurrences, each with a constant/literal label, and dense indices add logarithmic factors. No collection of exponentially many implicit parallel edges is being encoded. Increasing the single A,k absorbs those factors and finite exceptional lengths. Selecting B_(m_r) independently for different root lengths uses the same arbitrary-advice family, and does not select a new exponent or demand an effective procedure to find the family. Equal lengths use the same graph with different fixed prefix bits. The equality is not replaced by a uniform class equality or by efficient advice generation.

For the ER conversion, every distinct original identifier occurs in F, and every extension identifier occurs in a definition, so each written name has at most T bits and the sum of distinct original-name lengths is at most T. Sorting these strings and retaining their association with dense inputs is polynomial in the actual string lengths. The order need not agree with their numerical magnitudes. At a definition, a positive operand already has its input or positive-extension vertex; a negative operand needs at most one fresh NOT vertex before the new AND vertex. Repeated operands may be copied twice, and complementary operands use the same earlier positive vertex with opposite signs. Both cases obey the three-new-vertices bound. Constants are present even if unused. Appending n+e negations after all positive vertices supplies every remaining sign, with all predecessor indices still earlier. Hence v<=2n+4e+2 and K=2(n+e)+2 hold without unfolding shared ancestry or assuming that a definition is used. Every induction step is a Boolean operation on an arbitrary input assignment; the refutation and the truth of its original clauses play no semantic role. A legal proof with no original variables and no possible first signed operand has e=0, while the evaluator itself also handles input-free constant circuits. Empty extensions, input outputs and ignored original variables are included.

Every evaluator integer is bounded by a fixed multiple of T+2. Since gamma(a+1) has length 2 floor(log₂(a+1))+1, the O(T+1) fixed-arity records, headers and selected-root field cost O((T+2)log₂(T+2)); the n assignment positions add at most T. This verifies the common M(T)=c(T+2)^2 with fixed integral c. Selected-root codes can differ in length, shifting the suffix positions; the specialization uses the actual |d_r| for each graph, so it fixes exactly the appropriate prefix coordinates and retains exactly its n assignment coordinates.

At a fixed x, the substitution checks all four literal cases individually: y_j and NOT y_j in the prefix become d_r[j] and 1+d_r[j]; the corresponding suffix literals become x_i and 1+x_i. Constants remain constants. In each case the evaluated affine label equals the old enabled bit. The graph and its edge occurrences are unchanged, so every original path has the same product of evaluated labels and the finite sum over paths has the same parity. This remains true for zero edges, cancellation between parallel occurrences, repeated variables, unequal lengths and dead vertices. When the endpoints coincide, acyclicity leaves exactly the empty path and both evaluations give its value one. When distinct endpoints are unreachable, both give zero. Leaving a nonextremal sink cannot create an accepting path that returns to it in a DAG. No endpoint repair, path enumeration or polynomial-time reduction closure premise is needed.

For the complete ledger, put B=b(M(T)) and D=B+T+2. Explicit source graph framing bounds its vertex/edge record count by O(B). Dense vertex and endpoint indices have O(log(B+2)) bits. A specialized label writes at most one variable index, of O(log(T+2)) bits, and a constant coefficient; the negative literal 1+x_i has two explicit terms, not a circuit-valued label. The original dictionary is copied in full for each graph. Its payload is at most T; its n length fields and count occupy O((T+2)log(T+2)), even if one original name alone has almost T bits. Thus the graph cost is O(B log D+(T+2)^2)<=O(D^2). Count fields and self-delimiting graph-length headers have logarithmic length and fit this estimate. None of these estimates assumes absent variables can be dropped from the dictionary.

Writing K<=4T+2 such graph records costs O((T+2)D^2). The K root entries each contain a bounded tag, a name of at most T bits when applicable, and a graph index of O(log(T+2)) bits; their lengths and the collection count add at most O((T+2)^2+(T+2)log D). This is within the same aggregate bound. Consequently a fixed C_0 gives exactly the displayed q(T); neither root-name copying nor unequal m_r introduces an unbounded parameter. All framing constants depend only on the fixed encoding, while A,k depend on the one chosen evaluator family. If n=0 dictionaries are empty and the substitution is all constants. Small T and empty extension sets are absorbed by the same majorants.

Finally, U asks for Boolean-value fidelity. Separately selected positive and negative graph polynomials need not be formal complements, and a graph representing an original variable need not be the identical free polynomial x_i. This is sufficient for U and is not asserted to satisfy L037's exact signed/base conventions or formal H identities. The result is existential and allows arbitrary advice. Restricting its stronger all-supplied-proofs conclusion to used-ancestry proofs yields precisely U; only then is L039's already audited forward implication invoked for the equivalence. The direct local input remains L039. L027's ancestry construction is not used by this converse, and the stopped prescribed-output routes are not reopened.
