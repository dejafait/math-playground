# L107 — Opaque-OR soundness and an exact source endpoint obstruction

## Hypotheses

Use finite source trees generated by legal variable names, the distinct
constants `true`,`false`, unary ¬ and primitive binary ∨. Names and trees
are those parsed by `scripts/parity-source/checker.py`; parentheses and
literal child order are retained. Define A→B **as the exact tree** ¬A∨B,
and A∧B as ¬(¬A∨¬B). The only assumption-free axioms are arbitrary
uniform formula instances of

    A→(B→A),
    (¬A→¬B)→((¬A→B)→A),
    (A→(B→C))→((A→B)→(A→C)),

together with the two ground formulas true and ¬false. The only rule
takes exactly A and A→B on earlier lines and writes B. There is no
inference allowing semantic rewriting, substitution of an already
proved formula, or additional primitive-∨ rule. Schematic instantiation
is allowed on axioms with arbitrary legal formula arguments. This is
the serialized interface tested here, as opposed to a claim about all
presentations of ordinary Frege.

For the endpoint fix the canonical forbidden-assignment clauses, in order,
of the three rows x+y=0, y+z=0, x+z=1 over F₂:

    C₀=x∨¬y,  C₁=¬x∨y,  C₂=y∨¬z,
    C₃=¬y∨z,  C₄=x∨z,   C₅=¬x∨¬z.

Fix the actual conjunction tree

    F=(C₀∧(C₁∧C₂))∧((C₃∧C₄)∧C₅).

All abbreviations are literally expanded. Its requested endpoint is
exactly ¬F, the retained `three-row` endpoint in
`scripts/parity-source/output/report.json` at
`/fixtures/three-row/endpoint_diagnostic/exact_formula`.

## Conclusion

There is one globally defined, collision-free syntax-dependent evaluation
under which every accepted axiom instance has value 1 and exact MP
preserves value 1, but this exact ¬F has value 0. Consequently no finite
assumption-free proof accepted by the specified interface ends at ¬F.
Under ordinary Boolean semantics F is unsatisfiable, so this presentation
is incomplete on its unrestricted primitive-∨ syntax.

This is an obstruction to the precise source presentation/template
reception, stronger than a failed synthesis experiment. It does not
establish an ordinary-Frege lower bound, refute the existence of alternative
occurrence-product witnesses, or settle any SAT complexity question.
It does not supply or approve a repaired basis or extraction.

## Proof

**1. A global evaluation on all legal trees.** Let N be the set of legal
names except true and false. Let O be the set of all legal trees
o=(A∨B) whose literal left child A is **not** a negation node. Introduce
the disjoint tagged atom sets

    V={ (source,a) : a∈N },
    W={ (opaque,o) : o∈O }.

These are mathematical pairs, not additional strings supplied to the
source parser. Distinct tags prevent collisions with every legal source
name, including names of the form `opaque_0`. Equality within W is
equality of the complete original source tree; no per-line allocation or
substitution-dependent freshness is used.

For any one assignment ν:V⊔W→{0,1}, recursively define eν on legal
source trees by

    eν(true)=1,                  eν(false)=0,
    eν(a)=ν(source,a)            (a∈N),
    eν(¬A)=1−eν(A),
    eν((¬A)∨B)=(1−eν(A)) OR eν(B),
    eν(o)=ν(opaque,o)            (o∈O).

Here OR on values is ordinary Boolean disjunction. The two binary cases
are disjoint and exhaustive, and every recursive call is on a proper
subtree. Thus the definition is total and unambiguous on all finite
legal trees. It gives identical values to identical subtrees in every
proof line, including when the same subtree occurs inside other opaque
trees. Such an opaque parent's value is independent of its children's
values; this is intentional, not ordinary truth-functional ∨ semantics.

In particular, for **every** legal A,B, including negative formulas,
constants and formulas containing opaque ORs,

    eν(¬A)=1−eν(A),
    eν(A→B)=(1−eν(A)) OR eν(B).                 (1)

The second identity holds because the constructed implication has the
literal left child ¬A. Even if A is already a negation, that child is
still a negation and recognition recovers precisely the original A.
Likewise, literal expansion of conjunction gives

    eν(A∧B)=eν(A) AND eν(B).                    (2)

Indeed the inner OR is (¬A)∨(¬B), so its value by (1) is
(1−eν(A)) OR (1−eν(B)); the outer negation gives (2).

**2. Every axiom instance and rule.** Fix any ν once for the whole proof.
Let a=eν(A), b=eν(B), c=eν(C) for arbitrary legal substitution arguments.
Only (1) is needed on the scheme skeleton; no claim that eν commutes
with arbitrary source substitution is made or needed.

For the first scheme, if a=0 its outer implication is true; if a=1,
the consequent B→A is true. For the second scheme, if a=1 its
consequent (¬A→B)→A is true. If a=0, then ¬A→¬B has value 1−b,
and (¬A→B)→A also has value 1−b. The outer implication is therefore
an implication of a bit to itself and is true. For the third scheme,
suppose both A→(B→C) and A→B have value 1. If a=0 then A→C is
true. If a=1 the second premise gives b=1; the first then gives c=1,
so A→C is again true. This proves the whole third implication true in
all cases; when either of the stated premises is false its corresponding
implication is true. Thus all three schemes have value 1 under every
assignment and every syntactically matched substitution.

The ground axioms have values eν(true)=1 and eν(¬false)=1. For exact
MP, eν(A)=1 and eν(A→B)=1 imply eν(B)=1 by (1). The checker's
strictly earlier references therefore permit induction on the finite
accepted record list: each line has value 1 under every ν. Its pattern
matcher requires identical repeated schematic arguments, so the same
a,b,c are used at every scheme occurrence. Declared substitutions add
no inference rule. The final endpoint comparison requires literal tree
identity and cannot replace this invariant by semantic equivalence.

This specializes the standard proof-induction independence method:
Joseph R. Shoenfield, *Mathematical Logic*, Addison-Wesley (1967),
Validity Theorem, p.23, and alternative-evaluation Problems 5(a)–(i),
pp.24–25 ([primary scan](https://w2.cs.uni-saarland.de/op/fullpaper/logics/SHOENFIELD_Mathematical_logic.pdf)).
Those exercises are not imported as proofs of this interpretation.
The explicit checks above are necessary because the syntax-sensitive
evaluation and exact local rule set are different. The basis comparison
uses Fu Li, Iddo Tzameret and Zhengyu Wang, *Characterizing Propositional
Proofs as Non-Commutative Formulas*, SIAM J. Comput. 47(4), 1424–1462
(2018), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1107632),
[40-page author manuscript](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~itzamere/Ideal_non-commutative.pdf),
p.2 displayed schemes and Definitions 2.2–2.4 p.11. No completeness
theorem for the exact local interface is imported from that display.
Primary passages were inspected in step 421 and their unchanged coverage
is reused here; the manuscript's revision date remains unestablished.

**3. The exact endpoint.** Choose one global ν₀ assigning 0 to every
source atom in V and 1 to every opaque atom in W. In particular the
three distinct trees x∨¬y, y∨¬z and x∨z belong to O and have value 1.
The other three clauses are respectively x→y, y→z and x→¬z.
Their antecedent values are 0, so each has value 1. Thus

    (eν₀(C₀),…,eν₀(C₅))=(1,1,1,1,1,1).

By (2), following the exact written conjunction tree gives eν₀(F)=1
and eν₀(¬F)=0. Section 2 proves that no accepted finite proof can
have this last formula. The restriction of ν₀ to the saved diagnostic's
visible atoms is exactly x=y=z=0 and all its three opaque tokens 1;
replacing those token strings by disjoint tagged trees removes the
diagnostic's global-name collision risk without changing this evaluation.

For comparison, ordinary Boolean satisfaction of C₀,C₁ forces x=y,
and that of C₂,C₃ forces y=z. The pair C₄,C₅ forces x≠z.
Hence F has no ordinary satisfying assignment and ¬F is an ordinary
tautology. This uses the original clauses and actual tree, with no
normalization or replacement endpoint. The script below reconstructs
that exact tree from the saved original fixture, compares it to the
complete saved endpoint string, and verifies the six values and endpoint
under the explicitly defined tagged interpretation. This finite check
corroborates the endpoint computation; the universal invariant is the
all-instance argument in §§1–2, not enumeration of a finite formula set.

**4. Consequences and limits.** The earlier component failure alone
did not establish nonderivability. Sections 1–3 now establish it for
this exact interface. In particular ordinary completeness cannot justify
arbitrary literal-OR tautology templates in this presentation. L097's
§3 completeness invocation, L098's guarded-template invocation and
L106's general exact-source proof compilation therefore remain
unsupported as written. The obstruction concerns those intermediate
source arguments; it neither refutes their affine-witness existence
statements by other methods nor certifies another presentation or bridge.
All previously saved proof strings and failed attempts are preserved.

**5. Independent critical audit — step 424, 2026-10-23.** The audit
reuses the approved SPECIALIZE comparison in
`drafts/literature/2026-10-23-opaque-or-critical-audit.md` and the precise
primary citations in §2. It checks the universal argument independently
of the finite certificate. No rule, endpoint, bound or theorem statement
has been changed.

The parser admits nonempty strings of alphanumeric characters and
underscores as leaves, including Unicode names and names beginning with
digits; only the exact strings true and false are constants. The proof
does not restrict names to the fixture alphabet or ASCII. Each such
nonconstant string is a source-tagged atom. An opaque atom instead has
an opaque tag and a complete parsed tuple tree. Hence even a source
name such as opaque_0 cannot coincide with an opaque atom. Structural
tuple equality, including literal child order, fixes the same opaque
value across all lines. An occurrence inside a larger opaque parent
does not impose a second evaluation of that subtree: the parent simply
does not consult it. This is consistent with a single total assignment.
The disjoint negated-left/other-left cases exhaust all parsed OR nodes.

For a scheme argument A=¬D, the constructed implication's left child
is ¬¬D, and recognition removes just its outer negation to recover A.
Thus (1) holds for negative arguments as well as for arbitrary nested
opaque trees and constants. The conjunction expansion always has a
negated literal left child at its inner OR, so (2) holds regardless of
either argument's shape. Neither fact requires substitution invariance
of the evaluation.

The checker's ax1, axS and ax2 patterns are respectively the first,
second and third displayed schemes. The matcher treats only their
schematic leaves as variables and requires repeated arguments to be
identical trees; declared substitution strings must parse to exactly
those matched trees. The first scheme is true for both values of a.
For axS, a=1 makes its final consequent true; for a=0 the outer
antecedent and consequent both equal 1−b. For ax2, possible falsity
would require a=1, c=0 and A→B true, hence b=1, which makes its
outer antecedent A→(B→C) false. These exhaust all bit values and
therefore all legal schematic arguments, not merely a tested formula
pool. The only additional accepted axiom branches require exactly
true or ¬false. An unrecognized rule reaches pattern rejection; there
is no assumption, rewriting or proved-formula substitution branch.

Both MP indices must be integers strictly less than the current record
index, and the second referenced tree must equal implies(first,current).
Consequently (1) and induction on the actual record list apply even
with unused records, repeated references or shared subtrees. The final
checker comparison spells the last parsed tree and compares it exactly
to the requested endpoint. A semantically equivalent last line cannot
evade the invariant. Transforming a whole proof by uniform variable
substitution, as in L098 §2, is compatible with its syntactic rules;
this fact does not introduce a new inference or supply missing templates.

For the endpoint, the actual fixture has λ=(1,1,1): each of x,y,z
occurs twice in the selected rows, and their right sides sum to 1.
The lexicographic forbidden assignments give precisely C₀,…,C₅ in
§3 and the stored global tree is [[0,[1,2]],[[3,4],5]]. The audit
inspected the complete saved endpoint, all three opaque-tree mappings,
all six clause values and all eight ordinary assignments. In ν₀ the
three positive-left clauses are opaque and true; each other clause
has a zero antecedent. The five literal conjunction nodes therefore
make F true and its outer negation false. In ordinary semantics the
first two clause pairs force x=y=z and the last forces x≠z; this
independent comparison agrees with every saved truth-table row.

Rerunning `python3 scripts/parity-source/opaque_or_soundness_check.py`
reproduced the retained certificate byte-for-byte: 7/7 required checks,
actual endpoint value 0, required value 0, no failures. This corroborates
the literal fixture reconciliation, not universal rule soundness. The
certificate explicitly makes no such universal claim. Its diagnostic
tokens are reconciled only on this fixture; collision freedom globally
comes from the tags in §1, not those token strings.

The three qualifications have distinct strengths. L097 §3 and L098 §3
invoke completeness to supply additional literal-OR templates, which
this exact presentation does not justify. The audit does not assert
that every particular template fails, or that their affine-witness
conclusions are false by other methods. A supplied proof in L098 does
not discharge its separate template construction. For L106 the canonical
three-row source itself satisfies the parity and exact-CNF conditions,
yet its exact endpoint is unprovable here; thus its universal source
compiler cannot retain this interface. The finite fixture does not
implement the L087 occurrence pair, provenance or original-Q ledger,
and no claim about those exports is inferred from it. Ordinary Frege
in a justified complete presentation remains outside the obstruction.

Verdict: the all-instance invariant, exact endpoint and stated limited
consequences survive the audit. This verifies the existing local result;
it adds no new mathematical separation, witness bound or certified
originality. The unchanged exact-interface compiler remains stopped.
