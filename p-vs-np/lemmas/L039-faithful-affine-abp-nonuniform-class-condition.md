# L039 — Universal faithful affine-ABP values imply a nonuniform class equality

## Hypotheses

Use the notebook's fresh signed-AND ER convention: z iff (a AND b), for signed earlier literals, introduces (NOT z OR a), (NOT z OR b), (z OR NOT a OR NOT b). Clauses are sorted sets; repeated literals are removed but complementary literals are retained. Repeated operands, weakening, selected-pivot resolution and explicit earlier-premise references are permitted. Let T=2+|F|+|pi| be the full binary length of an original width-at-most-three CNF and its supplied ER refutation. Original variables are precisely those occurring in F. An extension's value is its recursively defined Boolean function on **every** original assignment, regardless of whether that assignment satisfies F.

Assume the following hypothetical representative-only premise U. There is one fixed polynomial q, independent of F and pi, such that for every such supplied refutation in which every extension has a defining clause and a derived signed unit in the final inference ancestry, there is an explicitly framed collection of x-only affine-F₂ ABPs representing all its extension values (and any required signed/base values), of aggregate binary length at most q(T). Each representative agrees with its specified extension on every original Boolean assignment. No efficient constructor, formal local relation witness, certificate identity or degree promise is assumed. A stronger premise covering all supplied refutations also satisfies U. The proof in fact needs only a bound q(T) for the designated output representative in each constructed instance.

Each ABP is an explicit finite acyclic directed multigraph with designated source and sink; its edge labels are explicitly written finite affine linear forms over F₂ in original variables. Its free-algebra polynomial is the sum of ordered path products, including the empty path when endpoints coincide. Constants, zero labels, parallel edges, unequal path lengths, dead vertices and nonextremal endpoints are allowed. Vertex/edge records, all label entries, original-variable dictionary, roots and framing are charged; implicit graphs and circuit-valued labels are excluded.

Here P/poly and oplusL/poly have their ordinary **nonuniform** meanings. In particular oplusL/poly permits arbitrary polynomial-length advice, not necessarily polynomial-time-generated advice. We use its known equivalent characterization by polynomial-size parity branching-program families. A parity program accepts when its number of enabled source-to-sink paths is odd; its acyclic multigraph edges have labels 0,1,x_i or NOT x_i, enabled according to the input bits.

## Conclusion

Under U,

    P/poly = oplusL/poly.                                    (1)

More quantitatively, every n-input Boolean circuit with s gates over binary AND/OR, NOT and constants, n>=1, is the all-assignment value of a positive extension h in a genuinely used ER refutation with G<=s+2 circuit extensions, 2G total extensions and at most R_*=11G+n+2 proof records. Other fixed finite binary bases first undergo a constant-factor basis conversion. For the explicit full-bit encoding used in L027,

    T <= 128(R_*+n+3) ceil(log₂(R_*+2)).                      (2)

U then supplies an ABP of at most q(T) bits for h, and a parity program with O((q(T)+n+2)log(q(T)+n+2)) bits computes the same circuit function. All constants in these encoding bounds are fixed by the explicit encoding; there is no circuit-dependent exponent in U.

This is a conditional necessary condition on the faithful subroute, obtained by specializing established parity-BP/class comparisons and L027's ancestry mechanism. Neither U nor a separation between the classes in (1) is proved. It is not a P-versus-NP candidate, a uniform class equality, a general reduced-data construction or a formal-certificate simulation. The separate informal critical audit of 2026-10-08 found no substantive error; no certified originality or mechanically verified proof strings are claimed.

**Arbitrary circuits as signed-AND values.** Let C have n>=1 inputs and s gates over fan-in-two AND/OR, NOT and constants. This basis suffices for the ordinary definition of P/poly; XOR or any fixed finite binary basis can first be replaced by constant-size circuits over it. Enumerate C topologically. Introduce a fresh gate g_0 iff (x_1 AND NOT x_1), with all-assignment value zero; represent constant zero by g_0 and constant one by NOT g_0. An original input is its own signed-literal alias. A NOT gate only flips the sign of the alias of its predecessor. For an AND gate with predecessor aliases a,b introduce a fresh gate z iff (a AND b) and use z as its output alias. For an OR gate introduce z iff (NOT a AND NOT b) and use NOT z as its output alias. Repeated and complementary aliases are allowed. Finally, if ell is the circuit output alias, introduce the positive root h iff (ell AND ell). Every operand refers to an original input or an earlier extension. Induction on the circuit gates, using De Morgan's law and b AND b=b on bits, proves h(x)=C(x) on every assignment.

Let g_1,...,g_G enumerate all these positive extension gates in order, including the zero gate and h. Then 2<=G<=s+2. Constant-output, input-output and NOT-only circuits are included. The zero-input slice of a language has only one string and can be handled by a constant program/circuit, so it does not affect either class.

**The supplied ER proof and genuinely used ancestry.** Set

    F=(x_1) AND ... AND (x_n) AND (NOT x_1).

Every input is an original variable occurring in this width-one CNF, including circuit inputs that C ignores. The first n units specify the all-one assignment only for deriving signed gate units. They do not restrict the assignments on which U demands faithful extension values. In particular U is not vacuous merely because F is contradictory.

For each g_k compute its value at the all-one assignment topologically and write epsilon_k=g_k when that value is one, NOT g_k otherwise. Introduce the three defining clauses of g_k and derive its unit (epsilon_k). For z iff (a AND b), if a,b both evaluate to one, their already available true units resolve with (z OR NOT a OR NOT b) to produce (z), in at most two steps. If a=b, duplicate-literal deletion leaves one pivot and one step suffices. Complementary operands cannot be in this true case. If a evaluates to zero, resolve its true unit (NOT a) with (NOT z OR a) to obtain (NOT z); use the second projection if b is the selected false operand. Negative operand aliases are treated by their actual signs. Freshness of z prevents any pivot from deleting the output literal. These cases cover the zero gate too, whose opposite operands put it in the false branch. Each derived unit thus has one of its own gate's defining clauses as an ancestor.

Introduce fresh accumulator gates a_1,...,a_G, with a_0=x_1 an alias, by

    a_k iff (a_(k-1) AND epsilon_k).

The operands have distinct underlying variables: one is an original anchor or accumulator and the other is a circuit gate. From the third defining clause, (a_(k-1)) and (epsilon_k), derive (a_k) in two selected-pivot resolutions. From the first projection (NOT a_k OR a_(k-1)) and (NOT a_(k-1)), derive (NOT a_k) in one resolution. Start the positive and negative chains with the respective original units (x_1),(NOT x_1), and finish by resolving (a_G),(NOT a_G) to the empty clause.

All premises precede their uses, and every definition is fresh. Tracing the positive final unit backwards reaches every epsilon_k and every positive accumulator unit. Thus every circuit extension's derived unit and one of its defining clauses, and every accumulator's derived unit and third defining clause, are in the final inference ancestry. The negative chain also uses each accumulator's first projection. In particular the actual signed value of h is an operand in the final accumulator. Unused individual defining-clause slots do not constitute unused extension definitions. There is an immediate alternative refutation of F, but U concerns this supplied proof; no optimal-proof hardness is claimed.

The record count is n+1 initial units, 6G defining clauses, at most 2G circuit-unit resolutions, 2G positive-accumulator resolutions, G negative-accumulator resolutions, and one final resolution, at most R_*=11G+n+2. Every clause has width at most three, every identifier is at most n+2G, and every reference is at most R_*. L027's explicit gamma-coded record ledger therefore applies unchanged and gives (2). This is a polynomial-bit construction in n+s, without any truth-table enumeration. Topological evaluation at one fixed assignment costs polynomial time. All accumulators also have all-assignment recursive values; the construction makes no fidelity claim for representatives until U is applied.

**Affine labels and the parity interface.** For completeness, this is the local model specialization needed before applying the source characterization. Let B be the full bit length of one supplied ABP and let an edge u->v have label c+sum_j c_j x_j. Collect repeated entries modulo two, deleting zero coefficients. Replace that edge by parallel u->v edges labelled 1 if c=1 and labelled x_j for each nonzero c_j. A zero affine label produces no edge. For any Boolean input x, the parity of the enabled replacements equals the original affine label's F₂ value. The replacement choices along a fixed original path are independent, so the parity of its lifted enabled paths is the product of these values. Summing over all original paths proves that the new program's enabled-path parity is the original path polynomial evaluated on bits. This uses finite distributivity in F₂; it does not infer equality of arbitrary free-ring polynomials from bit agreement. Repeated variables on paths cause no difficulty, and original parallel edges retain their occurrence multiplicities.

Normalize the endpoints by adding a fresh source s' with a 1-edge to the original designated source s and a fresh sink t' with a 1-edge from the original designated sink t. No edge enters s' or leaves t'. There is a bijection between new accepting paths and original s-to-t paths by deleting these two connector edges. A path that leaves t internally cannot return to t in a DAG to take its exit connector; similarly no path starting at s can return to s. Consequently nonextremal endpoints introduce no additional accepting paths. If s=t, acyclicity forbids a nonempty s-to-s path, and the two connectors realize exactly the original empty path of value one. Unreachable endpoints give zero; zero labels and dead vertices can be retained or deleted. All replacements preserve acyclicity.

Explicit framing bounds the original vertex count, edge count and label-entry count by O(B). The replacement has O(B+2) vertices and edges. Renumber vertices densely and retain the original x_1,...,x_n dictionary; the full encoding is O((B+n+2)log(B+n+2)) bits, conservatively including all n inputs even if absent from its labels. Coefficient collection, record copying and renumbering take polynomial bit time. No complete path list is written. Conversely a literal parity edge labelled NOT x_i can be read as the affine label 1+x_i; positive literals and constants already are affine. The same endpoint convention and finite distributivity show equality of bit functions in this direction with polynomial full framing. Thus the two explicit Boolean-function interfaces match; formal certificate equality is a different requirement.

**One polynomial and the class quantifiers.** Let L be any language in P/poly. Choose its circuits C_n with s_n<=p(n) for one polynomial p depending on L. The construction above yields supplied used-extension proofs with T_n bounded by one polynomial in n (for this fixed language), by (2). Apply U and select h's representative, of B_n<=q(T_n) bits. If the collection also includes signed representatives, selecting one root cannot increase its bit length. Because the same q applies to every n and every supplied proof, B_n and the resulting parity-program encodings are polynomial in n. They compute C_n on all n-bit inputs, not merely inputs satisfying F. The imported nonuniform parity-BP characterization gives L in oplusL/poly. Arbitrary choices of these polynomial graphs are permitted as nonuniform advice; no constructor or common uniform algorithm is needed. This proves P/poly subseteq oplusL/poly. An exponent chosen separately for each circuit or input length would not establish this membership and is not U.

For the reverse containment, take a polynomial-size parity-program family supplied by the same characterization. Normalize to fresh endpoints as above. Fix a topological ordering for each graph. The start value is 1, and the value at each other vertex v is the XOR, over its incoming edges u->v, of the predecessor value AND the edge's input-literal/constant value. Partitioning enabled paths by their last edge proves this recurrence by induction; the final value is their parity. One AND per edge and a binary XOR tree for each incoming list give O(V+E+n) Boolean gates, since XOR has a constant-size implementation over AND/OR/NOT and input negations may be shared. No accepting paths are enumerated. These graphs and orders are nonuniform circuit descriptions, with polynomial full encoding and fan-in-two gate count. Hence oplusL/poly subseteq P/poly, proving (1).

**Qualifications.** The test survives with explicit polynomial full-bit overhead, so (1) is a necessary condition for U. The equality is neither contradicted nor proved unconditionally here. This does not assert ordinary uniform P=oplusL, even if some representation algorithm were polynomial-time: polynomial-time graph generation is not logspace generation. It also supplies no formal H_i identities, no representatives restricted independently of original clauses, no certificate for a hard formula, and no SAT transfer. Boolean agreement is used only for bit-function complexity. The formal requirements of L037/L038 remain separate, and their nonfaithful alternatives are not ruled out.

**Separate critical audit — 2026-10-08.** The approved SPECIALIZE audit found no substantive error and changes no hypothesis, conclusion, bound or mathematical dependency. The following checks concern the existing argument, not a new class theorem or a reproof of the imported characterization.

For basis normalization, the zero gate exists even when s=0, NOT changes only an alias, each AND/OR costs one extension, and the repeated-output gate is always fresh, giving 2<=G<=s+2. On a constant-zero circuit h has value zero, so its derived unit is NOT h; the accumulator still uses that actual unit. Constant-one, original-input and negated-input outputs likewise retain the correct positive root function. Distinct true operands require exactly the two indicated pivots; identical true operands require one; complementary operands necessarily use a false projection. A negative signed operand uses the opposite unit with its actual sign. No case needs a unit contradicting the chosen all-one evaluation of a circuit gate. For example the zero gate's false operand is NOT x_1, discharged by the original positive unit x_1, not by a semantic use of the inconsistent anchor.

Each positive accumulator derivation has two distinct operand variables, so neither resolution can accidentally skip epsilon_k. Backward induction from a_G therefore visits all circuit-unit derivations, including gates irrelevant to C's output, and a defining clause of every such gate. It also visits every positive accumulator unit and third clause. The negative chain uses one first projection per accumulator. Both chains occur in the final resolution ancestry. No claim requires all three clauses of each definition to be used. All n input variables occur in F, and the positive/negative units constrain only the syntactic derivation: no assignment is removed from U's fidelity domain.

For the ledger, the record sum is (n+1)+6G+2G+2G+G+1=R_*. The variable maximum n+2G and every reference/header integer are at most R_* (clause sizes are at most three and G>=2). With L=ceil(log₂(R_*+2)), each gamma-coded integer occupies at most 2L+1 bits. A width-three clause occupies at most 8L+7 bits, including size and signs. A resolution record adds two kind bits, two references and a signed pivot, totaling at most 14L+13<=27L; initial and extension records are smaller. Thus 64L per record is conservative. The two F headers and one proof header occupy at most 6L+3 bits, and F's n+1 unit clauses occupy at most (n+1)(4L+3). Adding the defining constant 2 in T is bounded by 128(R_*+n+3)L as claimed. There is no omitted separate circuit-description cost: the signed operands are recoverable from each fresh definition's projection clauses, including repeated and complementary operands.

For affine normalization, regard each original edge occurrence separately. A lifted accepting path projects to a unique original path, and the sum over the replacement choices of that path is the product of its affine labels evaluated on the fixed input. These finite sums are valid even when two parallel occurrences cancel, an edge becomes empty after coefficient collection, or a variable occurs repeatedly. The new source has only its connector as an outgoing edge and the new sink only its connector as an incoming edge. An accepting path cannot pass through t, leave it and return, by acyclicity; it therefore corresponds to exactly one original s-to-t path. When s=t this path must be empty, giving one enabled connector path, not zero. When distinct endpoints are unreachable the count is zero. Dead and nonextremal vertices do not affect this correspondence. Each nonzero label term costs an explicit input entry, so neither expansion nor retention of the n-input dictionary conceals an exponential graph or label.

Finally, for each fixed L in P/poly, its own circuit polynomial p and the universal q give a fixed polynomial bound on the graphs across all lengths. If necessary replace a polynomial upper bound by a nondecreasing polynomial majorant before composition; no exponent depends on an individual circuit or length. A representative may be retained with its entire supplied collection/dictionary, still within q(T), so root extraction does not presuppose a cheaper encoding. A finite zero-length exception can be hardwired. The cited characterization permits arbitrary advice; no computable graph-selection function is used. Conversely the fresh-source path recurrence initializes unreachable vertices with zero (the empty XOR) and sums every incoming edge occurrence separately, using polynomial Boolean gates and preserving cancellations. These checks establish precisely the two containments claimed under U. They establish no converse to U, no U construction and no formal H identity.
