# L046 — Pathwise-linear replacement of an arbitrary supplied affine-ABP IPS certificate

## Hypotheses

Work in the free associative algebra over F₂. Let F be an explicitly encoded width-at-most-three CNF with its written clause and literal orders, including repetitions and complementary literals. Its original variable dictionary lists exactly its original identifiers. For each written clause j let c_j be the ordered product of literal falsity factors v(x_i)=1+x_i and v(¬x_i)=x_i; the empty product is 1. Let A be the ordered list of these c_j followed by all original Boolean axioms x_i²+x_i and commutators x_i x_k+x_k x_i for i<k. Write t_a for the independent placeholder of axiom A_a. Distinct clause occurrences retain distinct placeholders.

Supply an explicit finite acyclic affine-F₂ ABP C over x,t with designated entrance s and exit z. Its polynomial is the sum of ordered edge-label products over all s-to-z paths, with the empty path contributing 1. Labels explicitly list their constant bit and symbol coefficients or occurrences. Parallel and zero edges, dead vertices, unequal path lengths, nonextremal and coincident designated endpoints, mixed labels, arbitrarily many placeholder edges on a path, and cancellation are allowed. No implicit graph or circuit-valued edge is allowed. Assume the formal free-algebra identities

    C(x,0)=0,                    C(x,A(x))=1.              (1)

Fix an ordinary explicit binary list encoding. Put Q=2+|F|+|C|+|A|, including complete names, alphabets, axiom/placeholder dictionaries, label entries, graph records, roots and framing. Every declared item must actually be written. In particular a binary count does not encode an implicit range of unwritten variables or edges.

## Conclusion

There is a deterministic polynomial-time construction of an explicit acyclic affine-F₂ ABP D using the same original variable and axiom-placeholder dictionaries, such that

    D(x,0)=0,                    D(x,A(x))=1,              (2)

and every accepting path of D has exactly one placeholder-bearing edge. That edge has a singleton placeholder label; all other edges have labels 1 or one original variable. For one absolute constant K depending only on the fixed encoding, the full output length, including the unchanged complete axiom list and dictionaries, is at most KQ⁴. D need not compute the same free polynomial as C before substitution. The constructor does not test (1).

This is a conditional representation result for a supplied graph. It supplies the pathwise input promise used in L045, but no downstream composition is proved here. It constructs no ABP from an arbitrary ER proof, establishes no universal witness bound or ER polynomial boundedness, and settles no unrestricted SAT question. A separate critical audit on 2026-10-10 found no substantive error; its all-input checks are recorded below. POTENTIALLY_NEW is the construction's assessment classification; the audit is REPRODUCTION, and certified originality is not claimed.

## Proof

**Source scope and reused input.** The prior EXPLORE assessment is `drafts/literature/2026-10-10-arbitrary-abp-pathwise-linear-replacement.md`. Forbes–Shpilka–Tzameret–Wigderson, [*Proof Complexity Lower Bounds from Algebraic Circuit Complexity*, Theory of Computing 17(10) (2021)](https://toc.cs.uchicago.edu/articles/v017a010/v017a010.pdf), §4.1 Proposition 4.4 and its supporting lemmas, pp.24–28, provides the inspected commutative formula/circuit linearization comparison; Proposition 4.5, pp.30–31, uses matrix telescoping for same-order roABP multilinearization. Grochow–Pitassi, [*Circuit complexity, proof complexity, and polynomial identity testing: The ideal proof system*, author manuscript](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~toni/Papers/jacm-gp.pdf), §3.6 Proposition 3.7, pp.1:24–25, gives the inspected sparse commutative Hilbert-like comparison. None is invoked as a theorem about the present free-algebra receiving graph. The necessary new verification is the ordered identity and explicit graph/bit implementation below, not a reproof of those theorems. L041 supplies the bounded ordered axiom gadgets and their substitution convention. Only those unconditional constructions from L041 are used, not its unproved universal premise W or its NP=coNP consequence.

**Normalization and two x-only graphs.** Collect each affine label's coefficients modulo two. Split its nonzero constant and symbol terms into distinct parallel edges with singleton labels 1, x_i or t_a; delete a zero label. Preserve distinct original edge occurrences. Let G be the resulting DAG with the original vertices and designated endpoints. Finite distributivity identifies its path polynomial with C as a free polynomial. This step does not assume that any individual placeholder-free path vanishes, or that an accepting path has bounded placeholder degree.

Let G_0 be G with every placeholder edge deleted. For any original vertices u,v, its u-to-v path polynomial is precisely the zero substitution of G's u-to-v polynomial, since this substitution is an algebra homomorphism applied to a finite sum.

Construct G_A by replacing each placeholder edge u→v labelled t_a with a separate bounded x-only gadget computing A_a. For a clause, expand only that one ordered product: it has at most eight words, each of length at most three. Use parallel word paths, retaining multiplicities, and a 1-edge for an empty word. For x_i²+x_i use its two word paths; for x_i x_k+x_k x_i use its two ordered word paths. No empty word identifies u and v: it is an edge. All internal vertices are fresh for each gadget occurrence. These are L041's bounded gadgets, now also used for the original clause axioms. No expansion over several clauses or over complete graph paths occurs.

Every accepting path through one such gadget traverses exactly one of its word paths. Collapsing these traversals to the corresponding edge gives a path in G; distributing its substituted labels over all such choices proves that every original u-to-v polynomial of G_A is the A-substitution of the corresponding polynomial of G. This holds also when u=v, since the sole internal path then is the empty path in both acyclic graphs. Original edges move forward in a topological ordering, and fresh gadget vertices can be placed strictly between their edge's endpoints; hence G_A is acyclic. Cancellations among duplicate word paths are handled by addition in F₂, not by Boolean simplification.

**Ordered finite identity.** Let E_t be the set of placeholder edges in G. For e:u→v with label t_a, define the x-only polynomials

    P_e = path polynomial of G_0 from s to u,
    S_e = path polynomial of G_A from v to z.

Define, for now algebraically,

    D(x,t) = sum_(e in E_t) P_e t_a S_e.                 (3)

Here all placeholders in every suffix have already been substituted; only the displayed central t_a remains free. To verify its substituted value, partition G's s-to-z paths into those with no placeholder edge and those with at least one. Each path of the latter type has a unique first placeholder edge e. Its preceding segment uses only x and constant edges, and its following segment may contain any number of placeholders. After A-substitution its contribution retains precisely that order: the preceding product, A_a, then the substituted following product.

For each e, every G_0 path s→u and every G path v→z concatenate with e into a G path. Indeed, any repeated vertex in this concatenation would give a nonempty directed closed walk, impossible in a DAG. Cutting at e gives the inverse correspondence. Distributivity therefore factors the entire substituted contribution of this class of paths as P_e A_a S_e. The placeholder-free class contributes C(x,0). Thus the following identity holds without either IPS hypothesis:

    C(x,A) = C(x,0) + sum_(e in E_t) P_e A_a S_e.        (4)

All sums are finite; no factors commute and no assumption of cancellation-free paths is used. The empty path, if present, belongs to the placeholder-free class. Equation (4), characteristic two, and (1) give D(x,A)=C(x,A)+C(x,0)=1. Equation (3) gives D(x,0)=0 term by term. Unlike deleting nonlinear terms of C, the substituted suffix retains their contributions to (4). No claim that D=C in the free algebra x,t is needed or made.

**Explicit graph and syntactic path invariant.** For each e:u→v independently, copy the full graph G_0 with entrance s and exit u, and copy the full graph G_A with entrance v and exit z. Join the designated exit of the first copy to the designated entrance of the second by a new edge labelled t_a. All copies for different e are disjoint. Add distinct fresh global endpoints, a 1-edge from the global entrance to each branch entrance, and a 1-edge from its branch exit to the global exit. If E_t is empty use two disconnected endpoints. This graph is D.

A path in this graph chooses one branch. Its only way from the prefix copy to the suffix copy is that branch's central placeholder edge. There is no way between different branches, no edge from a suffix back into a prefix, and no direct global entrance-to-exit edge. Thus every accepting path traverses exactly one placeholder-bearing edge, regardless of cancellations of its polynomial with other paths. All copied edges are x-only singleton edges. This proves the syntactic promise rather than merely linearity of the polynomial in the placeholders.

The entrance or exit of a copied factor need not be a graph source or sink. A path that passes its designated exit and continues internally cannot later return there to use an external connector, because that would create a directed cycle. Hence the accepted internal routes are exactly the designated subpaths, with no additional routes caused by unused internal incoming/outgoing edges. If a factor's designated endpoints coincide, its unique internal route back to that same point is the empty path and its factor is 1. If no designated subpath exists, that branch contributes no accepting paths and polynomial zero. The copies and new connectors are acyclic by ordering all prefix vertices before all suffix vertices and placing the global entrance/exit outside them. Their finite path polynomial is exactly (3).

**One fixed full-bit and time bound.** Let a_i be absolute encoding constants, independent of F,C,n, degree or path count. Explicitly written input gives at most a_1 Q vertices, edges, label entries, axioms and symbols. Coefficient collection and splitting produce at most a_2 Q vertices and edges in G. In particular r=|E_t|≤a_2 Q. The graphs G_0 and G_A each have at most a_3 Q records: every substituted axiom has a constant number of words of bounded length, including clause axioms because their width is at most three. Every surviving label is 1 or a single symbol.

There are two copied graphs and a fixed number of added edges per branch, and r branches. Consequently D has at most a_4 Q² vertices, edges and label fields in total. This includes dead vertices, all separate gadget occurrences and disconnected branch copies; no pruning is needed for the bound. Dense new vertex and symbol references have at most a_5 log₂(Q+2) bits, since the number of records and allowed symbols is polynomial in Q. Every actual original or placeholder identifier has at most Q bits. Even a redundant encoding that repeats the raw identifier at every singleton label therefore has payload at most a_6 Q³. Keeping instead one shared dictionary is smaller. Retaining every original name, unused permitted symbol, axiom and dictionary record supplied in the input adds at most a_7 Q bits. Branch-local framing can be discarded on writing the one final graph; even retaining complete dictionaries separately per branch would add only O(Q²) bits.

The fixed explicit list framing, endpoints, counts and references therefore give

    |D|_full ≤ a_8 (Q³ + Q² log₂(Q+2) + Q) ≤ K Q⁴       (5)

for one K and all Q≥2. This deliberately loose bound includes the complete original axiom list as well as the output graph. Long sparse names are charged as string payload, not treated as unit-cost integer indices. All operations are finite loops over polynomially many actual records: parsing and coefficient collection, constructing bounded word gadgets, copying graphs, remapping dictionaries/vertices and writing the result. Ordinary polynomial-time string comparison and topological sorting suffice. They yield deterministic polynomial bit time. There is no loop over accepting paths and no use of a general circuit-to-ABP conversion. The path sums above justify correctness only; the implementation uses the copied graphs.

**Boundary and scope checks.** Zero labels are removed only through coefficient collection, which preserves the free polynomial. Placeholder-free paths can cancel and are retained in G_0; their total is zero only by (1). A clause with an empty literal list has axiom 1 and a one-edge gadget. Repeated/complementary literals retain their factor order, and repeated clauses retain their separate placeholders. Constant axioms, empty x dictionaries and absent accepted subpaths require no new symbols. If C's endpoints coincide, its polynomial is 1 by acyclicity and the empty-path convention, so no such C satisfies the first hypothesis in (1); (4) itself still holds, with D's substituted value zero. If C has no accepting path, or if E_t is empty, the two IPS hypotheses cannot both hold, but the constructed zero or empty-sum graph and (4) remain defined. Nonextremal roots and coincident subpath roots were handled in the graph argument. The constructor uses neither semantic unsatisfiability nor fidelity nor any universal witness premise.

Attempts 020/021 obstruct identical-output conversions for different input representations. Here the supplied input already is an affine ABP, and the output certificate is allowed to change. Those obstructions are preserved. General ER-to-affine-ABP representation remains an independent missing input; the present proof establishes only the approved replacement target.

**Independent critical audit, 2026-10-10.** The audit uses the previously approved EXPLORE scope in `drafts/literature/2026-10-10-arbitrary-abp-pathwise-linear-critical-audit.md`. It checks the existing conclusion, not a broader ER construction or a composition with L045. No substantive error or necessary change to the argument was found. The following independent checks record the load-bearing evidence.

1. **Normalization and the ordered identity.** A normalized edge choice is an occurrence, so parallel edges remain distinguishable in the finite path partition even if their words later cancel. For any path with a placeholder, exactly one edge is first. Before it all labels are x-only; after it all placeholder occurrences, not merely the next one, must be substituted. The concatenation of any chosen prefix, that edge and any chosen suffix is a directed walk. A repeated vertex in this walk would exhibit a nonempty directed closed walk and hence a cycle. Thus it is a path, and cutting it recovers the unique prefix/suffix choices. This proves both directions of the factorization behind (4) without a commutative rearrangement or a no-cancellation assumption. The axiom-word gadgets give exactly the fully substituted suffix by a second finite distributive expansion. L041's bounded constructions apply to every clause occurrence and to the Boolean and commutator axioms; its universal W premise and its PIT theorem play no role here. In particular neither checking (1) nor assuming a Boolean-zero promise enters the constructor.

2. **A nonlinear, mixed-label cancellation test.** Take F=(x) AND (¬x), so its ordered axioms are A=(1+x,x,x²+x), with placeholders y₁,y₂,σ. Use direct entrance-to-exit edges labelled y₁+x, x, y₂ and σ, together with a disjoint length-two path labelled y₁ then y₂. Its free polynomial is C=y₁+y₂+σ+y₁y₂; the two x-only paths cancel, rather than vanish individually. At A the value is (1+x)+x+(x²+x)+(1+x)x=1, and C(x,0)=0. Formula (3) gives D=y₁+y₂+σ+y₁x. In the length-two branch the first edge has suffix x after substitution; the second has zero prefix after deletion of the first edge. Thus D(x,A)=1 but D need not equal C before substitution. This test detects the invalid alternatives of dropping nonlinear terms, leaving free placeholders in the suffix, or deleting each x-only path because its total is zero. It is an exact symbolic instance illustrating the general argument, not a finite verification of the theorem.

3. **Graph paths and arbitrary endpoints.** Each branch contains a prefix copy, a single central edge and a suffix copy with no shared vertices, including when designated endpoints in the original graph coincide across different factors. Only the global entrance reaches a branch from outside; only the branch's designated suffix exit reaches the global exit. A successful path must leave the prefix at its designated exit via the central edge. If it instead continues internally from that exit, it cannot return to it in an acyclic graph. The same reasoning holds at the suffix exit. Existing incoming edges at a designated entrance do not admit an additional entry from outside. A factor with identical endpoints contributes exactly its empty path; a factor with no designated path gives no accepting branch. Therefore disjoint copying realizes precisely (3), with exactly one singleton placeholder edge on every accepting path, including cancelling and dead branches. Ordering each prefix DAG before its suffix DAG and adding fresh outer endpoints supplies a topological order of D.

4. **Independent complete encoding ledger.** Let v be the number of input vertices and l the number of normalized edge terms. Explicit input implies v+l≤cQ and r≤l for a fixed c. Each original axiom replacement uses at most a fixed number b of new records: clause words have length at most three and number at most eight; the two other axiom types use two words of length at most two. Thus each copied factor uses at most c'(v+l+1) records. Across r branches, global endpoints and connectors, the total is at most c''Q², including all dead records. New graph identifiers can be dense and need O(log(Q+2)) bits. Each raw original or placeholder name has length at most Q, so even repeating it in every output label costs O(Q³); name-length framing and dense references cost at most O(Q² log(Q+2)). Retaining all complete input axiom and alphabet records costs O(Q), or O(Q²) if redundantly copied per branch. These are actual record bounds, independent of polynomial degree and of the number of paths. They give the claimed single KQ⁴ for all Q≥2. Sorting written symbol strings, collecting coefficients, constructing bounded gadgets, topologically ordering and copying O(Q²) records all take polynomial bit time; an implicit binary count never drives an exponential loop. No special numerical value of K, smaller exponent or stronger running-time bound is claimed.

5. **Boundary and source scope.** For an empty clause its axiom is 1, represented by an edge, not endpoint identification. For repeated or complementary literals the ordered word list is retained with its multiplicity. With no original variables and an empty clause, C equal to its clause placeholder is a valid constant-only instance; D computes the same placeholder, with its central placeholder edge and constant connectors. With coincident C endpoints, C(x,0)=1; with no accepting paths, C(x,A)=0; with no placeholder edges, the two substitutions are equal. These cases cannot satisfy both hypotheses and are correctly handled by the unconditional identity rather than silently excluded during construction. Empty and coincident endpoints of the copied subpaths are genuinely possible and are covered in check 3. A complete quadratic commutator dictionary is already part of |A|, so no omitted alphabet cost is inferred from a short graph. The cited commutative comparisons are not applied as free-algebra theorems; the proof's only direct local input remains L041's unconditional gadgets. Attempts 020/021 concern other input representations and identical-output obligations and remain intact.
