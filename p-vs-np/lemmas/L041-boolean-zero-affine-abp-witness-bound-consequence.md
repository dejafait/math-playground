# L041 — Universal Boolean-zero affine-ABP witnesses would imply NP=coNP

## Hypotheses

Work over the free associative algebra F₂⟨x₁,…,x_n⟩. An input G is an explicitly encoded finite acyclic affine-edge ABP, with designated entrance and exit, computing its ordered path polynomial f_G. Affine labels list their constant bit and individual symbol occurrences explicitly. The encoding includes every vertex, edge, label entry, alphabet/name dictionary, endpoint and framing bit; let E be its full length. Parallel/zero edges, unequal path lengths, dead vertices, nonextremal endpoints and coincident endpoints are allowed. An empty path contributes 1. Neither implicit graphs nor circuit-valued labels are allowed.

For its explicitly listed original variables define the ordered axiom list B consisting of

    b_i=x_i²+x_i,                  1≤i≤n,
    b_ij=x_i x_j+x_j x_i,          1≤i<j≤n,

with independent free placeholders σ_i and τ_ij. Names and the full axiom/placeholder dictionaries are charged to any witness encoding. Boolean-zero means f_G(a)=0 for every scalar assignment a∈{0,1}^n; it is not formal zero in the free algebra.

Assume the following **unproved universal premise W**: there is one fixed polynomial p, independent of n and G, such that every Boolean-zero G has some explicit affine-F₂ ABP H over exactly the alphabet x,σ,τ, of full bit length at most p(E+2), satisfying the formal identities

    H(x,0)=0,                    H(x,B(x))=f_G(x).          (1)

Nonlinear placeholder dependence and cancellation-based zero identities are allowed. No algorithm finding H or recognizing the Boolean-zero promise is assumed. The premise covers constants and n=0. It is enough for this lemma that W hold on the particular ordered 3-CNF graphs constructed below, including their full variable dictionaries.

Use the standard deterministic homogeneous noncommutative ABP PIT theorem and Cook–Levin 3-SAT completeness with the precise citations below. The finite constant-path normalization used here is the established construction in L038; no propositional proof-simulation conclusion of L038 is required.

## Conclusion

Under W, well-formed UNSAT 3-CNFs have a single polynomial certificate-length bound and one deterministic verifier polynomial in the complete formula/certificate bit length. Consequently

    W ⇒ NP=coNP.

Thus NP≠coNP would refute W even on ordered products of width-three clause truth polynomials. Neither NP≠coNP nor W is established here, and no converse is claimed. The result proves a conditional burden on the universal witness premise, not a bound, constructor, unconditional all-witness lower bound, SAT algorithm or P-versus-NP resolution. The separate critical audit on 2026-10-08 repaired the homogeneous PIT terminal interface below and found no further substantive error. This is an informal proof, without a verified implementation.

For an explicitly layered homogeneous linear-edge noncommutative ABP of size s (number of vertices) over n variables, with adjacent-layer edges, unique source/sink and singleton first/final layers, Theorem 4 gives deterministic zero testing in O(s⁵+s n⁴) field operations. F₂ is a field and each of its operations has constant bit cost. The theorem is not silently applied to affine, nonhomogeneous or constant-edge inputs; L038's finite closure followed by the terminal restriction below produces its actual receiving model. No PIT theorem or coefficient-basis algorithm is reproved.

Use the standard named **Cook–Levin theorem**, including NP-completeness of 3-SAT, as cited in `foundations/02-standard-results.md`: Stephen Cook, [*The P versus NP Problem*, §2, Definition 4, Proposition 1 and the SAT/3-SAT discussion, pp.4–5](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/pvsnp.pdf). The certificate/complement inference agrees with the **Cook–Reckhow characterization**, Cook and Reckhow, [*The Relative Efficiency of Propositional Proof Systems*, JSL 44(1), 36–50 (1979), §1, Definition 1.3 and Propositions 1.1/1.4, pp.37–38](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sacook/homepage/cook_reckhow.pdf), under the malformed-input convention in `foundations/05-refutation-systems-and-simulation.md`. We construct the verifier explicitly rather than infer a particular ER system or proof finder from that characterization.

**Ordered translation with a polynomial full-bit input.** Parse a well-formed width-at-most-three CNF F. Set N=|F| and L=N+2. List its distinct original identifiers in a deterministic order as x₁,…,x_n; retain their actual names in the dictionary. There are n≤N distinct variables and m≤N explicitly written clauses under the ordinary list encoding, up to fixed encoding constants. Each identifier has at most N bits. All forthcoming bounds may absorb those fixed constants. Literal repetitions, clause repetitions and complementary literals remain in their written order; no simplification premise is used.

For an ordered clause D_j define its free-algebra falsity polynomial

    c_j=∏_(literal ℓ in D_j, in written order) v(ℓ),
    v(x_i)=1+x_i,               v(¬x_i)=x_i.

An empty product is 1. Define s_j=1+c_j and

    f_F=s₁s₂…s_m,               f_F=1 if m=0.             (2)

For any scalar Boolean assignment, c_j(a)=1 exactly when every literal of D_j is false. Hence s_j(a)=1 exactly when D_j is true, and f_F(a)=1 exactly when F is satisfied. Scalar evaluation respects every ordered free-ring operation; no free-ring commutativity or multilinear identification is invoked. Therefore F is unsatisfiable exactly when f_F is Boolean-zero.

Expand each c_j only within its own at-most-three-factor expression. It is a list of at most eight words of length at most three, with multiplicity. Including the extra 1, s_j has at most nine words. Build an independent parallel word-path gadget with distinct entrance and exit; an empty word is a direct 1-edge. A zero list may instead have no connecting edge. Keep duplicate word paths if desired: their cancellation is precisely F₂ addition. Each gadget has at most 18 internal vertices and 27 edges. Concatenate the gadgets in clause order with constant-1 connectors and fresh outer endpoints. Every accepting path chooses a word in each gadget in that order, so finite distributivity gives exactly (2), with no expansion across clauses. For m=0 use one constant-1 edge between distinct endpoints. Thus G_F has O(m+1) records and single-symbol or constant labels. Dense graph/symbol references plus the original-name dictionary give full length E_F≤cL³ for one absolute c; this deliberately loose bound includes all framing and sparse original identifiers. Construction and writing take polynomial bit time in N.

Construct B and its independent placeholder dictionary deterministically from that original dictionary. There are n+n(n−1)/2 axioms, each with at most four original symbol occurrences in its two words. Even writing original names in every axiom gives |B|≤c'L³. Dense references can reduce this bound but are unnecessary. No implicit range of exponentially many variables is allowed.

**Fixed verifier, including invalid descriptions.** The verifier V(F,H) first checks F's explicit syntax and width at most three; it rejects malformed F. It recomputes G_F, B and their ordered dictionaries. It then checks that H is an explicit finite graph with unique vertex records, valid endpoints, existing edge references, finite affine lists with F₂ coefficients, and precisely the permitted original and placeholder symbols. Dictionaries must agree with the recomputed identifiers and axiom indices; additional original variables or placeholders are rejected. Unused permitted symbols are allowed and charged. Check acyclicity by topological sorting. Every declared count is compared with actually present records; an unfulfilled enormous binary count is rejected without allocating that many objects. Sorting/comparing written identifiers and traversing actual records takes polynomial time in N+|H|. Duplicate affine symbol occurrences or parallel edges are permitted; combine them modulo two or retain them during expansion. The verifier need not know p, and it does not reject merely for exceeding the existential bound p(E_F+2).

On syntactically valid inputs, V accepts exactly when formal zero tests confirm

    H(x,0)=0,                    H(x,B(x))+f_F(x)=0.       (3)

Here plus is subtraction over F₂. The tests apply to the full polynomials. No pathwise-placeholder condition or placeholder-linearity check is imposed, and no Boolean-zero-promise test is performed. The following construction establishes the tests' polynomial bit complexity on every valid graph, irrespective of whether (3) holds.

**Bounded substitution and endpoints.** Split each affine label of H into parallel constant-1 or individual-symbol edges, omitting zero coefficients. There are at most as many edges as written label entries, and finite distributivity preserves the path polynomial. For H(x,0), delete placeholder singleton edges and retain every original-variable and constant edge, including placeholder-free paths whose contributions might cancel. For H(x,B), replace each σ_i edge by the two word paths x_i x_i and x_i, and each τ_ij edge by the two word paths x_i x_j and x_j x_i. Their fresh internal vertices are disjoint for distinct edge occurrences, so each gadget has constant size and its internal path polynomial is exactly the required axiom. Every occurrence is replaced, even when several placeholders appear on one path. Ordered graph composition therefore realizes the full simultaneous substitution without deleting nonlinear sectors or enumerating accepting paths.

For the second test add a disjoint G_F branch in parallel with the substituted graph, using fresh entrance/exit connectors labelled 1. For either test wrap the designated endpoints in fresh outer endpoints. Existing incoming edges to an old entrance and outgoing edges from an old exit cause no extra accepting paths: an accepting path cannot leave its designated exit internally and return to take its connector in a DAG. The old entrance-to-exit empty path, when the endpoints coincide, remains one path through the two wrapper edges. A coincident-endpoint H has formal output 1 and fails the first test, but its syntax and zero testing are still defined correctly. Dead vertices, zero graphs and nonextremal roots need no extra promise.

Write R=2+N+|H|+|B|. Each resulting x-only graph J has O(R) vertices/edges, with labels 1 or one x_i, and polynomial complete bit length, including G_F and all dictionaries. Every copied name has length at most R. Dense renaming retains the original-name mapping; the verifier performs equality tests before renaming. No word gadget of unbounded degree is used.

**Actual homogeneous receiving model and the degree-zero case.** Apply L038's finite constant-path closure to J. Topologically number its V vertices with the fresh entrance first and exit last. Let E₀ and E_a be the strictly upper triangular F₂ coefficient matrices for constant and x_a edges, counting parallel edges modulo two. Compute

    K=I+E₀+…+E₀^(V−1),     G_a=E_a K,
    h₀=e_s K,               b_a=e_s K E_a K.             (4)

These are finite constant matrices/rows, not symbolic inverses. Each K entry is the parity of all constant paths between its coordinates, including the empty path. Thus the degree-zero output bit is h₀(t). For k=1,…,V−1, first construct a homogeneous layered graph with a single source, V coordinates in each of k subsequent layers, first-edge x_a coefficient row b_a, subsequent x_a coefficient matrices G_a, and designated exit coordinate t in layer k. A path's constant stretches before its first variable and after every variable are counted by the displayed K factors. In particular the coefficient of a word x_(a₁)…x_(a_k) at the exit is (e_s K E_(a₁) K … E_(a_k) K)_t, exactly its coefficient in J by the unique constant-stretch decomposition. This is the normalization proved in L038; its applicability follows because J is an explicit acyclic singleton/constant-edge graph with the same wrapper conventions. Both leading and trailing constant paths are included. Every path in J has at most V−1 edges, so there is no higher-degree component.

The all-coordinate graph is an intermediate representation, not yet the exact source model. Delete every layer-k vertex other than t and its incident edges. Such a vertex cannot occur on a path ending at t, since there are no within-layer or backward edges. For each adjacent-layer pair combine its coefficients into one homogeneous linear label and delete an edge when that label is formally zero. This preserves the path polynomial by distributivity and F₂ cancellation; checking whether a linear label is zero uses only its explicit coefficient list. Compute directed reachability from the source and reverse reachability from t on the remaining support graph. If there is no source-to-t path, its path sum is the empty sum 0, so declare this component zero without a PIT call. Otherwise retain exactly vertices in both reachability sets and edges between them. Every accepting path survives, and every removed vertex or edge lies on no accepting path, so the output polynomial is unchanged. Each surviving vertex lies on a source-to-t path. Consequently each non-source vertex has an incoming edge and each non-sink vertex an outgoing edge; the source and sink are unique. Both terminal layers are singletons, all edges remain adjacent-layer homogeneous linear labels, and a surviving path visits every layer 0,…,k. This includes k=1. Reachability does not decide cancellation between surviving paths: that is the imported PIT algorithm's task. These deletions and coefficient sums do not increase the number of records and take polynomial time in the written graph.

Accept zero for J exactly when h₀(t)=0 and every positive-degree component either has no support path or is accepted as zero by Raz–Shpilka on its trimmed graph. Distinct word lengths form independent homogeneous components in the free algebra, so this is equivalent to formal zero, including cancellation-based identities. Every graph actually passed to the algorithm now meets Definition 1's terminal and label hypotheses. Its zero output may still arise from empty coefficient spaces; PIT handles them. If n=0, every linear label is zero, so every positive-degree component takes the no-path branch and the degree-zero test alone remains decisive. There is no substitution of a Boolean-promise answer for PIT and no use of formula-generated normalization bounds.

For completeness of the bit accounting, V=O(R), n=O(R) and D=V−1=O(R). Matrix powers in (4) and the n matrix products use polynomially many F₂ operations on O(R²)-bit matrices. For component k, explicitly writing its linear coefficient lists takes O(n k V²) records. Summing over k≤D takes O(nV⁴)=O(R⁵) records. Complete graph/root/list framing and name dictionaries take at most O(R⁷) bits, conservatively charging up to R bits to every written reference/name. Dense references give a smaller bound. Terminal restriction, coefficient combination and reachability add only polynomial work on these records. Each individual trimmed homogeneous ABP has at most 1+kV=O(R²) vertices, the size parameter s in Theorem 4. Thus O(R) PIT calls take O(R¹¹) field operations in total. With dense addresses, even a sequential-list simulation of these operations, charging scans of all previously stored entries and logarithmic address lengths, has polynomial bit cost; O(R³⁰) conservatively covers it, finite closure and the preceding string operations. Both tests are of this type. Since |B|≤c'L³, this is polynomial in N+|H| on invalid-identity graphs too. Invalid syntax is rejected before these constructions. The complexity bound concerns actual written records, never an unverified numeric declaration.

**Soundness without any promise.** Suppose V(F,H) accepts and a satisfies F. Evaluation at the scalar assignment a extends to a homomorphism from the free associative algebra to F₂. Every b_i(a)=a_i²+a_i=0, and every b_ij(a)=a_i a_j+a_j a_i=0, since scalar F₂ multiplication commutes. Evaluate the second accepted identity in (3), using the first:

    f_F(a)=H(a,B(a))=H(a,0)=0.

But (2) gives f_F(a)=1. This contradiction proves every accepted F is unsatisfiable, for every H whatsoever. Soundness does not assume W, a Boolean-zero promise, correct proof ancestry or efficient construction. The argument also covers nonlinear placeholder dependence because it evaluates the complete formal polynomial.

**Completeness and one witness polynomial.** For every well-formed unsatisfiable 3-CNF F, (2) makes G_F Boolean-zero. W supplies some H satisfying (1), so its valid encoding is accepted. Its full length obeys

    |H|≤p(E_F+2)≤a(L+1)^(3d)

after choosing fixed a,d large enough to dominate p(z) on all nonnegative integer arguments and absorbing c and finite small cases. Monotonicity of p is not required: use a fixed increasing polynomial majorant. Both a,d are independent of F, n and the chosen H. An NP machine fixes this polynomial bound in its code, guesses only certificates up to it, and runs V. V itself is polynomial in N+|H|, so that machine has a single polynomial running-time bound in N. An existential premise provides the bound's constants; it need not provide an effective constructor. Thus UNSAT 3-CNF belongs to NP.

Empty clauses and constants do not invalidate this inference. An empty clause has c_j=1, s_j=0 and makes f_F formally zero; for any n the disconnected zero witness satisfies both identities. If m=0, f_F=1, F is satisfiable and soundness rejects every H. With n=0 there is one assignment: a conjunction is unsatisfiable exactly when it contains an empty clause. The same zero witness works then, with the empty axiom dictionary. Constant-only supplied graphs and the empty-path convention are fully handled by the degree-zero test. No separate truth-table enumeration is needed.

Finally, for any language A∈NP fix its Cook–Levin reduction to well-formed 3-CNF: u∈A iff F_u is satisfiable. Then u∉A iff F_u has one of these NP certificates. The reduction's output and the certificate bound are polynomial in |u| for that fixed A. Therefore the complement of every NP language is in NP. This proves coNP⊆NP; applying complement closure to those complements proves NP⊆coNP as well. Equivalently, the verifier is a polynomially bounded refutation system on the NP-complete 3-CNF encoding, with the Cook–Reckhow consequence described in foundations/05. Malformed encodings are polynomial-time recognizable and do not affect this reduction or soundness.

**Scope and downstream limit.** The conditional consequence remains true for W restricted to the small graphs (2); it does not obstruct L035's particular commutators or L036's particular Booleanity polynomials. Attempts 020/021 and L034 address fixed formal outputs, whereas (1) permits every alternative H. This proof needs neither L039/L040's hypothetical faithful-representative/class equality nor L037's supplied reduced data. A future application to L037's faithful local defects would still have to justify that they are Boolean-zero with small supplied graphs, preserve exact signed/base conventions and charge aggregate witnesses. U/class equality, efficient data construction, every-certificate hardness and unrestricted SAT transfer remain unresolved. No implication from NP=coNP to either answer to P versus NP is established here; W has not been proved or refuted. No possible complete solution is proposed.
