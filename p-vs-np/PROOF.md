## Assembly and unresolved gap

The [audited target](foundations/01-model-and-target.md) is ordinary uniform worst-case P versus NP. SAT in P is equivalent to P=NP; an unrestricted SAT circuit lower bound would be a stronger sufficient separation. No such lower bound, uniform SAT algorithm or complete resolution is established.

The current branch studies translations and reception of supplied algebraic/propositional certificates. Hardness of one representation, chosen order, extension ancestry or a particular extraction algorithm is not hardness of every proof or an unrestricted decision problem. Existing simulation and width results retain their exact certificate/model promises and resource measures.

## Partial results

L030/L031 test common-order width on a used extension predicate; polynomial-degree circuit certificates on that family show why such a width obstruction is not a general proof lower bound. Supplied affine-ABP translations and fixed-library reception give conditional simulations, with full name/reference and copying costs retained.

The current implementation work changes a bounded immutable-byte checker/equality interface used in finite extraction audits. L108 and its canonical supplied-proof inputs preserve the exact syntactic fragment, original-only corrections, occurrence multiplicities, ordered products and caller authorization. The new boundary/harness needs independent critical audit; positive occurrence reception, total interpreter bounds and generic certificate existence remain unresolved. Byte checks and finite harness passes do not prove unrestricted ER/Frege boundedness, universal extraction or SAT separation.

## Known traps checked

- Formal polynomial identities are not arbitrary propositional tree equalities. Account for Boolean/commutator axioms, ordering, all copied occurrences and whole encoding length.
- A valid supplied proof/certificate is an input, not an existence theorem. Fixed-template preparation has no automatic uniform polynomial bound.
- Cache, serializer, wrapper and trusted-path checks retain their stated byte/time/authorization boundaries; a hash or sample replay does not establish full soundness or total resources.
