# P versus NP: argument overview

No candidate resolution has been developed. The [audited target](foundations/01-model-and-target.md) is the ordinary uniform, worst-case decision problem P = NP. The [standard inputs](foundations/02-standard-results.md) give SAT ∈ P iff P = NP and P ⊆ P/poly, making SAT ∉ P/poly a sufficient, stronger separation target.

## Unresolved gap

There is no deterministic polynomial-time SAT algorithm and no argument excluding every such algorithm here. The circuit route additionally lacks a measure that forces superpolynomial **unrestricted** circuit size on SAT. A large collection of residual problems need not require a large computation: a compact circuit can generate their behavior without listing them.

The [Kannan fixed-exponent theorem](foundations/03-kannan-fixed-exponent-theorem.md) supplies a possibly different Sigma_2^P language for each polynomial circuit-size exponent. Assuming P=NP puts each witness in P with its own polynomial bound; it does not produce one language outside P/poly. A unary-tagged join preserves all these lower bounds, but its membership in NP requires one uniform verifier and one polynomial bound across the tags. A clocked padded join does obtain a common Sigma_2^P bound; the supplied circuit lower bounds then transfer only with bounded exponents. No joined language here has both established NP membership and a superpolynomial circuit lower bound.

The succinct-diagonalization route lacks a sound verifier with one fixed polynomial bound and polynomial-size certificates for a language that defeats every polynomial-time decider. For the particular full-input language D_full, the missing NP membership is equivalent to NP = EXPTIME: this is a stronger sufficient objective than the required separation. Replacing a long explicit computation by a circuit describing its configurations does not itself discharge the verification obligation. Unary padding fails for explicit transcripts: the same-input construction has arbitrarily large actual running-time exponents, whereas simulating only a shorter core loses the required disagreement on the full input. Exact F₂-affine lifting with affine bit decoding also cannot give a universal polynomial-dimensional representation: even a cyclic binary counter needs exponentially many coordinates. These results do not exclude other certificate families or different NP languages.

The unary-threshold minimum circuit size problem MCSP_u has direct NP certificates, but no unconditional argument here rules out polynomial-time recognition. Circuit counting concerns the function represented by a truth table, not the decision predicate receiving that entire table; rejection density alone does not bridge this gap. A polynomial-time MCSP decider would break an efficiently evaluated pseudorandom-function family through polynomial-length restricted tables. The needed security premise is unproved here and already implies P!=NP through a separate NP range-membership language, so the conditional reduction supplies no unconditional separation.

The proof-complexity route lacks a justified passage from restricted proof size to unrestricted SAT decision time. Resolution refutations can be superpolynomial even on a syntactically recognizable class that a total SAT decider handles in polynomial time. A lower bound for a stronger proof system would still need its own connection to arbitrary decision algorithms; no such simulation is established here.

## Partial results

[L001](lemmas/L001-balanced-rank-small-circuits.md) gives SAT slices whose real communication matrices have rank at least 2^(n/8)−1 at every balanced split of n free encoding bits, yet whose decision functions have O(n²)-gate circuits. Their total formula length is O(n² log n), so the rank exceeds every polynomial in that length. This is an obstruction to the raw rank/residual-count route, including minimization over balanced partitions; it is not a lower bound for unrestricted SAT.

[L002](lemmas/L002-succinct-trace-validity.md) shows that validating an arbitrary supplied circuit-described trace is coNP-complete, even for a fixed machine that accepts after one step, correct endpoints, and constant-size configurations. A general deterministic polynomial-time validator would imply P=NP; polynomially bounded NP certificates for this validity language would imply NP=coNP. A trace can have just two illegal transitions among exponentially many, so uniform local sampling also fails to give reliable detection with polynomially many samples. These facts reject an unsupported generic-validator shortcut; they leave open certificate families with additional structure and prove no complexity separation.

[L003](lemmas/L003-unary-padding-diagonalization.md) makes the padding obstruction precise. Its language D_full, simulating the described machine on the full padded input, is outside P, but its explicit rejecting transcripts exceed every fixed polynomial witness bound. A corresponding construction that simulates the shorter core, using padding to pay for that clock, lies in P and does not diagonalize on the same input. [L005](lemmas/L005-full-input-diagonal-exptime-completeness.md) classifies D_full as EXPTIME-complete, with a 2^(O(N log(N+1))) upper bound and hardness even for unpadded cores. Thus polynomial NP certificates for this entire language exist iff NP = EXPTIME; that equality would imply both P ≠ NP and NP = coNP. No such certificates or equality are established here, and completeness alone is not a proof that D_full is outside NP.

[L004](lemmas/L004-exact-affine-counter-dimension.md) determines the minimum dimension of an exact affine lift of the cyclic w-bit counter over F₂ with affine bit decoding: it is exactly 2^(w−1), even with an arbitrary nonlinear encoding. Successive differences of the most significant bit force the lower bound, and binomial coordinates attain it. This rejects the proposed polynomial-dimensional lift; the counter itself still permits efficient ordinary arithmetic, and implicitly described vectors or different coefficient rings are outside the obstruction.

[L006](lemmas/L006-mcsp-counting-versus-decision.md) proves MCSP_u belongs to NP and gives the counting-versus-recognition discriminator. For an N=2^r-bit table, almost every represented function needs more than N/(32r) gates, while every function has a 2rN-gate construction. MCSP with thresholds up to floor(r/(16 ceil(log₂(r+2)))) is exactly decidable in polynomial time despite a YES fraction at most N³/2^N. Thus exponentially high rejection density and hard represented functions coexist with efficient recognition on an unbounded threshold range. Using a structured distribution, [L007](lemmas/L007-mcsp-prf-restriction-and-premise.md) proves the conditional restriction reduction: with k-bit keys and restricted circuit size at most c k^d, take r=min(k,(d+2)ceil(log₂ k)), N=2^r, and threshold floor(N/(32r)). A polynomial-time MCSP decider distinguishes keyed tables from uniform tables with advantage at least 1-2^(-3N/4) for large k, using polynomial time in k. Yet the same security premise already forces the NP language of tables generated by a key outside P: its hypothetical polynomial-time decider would have distinguishing advantage at least 1-2^(k-N). General MCSP_u recognition and the existence of such secure functions remain unresolved here.

The counting estimates also give a concrete quantifier test in [L008](lemmas/L008-kannan-quantifiers-and-tagged-join.md): decidable languages H_k have minimum circuit size divided by n^k tending to infinity, while each H_k has O_k(n^(k+2))-size circuits. Thus a class can defeat every fixed polynomial bound while every member belongs to P/poly. For any family A_k outside SIZE(n^k), the language of tagged pairs 1^k 0 x with x in A_k is outside P/poly, by hardwiring a fixed tag. This supplies the circuit side of aggregation, but not NP membership of the aggregate. The Kannan-plus-collapse inference therefore yields no separation.

The quantitative padding test in [L009](lemmas/L009-padded-join-exponent-tradeoff.md) uses constructive witnesses with alternating time O_j(n^(j^2)(log n)^(j+1)) and circuit size at least n^j/4 at unbounded lengths. A unary clock charges parameter-dependent machine construction and yields one language in Sigma_2^P. Its fixed-tag restrictions guarantee only a lower bound of order N^(1/j)/(log N)^((j+1)/j) along the padded hard lengths. More generally, padding to N=Theta_j(n^(a_j)(log n)^(v_j)) and paying the given budget within N^C requires a_j C>=j^2, so the inherited threshold j/a_j is at most C/j. These bounded thresholds do not exclude all polynomial circuit exponents. Under P=NP, the common padded predicate collapses to P, with circuit upper bounds consistent with all the supplied lower bounds. This is a limitation of this quantitative transfer, not an upper bound on the padded language or on other hardness mechanisms.

[L010](lemmas/L010-tseitin-resolution-versus-decision.md) gives connected graphs of degree at most 26 whose Tseitin CNFs require exp(Ω(n)) resolution lines for every odd charge vector, by the [Ben-Sasson–Wigderson width inputs](foundations/04-resolution-width-theorems.md). In binary formula length N=Θ(n log n), the bound is 2^(Ω(N/log N)). Yet one total SAT decider recognizes their parity blocks directly from the CNF and uses Gaussian elimination in polynomial time for both charge parities, with exhaustive search outside the recognized class. This refutes an instancewise polynomial simulation from every rejecting SAT computation to resolution, not a lower bound on unrestricted decision or a claim that arbitrary SAT is easy.

## Known traps checked

- **Restricted models:** matrix rank and residual counts are not identified with Turing-machine time or general circuit size. L001 explicitly tests that missing passage.
- **Proof size and decision time:** L010 covers general resolution with weakening and clause reuse, but no extension axioms. Its parity algorithm reads ordinary CNFs and handles both SAT and UNSAT instances; its general fallback is not polynomial. Graph selection is existential and is not performed by the algorithm. The bound is exponential in graph size, only 2^(Ω(N/log N)) in binary encoding length, and refutes the specified instancewise transfer rather than every possible global consequence of SAT∈P.
- **Uniformity and membership:** the graph existence in L001 is nonuniform. No efficient graph-selection algorithm or new fixed language in NP outside P is claimed; evaluation is efficient when the graph is supplied.
- **Succinct certificates:** L002 distinguishes validation of a supplied descriptor from existence of some valid descriptor. Hardness persists with legal configurations and correct endpoints, but does not exclude a verifier accepting only a structured family. Its NP=coNP consequence is distinguished from its deterministic P=NP consequence.
- **Padding:** L003 uses genuinely long rejecting runs, not just large nominal clock limits. The transcript-length obstruction concerns an explicit certificate format; the deliberately slow machines themselves decide an easy constant language. Its polynomial shorter-core construction exposes the lost same-input diagonal contradiction.
- **Completeness:** L005 includes unary exponent length, full-input erasure, and transitions of the described machine in its reduction. Its polynomial output degree may depend on the fixed source language. EXPTIME-completeness does not supply an unconditional separation from NP, and a restricted sublanguage does not automatically inherit the diagonal contradiction.
- **Affine representations:** L004 includes the cyclic wrap and the affine constant coordinate in its dimension argument. It concerns exact F₂-affine evolution with affine decoding, not unrestricted circuit size, intrinsic counting time, or the length of an arbitrary implicit description of the lifted state.
- **Encoding and quantifiers:** all input lengths include syntax and variable indices. A separation must defeat every fixed polynomial bound. Exponential statistics on an easy subfamily do not do this.
- **Fixed exponents and joins:** L008 keeps the language choice after the exponent in Kannan's theorem. P=NP collapses each fixed Sigma_2^P predicate without supplying a common resource exponent. The explicit H_k example claims decidability and polynomial circuits, not NP membership; its full-length tagged join has a circuit lower bound but no claimed NP verifier. Constant wires in restrictions are charged, and finite exceptional lengths are absorbed only into fixed-language constants.
- **Resource normalization:** L009 charges both parameter-dependent machine generation and the unary clock, keeps the universal stage, and tracks logarithmic factors and the source's wire-versus-gate convention. A unit-coefficient n^j lower bound is not promoted to exclusion of O(n^j). The padding constraint concerns the supplied time upper bound, not an intrinsic lower bound on verification; its bounded exponent ratio limits the stated fixed-tag transfer, not every possible proof.
- **Counting and decision:** L006 measures truth-table inputs in their full length and separates a represented function from the MCSP predicate. Almost universal rejection gives a distributional estimate, not exact recognition hardness; its polynomial algorithm is restricted to the stated low-threshold range.
- **Conditional pseudorandomness:** L007 charges all oracle-query writing and unary threshold costs, counts the gates implementing fixed constants, and uses one fixed restriction exponent. Its security premise ranges over every polynomial-time distinguisher and already supplies a separation independently of MCSP. No secure family, converse implication from P!=NP, or unconditional cryptographic lower bound is assumed established.
- **Algebraic fields:** L001 proves the passage from binary cut rank to real sign-matrix rank, then separately bounds the real 0/1 rank.
- **Relativization:** Baker–Gill–Solovay is a check on arguments that persist unchanged for all oracles, not a global prohibition on diagonalization or discovery.
- **Evidence:** the negative results have full informal proofs. Neither conditional completeness consequences nor costs of particular enumeration or sampling algorithms are asserted to be unconditional time lower bounds. No finite calculation, source audit, or barrier result is presented as a resolution of P versus NP.
