    Tr((E_int/54)z)=(1/2) t_T z,  t_T=tau E_int.

### Ordinary finite sums, boundedness and Nakayama

Every h in Gamma preserves P and commutes with rational scalars.
The first inclusion therefore gives h(D) subset 3^(-4)P for every
finite word. Their ordinary finite Z_3-sum S is a submodule of the
finite free module 3^(-4)P; it is bounded before any finite-generation
claim. By the cited Noetherian-submodule lemma, S is finitely generated.
It contains the full lattice D, so it spans H and has rank 106.
Being torsion-free over the DVR it is free; its finite basis realizes
it as a compact, hence closed lattice in the Hausdorff H. In particular
no closure has been added to its definition.

The group includes inverses, so h(S)=S: multiplying each finite-word
image by h permutes the available words, and its inverse gives the
reverse inclusion. Put S'=81S. We have S' subset P, and the second
projected inclusion gives 81D not subset 3P. Hence S' has a nonzero
image in P/3P. This image is invariant under exactly Gamma, so audited
L033's irreducibility makes it all of P/3P. Thus

    P=S'+3P.

The module P/S' is finitely generated as a quotient of finite free P,
and this equality says P/S'=3(P/S'). The ideal (3) is the maximal
ideal of Z_3 and hence lies in its Jacobson radical. Nakayama in the
cited form yields P/S'=0. Thus S'=P and S=3^(-4)P as embedded
modules in this same H, establishing the asserted level and uniformity.
This needs no profinite closure, compact-group averaging or larger
group-image theorem. A generator of S may be a finite sum of images;
the result is not set-orbit equality or an attained field extreme.
