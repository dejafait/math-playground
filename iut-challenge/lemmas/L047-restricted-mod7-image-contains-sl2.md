# L047 — Restricted mod-7 image contains SL_2(F_7)

## Hypotheses

Choose the same existential E_0/Q supplied by audited L046 and
apply audited L045 to this choice. Retain their embedded algebraic
closure Q-bar in Omega and put

F=Q(zeta_60,E_0[30]), D=F(E_0[7]).

All coordinate fields use both coordinates of every geometric torsion
point. Fix an F_7-basis of E_0[7]; the restricted representation is
rho_7 on G_F=Gal(Q-bar/F). L046 gives rho_7(G_Q)=GL_2(F_7),
and L045 gives F/Q finite Galois with degree dividing
2^14*3^3*5. No disjointness of division fields is assumed.

## Conclusion

The image rho_7(G_F) contains SL_2(F_7). It is a normal subgroup
of GL_2(F_7) of index dividing [F:Q], hence of index prime to 7.
For this same choice of curve, L045's full rational 30-torsion and
embedded completions F_u=H and D_w=N are retained.

This known-method specialization closes only the specified restricted
large-image prerequisite. The separate 2026-10-11 critical audit below
validates this unchanged conclusion. It
does not assert full GL_2(F_7) image over F, effective coefficients,
sufficient initial theta data or an essential IUT flaw.

### Finite Galois restriction for the actual curve

Set K=Q(E_0[7]) inside the same Q-bar. The coordinate-kernel
identification used in L045 says that the kernel of rho_7 is exactly
the pointwise fixing group of K. Consequently K/Q is finite Galois,
and rho_7 identifies Gal(K/Q) with GL_2(F_7), by L046's
surjectivity. Base changing the same curve does not change its full
geometric torsion coordinate set, so D=FK.

Let I=K intersect F. Both K and F are finite Galois over Q,
so Milne Proposition 3.21 makes I/Q Galois. The restriction theorem
identifies Gal(FK/F) with Gal(K/I). Restriction of G_F onto
Gal(FK/F) is onto: each F-automorphism of this finite Galois
extension extends to Q-bar. Thus under the fixed representation
identification the actual image R=rho_7(G_F) is Gal(K/I),
not merely a subgroup of it.

Since I/Q is Galois, Theorem 3.17 gives R normal in
Gal(K/Q)=GL_2(F_7), with

[GL_2(F_7):R]=[I:Q].

The tower F/I/Q gives [I:Q] dividing [F:Q]. L045's degree
divisibility therefore makes the finite quotient GL_2(F_7)/R
have order prime to 7. This uses a divisibility statement and
Galoisness, without asserting I=Q or assuming linear disjointness.

### The elementary generators vanish in the finite quotient

For t in F_7 let

U(t)=[[1,t],[0,1]], V(t)=[[1,0],[t,1]].

For either matrix write it as 1+A with A^2=0. Induction gives
(1+A)^n=1+nA for every nonnegative integer n. In characteristic 7
the seventh power is 1. If t is nonzero, none of the powers
1 through 6 equals 1 because n*t is nonzero in F_7; hence its
order is exactly 7. At t=0 the matrix is the identity.

Let pi be the quotient homomorphism GL_2(F_7) -> GL_2(F_7)/R.
Each pi(U(t)) and pi(V(t)) has order dividing 7. By Lagrange its
order also divides the quotient's order, which is prime to 7.
Every such quotient image is therefore the identity, so every U(t)
and V(t) belongs to R. Conrad Theorem 3.3 now yields
SL_2(F_7) subset R, as required. This is an application of the
cited generation theorem, not a reproof or a subgroup classification.

### Separate critical audit — 2026-10-11

**Verdict and scope.** The unchanged statement passes the approved
SPECIALIZE audit in
`drafts/literature/2026-10-11-restricted-mod7-image-critical-audit.md`.
The inspected Milne and Conrad results above cover the general
theorems; their proofs need not be reproduced. The following checks
verify their application independently of the construction's verdict.
No stronger image conclusion or additional lemma input is used.

**Actual coordinate field and restriction equality.** For each
sigma in G_Q, rho_7(sigma)=1 if and only if sigma fixes every
geometric 7-torsion point. By the full-coordinate convention, this
is equivalent to fixing every generator of K=Q(E_0[7]). The point
at infinity is already rational and introduces no exception. These
coordinates form a finite algebraic set, stable under G_Q because
E_0 and multiplication by 7 are defined over Q. Thus K/Q is finite
Galois, and its faithful action is the full GL_2(F_7) by L046.
It is not the field of x-coordinates alone or of one chosen point.

Base change to F retains precisely the same geometric points and
coordinates in Q-bar, giving D=FK. Milne Proposition 3.19 applies
to K/Q and F/Q in this common closure and identifies restriction
Gal(D/F) -> Gal(K/I), I=K intersect F, as an isomorphism. The
absolute restriction G_F -> Gal(D/F) is surjective: an F-automorphism
of D extends to an F-automorphism of the algebraic closure Q-bar.
Conversely every element of G_F restricts to such an automorphism
and fixes I. Consequently both containments in
rho_7(G_F)=Gal(K/I) hold under the fixed faithful identification.
This is an equality of the actual image, not a possible abstract
subgroup with the same order.

**Normality and index.** L045 gives F/Q Galois for this curve,
and K/Q is Galois as just checked. Their intersection I is Galois
over Q by Milne Proposition 3.21. Equivalently every Q-automorphism
of Q-bar preserves both fields and hence their intersection; finite
extensions in characteristic zero are separable. The Galois
correspondence now makes R=Gal(K/I) normal in Gal(K/Q) and yields
[GL_2(F_7):R]=[I:Q]. The tower law in F/I/Q gives
[F:Q]=[F:I][I:Q]. Therefore the quotient order divides the integer
2^14*3^3*5 from L045 and is prime to 7. A numerical upper bound
on [F:Q] would not suffice; its established divisibility does.
Nothing here sets I=Q, computes I, or assumes disjoint fields.

**Both elementary families.** Write U(t)=1+t E_12 and
V(t)=1+t E_21. Both matrix units square to zero. Multiplication
therefore gives (1+t E_ij)^n=1+n t E_ij for every n>=0, for
each of the two indicated indices. For t nonzero and 1<=n<=6,
n t is nonzero in F_7; for n=7 it is zero. Both matrices thus
have exact order 7 for t nonzero, and order 1 for t=0.
Their determinants are 1, so they are exactly the upper and lower
elementary subgroups in Conrad Theorem 3.3 over the field F_7.

Since R is normal, the quotient map is a homomorphism. The order
of the image of either elementary matrix divides its order and,
by Lagrange, the quotient order. These divisors are coprime unless
the order is 1, so every image is the identity. All upper and
lower elementary matrices lie in R. The cited generation theorem
therefore places SL_2(F_7) in R. No exceptional-field simplicity
claim, perfectness theorem, or determinant-surjectivity assertion
over F is required. Changing the original torsion basis does not
weaken the conclusion: determinant one is invariant under conjugation.
