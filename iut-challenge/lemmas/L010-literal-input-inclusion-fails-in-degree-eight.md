# L010 — Literal-input lattice inclusion fails in degree eight

## Hypotheses

Let pi^8 = 2, K = Q_2(pi), O = O_K, and v_2(2) = 1. Use the single-factor finite tensor packet L = K, with

T = O, J = log(O^times), I = (1/4) J, a = pi, beta = 1.

The logarithm is the convergent p-adic logarithm on principal units. This is exactly the p = 2 normalization I = (2p)^(-1) log(O^times) of [Dupuy–Hilado, arXiv:2004.13108v2, Definition 4.3.1, printed p. 17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.13108v2#page=17). In a one-factor packet the derivative-product conductor element of [Theorem 2.8.1 and Remark 2.8.2, printed pp. 9–10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.13108v2#page=9), omitting a maximal-different factor, is the empty product beta = 1. It satisfies beta O subset T and v_2(beta) = sum(d_i)-max(d_i) = 0. The nonzero distinguished scalar a acts on that sole field factor and satisfies |a|_2 < 1.

These are the previously screened local hypotheses. No identification with original initial theta data, the complete IUT indeterminacy family, or the normalized input/output degrees A and B is assumed.

## Conclusion

The proposed general genuine inclusion is false:

a(beta^(-1) T union J) is not contained in 2^floor(v_2(a)-v_2(beta)) I = I.

The failure is on the literal logarithm branch, with the exact point

x = pi log(1+pi) in a J but not in I.

In contrast, a T subset I. A separating Q_2-linear functional lambda satisfies lambda(I) subset Z_2 and lambda(x) in 1/2 + 4 Z_2, so the failed membership is certified without a numerical approximation.

This stops the universal sufficient lattice-inclusion mechanism. The statement alone does not decide the full-orbit component-hull comparison, the source's final enclosure, or the essential IUT-flaw challenge.

## Proof

### Field, conductor and required lattice

The Eisenstein polynomial X^8-2 has degree eight, pi is a uniformizer, and every element of K has a unique expression sum_(r=0)^7 c_r pi^r with c_r in Q_2. The valuations v_2(c_r)+r/8 of its nonzero summands have distinct fractional parts. Therefore the valuation of the sum is their minimum, and it is nonnegative exactly when every c_r is in Z_2. This proves O = Z_2[pi], with residue field F_2.

For completeness, the monogenic different formula used in the cited conductor construction gives the generator 8pi^7 and different valuation 31/8. Omitting the only factor gives beta = 1; the tensor order is already its own normalization, so its conductor is O. The rounded exponent is floor(1/8-0) = 0.

The logarithm and exponential are inverse on 4O and 1+4O, because every nonzero element of 4O has valuation at least 2 > 1/(2-1). Thus 4O subset J, O subset I, and a T = pi O subset O subset I. The witness must come from the other branch.

### Generators and a rigorous logarithm-tail ideal

Write U_j = 1+pi^j O and H = pi^9 O. Since 9/8 > 1, the convergent logarithm and exponential are inverse on U_9 and H, giving log(U_9) = H. One can check convergence and preservation of the leading term directly: for t in H, the differences between the valuations of the n-th logarithm or exponential term and that of t are at least (9/8)(n-1)-v_2(n) or (9/8)(n-1)-v_2(n!), respectively, and are positive for n >= 2, since v_2(n), v_2(n!) <= n-1. The inverse power-series identities then apply on this domain.

Every unit is in U_1 because the residue field is F_2. Each U_j/U_(j+1) is the additive residue field, generated by the class of 1+pi^j. Successively removing those classes expresses any u in O^times as

u = product_(j=1)^8 (1+pi^j)^(epsilon_j) times v,

with epsilon_j in {0,1} and v in U_9. The convergent logarithm is a homomorphism on U_1, so it suffices to control the eight logarithms log(1+pi^j) and the ideal H. This accounts for all units, rather than only the displayed generators or a sampled subset.

For 1 <= j <= 8 define the finite rational expression

S_j = sum_(k=1)^63 (-1)^(k+1) pi^(jk)/k.

Its difference from log(1+pi^j) lies in H. Indeed, in the integer valuation v_pi = 8v_2 the k-th term has valuation jk-8v_2(k). For k in [2^h,2^(h+1)) with h >= 6, this is at least 2^h-8h. That lower bound is 16 at h = 6 and increases thereafter, because its next increment is 2^h-8 > 0. All terms with k >= 64 therefore lie in H, and their valuations tend to infinity. The convergent tail lies in the closed ideal H.

In the rational-coordinate basis 1,pi,...,pi^7, the ideal H consists of vectors with c_0 in 4Z_2 and c_1,...,c_7 in 2Z_2. Reducing the finite sums S_j modulo that ideal gives the following exact representatives q_j. Reduction of an odd denominator means its inverse in Z_2; these are congruences, not rounded real values.

| j | q_j coordinates (c_0,...,c_7) | lambda(q_j) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | (0, 1, 1/2, 1, 5/4, 1, 3/2, 1) | -4 |
| 2 | (0, 0, 1, 0, 1/2, 0, 1, 0) | -4 |
| 3 | (3, 0, 0, 1, 3/2, 0, 3/2, 0) | 0 |
| 4 | (0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0) | -4 |
| 5 | (0, 0, 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 0) | -4 |
| 6 | (2, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 1, 0) | 0 |
| 7 | (0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 1) | 0 |
| 8 | (0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0) | 0 |

Each row can be checked by substituting pi^8 = 2 into S_j and testing the stated coordinate congruences. The reproduction script checks all eight rows also by unreduced exact rational sums. We have proved log(1+pi^j)-q_j in H, not merely small error in an unspecified norm.

### Separation of the literal input

Define, for y = sum_(r=0)^7 c_r pi^r,

lambda(y) = 2c_0 - 2c_2 - 4c_4 + 2c_5.

The table shows lambda(q_j) in 4Z_2 for all eight j. The description of H also gives lambda(H) subset 4Z_2: its c_0 is divisible by 4, its other coordinates by 2, and the nonzero functional coefficients are all even. Using the unit decomposition and the tail congruences therefore gives lambda(J) subset 4Z_2. Since I = J/4, it follows that lambda(I) subset Z_2.

Multiplication by pi sends (c_0,...,c_7) to (2c_7,c_0,...,c_6). Applied to q_1 this gives

pi q_1 = (2,0,1,1/2,1,5/4,1,3/2),

and lambda(pi q_1) = 4-2-4+5/2 = 1/2. On the other hand, log(1+pi)-q_1 belongs to H and pi H subset H. Therefore

lambda(pi log(1+pi)) in 1/2 + 4Z_2,

whose elements have valuation -1 and are not in Z_2. This excludes x from I. Its membership in a J is literal: 1+pi is a unit, and x = a log(1+pi). The required lattice has rounded exponent 0, so this is precisely the failed inclusion stated in the conclusion.
