The ordinary finite-sum orbit spans of the two literal branches are exactly

\[
\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\mathbb Z_3h((\pi O)\cap V)=3^{-1}M,
\qquad
\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\mathbb Z_3h((3\pi J)\cap V)=3^{-2}M.

The actual finite-word orbits have attained minimum valuations -7/3 and -10/3, respectively. Consequently

\[
\min_{h\in\Gamma,\ x\in U\setminus\{0\}}v_3(h(x))=-10/3>-4,

Here hull means the smallest centered closed field valuation disc containing the orbit, not equality of the orbit with its span. Its sharp radius is 3^(10/3), strictly below the prescribed 81=3^4. ## Proof

### The ideal branch and its bounded full orbit span

By L016, O has Z_3-basis 1,pi,...,pi^26. Multiplication by pi sends its constant-coordinate ideal to 3Z_3 and leaves coordinates 1,...,26 arbitrary in Z_3. By L025, Tr(sum c_r pi^r)=27c_0, so

\[
A=(\pi O)\cap V=D=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{26}\mathbb Z_3\pi^r.
\]

In particular this branch is a full rank-26 lattice in V. The least uniformizer valuation among generators of 3D is 27+1=28, so

\[
3D\subseteq\pi^{28}O\cap V\subseteq H\cap V\subseteq M.
\]

Thus D subset 3^(-1)M without assuming an O-module structure on J. Gamma preserves M, and every h(D) lies in that same bound. Let S_A=sum_(h in Gamma) Z_3 h(D), where sums are finite. It is a submodule of the finite free module 3^(-1)M, hence finitely generated by Stacks 00FM. It is torsion-free over the DVR and contains D, so it is a full free lattice in V. Products and inverses in Gamma permute the summands, giving g(S_A)=S_A for each g in Gamma. All hypotheses of L025's embedded homothety criterion therefore hold, and S_A=3^t M for an integer t. The upper inclusion forces t>=-1.

The literal point pi belongs to D and has, using L016's full-J integral separator,

\[
\phi(\pi)=-(31/3)\notin\mathbb Z_3.
\]

Since phi(J) subset Z_3, pi is not in J or M. Hence S_A is not contained in M and t<0. The only possible integer exponent is t=-1, proving the first exact span equality.

L026's exact minimum on M gives minimum -7/3 on S_A, attained by m_0/3. Since this point belongs to the ordinary span, it has a finite expression sum a_k h_k(x_k) with a_k in Z_3, h_k in Gamma and x_k in D. Every nonzero orbit image has valuation at least -7/3 by the span bound. If all such images in this expression had strictly larger valuation, their finitely many integral multiples and their sum would also have larger valuation, contradicting v_3(m_0/3)=-7/3. Some actual finite-word image of an actual ideal-branch point therefore attains -7/3. This argument makes no claim that the orbit set equals S_A or uses a limiting action.

### The literal logarithm branch by rational scaling

Let B=(3pi J) intersect V. Because 18 is a nonzero rational scalar, it preserves V bijectively, and

\[
B=18((\pi J/6)\cap V)=18N.
\]

Indeed x=18y has x in V exactly when y is in V, and 18(pi J/6)=3pi J. Multiplication by pi itself is not assumed to preserve V. Each h in Gamma is Q_3-linear, so h(18N)=18h(N), with exact equality for finite words. Thus L026 gives

\[
S_B=\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\mathbb Z_3h(B)
=18\left(3^{-4}M\right)
=(2/9)M=3^{-2}M.
\]

The last equality uses only that 2 is a Z_3-unit, which fixes every Z_3-lattice. B is a bounded full lattice as the rational scaling of L026's bounded full N. Its span minimum is -2-4/3=-10/3.

Attainment is on the literal branch itself: L026 supplies a finite word h and an actual x in N with v_3(h(x))=-16/3. Then 18x belongs to B and h(18x)=18h(x) has valuation 2-16/3=-10/3. Although that word and point are not given in field coordinates, this is an attained existential conclusion for every admitted presentation, with neither a larger group nor a closure substituted for Gamma.

### Return to the union and comparison with the prescribed radius

Intersection distributes over the literal union, so U=A union B, and Gamma(U)=Gamma(A) union Gamma(B). Both branch bounds and attainments were proved on their own inputs. Taking their minima gives -10/3, with an actual attaining point in B. Since -10/3=-90/27, the smallest centered closed field disc is pi^(-90)O, whose radius is 3^(10/3). The proposed radius-81 disc is pi^(-108)O; -90>-108, so the orbit is contained in it and has strictly smaller sharp radius. No sum of points from different literal branches is used as an input.
