## Conclusion

\[
S=\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\mathbb Z_3\,h(N),
\qquad S=3^{-4}M.
\]

The notation sum means finite sums. The orbit set Gamma(N) itself need not equal S. Nevertheless

\[

Thus Gamma(N) is **not contained** in 3^(-4)O. There exist an actual finite word h in the specified reference subgroup and an actual x in N with this valuation. ## Proof

### The exact finite-word span is a stable full lattice

The trace formula from L025 is Tr(sum_(r=0)^26 c_r pi^r)=27c_0. L016 proves O=Z_3[pi]. In particular V is the Q_3-span of pi,...,pi^26, and M is a full lattice in V by L025. Since 6=2*3, rational scaling of the input gives

\[
81(\pi J/6)=(27/2)\pi J
\subseteq \pi^{28}O\subseteq H\subseteq J.
\]

The first containment uses J subset pi^(-54)O and v_pi(27pi)=82, so 82-54=28; multiplication by the unit 1/2 preserves each valuation ideal. For x in N, rational scaling also preserves trace zero. Hence 81N subset M, or N subset 3^(-4)M. This does not treat J as an O-module.

N is full in V. It contains (pi H/6) intersect V=(pi^(-12)O) intersect V, since 1/2 is an O-unit. This last module contains sufficiently large rational powers of 3 times every pi^r, 1<=r<=26, and therefore spans V. N is a Z_3-submodule of the finite free module 3^(-4)M; the Noetherian submodule theorem makes it finite. Being torsion-free over the PID Z_3, it is free, and its rank is 26 by fullness. Thus it is a full lattice, including its usual closedness in V.

Because Gamma preserves M, every h(N) is contained in 3^(-4)M. Thus the ordinary finite-sum module S is bounded by this same lattice and contains N. The same Noetherian and torsion-free arguments make S a full lattice. For g in Gamma, left multiplication permutes Gamma, so g(S)=S, including equality for inverses. These obligations are the exact bounded orbit-sum specialization needed here. No topological closure of the orbit or primitive-vector transitivity is used.

L025's audited residual-irreducibility criterion therefore applies to this S in the same V and gives S=3^t M for some integer t. Its proof gives equality of embedded lattices, not merely an abstract module isomorphism. The inclusion S subset 3^(-4)M implies t>=-4: check coefficients in any Z_3-basis of the nonzero full lattice M.

### An actual input fixes the scale

Take L016's exact representative q_1 in J. Its pi^26 coefficient is zero, so pi q_1 has constant coefficient zero. Consequently

\[
x_0=\pi q_1/6\in N.
\]

This is an actual logarithm-derived envelope point, since L016 proves q_1 in the genuine J by its H-valued error and inverse log/exp argument; it is not merely a finite approximation.

Use L016's integral separator phi(y)=3c_0-(31/3)c_1+9c_15-9c_18, with phi(J) subset Z_3. That lemma establishes exactly phi(18pi q_1)=-992/3. Since 27x_0=(9/2)pi q_1, Q_3-linearity gives

\[
\phi(27x_0)=\frac14\phi(18\pi q_1)=-248/3\notin\mathbb Z_3.
\]

Thus 27x_0 is not in J, and x_0 is not in 3^(-3)M. As x_0 belongs to N subset S, S cannot be contained in 3^(-3)M. For S=3^t M this says t<-3. Together with t>=-4 and integer t, it proves t=-4. This computes the scale independently of the unknown presentation coordinates.

### Complete trace-zero lattice valuation

Every m in M has an expression m=h+sum_(j=1)^13 a_j q_j with h in H and a_j in Z_3, by the complete-image equality. In that table the only nonzero constant coefficients are

\[
q_{1,0}=16/9,\quad q_{2,0}=1/3,\quad q_{3,0}=7/3,
\quad q_{6,0}=q_{9,0}=1.
\]

Write h_0=3b_0 with b_0 in Z_3. Trace zero is exactly

\[
16a_1+3a_2+21a_3+9a_6+9a_9+27b_0=0.
\]

Reducing modulo 3 forces a_1 in 3Z_3. Therefore

\[
M\subseteq H+\mathbb Z_3(3q_1)+\sum_{j=2}^{13}\mathbb Z_3q_j
\subseteq \pi^{-36}O.
\]

For clarity, the second containment checks the entire L016 table, not just trace-zero examples. H has valuation at least 14/27. In 3q_1 the only denominator-3 coefficients occur at r=0,9,18, giving valuations at least -1. In q_2 the only denominator-9 coefficient is at r=18 and gives -2+18/27=-4/3; its denominator-3 coefficients occur at r=0,6,9,12,24 and give valuations at least -1. For q_3 through q_13 every nonintegral coefficient has denominator 3 and index at least zero, giving valuation at least -1. All other coefficients are integral. Hence every generator has valuation at least -4/3=-36/27; integral linear sums retain that bound. Distinct power-basis indices have distinct fractional valuation parts, as proved in L016, so these coordinate bounds are field valuation bounds.

The bound is attained in M by

\[
m_0=3q_1-16q_2.
\]

Its constant coefficient is 3*(16/9)-16*(1/3)=0, so m_0 belongs to M. Its pi^18 coefficient is (3-16)/9=-13/9, with valuation contribution -2+18/27=-4/3. All other coefficients obey the just-proved bound. Distinct fractional valuation parts prevent cancellation of this minimum, so v_3(m_0)=-4/3. This establishes the exact minimum of M.

### Return from finite sums to an actual orbit point

We now have S=3^(-4)M subset pi^(-144)O, with the point s_0=3^(-4)m_0 in S satisfying v_3(s_0)=-16/3=-144/27. By the definition of S there is a finite expression

\[
s_0=\sum_{k=1}^n a_k h_k(x_k),\qquad
a_k\in\mathbb Z_3,\ h_k\in\Gamma,\ x_k\in N.
\]

Every nonzero h_k(x_k) has valuation at least -16/3 since it belongs to S. If all nonzero terms h_k(x_k) had valuation strictly greater than -16/3, then multiplication by the integral a_k and the nonarchimedean inequality for this finite sum would imply v_3(s_0)>-16/3, a contradiction. At least one actual h_k(x_k) therefore has valuation exactly -16/3. This proves both attainment and failure of the radius-81 containment. Equivalently, even without sharpness, if every orbit image belonged to the Z_3-module C, all finite sums would belong to C, contradicting s_0. The argument returns to the original finite-word Gamma without replacing it by its compact closure or identifying orbit set and span.
