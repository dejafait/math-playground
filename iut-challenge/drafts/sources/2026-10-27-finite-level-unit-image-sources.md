## Ordinary-field dictionary and its limits

Example 1.8(ii) identifies the reconstructed monoid, with its Galois action, with the ordinary algebraic-closure monoid model. Part (iii) takes units; part (iv) takes the quotient by the entire torsion subgroup and defines Ism(G) using images of full-unit invariants for every open subgroup. Definition 4.9(i) carries exactly this indexed-image condition into a chosen Kummer structure.

Fix the ordinary realization G=Gal(Omega/Q_3), and an open subgroup H_E=Gal(Omega/E). The fundamental theorem of infinite Galois theory identifies the fixed field with E. With the valuation fixed as in L125, the full-unit invariants are O_E^times. Thus the image prescribed by these source definitions in this realization is precisely M_E=image(O_E^times -> U_Omega/mu(Omega)). This is an interpretation of the inspected definitions under their ordinary model, not an assertion that an arbitrary reconstructed Kummer structure has been canonically identified with that model. A different Kummer marking must be transported along its specified isomorphism.

In particular, taking invariants after quotienting is not the prescribed construction. Nor does the source replace the indexed image by its divisible hull. Ism(G) consists of G-equivariant automorphisms of ind-topological modules preserving these indexed lattices, hence the source condition is stronger than the proposed one-level containment test. Failure of containment would rule out F_{Q_3} as an isometry of this same ordinary realization with the identity indexing. Success would establish neither equality, invertibility, topology nor compatibility with all indices.

Remark 3.9.5(x) does not state that every imaginable norm operation must be such a same-index endomorphism. Its discussion concerns a structured replacement of arithmetic line bundles, retained local unit action and the gluing. Accordingly, this test diagnoses one proposed use of L125 only. Neither outcome establishes an essential flaw in IUT or a general impossibility of norm constructions. The special case k=Q_3 is a permitted diagnostic model, not an asserted native bad-place instance satisfying complete initial data.

## Imported norm and unit inputs for a later test

Kedlaya Proposition 4.2.5 assumes a finite unramified extension of local fields and concludes surjectivity of the ordinary norm on their full unit groups. There is no requirement that the degree be prime to the residue characteristic. The proof first lifts a residue-field norm preimage, then corrects through the principal-unit filtration using the surjective residue trace and a convergent product. Completeness is used for that product. All of these hypotheses match the proposed finite unramified cubic extension of Q_3. The proof uses trace on the residue extension, not multiplication by the extension degree. The subsequent cohomological vanishing is stronger background but is unnecessary for this target.

Zhou–Tomczak Theorem 6.11 supplies the finite unramified extension and its Galois/residue-field realization for every degree. Theorem 2.5 supplies multiplicative Teichmuller lifts over a complete discretely valued field with perfect residue field; Lemma 2.7 identifies the lifts as roots of unity when the residue field is algebraic over F_p. Proposition 6.15 identifies the full-unit/principal-unit and successive principal-unit quotients. Proposition 6.14 gives the logarithm/exponential isomorphism at integral depth r strictly greater than e/(p-1). For the proposed unramified field, e=1 and p=3, so depth r=1 is within its stated range. These are applicability checks of known results, not a computation of F_{Q_3} or its unit image. Conrad's already reviewed strict-disc theorems additionally supply an isometric logarithm and its multiplicativity with the normalized absolute value.

No source above asserts that a degree-normalized rational power stays in the units of the original finite field modulo torsion. Ordinary norm surjectivity must not be misquoted as that assertion. The next mathematical test must handle equality modulo every root of unity, including possible roots outside E. It may use the defining equality in the quotient to reduce the representative question to finite-field unit/torsion data, but that argument and any witness have not been supplied in this review. A non-E chosen root alone would not prove failure; all E representatives of the class must be excluded.
