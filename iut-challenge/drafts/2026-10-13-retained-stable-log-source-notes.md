## Inspected native convention and construction input

[EtTh, published PRIMS 45 (2009), 227–349](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/41069?nt=1),
section 0 Curves p.236 and section 1 opening p.237, requires the formal
closed-point log base, singular split special fiber, and smooth algebraized
generic log fiber. Split means geometrically irreducible components and
nodes. Its definition reference is [Semi-graphs of Anabelioids, PRIMS
42 (2006), 221–322](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/40941),
section 0 Curves pp.230–231: for 2g−2+r>0 a stable log curve is the
pullback of the tautological log curve along a log classifying map into
the boundary-log moduli stack. The tautological boundary includes the
marked divisor. One marking makes ordered/unordered conventions coincide.
This is the native definition to use, rather than introducing a different
intrinsic definition and silently equating it with EtTh's convention.

The cited primary construction reference is [Mochizuki, The Geometry of
the Compactification of the Hurwitz Scheme, PRIMS 31 (1995), 355–441](https://content.ems.press/assets/public/full-texts/serials/prims/31/3/3772/online/10.2977-prims-1195164048.pdf).
Read sections 3.2–3.4, pp.373–375, and the relevant node passage of 3.10,
pp.379–380. The first passages specify boundary log structures on the
moduli stack and universal marked curve, and define stable log curves by
logarithmic pullback; the natural pulled-back base log makes the ordinary
classifying map logarithmic. The node passage describes base generators
indexed by nodes and compatible branch sections with the smoothing
relation. It occurs in a covering discussion: only the underlying
canonical-curve chart description is used as source coverage, not the
admissible-cover hypotheses or theorem. The OCR of the older ordinary
stability definition loses decorations; use L051's already audited
Hassett weight-one criterion, not that OCR, for scheme stability.

Neither passage identifies the natural pulled-back base log with the
closed-point log on a DVR. The continuation must construct the comparison
map; even a single node can have a nonprimitive smoothing parameter.
These definitions supply the global construction venue and marking
convention. They do not constitute a completed construction on L051.

## Inspected chart and base-change criteria

[F. Kato, Log Smooth Deformation Theory, alg-geom/9406004v3,
6 September 1994](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9406004v3), printed
pp.3–8 and the sufficient-direction proof on p.13:
Definition 2.1 gives associated log structures and their universal map;
Definitions 2.7–2.12 specify charts, fineness and saturation. Corollary
2.15 and Lemma 3.4 distinguish fs products from ordinary scheme products;
the latter equality is guaranteed for a strict factor. Proposition 3.9
preserves log smoothness under log base change. Theorem 4.1 requires
finitely generated integral charts, finite kernel and torsion cokernel of
invertible orders on groupifications, and a smooth morphism to the
monoid-algebra base change. Its proof of sufficiency uses the toric
lifting criterion, ordinary smoothness and Proposition 3.9. It does not
require a regular total space. General theorems are imported, not reproved.
This is the inspected 1994 arXiv version, not the inaccessible 2000
moduli paper or an asserted theorem-number match with the 1996 publication.

[Stacks, tag 0C58](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C58), Lemma
53.20.1 and Definition 53.20.2, characterizes at-worst-nodal families by
flatness, local finite presentation, one-dimensional equidimensional
fibers and nodality. Lemma 53.20.4 preserves this under any scheme base
change. Its proof uses syntomic/unramified base change and the Fitting
singular locus. These hypotheses can be compared with L051 without a
regular-model hypothesis. No base-changed-family verdict is made here.

[Stacks, Lemma 53.21.1, tag 0CBY](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CBY),
for an at-worst-nodal family and a singular fiber point, gives an étale
diagram modeled on uv=a. Read the statement and both proof cases:
approximation from finite-type bases, then the split-node completion
case; the alternative proof constructs coordinates in a smooth ambient

## Comparison, remaining application and limits

These primary inputs cover a bounded known-method attempt via the native
moduli pullback. The later application must use the actual stable pointed
family, extend its classifying map to the closed-point log base, and check
its node and marking charts. In particular it must verify integrality and
saturation of the relevant chart monoids before claiming that taking an
fs product leaves the scheme cubic unchanged. Fine saturated objects and
a saturated morphism are distinct notions; no unnecessary extra stability
criterion is imposed. It must track the base map under O_H to O_N, where
a ramified map of closed-point log bases need not be strict, and compare
the resulting construction with the direct one over O_N. Finally it must
give compatible strict reductions modulo powers of the maximal ideal and
identify their formal limit and its already algebraic generic fiber.
The general strict-product lemma cannot replace the nonstrict node check.

This is SPECIALIZE coverage, not a new log-smoothness or fs proof.
The local chart verification is necessary because a theorem about log
fiber products alone does not preserve underlying schemes in general.
No full reproof of Kato, moduli representability, stable reduction,
duality or étale nodal normal forms is justified. No computation of
node thickness, graph quotient, theta splitting or numerical bound is
needed for this limited object-construction test.
