## Primary statements and essential cases

[Kedlaya, *Introduction: the Tate curve*, fall 2004](https://kskedlaya.org/18.727/tate-curve.pdf),
Theorem 1, pp.3–4, and Theorem 2, p.4, were read with their proof
discussions. For finite p-adic K and 0<|q|<1, parametrization is
Galois equivariant, with kernel q^Z, also on every finite extension.
Negative j-valuation gives a unique small q in K and a geometric
comparison; a comparison over K requires split multiplicative reduction.
The torsion generators are explicitly discussed in Proposition 3's proof,
p.5. Its transvection conclusion is not used: its valuation-coprimality
hypothesis is not available over the full torsion completion. Theorem 1
imports Silverman V.3.1, and Theorem 2(b) imports V.5.3; no new proof of
Tate uniformization is contemplated.

[Sutherland, MIT 18.786 Lecture 21, 6 May 2024](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.786/2024/LectureNotes21.pdf),
section 21.2, p.2, was read. Over a field k, pointed nonexceptional
elliptic twists are classified by continuous quadratic characters of
G_k; trivial character means a k-isomorphism. The characteristic-zero
local fields and j!=0,1728 required here are within this scope.
The general-field statement covers local fields directly. L070's old
number-field specialization is not an input to a new local theorem.
The future test must track the character on actual torsion, rather than
assume that geometrically matched points make the isomorphism rational.

[Dokchitser–Dokchitser, arXiv:1208.5519v4, 28 October 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.5519v4),
Appendix A, Theorem A.1 and Lemma A.2, p.19, were read with their
proof references. They describe split Tate torsion as generated by a
root of unity and a radical of q, the power isogeny and other cyclic
isogenies, and a quadratic splitting twist for potentially multiplicative
reduction over a finite p-adic field. They refer to Silverman V.3–V.5
and Roquette A.1.4. These corroborate the known mechanism; they do not
state the desired same-member graph-kernel equality. No rational radical
or splitness is inferred for our completions in this review.

[Conrad, *Arithmetic moduli of generalized elliptic curves*](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/kmpaper.pdf),
author version 18 July 2006: section 2.5 pp.17–18, Example 2.5.1,
Theorem 2.5.2 and its full proof, and Theorem 3.2.4 pp.24–25 with
its equal-polygon proof case were reinspected. The polygon rotation
construction works for arbitrary n and an adic noetherian base with a

The generic identification respects the formal group at the origin;
extension of generic isomorphisms over a normal base requires matching
singular polygon sizes. Thus no theorem is restricted to n=7 or the
old field N. The symbol q^(1/n) is the formal base variable, not an
instruction to adjoin a root in the application. Reuse the earlier
algebraization/log coverage; no suspect annulus-overlap formula is
certified by this reinspection. Journal byte identity remains unchecked.
