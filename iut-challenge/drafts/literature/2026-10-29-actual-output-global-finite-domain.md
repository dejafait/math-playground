### What the sources actually state

1. **Fixed input and indices.** IUT I Definition 3.1(b),(c),(e), pp.61–63,
   fixes the elliptic curve, finite division field K, chosen bad support and
   section V. Its good set need not be the full good-reduction set. Definition
   4.1(iii)–(iv), p.96, defines mono-analytic prime-strips over this V and
   morphisms componentwise over V. Capsule symmetries do not thereby license
   moving a component to a new rational residue characteristic. IUT II
   Definition 4.9(vii), p.158, also makes its morphisms V-indexed. These are
   definitions, not a proof of uniform support of every resulting region.
2. **The pilot is specified globally.** III Definition 3.8(i), p.112, constructs
   it from the splitting generators at Vbad, using Proposition 3.7(v),
   pp.111–112. The local fractional-ideal realization in Proposition 3.7(ii),
   p.110, cites Example 3.6(ii), pp.107–108, where an individual object has
   J_v=O_(K_v) at all but finitely many places. This individual finite-support
   statement alone is not a common-support statement for a union of choices.
3. **Common unit upper container.** III Proposition 3.5(ii)(a), pp.104–105,
   quantifies over n,m, 0<=j<=ell', nonarchimedean rational places, the
   Galois-invariant unit submodules, and every positive iterate of the log-link.
   Both the direct tensor Kummer images and their indicated precomposites
   lie in the displayed coric tensor log-shell. Iterates retain their actual
   domains. Part (ii)(b) gives the archimedean ball/surjectivity version;
   part (ii)(c) treats bad splitting-monoid action overlaps separately.
   None makes every ambient-module element a pilot image.
4. **Proof-case scope.** Proposition 3.5's p.106 proof cites its definitions
   and references. Its explanatory input is Proposition 1.2(v),(vi),(ix),
   pp.31–34 and Remark 1.2.2(i),(iii), pp.36–37: integral elements and
   logarithmic unit images are contained in the coric shell, including
   partial iterates. The concrete nonarchimedean shell is
   (p*)^(-1) log(O_k^times), with p*=p for odd p and p*=4 for p=2.
   This was read rather than replaced by an arbitrary logarithm-lattice model.
5. **Ind1/Ind2 domain.** Proposition 3.2(ii), pp.98–99 forms integral
   tensor packets by direct sums and tensor products of these shells;
   Proposition 1.2(vi) requires its poly-isomorphisms to preserve shells and
   log-volumes. IUT II Example 1.8(iv), pp.38–39 defines Ism by preservation
   of the unit-image lattices for every open subgroup; Definition 4.9(i),
   p.154 retains that entire collection in a Kummer structure. Theorem
   3.11(i)(a),(Ind1),(Ind2), pp.153–154 uses these integral packets and
   factorwise/summandwise actions. Its (ii)(a),(Ind3), pp.155–156 explicitly
   points to Proposition 3.5(ii)(a),(b); it does not quantify over arbitrary
   automorphisms of a rational vector space.
6. **Tail normalization.** Proposition 3.9(iii), p.117 defines the global
   finite-sum domain. Remark 3.9.7(i), pp.145–146 states that at the indicated
   unramified places O=I has zero log-volume, is mono-analytically invariant
   and equals its holomorphic hull. Part (iii), pp.146–147 requires a single
   componentwise dominating collection of this kind to define its broader
   infimum; it allows minus infinity. A dominating tail gives an upper-domain
   condition, not by itself a finite lower bound. Hull definition and
   monotonicity are in Remark 3.9.5(i)–(ii), p.127.
7. **Different global statement.** Proposition 3.10(ii), pp.148–149 says the
   global field's intersection with all local integral monoids is its torsion
   subgroup. Its proof invokes the all-place integral-element/root-of-unity
   fact (including archimedean constraints), citing Lang p.144, proof of
   Theorem 5. No independent Lang import or reproof is needed here. This
   intersection equality is not a uniform upper bound on the actual union.
   Part (iii) and Remark 3.10.1(i), pp.149–150 explicitly separate MOD
   compatibility from the local mod construction's one-sided inclusions.
