## Primary inputs and differences

[Stacks, Definition 9.20.1, tag 0BIF](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BIF) defines the norm of a finite field extension by a multiplication determinant; the following discussion gives multiplicativity and the degree power on base-field elements. [Lemma 9.20.5, tag 0BIJ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BIJ) gives norm transitivity for a finite tower. Its proof uses [Lemma 9.20.4, tag 0BII](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BII), whose determinant proof treats a singular map separately and reduces invertible matrices to elementary matrices. These are finite-dimensional results with no Galois hypothesis. Finite subextensions of the present closure meet their field hypotheses. Unit preservation and the passage to a directed quotient still require checking; they are not stated as conclusions here.

[Keith Conrad, *Trace and Norm, II*](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/galoistheory/tracenorm2.pdf#page=5), Theorem 4.3, printed pp.5–7, assumes only a finite tower L/F/K. Its first proof case states that for an element of F the larger norm is the smaller norm raised to [L:F]. The second case treats a simple extension using a block companion matrix, and the third inserts F(alpha) in a general tower. The first case is particularly relevant to changing the auxiliary field; the theorem may be imported without reproof. Its conclusion does not itself assign rational powers in a quotient.

[Paul Fili and Zachary Miner, *Orthogonal Decomposition of the Space of Algebraic Numbers and Lehmer's Problem*, April 3, 2010 author copy](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/zminer/norms.pdf#page=9), §2.3, printed p.9, equation (2.2), explicitly identifies their projection with the class of N_{K(alpha)/K}(alpha) raised to 1/[K(alpha):K], modulo all torsion. The preceding construction averages a finite Galois orbit. Lemma 2.14, pp.11–12, proves covariance under conjugation of the base field, by conjugating Haar integration. This is a close mathematical precedent, not an exact local theorem: the paper works with algebraic numbers and height functions. Its height-topology estimates and identification via global places cannot be transferred to local units. Only its finite-orbit algebraic construction and normalized-norm comparison motivate the application audit.

[Stacks, Lemma 15.22.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0549) states that an abelian module over a domain modulo its torsion submodule is torsion free; take the domain Z. [IIT Kanpur, *MTH302: Set Theory and Mathematical Logic*, “Torsion free divisible abelian groups,” PDF pp.160–161](https://home.iitk.ac.in/~krashu/mth302.pdf#page=160), lists the hypotheses and explains uniqueness of division and the resulting Q-vector structure. These are general group inputs. The future audit must establish divisibility of the particular unit group using the algebraic-closure hypothesis and the valuation, identify its entire torsion subgroup, and check how rational powers interact with its natural action. No application proof is supplied in this literature step.
