# Fourth power plus square: cited source scope

## Hypotheses

Let A,B,C be positive coprime integers and n an integer with n>=4. This record uses only the positive, nonzero scope; it does not assert that the source's unqualified printed wording excludes zero-coordinate solutions. Bases equal to one are permitted.

## Conclusion

The equation A^4+B^2=C^n has no such solution. The source is Michael A. Bennett, Jordan S. Ellenberg and Nathan C. Ng, *The Diophantine equation A^4+2^delta B^2=C^n*, International Journal of Number Theory **6** (2010), 1–27, **Theorem 1**; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S1793042110002971), [Bennett's linked author manuscript, p. 1](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~bennett/BeElNgdraftFINAL.pdf#page=1), [publication-list record](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~bennett/publ.html). Manuscript page numbers below refer to the 23-page author copies, not independently inspected publisher pagination. No GRH or other conjectural hypothesis is used in this clause.

## Proof

Imported by the precise citation, without reproof. In the readable [Ng author copy](https://www.cs.uleth.ca/~nathanng/RESEARCH/BeElNgdraftFINAL.pdf), Sections 2–3 give irreducibility and Cartan alternatives, including 7,11,13 and the split case p=1 mod 8. The conductor-four analytic estimates cover p>=61 (pp. 9–10). Section 7 (p. 18) supplies the remaining modular forms, including higher-dimensional witnesses at 7,23,47. Proposition 7 (p. 4) and Section 8 (pp. 18–19) cover 4,5,6,9; 5 uses Bruin's cited result. Section 9's harder cases belong to the coefficient-two equation and are not needed here. The computations are cited, not rerun.

The coefficient-two clause has an exceptional solution and must not be substituted for the coefficient-one clause.

### Version and access qualifications

On 2026-10-06, Bennett's primary manuscript p. 1–4 was retrieved, including Theorem 1 and the explicit coprimality convention on p. 2. Later selected-range retrieval failed. The Ng and [Ellenberg copies](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~ellenberg/BeElNgdraftFINAL.pdf) have defective introductory wording: coprimality is omitted and an equation is duplicated. The positive coprime scope of Proposition 7 and the relevant proof cases were read from Ng's copy. Use Bennett's qualified statement together with that scope, not the defective unrestricted wording. The author copy's zero-coordinate omission is not extended beyond the positive scope stated above. Neither copy imposes A>1 or B>1. The publisher DOI and PDF could not be retrieved; no verbatim equivalence to a publisher PDF or revision date is claimed.

An independent primary input for the repeated-fourth endpoint p=5 is Michael A. Bennett, *The equation x^{2n}+y^{2n}=z^5*, Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux **18** (2006), 315–321, **Theorem 1.1**, printed p. 316: for integer n>=2, no coprime nonzero integer solution. [Author copy](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~bennett/bennetjtnb.pdf#page=2). The statement and full proof were read, in particular Section 4, pp. 319–320: the n=2 case retains both 5-divisibility alternatives and concludes using the rational points of 400D1. No descent or rank calculation is independently reproduced. Its n=2 specialization is a known matching (4,4,5) source, not a newly derived exclusion in this literature step.

Ellenberg's earlier *Galois representations attached to Q-curves and the generalized Fermat equation A^4+B^2=C^p*, American Journal of Mathematics **126** (2004), 763–787, Theorem 4.1, supplies only the older large-prime range. The [July 22, 2003 author copy, pp. 1–2](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~ellenberg/A4B2Cp.pdf#page=2) explicitly states p>=211 and AB=0 for coprime solutions. It is historical comparison, not an extra mathematical premise or a replacement for the small-prime coverage above.
