### Complete bounded eligibility

L123's monic integral equation for u=275 theta proves that theta is
integral at every maximal place above p outside {5,11}. Every prescribed
a−b theta is therefore integral at every place outside S. Its nonzero
norm is

    N(a−b theta)=H(a,b)/275,
    H(a,b)=275a⁸−2860a⁶b²+6930a⁴b⁴−3564a²b⁶+243b⁸.       (1)

This follows from the full monic polynomial F/275 and the conjugate
product, with b^8 retained. Nonzero follows also from L115's degree-eight
irreducibility: a rational a/b cannot be theta. The denominator in (1)
has no support outside S.

For p outside S, all v_P(a−b theta) are nonnegative and

    v_p(N(a−b theta))=sum_(P|p) f(P/p) v_P(a−b theta).

Every residue degree is positive. Consequently the element is an S-unit
if and only if its rational norm has no numerator prime outside S.
For the converse, if a residual numerator factor greater than one
remains after stripping all four primes, some prime divisor outside S
has positive norm valuation, and hence at least one positive maximal-place
valuation. No factorization of that residual integer is necessary.
This is an exact eligibility decision, not just a sufficient test;
integrality precludes cancellation between opposite valuations.

The certificate enumerates a=-16,...,16 and b=1,...,16, retains exactly
gcd(a,b)=1, and evaluates every integer H(a,b). There are 319 rows.
Reduce H/275 and strip 2,3,5,11 from numerator and denominator by exact

remainder is retained. An independent arithmetic replay obtains all

formula (1), and verifies every signed exponent and eligibility decision.
Thus no inconclusive pair is silently discarded.
