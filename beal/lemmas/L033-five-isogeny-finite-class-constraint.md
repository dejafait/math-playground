# L033 — A finite 5-isogeny class condition for the 54a1 branch

## Hypotheses

Let A,B,C be positive integers with gcd(A,B,C)=1 and A^3+B^4=C^11. Assume that the modular location in L032 has label 54a1. Thus B is odd, A=7 modulo 8, A=1 modulo 3, B^2 is 1 or 4 modulo 9, C is even and prime to 3, and the permitted Frey quadratic twists are d=1,-3. These remain necessary conditions only.

Let K be the degree-12 field attached to 54a1 in Freitas–Naskręcki–Stoll (FNS), *The generalized Fermat equation with exponents 2, 3, n*, Compositio Math. **156** (2020), 77–113, [final author manuscript](https://www.mathe2.uni-bayreuth.de/stoll/papers/genfermat-final.pdf), internally dated June 10, 2019, Section 7.2, printed pp. 25–27, especially the field-transport paragraph and Table 5. A defining polynomial is

    k^12-6k^10+6k^9-6k^8-126k^7+104k^6+468k^5
      +258k^4-456k^3-1062k^2-774k-380.

The field identification and degree are imported separately from the GRH-dependent Selmer dimensions in that table. No class-group calculation, rank, Selmer dimension, or Shafarevich–Tate vanishing is assumed. The field is not Q(mu_11).

General descent input: Schaefer–Stoll, *How to do a p-descent on an elliptic curve*, Trans. AMS **356** (2004), 1209–1231, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-03-03366-X), [June 13, 2001 long author manuscript](https://mathe2.uni-bayreuth.de/stoll/papers/p-descent-long.pdf), Proposition 4.6 (printed p. 7), Section 5 (pp. 8–10), and Section 9 (pp. 21–23). Their general theorem scope and the imported good-divisor/connecting-map identification are qualified in foundations/15-schaefer-stoll-isogeny-descent.md. We specialize those results; we do not reprove general isogeny descent.

## Conclusion

On the FNS model

    E0: y^2+y=x^3-x^2-10x-20,

the point T=(5,5) has exact order five. The nonconstant rational function

    f(x,y)=(x-4)y-4x^2+30x-55

has divisor 5(T)-5(O). The quotient h:E0 -> E1 by the subgroup generated by T has a model

    E1: Y^2+Y=X^3-X^2-7820X-263580.

Write theta for its dual, **theta:E1 -> E0**. The perfect isogeny pairing with T identifies E1[theta] with mu_5 over K, so the connecting map is an injection

    delta: E0(K)/theta E1(K) -> K^*/K^{*5}.

For P different from T and O it is [f(P)]. Its exceptional values are delta(O)=1 and delta(T)=[11^-1]. In particular, none of the zero/pole evaluations is discarded.

Let S consist of **all finite primes of K above 5 or 11**, and put

    K(S,5)={ [alpha] in K^*/K^{*5} : v(alpha)=0 modulo 5 for every finite v outside S }.

This is a finite group. The solution supplies a point P0 in E0(K), of degree 12 over Q, for which

    [f(P0)] in K(S,5),
    j(P0)=1728 A^3/C^11,
    (1728-j(P0))C^11/1728=B^4,     0<j(P0)<1728.

Moreover its class belongs to delta_v(E0(K_v)/theta E1(K_v)) at every v in S. In the j identities, j is the **modular j-map in FNS**, not the constant elliptic j-invariant of E0. The fourth-power identity and all original positivity/primitivity conditions remain required.

Equivalently, after choosing representatives of the finite group K(S,5), some representative alpha and some w in K^* satisfy f(P0)=alpha w^5, simultaneously with those identities. This is a necessary finite global class condition, not a computed class list, a sufficient description of solutions, an emptiness statement, or a bound on their heights.

## Proof

### Exact torsion and divisor arithmetic

The negation on E0 is (x,y) -> (x,-y-1). For a chord of slope s and intercept b the sum has coordinates x'=s^2+1-x1-x2, y'=-s x'-b-1. The tangent slope is (3x^2-2x-10)/(2y+1). Substitution verifies T is on E0. Its tangent slope is 5, giving 2T=(16,-61); addition and negation give 3T=(16,60), 4T=(5,-6), 5T=O. The first four points are distinct from O, so T has exact order five. These formulas are the ordinary chord-and-tangent group law, including the vertical-line case.

Set l_T=y-5x+20 and l_2=y+6x-35. Substituting the tangent lines into the equation gives respectively

    (5x-20)^2+(5x-20)-(x^3-x^2-10x-20)=-(x-5)^2(x-16),
    (-6x+35)^2+(-6x+35)-(x^3-x^2-10x-20)=-(x-16)^2(x-5).

The corresponding divisors are div(l_T)=2(T)+(3T)-3(O) and div(l_2)=2(2T)+(T)-3(O); here the leading integers denote divisor multiplicities, whereas parenthesized nT denotes the group multiple. Also div(x-16)=(2T)+(3T)-2(O). Hence

    f = l_T^2 l_2/(x-16)^2

has divisor 5(T)-5(O). Reduction using y^2=x^3-x^2-10x-20-y gives exactly the polynomial displayed in the conclusion; thus the apparent denominator at x=16 is removable. This verifies the claimed function on the whole curve. With the parameter t=-x/y at O, x has leading term t^-2 and y has leading term -t^-3. Therefore f has leading term -t^-5. Multiplying f by -1 would normalize that leading coefficient to one and has no effect on fifth-power classes.

### Explicit quotient and dual direction

Define

    X=x+110/(x-5)+121/(x-5)^2+1452/(x-16)+14641/(x-16)^2,
    2Y+1=(2y+1)dX/dx.

This is the explicit quotient map; it can also be obtained by summing x(P+Q)-x(Q) over the four nonzero points of the subgroup. We verify its required properties directly rather than assume a quotient model from a database. Let D=(x-5)^2(x-16)^2, N=DX and H=N'D-ND'. Exact polynomial multiplication gives

    (4x^3-4x^2-40x-79)H^2
      =4N^3D-4N^2D^2-31280ND^3-1054319D^4.

Dividing by D^4 proves the E1 equation. The source and target discriminants are -11^5 and -11 respectively, so both curves are nonsingular. N has degree five, D degree four, and N is nonzero at 5 and 16. Thus N/D has degree five. Since x and X each define degree-two maps to P^1, this morphism of curves has degree five. A rational map from a smooth projective curve to a projective smooth curve extends across its apparent poles; its poles here send precisely O,T,2T,3T,4T to the target origin. Its expansion at O sends O to O and has leading X=x, Y=y.

The standard origin-preserving morphism theorem for elliptic curves makes this morphism a group homomorphism (Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*, 2nd ed., 2009, Chapter III, Theorem 4.8). Its degree and the exhibited kernel therefore identify it as h:E0 -> E1, with kernel generated by T. We use the standard dual-isogeny theorem (ibid., Chapter III, Theorem 6.1) and perfect isogeny pairing, as in Schaefer–Stoll Section 9: theta h=[5] and h theta=[5]. These are standard structural inputs to the assessed isogeny construction, not additional arithmetic claims about K. Pairing E1[theta] with the rational generator T of E0[h] gives an isomorphism E1[theta] -> mu_5. It is Galois equivariant; mu_5 need not consist of K-rational points. It remains an isomorphism over every completion. Taking X={T} in Section 5 therefore gives D=K; the cohomology map is an isomorphism globally and locally, and its image is the whole Kummer group. This verifies both of that section's injectivity/image requirements. Using h itself as the descent direction would instead give a constant order-five kernel and would not justify this identification.

### Good divisors, normalization, and exceptional values

Section 5 identifies the connecting map for theta with evaluation of f on good degree-zero divisors representing a point. A concrete good divisor for P is (P+R)-(R), choosing R from {2T,3T,4T} so that P+R is neither T nor O. At most two of those three choices are forbidden; R itself is always good. Thus delta(P) is explicitly [f(P+R)/f(R)] for every P, including T and O. All evaluations here are nonzero and finite.

To prove the ordinary-point formula, let

    g=(x-5)/(y-5x+20),    F_2(P)=f(P+2T)/f(2T).

We have div(g)=(4T)+(O)-(T)-(3T), while div(F_2)=5(4T)-5(3T). Consequently F_2/(f g^5) is constant. At O, F_2 tends to one, g has leading term -t and f has leading term -t^-5. The constant is one. This proves F_2=f g^5 as rational functions, fixing the multiplicative normalization and giving delta(P)=[f(P)] away from the exceptional supports. The remaining evaluations are checked without limits:

| P | R for a good divisor | f(P+R)/f(R) | class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| O | 2T | 1 | 1 |
| T | 2T | -1/11 | [11^-1] |
| 2T | 2T | 1/121 | [11^-2]=[f(2T)] |
| 3T | 4T | 121 | [11^2]=[f(3T)] |
| 4T | 3T | -11 | [11]=[f(4T)] |

Indeed f(T)=0, f(2T)=-1331, f(3T)=121 and f(4T)=-11. The minus signs are themselves fifth powers. This covers every point excluded from the generic calculation and agrees with the homomorphism property. It also gives the check f(P)f(-P)=-(x(P)-5)^5 as a rational identity.

### Finite global condition and transport of the original solution

Both displayed integral models have unit discriminant at every prime not above 11, so both curves have good reduction there even after extension to K. Their Tamagawa numbers at those primes are one. Thus S above 5 and 11 contains all places required by Schaefer–Stoll Proposition 4.6; no assertion that primes above 11 can be omitted is made. With E1[theta]=mu_5, the unramified cohomology outside S is exactly the fifth-power classes with valuations divisible by five: at residue characteristic different from five, unit fifth-root extensions are unramified, while valuations detect the ramified part. The cited proposition and Section 5 therefore imply delta(E0(K)) lies in K(S,5), and the stated local-image conditions. Infinite places contribute no condition for this odd-degree isogeny.

Section 5's unit/class exact sequence specializes to

    0 -> O_{K,S}^*/O_{K,S}^{*5} -> K(S,5) -> Cl_S(K)[5] -> 0.

The unit group is finitely generated by the S-unit theorem, and the S-class group is finite by the number-field ideal-class theorem. Hence K(S,5) is finite unconditionally. This is a theoretical finiteness proof; no basis or group order has been computed.

L032 supplies the rational modular point and its original j identities. FNS Section 7.2 transports it to E0 over the specified K via the twelve cyclic subgroups of order 11; the fiber is irreducible of degree 12. Our j is neither 0 nor 1728 nor infinity, by positivity and nonzero bases. Thus P0 has that degree, cannot be one of the rational exceptional torsion points, and f(P0) is defined and nonzero. Applying the preceding map yields [f(P0)] in K(S,5). Transport preserves j, so the full fourth-power identity follows unchanged, rather than being replaced by a valuation or by elliptic x being a square. All primes of K above 5 and 11 enter S. Because h and f are rational, the construction commutes with every embedding of K into an algebraic closure. Conjugate points need not all be K-rational; no one-prime or one-embedding shortcut is used.

To make this a computed restriction requires certified S-units modulo fifth powers (or a subgroup of index prime to five), certified S-class-group five-torsion and principal generators for its lifted fifth powers, prime decompositions and completions at all v in S, and the local connecting images of theta. The 5-adic places require actual local precision/control, not just valuations. All this arithmetic concerns K itself, rather than the degree-36 two-torsion algebra. No GRH-based basis, rational-field Cassels ratio, rank-zero conclusion, or equality of global point classes with Selmer classes has been used.

The concrete downstream test is to form the finite global class image at every v above 5 and 11, then intersect it with classes [f(P_v)] of local E0-points whose modular j-values admit the simultaneous primitive local identities j=1728 a^3/c^11 and (1728-j)c^11/1728=b^4. For each rational prime p, a,b,c lie in Z_p and the same triple and j in Q_p must be used at every v above p; at least one of a,b,c is a unit. At v above 2, retain the L032 even-C branch as an additional test rather than presuming an obstruction there. Every branch and exceptional point must be retained. Empty intersection would exclude this branch; a proper intersection would only narrow it; unchanged images or uncertifiable class data would justify parking this mechanism. No part of that computation or comparison is performed here. The independent four twists and infinitely many signatures remain open.
