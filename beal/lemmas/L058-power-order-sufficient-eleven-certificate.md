# L058 — Sufficient power-order certificate for the eleven class

## Hypotheses

Use L033's characteristic-zero degree-twelve field K=Q(k), defined by

    m(X)=X^12-6X^10+6X^9-6X^8-126X^7+104X^6+468X^5
          +258X^4-456X^3-1062X^2-774X-380.

Let R=Z[k] and let O=O_K be the maximal integer ring. No equality R=O
is assumed. Use L041's complete maximal-order primes P1,P2,P3 above
eleven, ordered by residues of k equal to 8,2,10, with ramification
indices 1,1,10 and residue degrees one. Let S consist of every finite
prime above five or eleven. Use L045's ordinary class-group quotient

    V=Cl(K)/(5Cl(K)+<[Q]: Q above five>),   u=image([P1]) in V.

Set J=11R+(k-8)R. A fractional R-ideal here means a nonzero finitely
generated R-submodule A of K with dA contained in R for some positive
integer d; require its additive lattice to have rank twelve. Suppose
alpha in K* and e in Z are supplied and the following literal equality
of fractional R-ideals is verified:

    alpha R = J A^5 (5R)^e.                         (1)

For every integer e, (5R)^e means 5^e R, including negative e. It does
not denote an inverse of an arbitrary R-ideal. No witness satisfying
(1) is assumed to have been constructed in the notebook.

## Conclusion

The exact extension is JO=P1. Identity (1) implies

    (alpha)=P1 (AO)^5 (5O)^e,   [P1]=-5[AO] in Cl(K).

In particular u=0. The class [alpha] is in K(S,5) and its eleven
valuation vector modulo five is (1,0,0). Consequently both of L045's
local vectors (2,0,0) and (0,2,0) are global valuation vectors.

The exact rational-lattice verification procedure below is necessary
and sufficient to check the supplied lattice data and (1). It requires
neither a maximal-order basis nor factorization or inversion of A in R.
The resulting class certificate is sufficient, not equivalent to u=0:
it demands [P1] in 5Cl(K), whereas L045 allows arbitrary prime-five
classes as well. No class value, witness existence, global point,
integer solution or branch emptiness is established without a supplied
successful identity; even a successful identity only certifies the
stated valuation consequence.

## Proof

The [official PARI linear-algebra manual, mathnf](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/html/Vectors__matrices__linear_algebra_and_sets.html#mathnf),

The explicit inclusion-certificate alternative below avoids needing

### Exact extension at every finite prime

Since k is integral, J is an integral R-ideal and JO is generated over
O by 11 and k-8. In a maximal-order DVR the exponent of an ideal
generated by two elements is the minimum of their normalized orders.

At P1, L041 identifies the completion with Q11 and k with its integral
root congruent to 8. Thus ord_P1(11)=1 and ord_P1(k-8)>=1, and the
minimum is exactly one. No higher precision of that root is needed.
At P2 the residue of k-8 is 2-8=5 in F11, so it is a unit and the
minimum is zero. At P3 the residue is 10-8=2 in its actual residue
field F11, also a unit; the minimum is zero despite ord_P3(11)=10.
L041's full factorization accounts for all primes above eleven, rather
than just roots of m modulo eleven in a possibly nonmaximal order.
At every prime not above eleven, 11 is a unit, so the exponent is zero.
Localization equality and unique ideal factorization now give JO=P1.
In particular the prime extension conclusion is proved here by local
exponents, not assumed from a general extension rule.

### Fractional extension and the class consequence

Every full-rank rational lattice in the power basis has an integer
denominator clearing it into R. Choose d>0 with dA contained in R.
The module AO is a nonzero fractional O-ideal: its generators are the
finite generators of A, and dAO is contained in O. Integral product
preservation applied to J and dA gives

    (J(dA)^5)O = (JO)((dA)O)^5 = (JO)d^5(AO)^5.

Multiplying by the rational scalar d^(-5) proves
(JA^5)O=(JO)(AO)^5. Scalar extension commutes with multiplication by
every nonzero rational number, directly from the definition of the
generated O-module. Hence negative e causes no inverse-ideal issue:
extension of 5^e JA^5 is exactly 5^e(JO)(AO)^5. Extending (1) therefore
gives (alpha)=P1(AO)^5(5O)^e. All these are fractional ideals of O,
where the ordinary ideal class is defined. The ideals (alpha) and
(5O)^e are principal, so their classes vanish and [P1]=-5[AO]. Its
image u in V is zero.

For completeness the same equality tracks actual valuations. At a
prime outside S, ord(alpha)=5 ord(AO). At P1,P2,P3, rational five is
a unit, so the orders are respectively 1+5a1,5a2,5a3 for integers
ai=ord_Pi(AO). Orders at primes above five have no restriction; their
contributions include e ord_Q(5), with every Q retained. Thus [alpha]
is in K(S,5) and rho([alpha])=(1,0,0). Both local vectors belong to
the global projection by audited L045's dichotomy. Explicitly,
alpha^2 realizes (2,0,0), while (11/alpha)^2 realizes (0,2,0): L041's
complete indices give rho([11])=(1,1,10)=(1,1,0) modulo five. The
quotient and squares remain in K(S,5). This is a valuation statement;
it says nothing about matching unit classes or global points.

The scalar five factor in (1) is principal even before passing to V.
It only contributes the linked exponents e ord_Q(5), not arbitrary
independent exponents at the primes above five. General L045 witnesses
and fractional O-ideals need not descend to identities of the form
(1). No converse, contraction equality, or restricted completeness
claim is made, and a failed search for these identities could not show
u!=0.

### Exact rational-lattice verification specification

Use the fixed Q-basis E=(1,k,...,k^11), with all columns interpreted in
this order. A rational input is a finite list of integers and positive
denominators; all operations below are exact. The monic polynomial m
above defines reduction of every product to twelve coordinates. Let
T be its integer multiplication matrix for k. Validate the following
inputs: B in Mat_12(Q), det(B)!=0; a nonzero column a in Q^12 defining
alpha; and an integer e. Define the additive lattice A=B Z^12. A
common integer denominator of B gives dA contained in R. The test

    B^(-1) T B has every entry in Z                  (2)

is precisely kA contained in A. Because R=Z[k], closure under k and
addition makes A an R-module; conversely every fractional R-ideal
passes (2) for any Z-basis B. Thus rank and (2), rather than rank
alone, establish exactly the fractional-ideal hypothesis. The zero
column for alpha is rejected; in the field K, every other column is
a nonzero element and multiplication by it is invertible.

The columns of I_12 form a Z-basis of R. Consequently J has generator
matrix [11 I_12 | T-8 I_12]. Form the integer/rational multiplication
table by reducing E_i E_j modulo m. For two fractional R-ideals with
finite Z-generator lists (g_i) and (h_j), generate their product using
every reduced product g_i h_j. This Z-span equals the ideal product:
expanding arbitrary integer combinations gives inclusion in that span,
and each generator product belongs to the ideal product. It is already
R-stable, since k(g_i h_j)=(kg_i)h_j and kg_i is an integer combination
of the g_i. Thus no extra saturation, inverse, or maximal-order closure
may be inserted. Iterating this operation builds A^2,A^3,A^4,A^5 and
then JA^5; exact basis compression after each operation is allowed.
All these products have full rank: multiplication of a full-rank
lattice by any nonzero element of the other ideal is an invertible
Q-linear map whose image is contained in the product lattice.

Let M_alpha have column i equal to the reduced product alpha E_i.
The left lattice in (1) is M_alpha Z^12. Let C be the full generator
matrix for JA^5; the right lattice is generated by 5^e C. This uses
rational scaling for negative e, with no putative R-ideal inverse.

To compare the lattices, choose the same positive integer D clearing
all denominators of M_alpha and 5^e C. Obtain integer matrices
L=D M_alpha and N=D 5^e C. Test equality of their entire column
modules, not just covolumes. One valid certificate consists of integer
coordinate matrices X,Y satisfying

    L=N X,   N=L Y.                                 (3)

Exact verification of every entry proves both inclusions. Equality of
the modules conversely supplies such integer coordinate matrices, so
this certificate condition is exact. Alternatively use integer column
HNF with a fixed canonical convention. For each input matrix M a
certificate may supply H and a square unimodular U with

    M U=[0|H],  det(U)=+1 or -1,

and verify the chosen HNF conditions. Full rank makes H a 12-column
basis. Unimodularity proves that H spans precisely the input module;
equal canonical HNFs for L and N prove equality. An explicit check of
both integer inclusion matrices between HNF bases is also sufficient
without depending on a canonical convention. Finally multiplication by
the common nonzero scalar D is injective, so integer module equality
is exactly the original rational lattice equality (1).

A successful verification must preserve the supplied input data and
all exact product/equality certificates on disk; a summary reports
nonzero alpha, actual/required rank 12, stability, every inclusion or
HNF check, the identity outcome, failures, and artifact paths. This
paragraph specifies a future verifier; no implementation or successful
instance has been run here. Approximate lattice reduction, equal
determinants, equal norms or a bounded preview would not verify (1).

### Independent critical audit, 2026-10-12

The sufficient implication and the mathematical verification specification
pass without correction. This is a proof audit using the assessed standard
inputs cited above, not verification of an implementation or a witness.
In particular it does not establish any new ideal-class value. The checks
below retain the distinction between an exact verifier specification and
an executed certificate.

**Field and local orders.** The twelve coefficients below the leading
term of m agree with L033, including the zero coefficient of X^11.
The monic defining polynomial and degree twelve give the integral
generator and the stated Q-basis; no integral basis for O is inferred.
For the required part of L041, its reduction has the two simple roots
8,2 and the degree-ten block at 10. After X=U-1 the complementary
degree-two factor has unit constant term, while m(-1)=-209 has
eleven-adic valuation one. Its degree-ten Hensel factor is therefore
Eisenstein. These are exactly three field factors of degrees 1,1,10,
with residue degrees one and ramification indices 1,1,10, accounting
for the entire degree twelve. This justifies using maximal-ring primes,
even if the power order is nonmaximal.

In their normalized DVRs the pairs of orders for (11,k-8) have minima
min(1,ord_P1(k-8))=1, min(1,0)=0 and min(10,0)=0. The last two zero
orders follow from the nonzero residues 5 and 2, respectively. For any
other finite prime, 11 is a unit and both generators are integral, so
the minimum is zero. Thus the complete exponent list is exactly that
of P1. The first minimum requires only ord_P1(k-8)>=1; no claim that
this latter order equals one is needed. Neither a repeated factor
modulo eleven nor a prime-extension theorem substitutes for this check.

**Fractional data and the consequence.** If dA is integral in R, it is
an R-ideal, and extending the integral product J(dA)^5 gives
d^5(JO)(AO)^5. Dividing by the same scalar d^5 proves the fractional
product identity. Extending alpha R gives alpha O even when alpha
is not integral. For any integer e, multiplication by 5^e commutes
with extension; this does not require invertibility of A in R. The
nonzero fractional ideal AO is invertible in O by the imported
Dedekind theorem. Only there are ideal classes taken. Both scalar
ideals disappear in the ordinary class group, giving [P1]=-5[AO].
No sign or total-positivity condition is required.

At a prime outside S the resulting order of alpha is a multiple of
five. At the three eleven primes it is (1,0,0) modulo five, and all
primes above five remain allowed. Therefore alpha^2 has vector
(2,0,0). Since (11)=P1 P2 P3^10, the element (11/alpha)^2 has vector
2((1,1,0)-(1,0,0))=(0,2,0). Both elements remain in K(S,5).
This independently checks the explicit realization of both projected
vectors and agrees with L045's audited dichotomy. It gives no prescribed
unit classes. In particular the free integer e accounts only for the
linked exponents of rational five; it cannot replace the independent
prime-five classes in L045's necessary-and-sufficient criterion.

**Exact lattice verification.** Interpret B as a 12-by-12 rational
matrix of nonzero determinant. Rational denominators make B Z^12
commensurable with R. For every standard basis column e_i, the
coordinates of k B e_i in the B-basis are column i of B^(-1)TB.
Thus the integer-entry test is equivalent to kA contained in A;
iterating multiplication by k gives closure under every element of R.
Conversely an R-ideal with this basis necessarily passes the test.
The nonzero alpha column is essential: invertibility of its
multiplication matrix uses the field hypothesis from L033.

The two blocks 11I and T-8I generate exactly 11R+(k-8)R as additive
lattices. If G and H are full generator lists for two fractional
R-ideals, expansion of finite integer linear combinations proves that
all pairwise products generate their product. R-coefficients need no
extra generators: for r in R, r(g_i h_j)=(r g_i)h_j, and r g_i is an
integer combination of G. A single nonzero element of either ideal
times a basis of the other proves full rank of each successive product.
Induction therefore validates all four multiplications forming A^5
and the multiplication by J. Any intermediate compression must preserve
the whole lattice, verified by the same exact inclusion or HNF test;
choosing merely twelve independent columns would be insufficient.

More explicitly, if the final right generator list has q columns,
L has size 12-by-12 and N has size 12-by-q. In (3), require integer
X of size q-by-12 and integer Y of size 12-by-q. Their equations
certify the two inclusions column by column. Conversely equality of
the two Z-spans supplies all those integer coordinates, so existence
of these matrices is necessary and sufficient. The same denominator
D must clear both sides; multiplying both modules by D is injective
and reflects equality. Different denominators on the two sides without
rescaling would not be this test.

For any rectangular intermediate integer matrix M of size 12-by-q
and full row rank, q>=12. The HNF certificate requires an integer
q-by-q matrix U with determinant +1 or -1, a 12-by-12 matrix H,
and exactly q-12 zero columns in M U=[0|H]. Such U has an integer
inverse, proving equality of the column modules in both directions.
For q=12 the zero block is empty. Verify the stated HNF conditions
before using uniqueness, or use explicit two-way inclusions instead.
These checks apply also to every compression after denominator clearing.
No determinant comparison between L and N, floating approximation,
saturation or contraction of a maximal-order ideal is a substitute.
