# L037 — A faithful finite formula for the full-fiber image comparison

## Hypotheses

Use L033's degree-twelve field K, its defining polynomial m, the curve

    E0: y^2+y=x^3-x^2-10x-20,

and its connecting map delta. Put F=Q5 and A=F[k]/(m), the entire étale algebra of dimension twelve, with basis 1,k,...,k^11. Use L034's complete modular map j, including its degree, branch values, pole divisor O+11(3T), and removable value j(2T)=-121. Write

    j=(a_j(x)+b_j(x)y)/(x-16)^11

away from x=16. Here a_j and b_j are exactly L034's source polynomials: their complete rational coefficients are in `scripts/five-isogeny-modular-norm/check_norm.py`, variables `a`, `b1`, `b2`, with b_j=b1*b2. This is the audited source formula, not just the elimination polynomial F(x,j) from that result.

Let I_fiber and I_pow be exactly L035's sets in A^*/A^{*5}. All whole-fiber isomorphisms are included. L036 identifies the additional primitive scalar condition with its domain D. No nonemptiness of either image, precision cutoff, global arithmetic basis, rank, Sha or GRH hypothesis is added.

## Conclusion

The formulas below define I_fiber and I_pow through invertible representatives, and give a closed finite sentence S over Q5 such that

    S is true  if and only if  I_pow is a proper subset of I_fiber;
    S is false if and only if  I_pow = I_fiber.

All variables range over finitely many copies of Q5, all equations have rational coefficients, and the only valuation predicates have nonzero scalar arguments. Thus Cohen's cited decision procedure applies. **The sentence has not been decided.** This is a proved encoding, not an image computation, nonemptiness certificate, excluded class or integer exclusion. The potentially very large elimination has no asserted practical bound.

## Proof

### Coordinates and the full-fiber predicate

An A-variable means twelve F-variables in the fixed basis. Multiplication is explicitly polynomial: multiply the representatives and reduce modulo the monic polynomial m of L033. Thus each equality in A below is twelve polynomial equalities over F. A scalar s means s times 1_A.

Given x,y in A, let M(x,y) be the 12-by-144 matrix whose columns are the coordinates of

    x^i y^j,  0 <= i,j < 12,

in lexicographic order of (i,j). Define Gen(x,y) by the existence of a 144-by-12 matrix W over F with M(x,y)W=I_12. This is a finite polynomial predicate; it involves no quantification over subsets, bases or integer exponents.

Define Fib(s,x,y) by s(s-1728) != 0, the curve equation in A, Gen(x,y), and the existence of r,u in A such that

    r^2=r,
    r(x-16)=0,             r(y+61)=0,             r(s+121)=0,
    r u=0,                (x-16)u=1-r,
    (1-r)(a_j(x)+b_j(x)y-s(x-16)^11)=0.

Every displayed integer is interpreted as a scalar in A. In particular r is permitted to be a mixed idempotent; it is not restricted to 0_A or 1_A.

We prove that Fib(s,x,y) holds exactly when (x,y) arises from one of the whole-fiber isomorphisms B_s -> A in L035.

First, Gen(x,y) says exactly that x,y generate A as an F-algebra. To verify the bounded monomial assertion, apply Cayley–Hamilton to the two twelve-dimensional multiplication operators for x and y. Applying their monic degree-twelve identities to 1_A gives polynomial identities for x and y. Any monomial with an exponent at least twelve reduces to a linear combination with both exponents less than twelve. Consequently the 144 columns span F[x,y]. They span A exactly when the matrix has rank twelve, equivalent over the field F to the existence of a right inverse W. Neither linear independence of x alone nor selection of a field factor is being imposed.

Next decompose the already known étale algebra A as a product of fields solely to prove correctness of the formula; computing the decomposition is not a premise. In each field r is 0 or 1. If r=1, the equations force (x,y)=(16,-61)=2T and s=-121, whose modular value is correct by L034. If r=0, (x-16)u=1 makes the original rational formula defined, and the last equation gives j(x,y)=s. Thus every factor is a point of the actual finite fiber. The other point over x=16 is 3T=(16,60), a pole of j, and cannot satisfy either chart. Conversely every finite-fiber point with x=16 is 2T, while every other finite-fiber point has x-16 invertible. Assigning r factorwise in this way and choosing u=0 on r=1 factors, u=(x-16)^(-1) on the others, gives precisely these equations. This proves both directions of the chart description, including mixed exceptional factors. O is also a pole and is absent from every finite fiber.

The fiber j^(-1)(s) is a finite closed subscheme of the affine curve E0 minus O; its coordinate algebra B_s is therefore generated by the restrictions of x,y. The points just described induce an F-algebra homomorphism B_s -> A. If Gen holds, the image contains generators of A, so this map is surjective. By L034 both algebras have dimension twelve, hence it is an isomorphism. Conversely any B_s -> A isomorphism sends the coordinate generators to generators, so Gen and the chart equations hold. As s is outside 0,1728,infinity, this is also exactly the étale fiber used in L035. The argument retains every isomorphism: it places no normalization or ordering on the images of x,y or on the field factors.

### An invertible descent representative at every finite point

Put f(x,y)=(x-4)y-4x^2+30x-55. Define Rep(x,y,z) by the existence of e,h in A satisfying

    e^2=e,
    e(x-5)=0,             e(y-5)=0,
    e h=0,                (1-e) f(x,y) h=1-e,
    z=e/11+(1-e)f(x,y).

This predicate will only be used together with Fib. In a factor with e=1 it forces (x,y)=T=(5,5). In a factor with e=0 it forces f(x,y) to be nonzero. L033's divisor 5(T)-5(O) shows that, at finite points, f vanishes exactly at T. Thus e is exactly the idempotent selecting the T factors; such e,h always exist. The resulting z is invertible: its components are 1/11 at T and the nonzero ordinary values of f elsewhere. L033's good-divisor computation proves delta(T)=[11^(-1)] and delta(P)=[f(P)] for every other finite P. Therefore [z]=delta(P) in the complete product A^*/A^{*5}. In particular the scalar s=j(T)=-24729001 is retained whenever its whole fiber is isomorphic to A. No class is assigned to the zero f(T).

This uses the connecting-map identification imported and specialized in L033, not a new descent theorem: Schaefer–Stoll, *How to do a p-descent on an elliptic curve*, Trans. AMS 356 (2004), 1209–1231, [June 13, 2001 long manuscript](https://mathe2.uni-bayreuth.de/stoll/papers/p-descent-long.pdf), Section 5, pp.8–10 and Section 9, pp.21–24. Good-divisor evaluation and the dual-isogeny direction are essential. The finite-chart specialization above is needed because neither this general source nor its local subgroup-spanning criterion supplies the present restricted image formula.

Set R(s,x,y,z) = Fib(s,x,y) and Rep(x,y,z). We have proved

    I_fiber = { [z] : there exist s,x,y with R(s,x,y,z) }.

Although R selects a particular representative for each point, taking its fifth-power class loses no image class; every allowed point has such a representative.

### Primitive scalars and class collisions

For a scalar s, define Prim(s) to mean that there exist scalars a,b,c in F such that

    abc != 0,
    v5(a), v5(b), v5(c) >= 0,
    v5(a)=0 or v5(b)=0 or v5(c)=0,
    a^3+b^4=c^11,
    s c^11=1728 a^3.

All valuation arguments are nonzero, and there are only three field witnesses, shared across all twelve coordinates. This is L035's primitive condition literally, and L036 proves Prim(s) equivalent to s in D. Using these original witnesses avoids encoding D as an infinite disjunction of valuation shells. It neither omits nor adds any unit or valuation case of L036. In particular the same c supplies the fourth-power identity by subtraction. Consequently

    I_pow = { [z] : there exist s,x,y with R(s,x,y,z) and Prim(s) }.

For any two invertible representatives z,z', equality of their classes is exactly

    there exist w,v in A with wv=1_A and z=z' w^5.

The equation wv=1_A retains invertibility in every factor. This tests classes rather than equality of chosen representatives. No assumption about injectivity of the map from scalars or points to classes is made.

Define the closed sentence S as follows; s,s' are scalar variables and x,y,z,x',y',z',w,v are A-variables:

    exists s,x,y,z:
      R(s,x,y,z) and
      not exists s',x',y',z',w,v:
        R(s',x',y',z') and Prim(s') and
        wv=1_A and z=z' w^5.

Expand each predicate by its finite equations and existential variables specified above; the second R and Prim use fresh witness variables within the negated existential. This fixes the quantifier order: one baseline class must fail at every admitted scalar and every allowed isomorphism, allowing arbitrary fifth-power changes of representative.

If S holds, its outer witness gives a class in I_fiber. The proved image descriptions and class-equivalence test show that no point contributing to I_pow represents it. Thus the inclusion is strict. Conversely, a class in I_fiber minus I_pow has an R-witness by the first description. Any witness of the inner existential would place that class in I_pow, a contradiction, so S holds. Since I_pow is a subset of I_fiber by definition, failure of S is exactly equality. This also handles empty images correctly: if I_fiber is empty S is false, and both images are empty. No nonemptiness is smuggled into either direction.

### Imported decidability and the remaining computational gap

Every matrix, algebra variable and product power above expands into finitely many scalar variables and rational polynomial equations. Scalar inequalities s(s-1728)!=0 and abc!=0 are polynomial inequations; the only valuations are the nonzero arguments in Prim. There are no residue-ring sorts of variable depth, unrestricted integer multiplication, or quantifiers over algebra isomorphism sets. Thus the precise imported scope is Paul J. Cohen, *Decision Procedures for Real and p-Adic Fields*, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 22 (1969), 131–151, Theorem 3.1 and its elimination proof, pp.144–145, with the relative-decision qualification on p.146, [primary scan](https://math.uchicago.edu/~shmuel/lg-readings/Cohen-1969-Communications_on_Pure_and_Applied_Mathematics.pdf). Foundation 16 records the inspected statement and relevant proof cases; for Q5 the remaining fixed residue-ring questions are finite. We import that algorithm and prove only this applicability, without re-proving elimination or claiming to have run it.

The rank witness alone uses 1728 scalar entries, before introducing the remaining witnesses or duplicating R. This count is not a complexity lower bound or an impossibility result. It does explain why writing the finite formula supplies no small residue precision or practical computation. Neither an exact truth value of S nor a baseline realization was obtained in this step. L034's norm cannot decide S because it is already common to the baseline. The global five-twist emptiness problem and the other Beal signatures remain untouched.
