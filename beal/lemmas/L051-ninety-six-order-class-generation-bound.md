## Conclusion

The full-rank order R=Z[U,T] has discriminant D=d12^3 d3^12, where

    d12 = -307926804295486824275116956724109113198514489839954427904,
    d3  = -41229056.

Every ideal class of the maximal integer ring of L96 has an integral
representative of norm at most the integer

    B = 16460156850694399309804095571097723196641745023744202796810224532728857958603517413273185984617837444144736310140571124.

Its class group is therefore generated by prime ideals of norm at
most B. This conservative sufficient cutoff exceeds the proposed
enumeration threshold 10^6. It is an order-based upper bound, not a
lower bound for the actual class-generation workload. No field
discriminant, maximal order, actual signature, class/unit completeness,
Selmer completeness, branch exclusion or Beal resolution is asserted.

## Proof

Both generators are integral, since their equations are monic over Z.
Reduction by m and h makes R spanned over Z by U^i T^j,
0<=i<12,0<=j<3. Their Q-span is the entire compositum. Its cited degree
36 makes these products independent, hence a basis of the full-rank
order R. The tensor algebra Q[U]/(m) tensor_Q Q[T]/(h) consequently
maps isomorphically to L96. This uses the source degree: a nonzero trace
determinant alone would not establish that a tensor algebra is a field.

For a monic degree-n polynomial with descending coefficients c_j,
Newton's identities give power traces t_0=n and

    t_k = -(sum_{j=1}^{k-1} c_j t_{k-j} + k c_k), 1<=k<=n;
    t_k = -sum_{j=1}^n c_j t_{k-j}, k>n.

Apply these to m and h to obtain t_k,v_k respectively. Their trace
matrices are G12=(t_{i+i'}) and G3=(v_{j+j'}). Multiplication by
U^a T^b on the tensor algebra is the tensor product of the two
multiplication maps, with trace t_a v_b. The order trace matrix is
therefore G12 tensor G3, with determinant det(G12)^3 det(G3)^12.
The exact determinants are d12,d3 above.

Reproduce the arithmetic with

    python3 scripts/ninety-six-class-generation/workload.py

The full certificate is
`scripts/ninety-six-class-generation/workload-certificate.json`.
It saves every relevant power trace, all three trace matrices, exact
determinants and rounding evidence. Fraction-free Bareiss elimination
checks every division for exactness. The script also computes
(-1)^(n(n-1)/2) Res(f,f') from Sylvester matrices for m,h, checking
both Newton-trace discriminants independently of the trace construction.
A direct 36-by-36 determinant checks the tensor determinant formula.
These are necessary field-specific calculations, not reproofs of the
standard theorems or certification of the source's global computation.

Let Delta be the field discriminant. Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*,
v3.08, July 19, 2020,
[primary notes](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf),
Remark 2.25 and its proof, printed p.34, give
D=[O_L96:R]^2 Delta, hence |Delta|<=|D|. Theorem 4.3, printed p.70,
gives an integral representative of every ideal class of norm at most

    M = (36!/36^36)(4/pi)^s sqrt(|Delta|),

where s is the number of conjugate pairs of nonreal embeddings.
We use only 0<=s<=18, not an asserted actual signature. Since
3<pi<4, M is bounded above by

    C = (36!/36^36)(4/3)^18 sqrt(|D|) = (a/b) sqrt(|D|),
    a = 167669460258147894921875,
    b = 270340794539270092942081774497271692.

The certificate verifies exactly, with F=B-1,

    F^2 b^2 < |D| a^2 < (F+1)^2 b^2,
    |D| a^2 > (10^6)^2 b^2.

Thus B=ceil(C)>10^6 is a certified sufficient integer cutoff without
a decimal rounding premise. Factoring each such integral representative
into prime ideals shows that each factor has norm at most B; their
classes generate the class group. These are ideals of O_L96, not of R,
so enumeration still requires a rigorous maximal-order/ideal backend.
A large B rules out neither a smaller true field Minkowski bound nor
a smaller generating set, maximal-order refinement or a different
unconditional certification method.
