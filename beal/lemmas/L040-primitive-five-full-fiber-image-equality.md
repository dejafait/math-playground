# L040 — Primitive scalar witnesses do not shrink the full-fiber image at five

## Hypotheses

Work over Q5. Use L034's curve E0, complete modular map j and polynomials

    E0: y^2+y=x^3-x^2-10x-20,
    F(x,s)=h(x)^3+J(x)s-(x-16)^11 s^2,
    h=x^4-52820x^3+1333262x^2+4971236x+9789217.

Here J=2a-b is exactly L034's degree-eleven integral source polynomial;
its full coefficients are retained in the certificate below. Let A be
L035's entire algebra, identified by L038 with Q5 x U_11, where U_11/Q5
is unramified of degree eleven. Let I_fiber and I_pow be exactly L035's
images, permitting every whole-fiber isomorphism B_s -> A at every
s in Q5 minus {0,1728}. Let D be L036's primitive common-scalar domain.
No global solution, class list, precision bound, GRH, rank or Sha
assumption is imposed.

## Conclusion

Every scalar s with B_s isomorphic to A lies in

    (1+5 Z5) union (2+5 Z5) union (4+5 Z5)
      union [union over k>=1 of 5^(-11k) Z5^*],

which is a subset of D. Consequently

    I_pow = I_fiber != empty.

The equality retains the very same scalar, whole-fiber isomorphism and
point for each class. Thus primitive scalar witnesses at five add no
restriction to this baseline. This is a negative result for that filter,
not a computation of either image, global emptiness, or a Beal solution.

## Proof

### Source inputs and the degree-eleven point

Reuse L034's exact modular map and necessary relation F(x,j)=0 from
Freitas–Naskręcki–Stoll, *The generalized Fermat equation with exponents
2, 3, n*, Compositio Math. 156 (2020), 77–113, final author manuscript
June 10, 2019, Section 7.2, pp.25–27, equation (7.4),
[author copy](https://www.mathe2.uni-bayreuth.de/stoll/papers/genfermat-final.pdf).
The polynomial relation holds as an identity of rational functions on
E0. It therefore holds at every point where x and j are regular,
including the removable chart 2T=(16,-61), since a polynomial in regular
functions is regular there. We use only this necessary relation, never
claim that Q5[x]/(F(x,s)) presents every exceptional whole fiber.
L034 shows that O and 3T are poles, so every finite-fiber point is affine.

Fix any whole-fiber isomorphism B_s -> A. Project its universal point to
the U_11 factor and write it as P=(x,y). Since the finite fiber is a
closed subscheme of the affine curve, its algebra is generated by x,y.
Surjectivity onto U_11 therefore gives Q5(x,y)=U_11. The tower law shows
that [Q5(x):Q5] divides eleven. If it were one, the curve equation would
give [Q5(x,y):Q5]<=2, contradicting its degree eleven. Hence

    [Q5(x):Q5]=11.                                      (1)

This is a necessary consequence of the whole-fiber hypothesis, not an
assertion that a selected-factor point is sufficient for that hypothesis.
In particular every allowed isomorphism supplies such a point.

For lifting we reuse J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*,
[v3.08, July 19, 2020](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf),
Theorem 7.33 and Remark 7.36, pp.121–123: coprime monic residue factors
over a complete DVR lift to monic factors of the same degrees. These
primary hypotheses were already inspected for L038–L039. For simple
roots use Pete L. Clark, *Algebraic Number Theory II*,
[161-page author-hosted version](https://plclark.github.io/PeteLClark/Expositions/8410FULLL.pdf),
Theorem 1.79, printed p.58, retrieved 2026-10-08, revision date
unidentified: |G(r)|<|G'(r)|^2 gives a unique root in
|w-r|<|G'(r)|. This is the same input inspected for L036 and foundation
17. We import these standard results; only the explicit varying-scalar
specialization below is proved here.

### Integral scalars at the two branch residues cannot occur

If s is integral, F(x,s) is monic integral of degree twelve. For s=0 or
3 modulo five, its complete reductions, respectively, are

    Fbar_0=(x^2+x+1)^3 (x^2+4x+2)^3,
    Fbar_3=(x^3+3x^2+2)^2 (x^3+4x^2+4x+4)^2.

These equalities depend only on the residue of s, and so hold uniformly
on the two entire residue disks. In each line the two displayed powered
blocks are monic of degree six and coprime; their Sylvester determinants
modulo five are respectively 2 and 1. The full factors, products and
matrices are in the exact certificate below. Internal repeated roots in
a block are retained; neither block is assumed squarefree or irreducible.

Apply Milne's coprime factor lifting to these two blocks over Z5. In
either residue disk F(x,s) factors into two monic degree-six polynomials
over Q5. Any root has minimal polynomial dividing one of these factors,
so its degree is at most six. This contradicts (1), since 6<11. Thus an
integral compatible s is congruent to 1,2 or 4 modulo five. These three
whole residue disks are already in D by L036.

### Nonintegral scalars must have valuation divisible by eleven

Suppose v5(s)=-n<0 and put t=s^(-1), with v5(t)=n. First we show that
the x in (1) is integral. If v5(x)<0, set w=1/x, so v5(w)>0. The
polynomial

    G_s(w)=t^2 w^12 h(1/w)^3+t w^12 J(1/w)
             -w(1-16w)^11

has coefficients in Z5, since deg(h^3)=12 and deg(J)<=11. The relation
F(x,s)=0 gives G_s(w)=0. Its reduction is

    Gbar_s(w)=-w(1-w)^11.

In particular zero is a simple residue root with derivative -1=4
modulo five. Also G_s(0)=t^2, of positive valuation. Clark's theorem at
zero applies over Q5, because |G_s(0)|<1=|G_s'(0)|^2, giving a root
w0 in 5 Z5. Apply the same uniqueness statement over U_11: any root
with positive valuation equals this w0, since the derivative is a unit
and the uniqueness ball is exactly |w|<1. Thus w=w0 lies in Q5. It is
nonzero because G_s(0)=t^2 is nonzero, so x=1/w0 lies in Q5, contrary
to (1). This excludes all negative-valuation x without a Newton polygon
assumption or a finite-depth search.

Now x is integral in U_11. Divide F(x,s)=0 by s^2 to obtain

    (x-16)^11=t J(x)+t^2 h(x)^3.                        (2)

Reduction modulo the maximal ideal of U_11 gives (xbar-1)^11=0, hence
xbar=1. Therefore u=x-16 has positive valuation. Exact integer evaluation
gives

    h(16)=214358881 = 1 modulo 5,
    J(16)=81402749386839761113321 = 1 modulo 5.

Since x=16 modulo the maximal ideal, h(x) and J(x) are units. The two
terms on the right of (2) have distinct valuations n and 2n, so their
sum has valuation exactly n. In particular u is nonzero and

    11 v5(u)=n.

U_11 is unramified; with v5(5)=1 its value group is Z. Thus v5(u) is a
positive integer k, and n=11k. Every nonintegral compatible scalar lies
in 5^(-11k) Z5^*, as claimed. L036 includes every such shell in D.
