## Primary hypotheses and applicability

[Stacks Proposition 53.10.4, Section 53.10, tag 0C6L](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C6L),
statement and proof through the degree-one-sheaf reduction, was retrieved.
It characterizes P1, including the proper geometrically integral genus-zero
case with a degree-one invertible sheaf. For the proposed conic application,
the audit must check smoothness, geometric integrality and genus zero,
and that the rational point gives that sheaf. The theorem supplies no
particular slope formula or its inverse; those are L113's specialization
obligations, not a reason to reprove the general theorem.

[Stacks Theorem 53.2.6, tag 0BY1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BY1),
statement and proof, was retrieved in full. Transcendence-degree-one fields
correspond to normal proper curves and nonconstant maps; its proof extends
the generic map across the nonsingular source and proves uniqueness.
Over Q the audit must first verify the full inverse fields and the smooth
proper geometrically integral models. The theorem then extends those
maps; it neither computes rational exceptional fibers nor excuses their
explicit local-ring analysis. Equality of genus or elliptic invariants
alone is insufficient.

[Stacks Section 53.12, tag 0C1B](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C1B),
the differential/different derivation, characteristic-zero formula and
Lemmas 53.12.1–2, was retrieved. The genus formula needs smooth proper
curves with the stated constant fields and a finite separable extension.
In characteristic zero ramification contributes e−1, weighted by residue
degree. Verify squarefreeness, geometric integrality, degree two and all
finite/infinity ramification before using it. Counting eight affine roots
without examining infinity would not meet the audit scope.
