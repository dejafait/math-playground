## Primary theorem and essential cases actually read

J.S. Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, second edition, Booksurge,
2006, [author's corrected 1 July 2006 PDF](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/ADTnot.pdf),
I.4 pp.48–49 defines G_S using extensions unramified outside S and
requires every archimedean place in S. On pp.55–57, for a finite
G_S-module M with order invertible in the S-integer ring, put
M^D=Hom(M,bar(k)^*). Theorem 4.10(b) gives the exact local stage

    H^1(G_S,M) -> P_S^1(k,M) -> H^1(G_S,M^D)^*.

The last map is the dual of localization through local Tate duality.
At infinite places Milne uses Tate-modified cohomology. Read the
definitions on pp.55–56, statement on pp.56–57, Lemmas 4.12–4.13
on pp.58–60, and finite-module/real-place completion on pp.61–62.
The proof treats finite S; cofinite S is NOT an additional assumption
for this finite module. Corollary 4.15, p.62, supplies finiteness for
finite S. The author edition page and Stanford-hosted identical text
were both accessible. The mistaken initial URL ending ADT.pdf was not.
No reproof of this theorem is proposed.

For the intended application the base field in this quotation is D,
not K and not its rational cubic F. Take M=mu_2, S=S_D union the
infinite places. D is a characteristic-zero number field by L069;
2 is inverted, mu_2 is unramified, and all its roots already belong
to D. The dual is the order-two character module. These source
hypotheses present no access blocker. Translating the cohomology
statement into the particular norm-square containing space still
requires the separate application described below.
