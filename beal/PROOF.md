# Beal conjecture: argument overview

The exact target is the nonexistence of a positive primitive solution to A^x + B^y = C^z with x,y,z > 2. The primary statement and the equivalence of primitivity with pairwise coprimality for an actual solution are recorded in foundations/01-target-and-scope.md. No candidate proof or disproof has been developed.

L002 reduces the target exactly to exponents in S = {4} union {odd primes}, by taking divisors of each exponent and raising the corresponding bases to positive integer powers. C002a then excludes equal-exponent signatures, every placement of (p,p,3) and (p,p,4) for odd primes p >= 5, and the ordered signature (4,4,3). These exclusions use Fermat's Last Theorem and Darmon–Merel; Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor's full-modularity theorem discharges the latter's historical cube-clause hypothesis. Precise named sources are in foundations/02-standard-results.md.

For the residual family a^3+b^3=c^p, L003 factors the left side into s=a+b and q=a^2-ab+b^2. The exact possibilities are (s,q,c)=(u^p,v^p,uv), with 3 not dividing uv, or (3^(p-1)u^p,3v^p,3uv), with 3 not dividing v; in each case gcd(u,v)=1. The identity 4q=s^2+3(a-b)^2 gives reversible square-discriminant systems preserving positivity and primitivity. This supplies a further integer constraint, without excluding either branch.

L004 applies Freitas's Theorem 3 to exclude all three placements of (3,3,p) when p >= 17 is prime and p is congruent to 2 modulo 3. Signed cube rearrangements preserve nontriviality and primitivity. L002 transfers these exclusions to any original exponents with the corresponding divisors; L003's converse also makes both complete factor systems empty in this range. The precise published citation is in foundations/03-freitas-repeated-cube-theorem.md.

L005 imports Kraus's necessary restrictions through precise published restatements: for prime p >= 17, a primitive nonzero cube solution has c congruent to 3 modulo 6 and v_2(ab)=1, without a condition on p modulo 3. Hence L003's system I is empty throughout this prime range. In system II the parameters u,v are odd, v is congruent to 1 modulo 12, and d is odd and prime to 3. The source-access limitation and exact citations are in foundations/04-kraus-divisibility-restriction.md; Kraus's original full text was not retrieved.

These restrictions do not obstruct system II at 2 or 3. L006 constructs compatible roots of its discriminant at both primes and verifies the exact local valuations. Infinitely many coprime auxiliary pairs also meet the prime-support condition and admit a real root within the positivity bound. These roots need not be one integer, so this closes only the exceptional-prime exclusion route under the tested restrictions.

L007 retains the simultaneous power conditions through factorization in the norm-Euclidean Eisenstein ring. With W=3^(p-2)u^p it extracts (d+W sqrt(-3))/2=((r+t sqrt(-3))/2)^p, where r,t are odd and coprime and r^2+3t^2=4v. The coefficient condition is t U_p=3^(p-2)u^p for the integer recurrence U_0=0, U_1=1, U_(n+2)=r U_(n+1)-v U_n. It forces v_3(t)=p-2+p v_3(u), and the only common factor of t and U_p is possibly p, with v_p(U_p)=1 when p divides t. Thus U_p must be a signed pth power or p times one. The converse retains the strict positivity bound on the trace T_p. This is an exact global reduction, not an exclusion.

L008 verifies that these conjugates form a nondegenerate Lucas pair and applies Bilu–Hanrot–Voutier, including the cited small-index classification, at every prime p >= 17. A primitive divisor ell satisfies ell congruent to plus or minus 1 modulo 6p, forcing k >= 6p-1 in either complete coefficient shape. At p=31, however, a construction gives the primitive divisor 373 arbitrary exact multiplicity while retaining the norm restrictions, the first-case shape of t, and the strict trace inequality. It does not impose U_31=k^31. The named source and its range are in foundations/05-primitive-divisor-theorem.md.

L009 applies Bruin's Theorems 1 and 2 to exclude all three placements of (3,3,4) and (3,3,5), including base-one cases. Moving a cube supplies the signed rearrangements even when the singleton exponent is four. L002 transfers these exclusions to original signatures with the corresponding exponent divisors, and L003 makes both complete factor systems empty at n=4,5. The precise published statements and source-access qualifications are in foundations/06-bruin-small-exponent-theorems.md.

## Unresolved gap

The missing result is zero positive primitive integer solutions for every residual signature in S^3 after these exclusions. Infinitely many signatures remain, including (3,5,q) for primes q >= 7. Among repeated-cube primes p >= 5, the assembled exclusions leave p=11 and primes p congruent to 1 modulo 3. For the latter primes at least 19, only the constrained system II remains; p=7,13 are below L005's range. The boundary signature (4,3,4) and its summand swap (3,4,4) also remain outside these applications. This describes the scope of the assembled inputs, not the literature-wide status of every listed case.

Local solvability provides no exclusion of an unrestricted signature: L001 constructs admissible primitive residues for every modulus and nonzero primitive p-adic solutions for every prime. It does not produce a single integer solution. Darmon–Granville gives only finitely many primitive solutions for each fixed remaining signature, whose reciprocal sum is below one. Finiteness per signature supplies neither emptiness nor uniform control as the signatures vary.

## Partial results

The exponent reduction preserves positivity, base-one cases, and the prime divisors of each base. The global exclusions above are rigorous consequences of named theorems; their assembly narrows the target but is not a new theorem settling arbitrary mixed exponents. No effective height bound or counterexample has been obtained.

In the repeated-cube reduction, all prime factors of v are 1 modulo 3. Positivity restricts v to intervals whose widths grow proportionally to u^2 for fixed p, so these are not upper height bounds. Explicit coprime pairs satisfy each quadratic-factor power condition separately while failing the required sum condition. L004 and L009 meet the zero-solution threshold on their stated exponent ranges by cited global theorems. L005 reaches zero solutions for branch I at all primes at least 17, but its parity and divisibility restrictions do not exclude system II at the complementary primes.

The local-solubility result remains a limitation on one proof mechanism. An unrestricted congruence sieve needs zero surviving primitive classes, whereas at least one survives at every modulus, with pairwise coprime integer representatives above any prescribed lower bound. Compatible p-adic lifts prevent exclusion by increasing prime-power precision. Congruences combined with a global descent or height restriction are outside this negative result's scope.

For the constrained cube branch, the discriminant is automatically 1 modulo 8 and modulo 3. L006 proves lifting to every precision, including finite combinations of the two primes. Its real-feasible auxiliary family leaves the same missing requirement: an actual integer square. No obstruction at other primes or passage from local points to integer points is established.

The Eisenstein reduction exposes a perfect-power recurrence term while preserving its other constraints. The bounds |t| >= 3^(p-2) and k >= 6p-1 are lower bounds; neither gives an upper height bound or an exclusion. The primitive-divisor application supplies no incompatible valuation. Its multiplicity controls do not make the full recurrence term a perfect power: the three exact controls fail that condition modulo 311. A theorem forcing some divisor to have unsuitable multiplicity under the whole coefficient equation, or another global restriction, is still missing.

## Known traps checked

- The common divisor in the target is shared by all three bases. Pairwise coprimality is justified from the equation, not assumed equivalent for arbitrary triples.
- All variables are positive in the target, and bases equal to one remain included. A zero residue or a p-adic solution is not an integer counterexample.
- Power substitutions use divisors of each exponent separately; they preserve prime support without dividing by a common factor or asserting that unequal exponents become equal. No reverse lift to a prescribed original signature is assumed.
- Moving a repeated pth-power term into a sum is justified by (-a)^p = -a^p only for odd p. In particular the exclusion of (4,4,3) is not silently extended to (4,3,4).
- The square clause requires repeated exponent at least four: 1^3+2^3=3^2 shows why it cannot simply be extended to three. This example is not a Beal counterexample. All auxiliary triples meet the source's primitive and nontrivial hypotheses.
- Surviving congruences, numerical checks, and fixed-signature finiteness do not prove or disprove Beal. Local solutions may differ with the prime or modulus.
- The lifting estimate handles p dividing z, including p = 2; no simple-root hypothesis is silently assumed.
- The historical modularity hypothesis is discharged by a separately named theorem; no abc assumption or exchange of local and global quantifiers enters the result. Library coverage is explicitly not checked.
- The two cubic factors can share 3. In that case the quadratic factor has valuation exactly one and the sum has valuation p v_3(c)-1; 3 may divide the auxiliary u. Parity and coprimality in the reconstructed bases are proved, and the n=2 control solutions are explicitly outside Beal's exponent range.
- Freitas's numbered theorem requires p >= 17 as well as (-3/p)=-1. The lower bound is retained, all signed auxiliary coordinates are nonzero, and the prime condition is not extended to the complementary residue class. No density assertion or separate theorem quoted in that paper's introduction is silently imported.
- Kraus's divisibility restriction is imported separately through numbered published restatements, with the original-full-text access limit recorded. Its symmetric parity condition permits a swap of the cube bases; it cannot be applied as an asymmetric constraint to both labelings. Its prime lower bound is retained, and removal of one factor branch is not removal of the family.
- In the constrained discriminant test, binary lifting is proved with one extra power of 2 rather than an invalid simple-root hypothesis. Division by 2 in the reconstructed 2-adic bases preserves integrality and gives the exact product valuation. The real, 2-adic, and 3-adic roots remain different possible coordinates; prime support and real feasibility do not supply an integer square.
- The Eisenstein extraction proves coprimality of conjugates, handles all six units using gcd(p,6)=1, and retains odd half-integer coordinates. The coefficient factors may share p; assuming they are always coprime would lose a case. Their signs, the sum-power equality, and the strict positivity bound remain required. A lower height bound or a prime divisor without a multiplicity restriction is not an exclusion.
- Primitive-divisor eligibility includes the root-of-unity exclusion. The n>30 theorem and the small-index table are distinct inputs. Primitivity of a divisor does not mean valuation one; a given divisor's high multiplicity does not imply that every divisor has it, or that the complete coefficient term is a perfect power. The construction retains the trace inequality but is not a Beal counterexample.
- Bruin's numbered statements at n=4,5 apply to signed nonzero integer coordinates with no base-one exception. The fourth-power placement uses the odd cube for sign conversion; it does not cover two fourth powers and one cube. The abstract's reductions at 7,11,13 are not imported as completed exclusions. The published theorems are cited without claiming independent reproduction of their full computations.
- The residual nonexistence statement is equivalent to the target but remains unproved; writing the equivalence is not claimed as a resolution.
