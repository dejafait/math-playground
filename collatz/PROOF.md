# Collatz conjecture: argument overview

The exact target and shortcut-map conventions are recorded in [foundations](foundations/01-target-and-scope.md). No complete candidate argument has been developed.

## Unresolved gap

The missing claim is that every positive starting integer greater than 1 eventually has a shortcut iterate smaller than itself. Strong induction would then give convergence to 1. This criterion is equivalent to the target and is not an established input.

A proposed sufficient route sought descent of log(n)+h(n mod q) within a common finite number of steps, outside a finite base set. Finite sublevel sets would make this enough for termination, after the base set was proved to converge. L001 rules out this route for every finite modulus, window, and correction h.

Allowing an entire maximal odd run followed by all consecutive even steps gives a variable-length block. L002 derives its exact endpoint but rules out automatic descent during that first block. L003 extends the obstruction to every fixed number of complete blocks, including finite residue corrections at their endpoints. L004 also excludes decrease on every block for log(n+5)+F(v_2(n+5)), with arbitrary real-valued F: an unbounded correction using this single valuation does not repair the route. Unbounded return times depending on the start, richer arithmetic information, and other mechanisms remain open. There is still no universal eventual-descent estimate.

Within the restricted alphabet {(2,1),(3,1)}, L005 excludes infinite eventually periodic block itineraries by an exact valuation bound for repetitions of any fixed word. It leaves aperiodic itineraries open. L007 gives forced backward decoding except at the earliest block, with at most two histories of any fixed depth ending at one integer. L008 converts this into an exact endpoint density, but its finite-interval error prevents the resulting upper bound from excluding all endpoints at a fixed starting cutoff. A starting-size lower bound tending to infinity uniformly over all mixed words would exclude infinite aperiodic itineraries too; none of these results establishes it, and even that bound would leave other block types and later compensation unresolved.

Adding both single-letter shifts to a potential does not close this restricted gap. L006 excludes decrease on every permitted block for log(n+5)+F(v_2(n+5),v_2(11n+19)), with arbitrary F. A legal growing two-block path returns to the same pair of valuations, so the correction cancels. This leaves unbounded return times and arithmetic information beyond this pair unresolved.

Adjoining the contracting block (1,1) preserves forced backward decoding: L009 bounds the number of histories of any fixed depth at one endpoint by three. This now covers both growth and contraction, but inverse size can increase and the fixed point 1 has histories of every depth. Every start greater than 1 must leave this enlarged alphabet if it is to reach 1; that escape claim remains unproved. Allowing the additional block (1,2) breaks the rigidity: L010 gives overlapping extendibility classes and five histories of depth two at one endpoint. Thus the unique decoder does not directly extend to variable even-run lengths. Neither result gives the required forward escape or eventual-descent estimate.

Requiring each history to stay above its own start changes the question. L011 proves common suffixes through three blocks and restricts any deeper first merge to odd-run lengths 1 and 2, or 2 and 3, with two consecutive (1,2) contractions on one side. The paired states satisfy z=4y+1 at indices one block apart. L012 forces a (1,1) contraction on the companion side and gives exact paired inverse transitions retaining both starting thresholds. Whether earlier growth can compensate these contractions while both histories remain non-descending is unresolved. The transition calculation has produced no invariant valid at every depth. Even ruling out every such merge would leave the fixed-start depth estimate and other block types open.

A distinct forward reduction is now quantitative: L013 proves that every positive start reaches {1,2} or residue 20 modulo 27 within ceil(24 log(n)+14) shortcut steps. Its stopped modular rank reproduces a sourced certificate; the explicit constants specialize it with arithmetic control of the residue-26 loop. This supplies bounded excursions into the target set, but neither the rank nor its time bound compares successive target visits. The [inspected first-return obstruction](drafts/literature/2026-10-03-residue20-first-return-descent.md) rules out strict decrease at every first return. A terminating mechanism on those visits is still missing, so this is not universal eventual descent.

L014 imports a different restriction: if any positive start is nonconvergent, there is a least nonconvergent residue-20 start n, and v_3(n+7) is 3 or 4. L015 imports the refined ancestor theorem to restrict that same fixed root further to v_3(4n+1)<=12. L016 applies its individually guarded lower rows, leaving only t=1 when the valuation is 11 or 12 and giving lower caps in the other unit/residue cylinders. Each excluded case has a smaller positive residue-20 ancestor whose actual shortcut orbit reaches the original n, contradicting minimality. Those rows leave valuation 3 untouched; on that domain L017 imports the complementary bound v_3(128n-157)<=16. L018 additionally imports the two fixed-cylinder exclusions n!=4529+19683s and n!=17813+59049s for s>=0. The necessary conditions still leave an infinite residual class; no convergence or descent mechanism on that class has been established.

An [inspected bounded-ancestor obstruction](drafts/literature/2026-10-04-residue47-bounded-ancestor-cover.md) rules out a uniformly bounded smaller residue-20 ancestor cover even on the surviving class 47+243k, despite a finite base set. Variable-duration forward excursions and general coalescence remain available. The reviewed forward theorem requires a substantive final-halving guard; no universal satisfaction of that guard or root-relative termination follows.

## Partial results

L001 proves an exact obstruction: arbitrarily large starts have arbitrarily long growing shortcut prefixes that retain one residue modulo any prescribed modulus. Their corrected logarithmic size increases at every time in the window. This limits a proposed proof method; it establishes no new family of convergent orbits.

For odd n=2^a u-1 with u odd, L002 gives b=v_2(3^a u-1) and the next odd block endpoint R(n)=(3^a u-1)/2^b. Strict descent holds exactly when (2^(a+b)-3^a)u>2^b-1. Every positive pair (a,b) occurs infinitely often, and n=16t+11 gives R(n)=18t+13>n. This rules out first-block contraction; it does not control consecutive blocks.

L003 proves that the first K blocks all have (a,b)=(2,1) exactly when 2^(3K+1) divides n+5. Their endpoints satisfy R^j(n)+5=(9/8)^j(n+5), and no intermediate shortcut state descends below n. Multiplying n+5 by any modulus q keeps all endpoints in one residue, defeating bounded-block endpoint descent for log(n)+h(n mod q). Each fixed positive n has only floor((v_2(n+5)-1)/3) initial repetitions; this finite escape bound neither forces later compensation nor proves an infinite growing positive orbit.

That valuation loss can compensate for growth on (2,1) blocks, but L004 gives growing (3,1) blocks 128s+103 to 216s+175 with v_2(n+5)=2 at both endpoints. Every correction F of that valuation cancels, leaving potential change at least log(5/3)>0 for arbitrarily large starts. Other (3,1) blocks increase both size and valuation, with unbounded valuation gains. These facts refute the proposed correction at a single block; they supply no bound on later compensation.

For each finite mixed word, L005 gives one exact residue class and an affine endpoint (Pn+C)/2^D. The number of initial complete copies of that word is floor((v_2((P-2^D)n+C)-1)/D), finite for every positive start. Its starting-size bound diverges for a fixed word repeated increasingly often, but is not uniform over arbitrary words. Exact enumeration through length 12 supplies no infinite-length inference. The mixed word (2,3,2,2) starts at 603, below the repeated-(2,1) bound 8187, so that sharp bound cannot be transferred unchanged.

L006 constructs successive block endpoints 8192s+4699, 9216s+5287, 15552s+8923 for s>=0, with types (2,1),(3,1) and joint valuations (5,2),(2,6),(5,2). Every correction of this pair cancels over the two blocks, while the logarithmic increment is at least log(93/49)>0. Thus at least one block increases the potential by a fixed positive amount, at arbitrarily large starts. No linear or nonlinear correction using only this pair can meet the required per-block decrease.

L007 proves that an odd endpoint m has inverse branches G_2(m)=(8m-5)/9 when m=4 modulo 9 and G_3(m)=(16m-19)/27 when m=13 modulo 27. The respective predecessors can themselves have an allowed predecessor exactly when m=76 modulo 81 and m=175 modulo 243. These classes are disjoint, so at most one branch can extend. Induction bounds the number of depth-K histories at two, differing only in their earliest state. The backward size bound n_0<(8/9)^K m controls depth for a fixed endpoint, not for a fixed start with an unrestricted future endpoint.

L008 describes depth-K endpoints as 2^(K-1) disjoint odd residue classes, with exact density delta_K=(1/18)(4/27)^(K-1) among all integers. Their count below X differs from delta_K X by less than 2^(K-1). All histories starting at most N end below X_K=(27/16)^K(N+5), where the density contribution is 3(N+5)/(8*4^K), but the error bound grows. At N=175, K=4 the endpoint 1453 is below X_K while the density contribution is 135/512<1, refuting its use alone as an upper count. The true count's eventual vanishing and the required uniform least-start bound remain unresolved.

For {(1,1),(2,1),(3,1)}, L009 gives extendible inverse branches exactly on m=1 modulo 9, m=22 modulo 27, and m=13 modulo 81, respectively. These classes are disjoint; v_3(2m+1) forces the only possible extendible odd-run length. At most three histories end at any m at each depth, all sharing every state after the earliest. Only the constant history ends at 1. The new inverse (4m-1)/3 grows for m>1, so the old backward contraction bound does not extend and no forward escape estimate has been obtained.

For {(1,1),(2,1),(3,1),(1,2)}, L010 recomputes the allowed endpoint union as 3 not dividing m. The b=1 extendibility classes become m=1 or 4 modulo 9, m=13 or 22 modulo 27, and m=13 or 40 modulo 81; the new branch (8m-1)/3 extends on m=2 or 5 modulo 9. The first three sets are nested, with three extendible branches on infinite positive classes. At most five depth-two histories end at any m, and endpoint 661 attains five. This refutes the old uniqueness and bound three for this larger alphabet, without proving unbounded multiplicity or an infinite orbit. The fixed point 1 still has no nonconstant incoming history.

For a first merge in L011, write 2m+1=3^s w with 3 not dividing w. The two branches must have s in {2,3} and types (s-1,1),(s,1). If y=2^s w-1 is the latter penultimate state, the former history has the tail ((32y+7)/3,4y+1,(3y+1)/2,m), with y=1 modulo 3. The first two blocks of that tail contract. L012 additionally gives v_3(2y+1)=1 and y=1 modulo 9: the companion predecessor is v=(4y-1)/3 and the earlier pair is (8v+5,v), still one block apart. Its five simultaneous inverse choices and exact affine interval test specialize known inverse-word arithmetic. The saved paired search finds no failure through depth 11. These necessary conditions and finite tests do not bound compensating growth; the analytical rigidity theorem remains through depth three.

L013 checks all 25 internal edges of the fifteen-residue core with weights 16,28,49 and contraction at most 20/21. The initial multiples-of-3 phase enters at height at most 5n/3; the core exits below 9n. At residue 26, exactly v_2(y+1) odd steps are followed by an even exit to residue 13 and one step to residue 20. Adding these size-dependent phase lengths yields the stated logarithmic bound with the positive offsets retained. This is a quantitative specialization of known visitation, without an originality claim.

For n=243k+236, k>=0, L014 checks the sourced predecessor c(n)=216k+209, with c(n)=20 modulo 27, 0<c(n)<n, and the actual shortcut path through 324k+314 and 162k+157 to n. Thus any least bad residue-20 root has v_3(n+7) in {3,4}. This is a known imported reduction with a local applicability check, not an advance beyond the inspected source.

L015 applies the cited uniform ancestor cutoff at valuation 13 to the original least root. All positive-integer and residue hypotheses match, and convergence transfers even if the smaller ancestor reaches 1 before its specified hit of n. The resulting bound v_3(4n+1)<=12 is a local imported restriction, not progress beyond the checked literature or a termination theorem for the remaining roots.

L016 records the exact complement of the cited lower thresholds: t=2 permits only v<=6, t=5 or 8 only v<=10, and t=7 only v=3; t=4 has five separately guarded caps between 5 and 10. Here v=v_3(4n+1), u=(4n+1)/3^v and t=2^(v-2)u modulo 9. The source's guards, positivity and original-root order are retained. These imported exclusions narrow the least-root domain without a return transition theorem or an exclusion of valuation 3.

L017 applies the cited complementary-coordinate theorem at threshold 17 to the original least root when v_3(4n+1)=3. Its necessary bound v_3(128n-157)<=16 excludes a further infinite family surviving the older rows. The family n=47+243k, k>=0, still satisfies all recorded conditions, with v_3(128n-157)=3. These are admissible candidate integers, not nonconvergent starts; the application is known imported coverage and leaves their convergence unresolved.

L018 applies the cited fixed-cylinder certificates with ancestors 3179+13824s and 16679+55296s, reaching the respective roots in 9 and 11 shortcut steps. Positivity, residue membership and strict original-root order hold for all s>=0. Their old valuations are 3 and their complementary valuations are 7 and 4, so L014–L017 had left them admissible. The family 47+243k avoids both new cylinders and remains unresolved. These are imported least-root exclusions, not independent convergence proofs for the excluded families.

[Tao's Theorem 1.3](foundations/02-known-results.md) is recorded as background: every diverging bound eventually dominates the orbit minimum for a set of starts of logarithmic density 1. It supplies neither the bound 1 nor the universal quantifier, and is not used to prove L001.

## Known traps checked

- The source target concerns every positive integer. Neither density-one results nor finite computation remove all possible exceptions.
- Computations check identities, finite residues, and finite histories only; the lemmas have full elementary proofs and use no probabilistic parity assumption.
- A witness is chosen after fixing the finite window. Arbitrarily long growth does not produce one positive integer growing forever.
- The two Collatz maps and their time conventions are distinguished in foundations. Reaching 1 is equivalent for them.
- Eventual descent is explicitly an unproved target-equivalent criterion. The fixed-window and fixed-complete-block strengthenings have been refuted, not smuggled into a proof.
- The complete-block formula retains its positive additive term. Consecutive (2,1) blocks are established by exact congruences, without assuming independence. Satisfying those congruences for all K would require n=-5, outside the positive domain.
- Arbitrarily long growing prefixes and their sharp exponential starting-size cost do not decide what happens after the prefix ends. The finite residue correction obstruction for complete blocks concerns endpoints only.
- Compensation for valuation loss on one block type is not a global potential inequality. L004 keeps the logarithmic and valuation terms together and gives the opposite of the required sign; it excludes only the stated single-valuation correction with decrease required on each block.
- An odd final affine endpoint implies a prescribed mixed itinerary only because L005 proves all intermediate exact run conditions. Finite word enumeration and exclusion of eventual periodicity do not exclude aperiodic infinite words. The repetition estimate holds a word fixed; no exchange with a limit over all words is made.
- The valuation pair returning in L006 is not an integer orbit cycle. The obstruction uses one legal two-block path at each of unboundedly many starts, without assuming that these paths concatenate indefinitely. It excludes the specified potential certificate, not aperiodic itineraries or Collatz.
- Backward rigidity in L007 does not turn a finite past at each endpoint into a finite future at a fixed start. The inverse congruences include positivity and exact maximal run lengths; their multiplicity bound is not a uniform least-start estimate.
- L008 keeps the residue-class boundary error when both the depth and endpoint cutoff grow. A shrinking density contribution is not a count below one. Its endpoint count includes histories starting above N; failure of this sufficient upper bound to shrink does not show that the true count fails to vanish.
- L009 recomputes extendibility for the enlarged alphabet and checks positivity and exact maximal runs. The fixed point 1 is retained. A unique extendible inverse branch can grow, so backward rigidity is neither a decreasing height nor a bound excluding infinite forward itineraries. The proof uses fixed even-run length 1 and establishes no claim for variable even-run lengths.
- L010 recomputes extendibility again after adding (1,2); it does not reuse the old endpoint union. Its incoming branches are finite histories with different penultimate states, not cycles. A sharp depth-two count does not establish unbounded counts at larger depths, and unrestricted histories need not stay above their starting values. The negative result stops the stated decoder extension, not Collatz or every use of arithmetic constraints.
- L011 and L012 compare every endpoint with that history's own start; L002 ensures that this also covers all intermediate shortcut states. The paired coordinates lie at different block indices. The interval test adds the required extra inverse on the companion side and retains upper bounds as well as lower bounds. Forced contractions do not exclude earlier compensation. The exact finite screens and the three-block proof do not yield an all-depth theorem or a universal descent bound.
- L013 retains arbitrary repetitions of residue 26 and proves their arithmetic exit; it does not delete that graph loop as a fixed-length transient. Its +1 terms, phase-entry heights and exit steps are included. The stopped rank is zero on residue 20 and is not a decreasing rank for target-to-target returns. Finite regressions support the implementation, while exact inequalities prove the all-start hitting bound.
- The cited return-normalization obstruction concerns an auxiliary certificate process. Such a loop is not a positive Collatz cycle, and separate convergence-preserving reductions supply no global progress measure by themselves.
- L014 keeps the least residue-20 root fixed, verifies each predecessor parity and positivity guard, and uses the actual finite shortcut path to transfer convergence. A smaller predecessor of a larger later return need not be smaller than the root. The residual valuation restriction is not a proof of convergence on that residual class.
- L015 imports only the cited high-valuation ancestor theorem with its positive integer, target-membership and strict-order guarantees. The same original root is used throughout; uncovered lower valuations and selector boundary failures are not promoted to universal coverage. The shortcut cycle {1,2} handles a visit to 1 before the stated ancestor-to-root time.
- L016 preserves each lower-row guard and the source's valuation domain. Its five t=4 cylinders are exhaustive only for that unit case, and the complementary table is a necessary condition, not a description of nonconvergent starts. The selected ancestor of 425 is 668>425 despite a valid forward identity, so below-threshold cases are not admitted by dropping the size condition. No claim that the sharper lower rows are covered by the external uniform Lean declaration is made.
- L017 checks that 128n-157 is positive and imports all integer, residue, strict-order and finite-orbit guards at the stated threshold 17. Convergence transfer includes a hit of 1 before the ancestor reaches n. A slope calculation alone is not used to lower the threshold, and the old-coordinate Lean declaration is not claimed to cover this complementary theorem. The surviving infinite class is not a counterexample family.
- L018 uses the source's all-parameter guarded identities at their stated times, retaining strict order against the original root and convergence transfer through the shortcut cycle. Finite replay checks only transcription. Excluding a family from the hypothetical least root does not independently prove convergence of every member, and the remaining infinite class is still present.
- A finite base exception cannot repair the cited bounded-ancestor obstruction. The reviewed variable-duration forward theorem retains actual parity and final-halving guards; ending a growing spell alone does not repay its original-root overshoot.
- Methods controlling later blocks and richer arithmetic information remain open. No global research stop or resolution is claimed.
