# L002 — Characteristic order and the specialization defect

## Hypotheses

Let p be a prime, Lambda = Z_p[[T]], and X a finitely generated torsion
Lambda-module. Let f_X be a generator of its characteristic ideal, and put
h = ord_T f_X. Use the usual characteristic-divisor convention: the
multiplicity of a height-one prime is the length of X localized there.
Set

\[
A=\Lambda_{(T)},\qquad M=X\otimes_\Lambda A,\qquad
d=\dim_{\mathbf Q_p}\bigl((X/TX)\otimes_{\mathbf Z_p}\mathbf Q_p\bigr).
\]

For the arithmetic application, let E/Q and an odd good-ordinary prime p
satisfy the conventions and named theorems in
[the cyclotomic foundations](../foundations/03-cyclotomic-control.md), and
take X to be the dual of the classical p-primary Selmer group over the
cyclotomic Z_p-extension. Write

\[
r=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbf Q),\qquad
s=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbf Z_p}\Sha(E/\mathbf Q)[p^\infty].
\]

## Conclusion

There are positive integers e_1,...,e_k, allowing k = 0 and empty sums, with

\[
M\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^k A/(T^{e_i}),\qquad
h=\sum_{i=1}^k e_i,\qquad d=k.
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, with delta = length_A(TM),

\[
h=d+\delta,\qquad \delta=\sum_i(e_i-1)\ge0.
\tag{2}
\]

Equality h = d holds exactly when TM = 0. Equivalently, the natural map

\[
\eta:M[T]\longrightarrow M/TM,\qquad x\longmapsto x\bmod TM
\tag{3}
\]

is an isomorphism, where M[T] = {x in M : Tx = 0}. This is semisimplicity
at the augmentation prime; it makes no assertion at other primes of Lambda.

The characteristic ideal alone does not determine d: the modules

\[
X_a=(\Lambda/(T^2))^2,\qquad X_b=(\Lambda/(T))^4
\tag{4}
\]

both have characteristic ideal (T^4), while their specialized dimensions
are respectively 2 and 4. Both dimensions are even, and both modules are
free of rank four over Z_p, so neither has a nonzero finite submodule.

For the arithmetic X, good-ordinary control and Kummer give the exact defect

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}_T f_X=r+s+\delta.}
\tag{5}
\]

Neither s = 0 nor delta = 0 is assumed. The examples (4) are abstract
modules, not claimed elliptic-curve Selmer duals or BSD counterexamples.

## Proof

**Localization and length.** Every nonzero power series g in Lambda has a
unique factorization g = T^v u with v >= 0 and u(0) nonzero. In A the
element u is invertible because u is outside the prime ideal (T).
Consequently every nonzero element of A is a unit times a power of T.
Every nonzero ideal of A is generated by an element with the least such
exponent in that ideal, so A is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer
T. Its residue field is Q_p: reduction modulo T sends all inverted power
series to the nonzero elements of Z_p, which have all been inverted.

Since X is finitely generated and torsion over the domain Lambda, a product
of nonzero annihilators of a finite generating set annihilates X. After
localization this product is a unit times T^b for some b >= 0. Thus M is
finitely generated and killed by T^b, and has finite length over A. The
structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal
domain now gives the direct sum in (1). If b = 0, M is zero and k = 0.

For each summand, the filtration by powers of T has e_i successive
quotients A/(T). Its length is therefore e_i. The definition of the
characteristic multiplicity gives

\[
h=\operatorname{length}_A M=\sum_i e_i.
\]

This uses order at the prime (T), not the total degree of the distinguished
polynomial in the characteristic series.

**Specialization.** Localization commutes with passage to a quotient, so

\[
M/TM\simeq (X/TX)\otimes_\Lambda A
\simeq (X/TX)\otimes_{\mathbf Z_p}\mathbf Q_p.
\]

For the second identification, T acts as zero on X/TX, and every inverted
g outside (T) acts by the nonzero scalar g(0). Localizing this Z_p-module
is exactly tensoring with Q_p. Every summand A/(T^{e_i}) contributes one
copy of Q_p to M/TM. This proves d = k. Multiplication by T has image of
length e_i - 1 on that summand, proving (2).

For e_i = 1, the restriction of eta to the summand is the identity of
A/(T). For e_i > 1 its T-annihilator is
T^(e_i-1)A/T^e_i A, a one-dimensional Q_p-space contained in T M; its map
to the quotient is zero. Hence eta is an isomorphism if and only if all
e_i = 1, equivalently delta = 0 or TM = 0. The same conclusion holds for
the zero module.

**The explicit ambiguity.** The characteristic ideal of Lambda/(T^e) is
(T^e); characteristic multiplicities add in a direct sum. This gives
(T^4) for both modules in (4). Their quotients by T are respectively
Z_p^2 and Z_p^4. Their underlying Z_p-modules both have rank four, using
the basis 1,T in each Lambda/(T^2) summand. A free Z_p-module has no
nonzero finite subgroup, proving the additional claims. For X_a, delta = 2
and eta is zero between two-dimensional spaces; for X_b, delta = 0 and
eta is the identity on a four-dimensional space.

These examples also admit an abstract control map with no errors at the
base level: let S_infinity = X^vee and S_0 = (X/TX)^vee. Pontryagin
duality identifies S_0 with S_infinity^Gamma, so the control map can be
taken to be this isomorphism for either model. Consequently, finiteness of
control errors by itself supplies no missing module condition. This does
not impose, or purport to reproduce, all arithmetic local conditions.

**The arithmetic specialization.** For the Selmer dual X, the named
cotorsion theorem and finite generation in the foundations ensure the
algebraic hypotheses above. Mazur's theorem gives a map

\[
S_0\longrightarrow S_\infty^\Gamma
\]

with finite kernel and cokernel. Its Pontryagin dual is a map
X/TX -> S_0^vee, again with finite kernel and cokernel. Here TX is closed:
it is the image of the compact module X under continuous multiplication
by T. Thus the dual of invariants is the stated coinvariant quotient.
Tensoring the dual map with Q_p kills both finite errors and gives an
isomorphism. It follows that

\[
d=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbf Z_p} S_0.
\]

Take the filtered direct limit of the finite Kummer exact sequences from
the standard inputs. On E(Q)/p^a E(Q), the upward map to level a+1 is
multiplication by p; on Sha[p^a] it is inclusion. Filtered direct limits
are exact. The limit of the first terms is E(Q) tensor (Q_p/Z_p), and the
limit of the third terms is Sha[p^infinity]. This gives the exact sequence
in the cyclotomic foundations without assuming finite Sha.

Finite generation of E(Q) shows that its tensor product here is
(Q_p/Z_p)^r: its finite torsion subgroup contributes zero. Taking
Pontryagin duals and then tensoring with Q_p shows that the coranks add.
The groups have finite corank because S_0^vee is finitely generated over
Z_p; the dual of its Sha quotient is a submodule. We obtain d = r + s.
Substitution in (2) proves (5).

**Comparison with the required threshold.** The achieved unconditional
comparison in this setting is r <= d <= h, together with the exact two
nonnegative defects. Even if an independent analytic argument supplied
h = m(E), obtaining r = m(E) by this comparison would require both s = 0
and delta = 0. Finite Sha[p^infinity] would remove s; the identity would
still leave delta to control. Semisimplicity would remove delta; the
identity would still leave s to control. Neither calculation supplies the
separate complex/p-adic analytic comparison.

For h = 4, the characteristic data alone leave dimensions 2 and 4 possible
even after even parity is imposed, as (4) shows. This is a new explicit
test in this notebook of the standard distinction recorded by Greenberg's
Conjecture 1.12; it is not a new arithmetic counterexample. The inference
from a characteristic ideal and control alone to exact rank is therefore
stopped, while the upper bound remains usable with additional arithmetic
information.
