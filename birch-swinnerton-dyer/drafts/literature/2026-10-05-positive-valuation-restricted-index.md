## Primary statements and limits

**Sakamoto 2021**, *On the theory of Kolyvagin systems of rank 0*,
JTNB 33, 1077--1102, published 2022-01-26:
[Definition 5.7, Theorem 5.8 and Remark 5.9, pp. 1093--1094](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.1189.pdf#page=18).
Under its Galois hypotheses, Cartesian structure and core rank zero,
the scalar ideal at each index equals the initial Fitting ideal of the
modified dual Selmer group for a basis. The higher ideal generated by
indices with at most i factors is only contained in the i-th Fitting
ideal. Remark 5.9 asks when equality holds; it is not an equality
theorem. The permitted prime set contains a sufficiently deep
Frobenius-defined set. Thus this reference supplies no direct
higher-ideal attainment import.

**Kim**, *The structure of Selmer groups and the Iwasawa main conjecture
for elliptic curves*, publisher-hosted author text dated 2025-05-12:
[Section 1.2.2, p. 3](https://preprint.press.jhu.edu/ajm/sites/default/files/AJM-kim.pdf#page=4),
[Section 1.5.1 and Theorem 1.8, pp. 5--6](https://preprint.press.jhu.edu/ajm/sites/default/files/AJM-kim.pdf#page=6),
[Sections 5.3.2 and 5.4.1, pp. 27--29](https://preprint.press.jhu.edu/ajm/sites/default/files/AJM-kim.pdf#page=28).
Its prime set imposes congruences on ell and a_ell, rather than cyclic
E(F_ell)[p^k]. Its valuation minimum ranges over N_1. The theorem
assumes p >= 5, surjective residual representation, prime-to-p Manin
constant and nonzero integral collection. L021 already specializes its
corank and finite-length conclusions under residual primitivity.
The proof uses indices of sufficiently large coefficient length;
it does not state the requested conclusion for every prescribed m > h.
Keep the finite-Sha hypotheses of the separate rational-rank clauses.
Coefficient identification at all depths is cited in Section 5.3.1,
p. 27; the local integral/residual assessment already covers it.

**Angurel**, *Kolyvagin systems of rank 0 and the structure of the Selmer
group of elliptic curves over abelian extensions*,
[arXiv:2504.20759v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20759v2), 2026-06-08.
The earlier rank-zero assessment inspected its structural results but
did not cover this fixed-depth first-degree application. Read Definition
2.4, H1--H8 in Section 2.2, Corollary 2.30, Lemma 2.31, Theorem 3.27
and its first-degree proof case in Section 3.5. Theorem 3.27 admits a
DVR or a principal local artinian ring; case (i) gives equality at the
module's free rank. Its proof works at the coefficient length, selects
primes giving surjective primal and dual localizations, and uses Lemma
2.31 to compare transverse and strict Selmer groups. This is stronger
coverage than the 2021 inclusion. The first-degree case needs no
non-self-dual assumption or general all-degree equality.

The hypotheses to check in the specialization are: absolute residual
irreducibility (H1); two residual Galois-cohomology vanishings (H2);
a cyclotomic-trivial tau with rank-one coinvariants (H3); p >= 5 or
the alternative Hom vanishing (H4); a prime q with rank-one local
quotient and zero H^2 (H5); Cartesian conditions (H6); core rank zero
(H7); and a prime set containing a sufficiently deep eligible set
with the specified disjoint splitting extension (H8).
Use the q=p option, supported by Sakamoto Corollary 2.14 and the
retained local-torsion hypothesis, rather than silently granting
H^2=0 at an auxiliary Kolyvagin prime. The existing Mazur--Rubin
coverage and Kim Section 2.4.1 cover the residual Galois hypotheses.
Remark 4.8 explicitly gives congruence and cyclic p^k torsion as the
elliptic eligibility conditions. Check them at k=m in the later proof.

The first-degree proof case is the source coverage to specialize,
not a completed application to Sakamoto's family. Angurel's Section 3.4
uses a primitive rank-one system relaxed at q; the later argument may
instead use its prime-selection lemmas and Sakamoto's own scalar
identity. This avoids an unnecessary identification of entire systems.
Definitions 2.18/2.19 and the proof must be interpreted through the
actual local conditions: the displayed subscript/superscript convention
in Definition 2.18 is inconsistent with its following example. Check
strict versus relaxed versus transverse conditions explicitly; do not
propagate that typographical mismatch. Section 4.6 equation (41)
compares its p-singular scalar valuation with its modular-symbol
valuation, but is not an equality with our delta_m by definition.
