## Primary coverage and limitations

M. Polyak, *Feynman diagrams for pedestrians and mathematicians*,
[math/0406251v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0406251v1), 12 June 2004,
section 3.1, printed pp. 11–12, assumes a free, proper, isometric action
and invariant measure. Remarks 3.1–3.2 describe the section/orbit
Jacobian and constraint derivative. The change of variables uses a
unique zero on each orbit. This covers the mechanism, conditional on
a valid section and correctly normalized measure. Its formal delta
notation does not settle positive density or multiplicity for this box.

N. Reshetikhin, *Lectures on quantization of gauge systems*,
[1008.1411v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1411v1), arXiv version dated
8 August 2010, section 6.1, printed pp. 28–30, assumes free action,
smooth quotient, compact ambient manifold, invariant volume, analytic
phase and finitely many critical orbits. Theorem 3 assumes a section
meeting each orbit once and invertible orbit-constraint matrix. Its
proof (33)–(34) explicitly uses unique intersection to evaluate the
orbit integral. Section 6.2, pp. 31–32, restricts the expansion to
transverse neighborhoods of critical orbits. Section 6.3 starts with
the semiclassical term. These statements do not import a global
section for the lattice, justify a signed determinant as positive Haar
density, or provide the needed Laplace remainder. The audit must use
L280's actual local change of variables; no all-order diagram exercise
is a premise. The arXiv version identifier is controlling; the PDF
also displays an October 2018 internal date.

W. D. Kirwin, *Higher Asymptotics of Laplace's Approximation*,
*Asymptotic Analysis* 70 (2010), 231–248,
[0810.1700v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.1700v2), 5 June 2010,
Theorem 2.1, printed pp. 4–5, requires measurable phase and amplitude,
a unique zero minimum at an interior point, separation from zero away
from that point, positive leading radial phase, finite radial expansions
and convergence of a weighted integral. Equation (2.3) gives a little-o
remainder. Lemma 2.3, p. 7, refers the signed-amplitude reduction to
Fulks–Sather pp. 188–189; that source was not independently inspected.
Section 3.2, pp. 14–15, treats positive Hessians by a linear change of
coordinates. Reuse the prior L279 audit coverage of smooth compactly
supported amplitudes. No arbitrary nonsmooth signed-amplitude theorem
or source-differentiable remainder is needed. Fixed-source asymptotics
are covered; mesh-uniform and differentiated remainders are not.

The analytic inverse-function and ordinary change-of-variables theorems
remain standard named inputs. Reproving general gauge-fixing or Laplace
theory is unnecessary. Checking the particular finite-dimensional
application and its algebra is necessary because no inspected source
