# L048 — Two cubic action vertices in the exact forest Gaussian

## Hypotheses

Fix an admitted SU(2) mesh, all-face pinned links, bare coupling,
generator and both Wilson-flow probes of L010–L011, with tau = 1/16.
Take the small-coupling coefficient at fixed mesh first. Select exactly

\[
G^{33}_{i,F}=\tfrac12\kappa_F(f_{i,0},r_0,S_3,S_3),\qquad i=3,6.
\tag{1}
\]

Use L018's ordinary dimensionless forest coordinates x=theta/g,
one-color covariance Sigma_F=((D I_F)^T D I_F)^(-1), and symmetric
matrices B_(i,F), A_F. In particular,
f_(i,0)=sum_c (x^c)^T B_(i,F) x^c and
r0=sum_c (x^c)^T A_F x^c, up to irrelevant constants. There are
three independent colors with identical covariance. B_(i,F) includes
the actual full-link linear flow started on the slice, rather than
flow generated by the restricted forest Hessian. A_F is a residual
matrix and need not be positive.

For each plaquette p, let J_p be the one-color four-row signed-edge
selection map in traversal order. Boundary and forest rows are zero;
backward traversals carry a minus sign. Write y_p^c=J_p x^c.
The ordered product is exp(g y_(p,1)) through exp(g y_(p,4)).
All plaquettes present in the Wilson action are included, including
physical-boundary plaquettes and zero-row cases.

For r,s,t in {1,2,3,4}, define omega_(rst) to be zero on repeated
indices, +1 on increasing triples, and alternating under permutations.
Define one-color four-by-four blocks, for every ordered pair p,q,

\[
\begin{split}
C_{pq}&=J_p\Sigma_F J_q^T,\\
U_{i,pq}&=J_p\Sigma_F B_{i,F}\Sigma_F J_q^T,\\
V_{pq}&=J_p\Sigma_F A_F\Sigma_F J_q^T,\\
W_{i,pq}&=J_p\Sigma_F B_{i,F}\Sigma_F A_F\Sigma_F J_q^T.
\end{split}\tag{2}
\]

No inverse of a local block is used, and C_(pq) may be singular and
nonsymmetric for p unequal to q. Sigma_F retains the full forest path
covariance; no curvature-only transfer of the nonlinear action is made.

## Conclusion

Both actual probes have the exact finite contraction

\[
\boxed{
G^{33}_{i,F}=3\sum_{p,q}\sum_{r,s,t,u,v,w=1}^4
\omega_{rst}\omega_{uvw}
\left[
(U_{i,pq})_{ru}(V_{pq})_{sv}(C_{pq})_{tw}
+(W_{i,pq})_{ru}(C_{pq})_{sv}(C_{pq})_{tw}
\right].
}\tag{3}
\]

The sum is over ordered pairs, including p=q and distinct plaquettes.
The first term has one direct covariance between cubic vertices and
two paths through separate quadratic vertices. The second has two
direct covariances and one path through both quadratic vertices.
Neither direct covariance carries a positive-flow-time heat factor.
Every internal pair within a single cubic vertex vanishes by color;
the connected class attaching both legs of a quadratic vertex to one
cubic vertex also vanishes by color. These facts do not remove
cross-plaquette covariances or the second term of (3).

This contracts one complete fixed-mesh summand of L011. It supplies
no box evaluation, sign, cutoff power or logarithmic coefficient,
bounded remainder, finite matching, or interacting reflected-error
bound <= c_box/2. Its separate forest invariance is not asserted:
L019's action/measure and observable-jet compensation remains required.
There is no continuum construction or mass-gap conclusion.

## Proof

### 1. Actual cubic vertex and its color tensor

L025's ordered logarithm recurrence through degree two gives
w1=sum_r y_r and w2=(1/2) sum_(s<t) [y_s,y_t]. In the basis
t_c=i sigma_c/2, [u,v]=-u cross v. The single-plaquette action
4P=|log U_p|^2/2-|log U_p|^4/96+O(|log U_p|^6) therefore has
cubic coefficient

\[
q_3(y)=\langle w_1,w_2\rangle
=-\tfrac12\sum_{r<s<t}y_r\mathbin{\cdot}(y_s\mathbin{\times}y_t).
\tag{4}
\]

To verify the last equality, terms in which the index from w1
repeats an index from w2 vanish. For each increasing triple r<s<t,
the three remaining contributions are respectively +1,-1,+1 times
the same scalar triple product inside the sum before its -1/2 factor.
There is no cubic contribution from the fourth power. Consequently
S3=sum_p q3(y_p), with the sign in (4) and no additional mesh factor.
All dimensionful probe normalizations stay in B_(i,F), A_F.

With epsilon_(123)=1, the unique symmetric tensor in combined
edge/color indices is

\[
T_{(r,c)(s,d)(t,e)}=-\tfrac1{12}\omega_{rst}\epsilon_{cde},
\qquad q_3(y)=\sum T_{(r,c)(s,d)(t,e)}y_r^c y_s^d y_t^e.
\tag{5}
\]

Both alternating tensors change sign under a slot exchange, so their
product is symmetric. Each increasing edge triple occurs six times
in the ordered sum, giving -6/12=-1/2 as required. The local covariance
is (C_(pp))_(rs) delta_(cd). Contracting any two slots of T against
it gives zero since epsilon vanishes when two colors agree. Thus q3
is purely third Gaussian chaos for this law, even with zero rows or
overlapping selected edges. This is a color argument in the exact
forest law, not a statement that general cubic vertices have no
tadpoles or that S3 is a function of linear curvature.

### 2. Complete connected pairing classification

Import the standard **Isserlis Gaussian pairing theorem**: every even
centered joint Gaussian moment is the sum of products of covariances
over pairings, with no pairings for odd moments. This theorem applies
to the joint vector (x,y_p,y_q), including its degenerate local blocks.
The connected-pairing rule for polynomial cumulants follows here from
L011's finite partition definition: a pairing with more than one
connected component has coefficient zero after the cumulant partition
sum, whereas a connected pairing has coefficient one. Indeed its
compatible partitions are exactly the coarsenings of its component
partition; the partition-lattice cumulant coefficients sum to zero
unless there is one component. This is a finite combinatorial use of
the theorem; no exponential of a cubic polynomial is integrated.

For fixed p,q label the two quadratic vertices F,R and the two
cubic occurrences P,Q, with degrees (2,2,3,3). Occurrences remain
distinct also when p=q. An internal quadratic pair disconnects its
vertex and is removed by the cumulant. An internal cubic pair has
zero color contraction by (5), so these pairings contribute zero.
We may therefore enumerate graphs with no internal pairs.

Let h be the number of F--R edges. The case h=2 disconnects F,R
from P,Q. For h=0 or 1 the number k of P--Q edges is determined by
the cubic degree sum: 6=4-2h+2k, so k=1+h.

When h=0, there are three possibilities:

- F attaches twice to P and R twice to Q: 3 choices for the P--Q
  endpoint on each cubic and 2! choices at each quadratic, giving
  3*3*2!*2!=36 pairings.
- F attaches twice to Q and R twice to P: another 36 pairings.
- Each quadratic attaches once to each cubic: choose the P--Q
  endpoint on each cubic, allocate its other two legs to F,R, and
  allocate each quadratic's two legs to P,Q. This gives
  3*3*2*2*2*2=144 pairings.

Each of the first two possibilities identifies two colors at one
cubic through a color-diagonal quadratic matrix. Its epsilon factor
then vanishes, independently of the residual or probe signs. The
third possibility survives.

When h=1, choose its two quadratic endpoints in 2*2=4 ways.
Choose the external endpoint on each cubic in 3*3 ways and match
their two remaining legs in 2! ways. Attaching the remaining F leg
to P and R leg to Q gives 4*3*3*2!=72 pairings. The reversed
attachment gives another 72. These exhaust the graphs because the
degree equations leave no other allocations. Thus 288 nonzero-eligible
pairings remain, out of 360 connected pairings with no cubic self-pair.
No assumption of independence between plaquettes has entered.

### 3. Contraction, color factor, and path orientations

Use r,s,t for the P legs and u,v,w for Q. In the split class choose
the F path between r,u, the R path between s,v, and the direct
covariance between t,w. Symmetry of T and of the quadratic matrices
makes all 144 labeled pairings equal after summation. The matrix
products are precisely U_(i,pq), V_(pq), C_(pq) of (2).
Each path identifies the corresponding endpoint colors. The remaining
color sum is sum_(c,d,e) epsilon_(cde)^2=6. The two tensor factors
in (5) give 1/144, and (1) supplies 1/2. The total coefficient is
144*6/(144*2)=3, proving the first term in (3) for each pair p,q.

For the 72 pairings attaching F to P and R to Q, the length-three
path gives W_(i,pq). The other two paths are direct C_(pq) factors.
The same color sum and tensor normalization give coefficient
72*6/(144*2)=3/2. For the other orientation the block is

\[
\widetilde W_{i,pq}
=J_p\Sigma_F A_F\Sigma_F B_{i,F}\Sigma_F J_q^T
=W_{i,qp}^T.
\tag{6}
\]

For an individual p,q pair the path contribution is therefore the
omega-contraction of (3/2)(W_(i,pq)+tilde W_(i,pq)) with two
C_(pq) factors. Only in the complete ordered sum may it be written
with coefficient 3 and W alone: exchange p,q and simultaneously
exchange the triples (r,s,t),(u,v,w). Since C_(qp)=C_(pq)^T,
the two summed orientation contributions are identical, including
p=q. This justifies the second term in (3) without silently dropping
an orientation. Cumulant multilinearity over the two occurrences of
S3 completes the proof. The sign is positive because (1) contains
two action cubics and the outside factor +1/2.
