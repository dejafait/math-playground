# L014 — Polynomial auxiliary extension and free BRST Ward identity

## Hypotheses

Fix an even N >= 2, a = 8/N, and L013's relative cubical cochain
spaces in [-4,4]^4. For one colour write

\[
D:C^0\longrightarrow C^1,\qquad E:C^1\longrightarrow C^2,
\quad \delta=D^*,\quad L_0=\delta D,
\quad L_1=E^*E+D\delta.
\tag{1}
\]

These spaces use inner product a^4 times the cell sum, and all integration
coordinates are orthonormal for it. L013's cochain hypotheses give ED = 0
and positive definite L0 and L1. Put m = dim C0 and n = dim C1.
Take three colour copies of A in C1, real b in C0, and independent odd
c, bar c with coefficient space C0. Extend pairings complex bilinearly,
without complex conjugation. Use the unchanged Berezin orientation

\[
\mathcal B_r\left[\prod_{j=1}^m(-\bar c^r_jc^r_j)\right]=1,
\qquad \mathcal B_r[e^{-(\bar c^r,L_0c^r)}]=\det L_0.
\tag{2}
\]

Let P be any complex-coefficient polynomial in the even coordinates A,b
and the finite exterior algebra generated by all c,bar c. For epsilon >= 0
define the following **ordered**, initially formal integral:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I_\epsilon(P)=\int\prod_{r=1}^3dA^r\,
&e^{-\frac12\sum_r(\|EA^r\|^2+\epsilon\|A^r\|^2)}\\
&\times\int\prod_{r=1}^3d\gamma(b^r)\,
e^{i\sum_r(b^r,\delta A^r)}
\mathcal B\left[P e^{-\sum_r(\bar c^r,L_0c^r)}\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

where d gamma(b) = (2 pi)^(-m/2) exp(-||b||^2/2) db and
B is the product of the three orientations in (2). Perform the finite
Berezin operation and the b integral before integrating A. For epsilon > 0
this agrees with coefficientwise joint bosonic integration. No such
agreement is assumed at epsilon = 0.

The supporting known inputs are Caracciolo–Sokal–Sportiello,
*Algebraic/combinatorial proofs of Cayley-type identities*,
[1105.6270v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.6270v2), **Theorem A.3**
(finite real Gaussian integration with complex sources), Appendix A.4,
(A.48)–(A.64) (left odd differentiation and Berezin integration), and
**Theorem A.16** (finite fermionic Gaussian source and moment formulas).
For polynomial Fourier insertions use Melrose,
[*Preliminaries: Distributions, the Fourier transform and operators*](https://math.mit.edu/~rbm/18.199-S08/Chapter1.pdf),
(1.59)–(1.66), **Theorem 1.3**, together with the Gaussian transform in
[NIST DLMF Table 1.14.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.14.T1).
These are supporting inputs; the combined prescription and removal
statement below is the applicability specialization approved in
drafts/literature/2026-10-03-auxiliary-brst-ward-identity.md.

## Conclusion

Equation (3) is well defined for every such P, including epsilon = 0.
Writing H_epsilon = L1 + epsilon I, its normalization is

\[
Z_\epsilon:=\mathcal I_\epsilon(1)
=\left[\det L_0\,(2\pi)^{n/2}
 (\det H_\epsilon)^{-1/2}\right]^3>0.
\tag{4}
\]

Define <P>_epsilon = I_epsilon(P)/Z_epsilon. The epsilon = 0 connection
restriction is precisely L013's law, and at fixed N,

\[
\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\langle P\rangle_\epsilon
=\langle P\rangle_0.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the complex odd **left** derivation, separately in each colour,

\[
sA=Dc,\qquad sc=sb=0,\qquad s\bar c=i b,
\qquad s(PQ)=(sP)Q+(-1)^{|P|}P(sQ).
\tag{6}
\]

It is nilpotent. For parity-homogeneous P put
T = sum_r (delta A^r,c^r). The exact regulated identity and its fixed-mesh
removal are

\[
\boxed{\langle sP\rangle_\epsilon
=(-1)^{|P|}\epsilon\langle PT\rangle_\epsilon
\quad(\epsilon>0),\qquad
\langle sP\rangle_0=0.}
\tag{7}
\]

For each fixed P,N the breaking term is O_(P,N)(epsilon).
General polynomials are treated by their even and odd parts.
This is a reproduction of known finite Gaussian/Berezin Ward machinery
in the specified real Fourier prescription, not a claim beyond the
checked literature.

These identities do not give a positive joint auxiliary/ghost probability
law, absolute joint integration at epsilon = 0, smooth continuum boundary
traces, a nonlinear SU(2) gauge-fixing representation, or a bound uniform
as a tends to zero. They supply no interacting reflected-error estimate.

## Proof

### Polynomial extension and removal

Finite Berezin integration first leaves a polynomial in A,b. At fixed A,
every monomial in b has an absolutely convergent Gaussian Fourier
integral. For a multi-index alpha and x = delta A the cited Gaussian
transform and differentiation formula give

\[
\int b^\alpha e^{i(b,x)}d\gamma(b)
=i^{-|\alpha|}\partial_x^\alpha e^{-\|x\|^2/2}
=p_\alpha(x)e^{-\|x\|^2/2},
\tag{8}
\]

where p_alpha is a polynomial. Differentiation under this particular
b integral is justified by its finite Gaussian moments, or by the cited
Schwartz Fourier theorem. No A,b interchange is needed. Apply (8) to
the finitely many coefficient monomials in the three-colour expression.
There is a polynomial Q_P(A), independent of epsilon, such that

\[
\mathcal I_\epsilon(P)=
\int Q_P(A)e^{-\frac12\sum_r(A^r,H_\epsilon A^r)}
\prod_r dA^r.
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed, ||EA||^2 + ||delta A||^2 = (A,L1 A). For P = 1,
Q_1 = (det L0)^3. Positive definiteness of L1 proves absolute
convergence of the **transformed** A integral at every epsilon >= 0.
The cited real Gaussian normalization yields (4), including the same
orthonormal-coordinate factors used by L013. For P depending on A only,
(9) at epsilon = 0 is L013's integral, so this is an extension rather
than a changed connection prescription.

At this fixed N, let lambda = lambda_min(L1) > 0. For 0 <= epsilon <= 1,
the integrand in (9) has modulus bounded by

\[
C_P(1+\|A\|)^{d_P}e^{-\lambda\|A\|^2/2},
\tag{10}
\]

an integrable function on the fixed finite-dimensional space.
Dominated convergence proves continuity of every I_epsilon(P) at zero.
The positive normalization (4) is continuous and bounded away from zero
on [0,1]. This proves (5) and boundedness of each normalized polynomial
expectation on that interval. The same proof applies to PT, so
epsilon <PT>_epsilon tends to zero and is O_(P,N)(epsilon).
This supplies the needed bound on the insertion, not just its explicit
epsilon factor. Neither lambda nor these polynomial bounds is asserted
to control mesh refinement.

### Signs, nilpotence and regulated integration by parts

Write the full exponent as -S_epsilon, where for one colour

\[
S_\epsilon=\tfrac12\|EA\|^2+\tfrac12\|b\|^2
-i(b,\delta A)+(\bar c,L_0c)+\tfrac\epsilon2\|A\|^2,
\tag{11}
\]

and sum over colours. The square of an odd derivation is an even
derivation. Equation (6) makes it zero on each polynomial generator,
and hence s^2 = 0 on the whole polynomial algebra. Its action on the
unregulated terms in (11) is

\[
s\tfrac12\|EA\|^2=(EA,EDc)=0,\quad
s\tfrac12\|b\|^2=0,\quad
s[-i(b,\delta A)]=-i(b,L_0c),\quad
s(\bar c,L_0c)=i(b,L_0c).
\tag{12}
\]

Thus sS_0 = 0 and sS_epsilon = epsilon T. In particular the imaginary
factor in s bar c is fixed by L013's positive real b Gaussian and
positive Fourier phase; it is not a free sign convention after (3).

For epsilon > 0, the modulus of each Grassmann coefficient in the
joint integrand is bounded by a polynomial times

\[
\exp\left[-\tfrac12\sum_r
 (\|EA^r\|^2+\epsilon\|A^r\|^2+\|b^r\|^2)\right].
\tag{13}
\]

This is integrable in A,b, including every coefficient produced by the
derivatives below. It justifies bosonic Fubini and A integration by
parts for this regulated expression. There are no b derivatives and
no changes of its real contour.

In orthonormal coordinates the odd vector field is

\[
s=\sum_{r,e}(Dc^r)_e\partial_{A^r_e}
   +\sum_{r,j}i b^r_j\partial^L_{\bar c^r_j}.
\tag{14}
\]

Each coefficient is independent of the variable it differentiates.
The integral of the first terms of s(Pe^-S_epsilon) is zero by ordinary
A integration by parts, with the polynomial Gaussian decay (13).
The integral of every second term is zero because Berezin integration
of a left odd derivative is zero: that derivative removes its generator
and cannot have the complete top monomial. This is the finite algebraic
rule in the cited Appendix A.4; multiplying by b does not reintroduce
the removed generator. Thus the integral of s(Pe^-S_epsilon) is zero.

The even action satisfies s(e^-S_epsilon) =
-epsilon T e^-S_epsilon. The left graded product rule therefore gives

\[
0=\mathcal I_\epsilon(sP)
 -(-1)^{|P|}\epsilon\mathcal I_\epsilon(PT).
\tag{15}
\]

Divide by (4) to obtain the regulated part of (7). Apply (5) to sP
and the boundedness following (10) to PT. The epsilon term disappears,
which proves the unregulated ordered identity. No integration by parts
in the non-absolutely-convergent epsilon = 0 joint expression is used.

### Mixed moments and discriminating insertion tests

The phase, ghost order and removal argument can also be checked using
explicit mixed moments. For one colour the normalized bosonic source
function, with bilinear complex sources J in C1 and K in C0, is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathcal I_\epsilon(e^{(J,A)+(K,b)})}{Z_\epsilon}
=\exp\big[&\tfrac12(J,H_\epsilon^{-1}J)
 +i(K,\delta H_\epsilon^{-1}J)\\
 &+\tfrac12(K,[I-\delta H_\epsilon^{-1}D]K)\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Here the notation refers to the one-colour factor, with the other
factors equal to one. This source integral is well defined in the same
order: integrate b to get exp((K,K)/2 + i(K,delta A) -
||delta A||^2/2), then apply the cited complex-source Gaussian formula
with source J + i D K. Source derivatives give the polynomial moments;
the same Gaussian domination justifies those derivatives. Formula (16)
holds also at epsilon = 0 in the ordered sense.

Put M_epsilon = L0 + epsilon I. From ED = 0,
H_epsilon D = D M_epsilon. Thus

\[
\langle AA^*\rangle_\epsilon=H_\epsilon^{-1},\qquad
\langle A b^*\rangle_\epsilon=iD M_\epsilon^{-1},\qquad
\langle b b^*\rangle_\epsilon=\epsilon M_\epsilon^{-1}.
\tag{17}
\]

Stars in (17) denote matrix transpose of the coordinate column, not
Hermitian conjugation. For the unchanged fermionic factor, differentiation
of its determinant, or the cited Theorem A.16 with orientation (2), gives

\[
\langle\bar c_i c_j\rangle_\epsilon=-(L_0^{-1})_{ji},
\qquad \langle c_j\bar c_i\rangle_\epsilon=(L_0^{-1})_{ji}.
\tag{18}
\]

For example, differentiating det L with respect to L_ij integrates
-bar c_i c_j against exp(-bar c L c), so (18) fixes the order without
assuming a convention from a different contour. This ghost factor is
independent of A,b and epsilon.

For real u,w in C0 and v in C1 consider the two odd polynomials
P_1 = (bar c,u)(A,v) and P_2 = (bar c,u)(b,w). Equations (6), (17)
and (18) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle sP_1\rangle_\epsilon
 &=(u,[L_0^{-1}-M_\epsilon^{-1}]\delta v)
 =\epsilon(u,L_0^{-1}M_\epsilon^{-1}\delta v),\\
\langle sP_2\rangle_\epsilon
 &=i\epsilon(u,M_\epsilon^{-1}w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Computing -epsilon <P_k T>_epsilon with the same moments gives
exactly the corresponding right sides of (19). These generically
nonzero regulated values detect omission of the breaking insertion
and a wrong ghost-order or parity sign. Both vanish on removal.
Although <bb*>_0 = 0, <Ab*>_0 = iD L0^-1 is nonzero; auxiliary
integration must not be replaced by imposing b = 0.

An exact rational check uses the complete N=2 relative complex
(one interior vertex, eight links and twenty-four faces). It checks (7)
for every bosonic monomial of degree at most four in the eight A
coordinates and beta = -i b, multiplied by each of 1,c,bar c,c bar c,
at epsilon = 0, 1/3 and 2: 8,580 identities. Beta is an algebraic
coordinate for rational contractions, not a contour change. Both mixed
tests have nonzero breaking at positive epsilon and zero at epsilon = 0.
Reproduce with
`PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 scripts/auxiliary-cochains/check_ward.py`.
This finite test checks the signs and retained relative links; the
all-polynomial, all-fixed-mesh proof is (8)–(15).

### Scope of the Ward identity

Equation (3) adds no boundary degrees of freedom or strong boundary
condition. In particular it does not transfer a zero boundary value
of an auxiliary or ghost variable to a normal derivative of A. It is
an algebraic identity on the same finite relative spaces. Consequently
it does not remove L012's obstruction on unrestricted smooth continuum
jets. Nor does it identify this complex polynomial functional with
L011's nonlinear forest-gauge measure.

At epsilon = 0 the original joint bosonic modulus still has no decay
along im D, exactly as in L013. The proof consistently uses positive
epsilon for joint integration by parts and auxiliary-first integration
for its removal. It proves no exchange with mesh refinement or infrared
removal, no physical-boundary counterterm or interacting remainder,
and no improvement of the required reflected error <= c_box/2.
