# L025 — Nonradial quartic action contracted in the exact forest Gaussian

## Hypotheses

Fix an admitted SU(2) mesh in the pinned box, the bare coupling
convention, generator, and both Wilson-flow probes of L010–L011,
with tau = 1/16. Take g to zero at fixed mesh first. Use L011's
forest slice, x = theta/g, and its normalized Gaussian exp(-S2) dx.
Select only

\[
G^{\mathrm{nr}}_{i,F}
=-\kappa_F(f_{i,0},r_0,S_4-S_{4,\mathrm{rad}}),\qquad i=3,6,
\quad S_{4,\mathrm{rad}}=-\frac1{96}\sum_p|(Dx)_p|^4.
\tag{1}
\]

The radial convention is that of L023. No nonlinear vertex is
transferred to a Hodge Gaussian. In ordinary forest link coordinates
use L018's one-color matrices

\[
\Sigma_F=\big((DI_F)^T DI_F\big)^{-1},\quad
B_{i,F},\quad A_F,
\qquad
f_{i,0}=\sum_c(x^c)^TB_{i,F}x^c+\mathrm{constant},\quad
r_0=\sum_c(x^c)^TA_Fx^c+\mathrm{constant}.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular B_(i,F) represents the full-link linear Wilson flow
started on the forest slice, not flow generated by the restricted
forest Hessian. A_F is a residual matrix, not a connection. Combine
the three colors into one vector X and put
S = diag(Sigma_F,Sigma_F,Sigma_F),
B_i = diag(B_(i,F),B_(i,F),B_(i,F)),
A = diag(A_F,A_F,A_F).
Thus X is Gaussian with covariance S, f_(i,0)=X^T B_i X up to a
constant, and r0=X^T A X up to a constant.

For each oriented plaquette, let y=J_p X consist of its four signed
edge Lie-algebra vectors in traversal order. Backward traversals
have the negative of the positive-link coordinate; forest and fixed
boundary entries are zero. The resulting ordered link product is
exp(g y1) exp(g y2) exp(g y3) exp(g y4). On these twelve local real
coordinates define w1,w2,w3 by the following simultaneous recurrence,
starting at zero and processing each edge Y:

\[
\begin{split}
w_1^{\rm new}&=w_1+Y,\\
w_2^{\rm new}&=w_2+\tfrac12[w_1,Y],\\
w_3^{\rm new}&=w_3+\tfrac12[w_2,Y]
 +\tfrac1{12}[w_1,[w_1,Y]]
 +\tfrac1{12}[Y,[Y,w_1]].
\end{split}\tag{3}
\]

Every right side uses the old values. Inner products are
<U,V> = -2 Tr(UV); with t_c=i sigma_c/2 the bracket is minus the
vector cross product. Define the homogeneous quartic polynomial
and its unique symmetric coefficient tensor by

\[
q(y)=\tfrac12|w_2(y)|^2+\langle w_1(y),w_3(y)\rangle
=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta=1}^{12}
K_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
y_\alpha y_\beta y_\gamma y_\delta.
\tag{4}
\]

Equivalently K is 1/4! times the fourth derivative of q at zero.
It is a universal local tensor; signs and zero edges are in J_p.
Repeated indices below are summed over all twelve local coordinates.
Define four local matrices, with the precise covariance order shown:

\[
\begin{split}
C_p&=J_p S J_p^T,&
U_{i,p}&=J_p S B_i S J_p^T,\\
V_p&=J_p S A S J_p^T,&
W_{i,p}&=J_p S B_i S A S J_p^T.
\end{split}\tag{5}
\]

C_p, U_(i,p), V_p are symmetric. W_(i,p) need not be symmetric;
only its symmetric part contributes in the formula below.

## Conclusion

For both actual probes the exact fixed-mesh contraction is

\[
\boxed{
G^{\mathrm{nr}}_{i,F}
=-\sum_p K_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
\left(24(U_{i,p})_{\alpha\beta}(V_p)_{\gamma\delta}
 +48(W_{i,p})_{\alpha\beta}(C_p)_{\gamma\delta}\right).
}\tag{6}
\]

The first term contains no internal action pair. The second contains
one internal action pair, C_p, which has no positive-flow-time heat
factor. Equation (6) is an explicit finite tensor contraction using
the exact forest covariance, not a numerical evaluation on the box
or a mesh-uniform estimate.

On full boundary-compatible link coordinates the nonradial polynomial
is not a function of linear curvature Dx: there is a pinned gradient
x with Dx=0 and S4-S_(4,rad)>0. Consequently the curvature-only joint
moment transfer used for the radial action in L023 is unavailable
for this selected polynomial. This does not prove that any particular
forest and Hodge expectations differ or that G^(nr) is nonzero for
either probe. L019's cancellation obligations across action, measure,
and observable jets remain in force; no forest invariance is asserted
for (6) separately.

No logarithmic coefficient, absence of faster cutoff growth, bounded
remainder, interacting reflected error <= c_box/2, continuum theory,
or mass gap follows.

## Proof

### 1. Identify the actual selected quartic action

For fixed local coordinates and g sufficiently small, the logarithm
of the ordered product exists near zero and has expansion
g w1 + g^2 w2 + g^3 w3 + O(g^4). The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff
formula through cubic degree is

\[
\log(e^Ue^V)=U+V+\tfrac12[U,V]
 +\tfrac1{12}[U,[U,V]]+\tfrac1{12}[V,[V,U]]+O_4(U,V).
\tag{7}
\]

For these finite matrices (7) also follows directly by multiplying
the exponential series through degree three and using
log(1+Z)=Z-Z^2/2+Z^3/3+O(Z^4). Substituting
U=g w1+g^2 w2+g^3 w3 and V=gY gives exactly (3).
This use of the finite Taylor identity proves the specific ordered
coefficients; it makes no assumption about global logarithms.

For W in su(2), W^2=-|W|^2 I/4, so
Tr exp(W)/2=cos(|W|/2). Therefore the single plaquette action is

\[
4P=4(1-\cos(|W|/2))
=\tfrac12|W|^2-\tfrac1{96}|W|^4+O(|W|^6).
\tag{8}
\]

Its fourth coefficient is
|w2|^2/2+<w1,w3>-|w1|^4/96. Since w1=(Dx)_p,
summing (8) gives S4-S_(4,rad)=sum_p q(J_p X).
This also fixes the minus sign in (6). There are no additional powers
of a in (4)–(6): x is the dimensionless exponential coordinate of
L011, and 4P is the dimensionless action numerator. All mesh and
probe normalizations remain inside (2) and J_p.

### 2. Keep both connected pairing classes

Fix p and write C,U,V,W for its matrices. Isserlis' Gaussian moment
formula applies to the joint centered Gaussian (X,y); y may have
singular covariance because some edges are fixed. No inverse of C
is used. The third cumulant removes exactly all pairings disconnected
among the three polynomial vertices X^T B_i X, X^T A X, and q(y).
This follows either from L011's finite-partition formula or by
partitioning each Wick pairing according to its connected components.

The two quadratic vertices cannot have internal pairs: such a pair
uses both their legs and disconnects that vertex. If the quartic
vertex has no internal pair, its four legs all attach to the two
quadratic vertices. There are choose(4,2) times 2! times 2! = 24
pairings. Tensor symmetry and symmetry of B_i,A give the common
contracted value K_(alpha beta gamma delta) U_(alpha beta)
V_(gamma delta). These are the first term of the positive cumulant.

If the quartic vertex has one internal pair, choose its two internal
legs in six ways. There must be one edge between the two quadratic
vertices; choose its endpoints in four ways. The remaining two
quadratic legs attach to the remaining two quartic legs in two ways.
There are therefore 6 times 4 times 2 = 48 connected pairings.
The internal pair contributes C_(gamma delta). The three-edge path
through the two quadratic vertices contributes
(J_p S B_i S A S J_p^T)_(alpha beta), or its transpose.
Symmetry of K makes their contracted values equal. These are the
second term of the positive cumulant. Two internal quartic pairs
disconnect the action vertex, so there is no third class.

For completeness the local Wick decomposition is

\[
q(y)=K_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}:y_\alpha y_\beta y_\gamma y_\delta:
 +6K_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}C_{\gamma\delta}:y_\alpha y_\beta:
 +3K_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}C_{\alpha\beta}C_{\gamma\delta}.
\tag{9}
\]

The constant contributes zero to the cumulant. The fourth-chaos
term gives the 24 pairings, and the quadratic term gives 6 times
the eight connected quadratic-vertex pairings, agreeing with 48.
Thus all internal loops and connected subtractions are retained.
Cumulant multilinearity over plaquettes and the minus sign in (1)
prove (6), without independence between plaquettes and without a
linear-gauge transfer of q.

### 3. A pinned zero-curvature test

Choose an elementary plaquette with four interior vertices. Pick
u=t1, v=t2 and assign the site potentials, in traversal order,
0, -u, -u-v, -v. Set the site potential to zero elsewhere, including
all boundary vertices, and put x_e=phi_start-phi_end on every positive
edge. Then Dx=0 on every plaquette, and boundary links are zero.
On the selected plaquette the traversal entries are u,v,-u,-v.
Equation (3) gives w1=0 and w2=[u,v]=-t3 there. Therefore
q=|[u,v]|^2/2=1/2 on that plaquette.

On every other plaquette w1=0 as well, so its q is |w2|^2/2 >= 0.
The total nonradial quartic polynomial is at least 1/2, whereas
it is zero on the zero field, which has the same linear curvature.
This disproves factorization through Dx on full pinned coordinates.
It is a straight exponential family built from a linear gradient,
not an exactly flat nonlinear link family. It tests the hypothesis
of the available transfer theorem, not equality or inequality of
the two Gaussian integrals and not a continuum obstruction.
