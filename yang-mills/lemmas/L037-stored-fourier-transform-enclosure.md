|n|<=224 and H_0=6, H_1=9. Use L036's exact degree-144 four-panel

Define Fhat_h(n) to be the exact complex dyadic values recorded by their

## Proof

### Exact roots and weights

All intervals have integer endpoints scaled by S=10^180. Addition and
negation are exact; multiplication and division take extrema of all
four endpoint products/quotients, rounded outward by integer floor and
ceiling. Division requires exclusion of zero. Interval induction
therefore encloses every evaluated expression. The implementation
reuses L036's arithmetic at this higher scale in the current process;
it does not change that input's source or certificate.

Evaluate Legendre polynomials by P_0=1, P_1=z and
j P_j=(2j-1)zP_(j-1)-(j-1)P_(j-2). NumPy supplies only proposals
for 144 initial rational brackets. Their endpoint signs are certified
by outward evaluation of P_144; bisection preserves opposite signs
until the width is at most 1e-90. All final brackets are disjoint
and contained in (-1,1). Each contains a root by the intermediate
value theorem. Since P_144 has degree 144, these are all its roots
and each is simple. Proposal accuracy is not a premise.

Import the standard Gauss–Legendre quadrature theorem and its weight
formula, already used for Q in L036: at a root z of P_m the weight
on [-1,1] is 2/((1-z^2)(P'_m(z))^2). The standard Legendre derivative
identity gives P'_144=144(P_143-zP_144)/(1-z^2). Evaluating both on
each whole root bracket encloses its exact weight. All denominators
exclude zero, all weights are positive, and the sum of their enclosures
contains 2. The proof uses the named standard formulas to identify the
rule, not a fresh proof of quadrature exactness.

### Cutoff and phase enclosures

Map every root and weight to each positive panel [0,5/2] and [5/2,3]
by exact affine operations. The cutoff is identically one on the
first panel's interior. On the second, u=9-x^2 and v=x^2-25/4
have certified positive lower bounds, and
rho=exp(-1/u)/(exp(-1/u)+exp(-1/v)).

To enclose exp(-t), t>=0, reduce t to t/2^k<=1/8. Alternating
Taylor sums of orders 61 and 60 are lower and upper bounds; their
terms decrease in magnitude. Outward evaluation followed by k squarings
encloses the exponential on the entire argument interval. Very small
values are rounded outward at scale S; an upper endpoint is retained
even when a lower endpoint rounds to zero. The rho denominator excludes
zero at every node. No machine exponential accuracy is assumed.

Enclose pi using Machin's identity
pi=16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239). For either positive argument,
the rational alternating sum through j=79 of
(-1)^j q^(2j+1)/(2j+1) and that sum plus term j=80 enclose the
arctangent. The identity follows from the tangent addition formula
and 4 arctan(1/5)-arctan(1/239) in (0,pi/2). This standard identity
is used only for an explicit outward phase input.

For theta=(pi/8)n x with n>=0 and x on a positive panel, reduce
theta to theta/2^k<=1/8. The cosine sum through degree 60 and sine
sum through degree 61 end with positive terms. Subtracting the next
term gives lower bounds; all terms decrease in magnitude. Enclose
the partial sums outward and propagate using
cos(2t)=cos(t)^2-sin(t)^2 and sin(2t)=2sin(t)cos(t), with old
values in both right-hand sides. This supplies rigorous intervals
for the exact phase at every exact quadrature node.

### Every stored transform and the final margins

Legendre parity and the weight formula make the exact rule reflection
symmetric, including the affine negative panels. Since rho is even,

\[
 F_0^Q(n)=2\sum_{\text{positive panels}}w\rho(x)\cos(\pi n x/8),
 \qquad
 F_1^Q(n)=2i\sum_{\text{positive panels}}wx\rho(x)\sin(\pi n x/8).
\]

Negative frequencies are the complex conjugates. Outward summation
of all 288 positive-panel contributions encloses these exact values.
Symmetry is used only for Q. Every actual stored complex coefficient
at both frequency signs is separately compared as an exact dyadic
number. If real and imaginary differences have interval absolute
bounds d_r,d_i, their complex error is at most d_r+d_i.
