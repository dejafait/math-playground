# Yang–Mills existence and mass gap: argument overview

The [source audit](foundations/01-target-and-scope.md) records the current official target, including the requirement of finite positive mass. There is no candidate solution. The first tested construction uses the independent-link endpoint of the SU(2) Wilson measure, with conventions in the [lattice foundations](foundations/02-lattice-and-reflection-conventions.md).

This construction retains random continuum fluctuations but loses physical states: L001 proves that the rescaled fixed-orientation plaquette trace converges in all smeared joint laws and moments to white noise. Its positive-time polynomial reflection quotient contains only the vacuum. Thus this particular limiting observable field cannot be the required theory.

The [MRS ultraviolet source audit](foundations/03-mrs-ultraviolet-scope.md) supplies no established reflection positivity or positive centered norm to import. A comparison between a reflected curvature correlation and its free leading term remains conditional. L002 gives a finite, strictly positive separated reflection coefficient for the free SU(2) magnetic curvature square on R^4, with specified mollification and centering. L003 matches the same test to a centered, gauge-invariant Wilson plaquette in the fixed box [-4,4]^4 with trivial boundary links. It derives the quadratic gauge-quotient law, identifies it as the small-coupling limit at each fixed mesh, and proves that its separated reflection coefficient has a finite positive limit as the mesh tends to zero.

The [perturbative normalization audit](foundations/04-perturbative-curvature-renormalization.md) and L004 show that this oriented curvature square cannot use one common multiplicative counterterm: its scalar and traceless stress-tensor projections have different one-loop pole residues after including the bare coupling normalization. The complementary-plane difference isolates a traceless stress-tensor channel at tree level. L005 proves its fixed-box Gaussian reflection bound: the complementary curvature families are independent in the quadratic law, and the electric square supplies a nonnegative term with a finite separated mesh limit.

L006 resolves the homogeneous bulk mixing-space test for this channel. Among gauge-invariant curvature polynomials of dimension at most four, the hypercubic diagonal traceless triplet occurs once and has no lower-dimensional partner. A common-vertex average of plaquette traces transforms exactly as this triplet and retains the Gaussian lower bound. The [lattice matching source record](foundations/05-lattice-stress-tensor-matching.md) gives supporting precedent with different regulator details. One scalar parametrizes the permitted bulk matching, but its finiteness and the interacting lattice matching remain unproved.

L007 tests local translation normalization of that scalar. No nonzero compact displacement can have purely diagonal strain. Its explicit divergence-free localization preserves the desired core strain and removes the singlet, but retains shear in the transition region. The resulting Ward condition therefore needs an independently matched or canceled shear response and a controlled lattice residual. This is an obstruction to a triplet-only geometric projection, not to all Ward normalization schemes.

L008 supplies two explicit flowed Gaussian curvature-quadratic probes for joint triplet/shear normalization. Flowing the same two forms as occur in the Ward insertion makes their response matrix a Hilbert–Schmidt Gram matrix. Compact exact-curvature tests prove independence, and relative heat-mode bounds give a finite positive definite mesh limit. This removes the free response-rank obstruction but does not determine the Ward right-hand side.

L009 constructs a smooth gauge-covariant link variation from endpoint-averaged, transported clover curvatures. Its classical normalization is the desired local translation. After checking the configuration-space divergence and the Gaussian gauge-quotient identity, a polynomial mode bound and heat suppression prove that both flowed Gaussian Ward residuals vanish in the mesh limit. The resulting free triplet/shear coefficients tend to (1,1). Nonlinear generator normalization and interacting response and residual control remain open.

L010 computes that generator's exact nonlinear Haar divergence as a planar-strain weighted plaquette-trace sum and specifies Wilson-flow versions of both probes. The divergence begins at order g^2 despite its zero linearized trace. Its isolated contribution to the Ward response is (3/32) times the triplet response column, up to an O(a^2) error in the small-coupling coefficient. This supplies one finite nonzero correction, while the rest of the interacting residual is uncomputed.

L011 expresses the remaining order-g^2 coefficient at each fixed mesh as Gaussian cumulants in the exact forest gauge, with action, coordinate Haar density, insertion, and nonlinear flow terms retained. It distinguishes that coordinate-density correction from L010's divergence insertion. The lattice contractions and their leading cutoff logarithm are unevaluated; this is a perturbative reduction, not an additional ultraviolet bound.

L012 rejects a proposed off-shell BRST boundary-domain shortcut. At the zero connection, an explicit smooth Dirichlet ghost on the cube violates the normal Neumann condition after BRST variation. An auxiliary-field boundary formulation or a qualified spectral argument needs separate justification before it can support boundary subtraction; the existing Gaussian representation is unaffected.

L013 defines a real Fourier auxiliary-field prescription on the same finite relative cochains, with auxiliary and ghost variables on interior vertices. Integrating those variables before the connection gives exactly L003's Hodge Gaussian, with the nonzero ghost determinant canceled by normalization. The original joint bosonic integrand is not absolutely integrable along gauge directions. Thus the ordered prescription supplies a Gaussian representation, while boundary Ward identities and nonlinear measure replacement remain unproved.

L014 extends that prescription to all auxiliary/ghost polynomials and proves the free BRST Ward identity at each fixed mesh. A positive connection regulator justifies joint integration by parts; auxiliary-first Gaussian domination proves convergence of every normalized polynomial and removal of the explicit breaking insertion. This supplies a finite free Ward starting point, while a nonlinear Wilson-measure representation and physical-boundary identities remain unproved.

L015 applies Neuberger's known normalization obstruction to this boundary-pinned cube, using Testa, [hep-lat/9803025v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9803025v1), section 2, (1)–(9). The nonlinear BRST algebra preserves the pinned domains, which retain a nonempty ghost sector and compact group manifolds without boundary. Positive auxiliary width justifies the invariant integrals. Their gauge-fixed normalization and gauge-invariant numerators vanish, so this standard smooth global extension stops. L014 remains a local free input, and the exact forest measure is retained.

L016 specializes the known tree path map to that forest and L009's generator. Its unique pinned compensator freezes all tree links, and its differentiated reduced divergence equals L010's full divergence pointwise on the slice. Thus both existing Wilson-flow probes retain the exact nonlinear Ward insertion in forest coordinates. L017 gives its free operator pi_F R_F K_a I_F, proves compatibility with the forest Hessian and recovers L009's two Gaussian Ward responses. L018 contracts L011's coordinate-Haar term, proves that its lifted quadratic density changes with the forest, and gives the exact cancellation requirement for the other terms. Original-probe randomized traces at one admitted mesh suggest forest dependence of both contractions; no full logarithm or interacting bound follows.

## Unresolved gap

A construction must remove the ultraviolet cutoff while preserving a nonzero physical observable sector, establish the required gauge-invariant field content and axioms, and control the infinite-volume spectrum with a finite positive mass. None of these Yang–Mills construction steps has been completed here. In particular, a positive ordinary variance does not supply a lower bound on a centered reflection norm.

For the complementary-plane observable comparison, a bare gauge-invariant difference, an exactly covariant site-centered representative, and positive fixed-box Gaussian coefficients are specified. An interacting renormalization trajectory, a finite bulk matching coefficient with control of boundary and higher-dimensional contributions, and an ultraviolet-uniform error smaller than the free lower bound are missing. The fixed-mesh small-coupling limit and the operator classification supply no such uniform estimate. L002 also proves that the ordinary variance of its Wick-centered mollified composite diverges, so separated reflection limits cannot be used as all-moment composite-field constructions. Reflection positivity on the full limiting algebra and infrared-uniform control are separate unresolved requirements.

## Partial results

The exact independent-plaquette calculation supplies a discriminating negative test: its Euclidean variance survives, but every centered positive-time polynomial has reflection norm zero. This stops the direct beta = 0 plaquette-field construction. It leaves scale-dependent couplings and other observable sectors open; it supplies no physical mass lower bound for Yang–Mills.

For the explicitly normalized free curvature-square test f_*, L002 obtains q_0(f_*) >= (3/2)[224/(pi^2 67^3)]^2 > 0. This clears the leading-coefficient test for the conditional comparison. At the same time, its ordinary variance grows as a positive constant times epsilon^-4. Neither result establishes an interacting limit or a mass gap.

For the fixed-box Wilson Gaussian coefficient, L003 obtains q_box(f_*) >= (3/2)d_box^2 and q_a(f_*) >= c_box = (3/4)d_box^2 > 0 for all sufficiently small meshes, with d_box explicitly specified there. The proof retains one magnetic mode in a nonnegative spectral expansion and controls the entire separated limit. For an interacting comparison along a specified coupling trajectory, an additional bound |Q_(a,g(a)) - q_a| <= c_box/2 would suffice for this observable's positive lower bound; it has not been proved.

L004 gives a formal ultraviolet obstruction to a single-factor operator prescription: the normalized scalar residue is b_0 = 11/(24 pi^2), and the traceless stress-tensor residue is zero. Its exact tree identity h_12 - h_34 = (T_11 + T_22 - T_33 - T_44)/4 supplies a specific alternative channel. This is a renormalization diagnostic, not a nonperturbative estimate or a proof that the full reflection correction diverges.

For that complementary-plane channel, L005 obtains q^D_a = q_(12,a) + q_(34,a), with zero mixed Gaussian terms and q_(34,a) >= 0. Its finite box limit satisfies q^D_box >= (3/2)d_box^2, and q^D_a >= c_box for sufficiently fine meshes. A correctly matched interacting comparison would still need |Q^(D,matched)_(a,g(a)) - q^D_a| <= c_box/2. No bound on this error, or independence at positive coupling, has been proved.

L006 reduces the allowed bulk dimension-four mixing to one coefficient by exhausting the reflection-even polynomial candidates and their permutation-equivariant maps. Its site-centered representative changes the separated Gaussian form by O(a^2), has the same box limit, and also satisfies qhat^D_a >= c_box eventually. Its matched interacting comparison still requires an error <= c_box/2. Neither a finite coefficient nor interacting smallness follows from the symmetry or test-function estimates.

L007 gives the compact-strain obstruction, retains the affine boundary flux, and derives the exact finite-lattice identity E[VO] = Cov(O, VS - div_H V). Its conditional normalization equation is W = z_3 C_3 + z_6 C_6 + r. It does not determine the coefficients, and the interacting residual r remains unbounded.

For fixed flow time tau = 1/16, L008 proves C_a -> C_tau with det C_tau > 0 for its specified two probes. If eta_tau = lambda_min(C_tau), then ||C_a^(-1)|| <= 2/eta_tau eventually. L009 supplies the free right-hand side W^G_a = C_a(1,1) + r_a with r_a -> 0, hence ||z^G_a - (1,1)|| <= (2/eta_tau)||r_a|| -> 0. This controls the free normalization residual at fixed flow time and box; no interacting perturbation bound is supplied. The required matched reflected error remains <= c_box/2.

For L010's specified nonlinear probes, take the small-coupling limit at each fixed mesh in the response to minus the Haar divergence. Its g^2 coefficient gamma^H_a obeys gamma^H_a = (3/32) C_a(1,0) + O(a^2), so C_a^(-1) gamma^H_a = (3/32,0) + O(a^2). Uniform coefficient comparison and a heat-trace bound control all Gaussian modes. This isolated contribution has no ultraviolet logarithmic growth; it does not determine the full one-loop response, a joint cutoff/coupling limit, or the interacting reflected error.

L011 proves existence of the remaining fixed-mesh coefficient and gives its connected expansion and flow-derivative recursions. External heat suppression does not bound every internal cubic/quartic contraction in that expression. No value, including zero, is established for its cutoff logarithm, and no improvement of the required reflected-error threshold c_box/2 follows.

For the proposed smooth domain A_t = 0, partial_n A_n = 0 and c = bar c = b = 0, L012 computes the uncanceled normal trace s(partial_n A_n) = partial_n^2 c + [A_n,partial_n c]. Its cube counterexample disproves closure with unrestricted off-shell ghosts. This determines a failed Ward-domain premise without establishing any boundary counterterm, logarithm or interacting error estimate.

At every fixed mesh, L013's normalized auxiliary-first connection law is the Hodge law, and its curvature covariance operator is d L_1^(-1) d*. Point covariance retains the factor a^(-4) from the cochain inner product. All Gaussian curvature moments and L003's separated coefficients are therefore unchanged. This reproduction fixes the integration prescription without improving the interacting reflected-error threshold <= c_box/2 or defining smooth continuum auxiliary traces.

L014 fixes the Fourier-compatible transformation s bar c = i b and the graded regulated identity <sP>_epsilon = (-1)^|P| epsilon <P(delta A,c)>_epsilon, summed over colours. Every polynomial expectation has its auxiliary-first limit, and the breaking is O_(P,N)(epsilon) at fixed N. This is reproduction of finite Gaussian/Berezin machinery; no mesh-uniform bound or improvement of the interacting reflected-error threshold <= c_box/2 follows.

For all admitted meshes, L015 leaves the compact site group SU(2)^((N-1)^4). Its standard smooth BRST gauge-fixing orbit factor is zero at every positive auxiliary width. This known-theorem application closes a proposed nonlinear representation route, without altering the positive original Wilson normalization or improving the interacting reflected-error threshold <= c_box/2.

L016's reduced compensator divergence equals the omitted forest-link divergence and need not vanish; the exact rational checks detect both as nonzero. L017's free reduced trace is zero, with individually nonzero diagonal entries in its samples; deleting path terms already changes curvature at this order. L018 gives Gamma^M_(i,F) = -2 Tr(B_(i,F) Sigma_F A_F Sigma_F^2). An exact unit-field test changes M2 by 11/2 between two N=24 forests. Qualified 64-sample trace differences for the actual triplet/shear probes are about (4776.32,16814.47), with sample standard errors (315.38,256.75). Exact nonzero contraction differences for these probes, mesh asymptotics and the full Gamma remain unproved. The path operator still has no mesh-uniform locality or norm bound. The required reflected error <= c_box/2 is unchanged.

## Known traps checked

- Beta = 0 is the infinite bare-coupling endpoint, not the weak-coupling continuum regime. No continuation between these regimes is assumed.
- Nonconstant Euclidean random fields need not produce nonvacuum Hilbert-space vectors. L001 computes the full polynomial reflection form of its white-noise limit.
- A vacuum-only Hilbert space has no excited spectrum and an infinite gap supremum; this does not meet the finite-mass requirement.
- L001 concerns one plaquette orientation and its limiting field algebra. It is not a theorem excluding every possible gauge observable or a refutation of the target.
- In L001, joint-law convergence is supplemented by all polynomial moment limits. No distribution-space tightness, complete reconstruction axioms, or asymptotically free short-distance behavior is inferred from that calculation.
- A fixed-lattice or finite-volume construction alone cannot establish the four-dimensional continuum target. The test uses the infinite lattice at beta = 0 but does not solve the missing cutoff-dependent interacting problem.
- A gauge-identity defect tending to zero is not a lower bound on a reflected correlation. A source's proposed positivity argument is not used as an established theorem.
- The free calculation uses a fixed trace and field normalization. It is not silently identified with a renormalized interacting observable or with the free coefficient of a different infrared regulator.
- The fixed-box matching fixes links contained in the boundary, which gives relative cochain boundary conditions. It does not replace them with scalar Dirichlet conditions on every gauge-potential component. Gauge zero modes are removed before the fixed-mesh Laplace argument.
- A small-coupling limit at each fixed lattice spacing cannot be exchanged with the ultraviolet limit without an additional estimate. Choosing smaller bare couplings separately at each mesh does not establish a prescribed renormalized Yang–Mills trajectory.
- Separated insertions avoid coincident singularities. Wick subtraction removes their means but does not by itself construct this composite as a Euclidean L^2 random distribution; the divergent ordinary variance is retained explicitly.
- Positivity on linear combinations of single free composite insertions is not positivity on the entire interacting polynomial algebra. A positive free coefficient supplies no interacting remainder bound or positive physical mass.
- Discrete mode energies caused by a fixed box are regulator scales, not a physical mass gap after infrared removal.
- An oriented curvature square is not its scalar sum. The factor 1/g_0^2 affects its pole residue, and finite tensor traces cannot be interchanged with minimal subtraction. L004 uses pole residues and traceless projections and retains the uncomputed tensor sector.
- Continuum MS pole identities do not compute fixed-boundary Wilson remainders. The protected stress-tensor channel still needs finite interacting lattice matching. L005 obtains its free lower bound by calculating the mixed terms, not by subtracting two positive plaquette forms.
- The electric curvature is reflection-odd while its square is even. L005 retains the temporal edge Neumann resolvent and its contact term until the insertion supports are separated; it does not apply the magnetic Dirichlet kernel to an electric plaquette.
- Multiplicity one classifies allowed homogeneous bulk counterterms; it does not prove a finite renormalized limit. Boundary tensors, boundary-supported terms, and higher-dimensional remainders are not controlled by that classification. The SU(3) clover matching coefficients in the cited source are not assigned to this SU(2) plaquette average.
- Complementary plaquettes lie on different shifted grids. L006 uses an exact common-vertex average, proves its Gaussian comparison on separated supports, and makes no interacting O(a^2) claim from smooth test-function averaging alone.
- A triplet's multiplicity-one mixing does not make a local translation Ward identity triplet-only. Compact localization introduces shear; an affine displacement moves the fixed boundary. Nonzero shear coefficients do not by themselves prove a nonzero response for every probe. Finite-lattice change of variables leaves a matching residual, and continuum translation restoration is not assumed as a proved construction theorem.
- The positive Gram matrix of L008 uses ordinary mixed Gaussian responses with flowed probes. It neither constructs unflowed continuum quadratic random variables nor gives reflection positivity. Heat flow is nonlocal in Euclidean time; these normalization probes are not identified with the original positive-time local observable algebra. Free invertibility does not determine the Ward right-hand side or control its interacting residual.
- L009 retains the divergence when linearizing the link identity and checks the extra Hodge gauge-fixing term by conditioning on the transverse Gaussian field. Its clover stencil has zero linearized trace; this is not a claim that the nonlinear Haar divergence vanishes. Smooth-field consistency is supplemented by a summable bound on all heat-weighted modes. Neither that bound nor the limiting free coefficients imply an interacting residual estimate.
- L010 differentiates the endpoint transport as well as the clovers and retains the resulting nonzero nonlinear divergence. Wilson flow linearizes to the action Laplacian, agreeing with Hodge heat flow on curvatures. Its fixed-mesh Laplace limit is taken before mesh refinement. The O(a^2) comparison is for Gaussian mixed responses only, and other one-loop contributions can alter or cancel its finite coefficient.
- L011 uses the exact product Haar measure after forest gauge fixing. Higher coordinate polynomials are not transferred to the Hodge Gaussian by the tree-level quotient argument. The Haar coordinate-density correction and the Ward-divergence insertion are distinct. Its nonlinear flow expansion includes flow times approaching zero, so damping at the final probe time is not asserted for every internal loop.
- L012 tests an unrestricted smooth off-shell domain. Ghost eigenfunction equations can impose extra normal boundary jets, and eliminating an auxiliary field can change the closure claim. Its counterexample does not invalidate relative spectral kernels or imply a nonzero boundary divergence.
- L013 uses a specified auxiliary-first Fourier integral. The joint bosonic modulus has no decay along gauge directions, so absolute Fubini and a positive joint measure are unavailable. A canceled constant ghost determinant does not justify auxiliary/ghost Ward identities, nonlinear forest-measure replacement or transferring an auxiliary boundary value into a strong normal connection constraint.
- L014 justifies polynomial Ward identities through positive connection damping followed by ordered removal at fixed mesh. Its complex functional is not a positive joint measure, and its bound on the breaking insertion may depend on the mesh. Zero pure auxiliary covariance does not imply b = 0: the mixed A,b covariance is nonzero. No strong continuum boundary condition, nonlinear Haar-measure replacement or interchange with the ultraviolet limit is inferred.
- L015 verifies that boundary pinning retains the hypotheses of standard globally smooth compact BRST gauge fixing with all signed copies. Its zero normalization gives an undefined ratio, not a failure of the original Wilson measure. A zero-width delta prescription needs a separate definition; the positive-width limit supplies no nonzero normalization. A local Gaussian, a forest slice or changed gauge data are different prescriptions and supply no interacting ultraviolet bound here.
- L016 differentiates the compensating paths and uses equivariance with the exact gauge/quotient product measure. Fixed-transformation Haar invariance alone would not justify deleting these derivatives. L017 retains the free path potential Phi_F K_a A, including the factor a in the root sum. Its zero total trace does not imply a zero reduced diagonal or safe deletion of path terms. L018 transfers M2 only after an explicit free-gauge-invariant path lift; nonlinear vertices retain their forest law. A changed density function does not prove every probe contraction changes, and sampled fixed-mesh traces prove neither exact nonvanishing nor a full logarithm. Equality of full and reduced insertions supplies no local ultraviolet estimate; flow starts on the slice and acts on full links.
