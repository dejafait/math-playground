# Yang–Mills existence and mass gap: argument overview

The [source audit](foundations/01-target-and-scope.md) records the current official target, including the requirement of finite positive mass. There is no candidate solution. The first tested construction uses the independent-link endpoint of the SU(2) Wilson measure, with conventions in the [lattice foundations](foundations/02-lattice-and-reflection-conventions.md).

This construction retains random continuum fluctuations but loses physical states: L001 proves that the rescaled fixed-orientation plaquette trace converges in all smeared joint laws and moments to white noise. Its positive-time polynomial reflection quotient contains only the vacuum. Thus this particular limiting observable field cannot be the required theory.

The [MRS ultraviolet source audit](foundations/03-mrs-ultraviolet-scope.md) supplies no established reflection positivity or positive centered norm to import. A comparison between a reflected curvature correlation and its free leading term remains conditional. L002 gives a finite, strictly positive separated reflection coefficient for the free SU(2) magnetic curvature square on R^4, with specified mollification and centering. L003 matches the same test to a centered, gauge-invariant Wilson plaquette in the fixed box [-4,4]^4 with trivial boundary links. It derives the quadratic gauge-quotient law, identifies it as the small-coupling limit at each fixed mesh, and proves that its separated reflection coefficient has a finite positive limit as the mesh tends to zero.

The [perturbative normalization audit](foundations/04-perturbative-curvature-renormalization.md) and L004 show that this oriented curvature square cannot use one common multiplicative counterterm: its scalar and traceless stress-tensor projections have different one-loop pole residues after including the bare coupling normalization. The complementary-plane difference isolates a traceless stress-tensor channel at tree level. L005 proves its fixed-box Gaussian reflection bound: the complementary curvature families are independent in the quadratic law, and the electric square supplies a nonnegative term with a finite separated mesh limit.

L006 resolves the homogeneous bulk mixing-space test for this channel. Among gauge-invariant curvature polynomials of dimension at most four, the hypercubic diagonal traceless triplet occurs once and has no lower-dimensional partner. A common-vertex average of plaquette traces transforms exactly as this triplet and retains the Gaussian lower bound. The [lattice matching source record](foundations/05-lattice-stress-tensor-matching.md) gives supporting precedent with different regulator details. One scalar parametrizes the permitted bulk matching, but its finiteness and the interacting lattice matching remain unproved.

L007 tests local translation normalization of that scalar. No nonzero compact displacement can have purely diagonal strain. Its explicit divergence-free localization preserves the desired core strain and removes the singlet, but retains shear in the transition region. The resulting Ward condition therefore needs an independently matched or canceled shear response and a controlled lattice residual. This is an obstruction to a triplet-only geometric projection, not to all Ward normalization schemes.

L008 supplies two explicit flowed Gaussian curvature-quadratic probes for joint triplet/shear normalization. Flowing the same two forms as occur in the Ward insertion makes their response matrix a Hilbert–Schmidt Gram matrix. Compact exact-curvature tests prove independence, and relative heat-mode bounds give a finite positive definite mesh limit. This removes the free response-rank obstruction while leaving the translation generator, residual, and interacting stability unproved.

## Unresolved gap

A construction must remove the ultraviolet cutoff while preserving a nonzero physical observable sector, establish the required gauge-invariant field content and axioms, and control the infinite-volume spectrum with a finite positive mass. None of these Yang–Mills construction steps has been completed here. In particular, a positive ordinary variance does not supply a lower bound on a centered reflection norm.

For the complementary-plane observable comparison, a bare gauge-invariant difference, an exactly covariant site-centered representative, and positive fixed-box Gaussian coefficients are specified. An interacting renormalization trajectory, a finite bulk matching coefficient with control of boundary and higher-dimensional contributions, and an ultraviolet-uniform error smaller than the free lower bound are missing. The fixed-mesh small-coupling limit and the operator classification supply no such uniform estimate. L002 also proves that the ordinary variance of its Wick-centered mollified composite diverges, so separated reflection limits cannot be used as all-moment composite-field constructions. Reflection positivity on the full limiting algebra and infrared-uniform control are separate unresolved requirements.

## Partial results

The exact independent-plaquette calculation supplies a discriminating negative test: its Euclidean variance survives, but every centered positive-time polynomial has reflection norm zero. This stops the direct beta = 0 plaquette-field construction. It leaves scale-dependent couplings and other observable sectors open; it supplies no physical mass lower bound for Yang–Mills.

For the explicitly normalized free curvature-square test f_*, L002 obtains q_0(f_*) >= (3/2)[224/(pi^2 67^3)]^2 > 0. This clears the leading-coefficient test for the conditional comparison. At the same time, its ordinary variance grows as a positive constant times epsilon^-4. Neither result establishes an interacting limit or a mass gap.

For the fixed-box Wilson Gaussian coefficient, L003 obtains q_box(f_*) >= (3/2)d_box^2 and q_a(f_*) >= c_box = (3/4)d_box^2 > 0 for all sufficiently small meshes, with d_box explicitly specified there. The proof retains one magnetic mode in a nonnegative spectral expansion and controls the entire separated limit. For an interacting comparison along a specified coupling trajectory, an additional bound |Q_(a,g(a)) - q_a| <= c_box/2 would suffice for this observable's positive lower bound; it has not been proved.

L004 gives a formal ultraviolet obstruction to a single-factor operator prescription: the normalized scalar residue is b_0 = 11/(24 pi^2), and the traceless stress-tensor residue is zero. Its exact tree identity h_12 - h_34 = (T_11 + T_22 - T_33 - T_44)/4 supplies a specific alternative channel. This is a renormalization diagnostic, not a nonperturbative estimate or a proof that the full reflection correction diverges.

For that complementary-plane channel, L005 obtains q^D_a = q_(12,a) + q_(34,a), with zero mixed Gaussian terms and q_(34,a) >= 0. Its finite box limit satisfies q^D_box >= (3/2)d_box^2, and q^D_a >= c_box for sufficiently fine meshes. A correctly matched interacting comparison would still need |Q^(D,matched)_(a,g(a)) - q^D_a| <= c_box/2. No bound on this error, or independence at positive coupling, has been proved.

L006 reduces the allowed bulk dimension-four mixing to one coefficient by exhausting the reflection-even polynomial candidates and their permutation-equivariant maps. Its site-centered representative changes the separated Gaussian form by O(a^2), has the same box limit, and also satisfies qhat^D_a >= c_box eventually. Its matched interacting comparison still requires an error <= c_box/2. Neither a finite coefficient nor interacting smallness follows from the symmetry or test-function estimates.

L007 gives the compact-strain obstruction, retains the affine boundary flux, and derives the exact finite-lattice identity E[VO] = Cov(O, VS - div_H V). Its conditional normalization equation is W = z_3 C_3 + z_6 C_6 + r. It does not determine the coefficients, and the residual r remains unbounded.

For fixed flow time tau = 1/16, L008 proves C_a -> C_tau with det C_tau > 0 for its specified two probes. If eta_tau = lambda_min(C_tau), then ||C_a^(-1)|| <= 2/eta_tau eventually. This is a bound on a free response inverse, not on a matching residual or an interacting correlation. No Ward right-hand side or interacting perturbation bound is supplied; the required reflected error remains <= c_box/2.

## Known traps checked

- Beta = 0 is the infinite bare-coupling endpoint, not the weak-coupling continuum regime. No continuation between these regimes is assumed.
- Nonconstant Euclidean random fields need not produce nonvacuum Hilbert-space vectors. L001 computes the full polynomial reflection form of its white-noise limit.
- A vacuum-only Hilbert space has no excited spectrum and an infinite gap supremum; this does not meet the finite-mass requirement.
- L001 concerns one plaquette orientation and its limiting field algebra. It is not a theorem excluding every possible gauge observable or a refutation of the target.
- In L001, joint-law convergence is supplemented by all polynomial moment limits. No distribution-space tightness, complete reconstruction axioms, or asymptotically free short-distance behavior is inferred from that calculation.
- A fixed-lattice or finite-volume construction alone cannot establish the four-dimensional continuum target. The test uses the infinite lattice at beta = 0 but does not solve the missing cutoff-dependent interacting problem.
- A gauge-identity defect tending to zero is not a lower bound on a reflected correlation. A source's proposed positivity argument is not used as an established theorem.
- The free calculation uses a fixed trace and field normalization. It is not silently identified with a renormalized interacting observable or with the free coefficient of a different infrared regulator.
- The fixed-box matching fixes links contained in the boundary, which gives relative cochain boundary conditions. It does not replace them with scalar Dirichlet conditions on every gauge-potential component. Gauge zero modes are removed before the fixed-mesh Laplace argument.
- A small-coupling limit at each fixed lattice spacing cannot be exchanged with the ultraviolet limit without an additional estimate. Choosing smaller bare couplings separately at each mesh does not establish a prescribed renormalized Yang–Mills trajectory.
- Separated insertions avoid coincident singularities. Wick subtraction removes their means but does not by itself construct this composite as a Euclidean L^2 random distribution; the divergent ordinary variance is retained explicitly.
- Positivity on linear combinations of single free composite insertions is not positivity on the entire interacting polynomial algebra. A positive free coefficient supplies no interacting remainder bound or positive physical mass.
- Discrete mode energies caused by a fixed box are regulator scales, not a physical mass gap after infrared removal.
- An oriented curvature square is not its scalar sum. The factor 1/g_0^2 affects its pole residue, and finite tensor traces cannot be interchanged with minimal subtraction. L004 uses pole residues and traceless projections and retains the uncomputed tensor sector.
- Continuum MS pole identities do not compute fixed-boundary Wilson remainders. The protected stress-tensor channel still needs finite interacting lattice matching. L005 obtains its free lower bound by calculating the mixed terms, not by subtracting two positive plaquette forms.
- The electric curvature is reflection-odd while its square is even. L005 retains the temporal edge Neumann resolvent and its contact term until the insertion supports are separated; it does not apply the magnetic Dirichlet kernel to an electric plaquette.
- Multiplicity one classifies allowed homogeneous bulk counterterms; it does not prove a finite renormalized limit. Boundary tensors, boundary-supported terms, and higher-dimensional remainders are not controlled by that classification. The SU(3) clover matching coefficients in the cited source are not assigned to this SU(2) plaquette average.
- Complementary plaquettes lie on different shifted grids. L006 uses an exact common-vertex average, proves its Gaussian comparison on separated supports, and makes no interacting O(a^2) claim from smooth test-function averaging alone.
- A triplet's multiplicity-one mixing does not make a local translation Ward identity triplet-only. Compact localization introduces shear; an affine displacement moves the fixed boundary. Nonzero shear coefficients do not by themselves prove a nonzero response for every probe. Finite-lattice change of variables leaves a matching residual, and continuum translation restoration is not assumed as a proved construction theorem.
- The positive Gram matrix of L008 uses ordinary mixed Gaussian responses with flowed probes. It neither constructs unflowed continuum quadratic random variables nor gives reflection positivity. Heat flow is nonlocal in Euclidean time; these normalization probes are not identified with the original positive-time local observable algebra. Free invertibility does not determine the Ward right-hand side or control its interacting residual.
