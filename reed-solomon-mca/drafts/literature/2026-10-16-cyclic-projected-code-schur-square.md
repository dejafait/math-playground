## Inspected primary sources and applicability

1. Christine Bachoc, Oriol Serra and Gilles Zemor, *Revisiting Kneser's
   Theorem for Field Extensions*,
   [arXiv:1510.01354v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.01354v2),
   24 February 2017, Theorem 3, printed pp. 2–3; stabilizer remark
   before Corollary 14, p. 9; Section 4, pp. 15–16. Read the theorem,
   that remark and both final proof cases, including L strictly
   containing the field generated by the normalized first factor.
   It treats arbitrary field extensions and nonzero finite-dimensional
   factors; a product below dim S+dim T-1 has a larger stabilizing
   subfield. Its remark makes every element stabilizing a nonzero
   finite-dimensional space algebraic over the base field.

   Applicability: identify Ann(P) with W inside F_97[X] of degree at
   most seven; its dimension is six. Work in F_97(X), whose elements
   algebraic over F_97 are constants: a nonconstant r=p(X)/q(X)
   would make X algebraic over F_97(r) by p(X)-r q(X)=0, so r cannot
   itself be algebraic over F_97. Thus the larger stabilizer
   alternative is unavailable for the nonzero finite-dimensional W W,
   and the theorem gives dim(W W)>=6+6-1=11. Products have degree at
   most fourteen. Evaluation at sixteen distinct labels is injective
   on that space by the polynomial root bound. Consequently this is
   the correct source specialization for the lower comparison eleven;
   neither MDS nor rationality of an original affine chart is needed.
   The source proof is imported, not reproved.

2. Diego Mirandola and Gilles Zemor, *Critical pairs for the Product
   Singleton Bound*,
   [arXiv:1501.06419v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.06419v2),
   arXiv version 20 August 2015 (PDF title date 6 September 2018),
   *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 61(9) (2015), 4928–4937,
   DOI 10.1109/TIT.2015.2450207. Read Lemma 2.1 (p. 3), Theorem 2.5
   (p. 4), Theorem 3.3 (p. 7), its full-support reduction and entire
   p. 9 proof, and Theorem 4.2 (p. 10).
   Theorem 2.5 assumes a full-support pair with an MDS factor.
   Theorem 4.2 assumes both codes MDS and product dimension equal to
   the dimension sum minus one, at most n-2. Neither is applicable to
   C_P as an MDS code. The general Theorem 3.3 instead subtracts the
   dimension of the product's stabilizer. Its finite-field proof uses
   scalar extension and descent of the stabilizer, so it has no hidden
   infinite-base-field restriction. Trivial stabilizer of C_P's square
   has not been established; use source 1 for the lower comparison.
   None of these statements is an upper bound ten.

3. Hugues Randriambololona, *On products and powers of linear codes
   under componentwise multiplication*, *Contemporary Mathematics*
   637 (2015), 3–78, DOI 10.1090/conm/637/12749,
   [author's AGCT-14 manuscript](https://perso.telecom-paristech.fr/randriam/maths/HR_AGCT-14.pdf),
   inspected 16 October 2026; PDF has no version identifier inspected.
   Read Proposition 1.28 (printed p. 13), Proposition 2.38 and
   Corollary 2.39 with proofs (p. 28), and Proposition 3.5 with proof
   (pp. 33–34). The square is a degree-two Hilbert-function value;
   multiplication by a fixed vector is self-adjoint for the ordinary
   coordinate pairing. Accordingly the exact dual test is

       z in (C_P * C_P)^perp  iff  z * C_P is contained in C_P^perp.

   This is the coordinate adjunction specialization, with no MDS
   hypothesis. It requires annihilating all products, not just C_P.
   Proposition 3.5 supplies a lower bound involving dual distance,
   not an upper bound from local parities. No general operation of
   squaring a block parity is imported.

4. Alain Couvreur, Rakhi Pratihar, Nihan Tanisali and Ilaria Zappatore,
   *On the structure of the Schur squares of Twisted Generalized
   Reed-Solomon codes and application to cryptanalysis*,
   [arXiv:2412.15160v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15160v1),
   19 December 2024, Section 2.4.1/Proposition 6, Definition 6,
   Section 4.1.1/Proposition 10 and its entire proof, and
   Theorem 4.1 with proof. Proposition 6 uses polynomial products
   before evaluation; degree less than the number of points ensures
   injectivity in the all-one multiplier case used here. Proposition
   10 assumes a specified quasi-GRS decomposition and gives three
   upper bounds, including 2d+1 for maximum polynomial degree d.
   At degree seven that ceiling is fifteen, short of the required ten.
   Merely being a codimension-two subcode of the dimension-eight RS
   code is not the definition of their two-quasi-GRS decomposition.
   The shortening theorem additionally requires dimension k-|I|.
   No such changed code or shortening hypothesis is used here.
