[Verbitsky, *Deformations of trianalytic subvarieties of hyperkahler
manifolds*, Selecta Math. (N.S.) 4 (1998), 447--490,
alg-geom/9610010v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9610010v1):
the inspected version is dated 9 October 1996. PDF page numbers below
count the title page, rather than the paper's printed numbering.

- Proposition 3.3/Remark 3.4 and complete proof, PDF pp. 18--19:
  compact M and smooth compact embedded X give an orthogonal parallel
  tangent/normal splitting and a flat trivial summand for a nonzero
  holomorphic normal section. The printed wording must exclude the
  zero section in (iii). The remark extends (i)--(ii) to noncompact
  manifolds; it does not assert arbitrary normal-section integration.
- Proposition 4.8, PDF pp. 26--28: (i) concerns local transport along
  an existing family with parallel normal variation and real-analytic
  metric; (ii) explicitly adds a smooth Riemannian compactification
  with a compactification of that family. Its global-integration
  discussion uses uniform bounds away from the singular sets. Do not
  read its quasiprojectivity parenthesis as a theorem that the given
  L2 metric extends smoothly to an algebraic compactification.
- Propositions 6.2--6.3 and Corollary 6.4 with complete proofs, PDF
  pp. 31--33: M may be noncompact, and X need not be closed. One starts
  with a real-analytic family of embedded submanifolds and a path along
  which all members are trianalytic. Its normal variation is
  triholomorphic, hence parallel; the resulting local transport is
  isometric and compatible with the hyperkahler structures. This is
  transport of existing deformations, not their existence theorem.
- Section 7.1/Proposition 7.1, PDF pp. 34--36, and Proposition 7.5,
  Corollary 7.6/Claim 7.7 with their arguments, pp. 37--38, explicitly
  use compact ambient M. In particular the compactness argument uses
  compactness of the target of distance-preserving maps; compact X
  alone does not meet the stated hypothesis.
- Theorem 8.6 and complete proof, PDF pp. 40--41, give a flat natural
  connection on the universal family over the Douady space in that
  compact setup. The quaternionic base structure from Section 7 is
  used before the curvature weight argument. Theorems 9.1--9.2,
  pp. 41--42, compare existing members as metric/complex varieties;
  their compact ambient hypothesis and pre-existing deformation path
  are retained. No dimension or nonconstancy conclusion is stated.
- Theorem 10.5 and complete proof, PDF p. 49, give a compact
  hyperkahler variety structure on that Douady space. Its construction
  uses the preceding universal-family and isomorphism results. It
  supplies no positive lower bound on its dimension, no assertion that
  every infinitesimal normal section integrates, and no smoothness
  assertion for an arbitrary Douady space. Use this precise statement,
  rather than the introduction's Theorem 1.2, which omits the
  trianalytic qualification in its displayed wording.
