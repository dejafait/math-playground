## Primary inputs and qualifications

**Yoshioka (2000 preprint).** [Irreducibility of moduli spaces of vector bundles on K3 surfaces, math/9907001v2, 7 February 2000](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9907001v2): Theorem 0.1 (p. 2) requires a primitive positive-rank algebraic Mukai vector and general ample divisor; nonemptiness is equivalent to square at least -2. Corollary 0.3 supplies semistable existence for multiples of vectors with square at least -2. Section 1.1 (p. 4) fixes general H satisfying condition (♮): equal slopes force equal normalized first Chern classes. Lemma 1.5 (pp. 5–6) applies to arbitrary positive-rank vectors: if v²/2 >= ell(v)², the properly slope-semistable substack has codimension at least v²/(2ell(v))-ell(v)+1; nonempty slope-semistable locus then contains slope-stable sheaves. Here ell(v)=gcd(rank,c_1), not divisibility of the whole vector. Lemma 1.7 (p. 6) handles nonprimitive positive-square Gieseker stable loci. Proposition 0.5 (p. 4) classifies entirely non-locally-free moduli under Theorem 0.1’s primitive hypotheses; it cannot cover the nonprimitive case. Read the filtration proof of Lemma 1.5 and its rank-one-factor qualification. Neither separate nonempty open loci nor primitive-only freeness establishes their intersection for both cases.

**The essential rank-one-factor proof case.** [Yoshioka, An application of exceptional bundles to the moduli of stable sheaves on a K3 surface, alg-geom/9705027v2, 18 June 1997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9705027v2), Lemma 4.4 and proof, pp. 10–11: inspected the Jordan–Hölder dimension argument through its exceptional r=1, ell=2 case. The proof retains the trivial double dual and bounds the defect family by 3c_2-3, repairing the naive filtration estimate. This is a supporting proof case, not an independent nonprimitive existence citation: use the arbitrary-vector statement in the later Lemma 1.5. The earlier preprint is not the extended 1999 journal version; do not attach the latter’s section numbers to it. Its asymptotic primitive theorem is not being substituted for this target.

**Point-defect dimension.** [Ellingsrud–Lehn, Irreducibility of the Punctual Quotient Scheme of a Surface, alg-geom/9704016v1, 15 April 1997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9704016v1), Theorem 1, p. 1: for a locally free rank-r sheaf on a smooth projective surface and length t>=1, Quot(E,t) is irreducible of dimension (r+1)t; the fixed-support fibre has dimension sum_x(rt_x-1). This provides the complete length-t quotient bound, including collisions. It does not establish that the double-dual moduli is nonempty or stable. A next proof must justify double-dual stability, its possible lengths, moduli dimensions and relative Quot parameterization; subtracting dimensions without those hypotheses is insufficient. Only the global dimension conclusion is needed, so no reproof of punctual irreducibility is proposed.

**Polarization walls.** [O’Grady, Modular sheaves on hyperkähler varieties, Algebraic Geometry 9 (2022), 1–38](https://content.algebraicgeometry.nl/2022-1/2022-1-001.pdf), Definition 1.1 (p. 2), Section 3 (pp. 7–10): real surface ample cones have locally finite rational walls determined by Chern character. An a-wall is orthogonal to an integral (1,1)-class with -a<=q<0; a(F)=rank(F)² d(F)/(4c_X). Proposition 3.10 bounds the destabilizing class rank(F)c_1(E)-rank(E)c_1(F) between -a(F) and zero, with equality zero only for the zero class. Its proof uses the discriminant identity and Bogomolov. Proposition 3.4 preserves stability within an open a(F)-chamber. Read Proposition 3.8’s bounded-subsheaf crossing proof and Section 3.4’s application. The numbered propositions use ample divisor classes: extending the surface argument to the real endpoint h must be justified, rather than silently replacing h by a nearby rational polarization. The opening real-cone statement supplies the relevant framework; a(F), possible walls and the zero-class exception still require checking.
