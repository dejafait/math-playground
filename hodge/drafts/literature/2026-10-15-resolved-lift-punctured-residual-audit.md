## Primary inputs and their limits

### Actual universal family and flatness

[Stacks Section 44.2, Tag 0B94](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B94)
defines the degree-d points functor by closed subschemes finite locally
free of degree d over the test scheme. This includes nonreduced test
schemes. Its definition supplies an actual subscheme, not a support
cycle. The algebraic comparison uses d=3. The relative framework already
assessed supplies the induced Hilbert cube.

[Stacks Lemma 10.39.7, Tag 00HI](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00HI)
allows arbitrary ring base change of a flat module. Its tensor-functor
proof was read. [Lemma 10.39.4, Tag 00HC](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00HC)
gives transitivity of flatness. These are the relevant inputs for an
actual universal pullback over an A-flat source; neither asserts
flatness of its pushforward image through a blowdown. L043's central
family must still be identified on the open where the blowdown is an
isomorphism.

Buchweitz--Flenner's [Hilbert/Douady discussion, printed p. 49](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9912245v1#page=49)
states the analytic functor in terms of closed subspaces proper and flat
over the test space, with pullback as its functorial operation. This
provides analytic scope alongside the reused relative-Hilbert coverage.
No algebraization of an arbitrary analytic deformation is inferred here.

### Distinguished component over the Artin base

[Stacks Lemma 10.32.6, Tag 00J9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00J9)
gives a bijection of idempotents after quotient by a locally nilpotent
ideal. Both the topological proof and the nilpotent correction/uniqueness
proof were read. The square-zero reduction ideal fits this hypothesis.
It selects a central disjoint component only where such a component is
already present; it does not separate intersecting sheets.

[Stacks Lemma 10.101.8, Tag 06A5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/06A5)
assumes ring maps R -> S -> S', a nilpotent ideal I in R, an S'-module M
flat over R, and M/IM flat over S/IS. It concludes that M is S-flat; its
proof uses the injectivity in the nilpotent flatness criterion. This
covers the framework for a component known A-flat with flat central
projection; the actual component and its hypotheses remain to be checked.

[Stacks Lemma 10.77.7, Tag 05CG](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/05CG)
assumes a nilpotent ideal, a flat module and projective reduction; it
concludes projectivity. The proof compares the nilpotent filtrations.
Together with the named Nakayama lemma it supports the degree-one
component step after flatness is established. Reduction alone is not a
flatness or isomorphism criterion. These commutative-algebra inputs also
apply to analytic local rings; their statements do not require finite
type over a field.

### Embedded parameters and the residual quotient

Read [Buchweitz--Flenner, Lemma 7.6, printed p. 50](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9912245v1#page=50),
with the preceding deformation-theory definition. For an S-flat subspace
Z of X_S with ideal J and coherent base module N, it identifies the
infinitesimal embedded deformations with Hom(J,O_Z tensor N). Properness
of Z is explicitly unnecessary in that deformation theory. An lci
assumption appears only in the subsequent normal-bundle interpretation,
not in this Hom description. Thus the source covers punctured analytic
subspaces and the non-lci central support; it does not identify L008's
normal sheaf or compute this residual ideal.

[Stacks Lemma 10.101.6, Tag 051K](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/051K)
requires an Artinian local ring R and proper ideal I; flatness of M is
equivalent to flatness of M/IM and vanishing of Tor_1^R(R/I,M).
The proof through a lifted residue basis was read. It does not require
M finite-dimensional over R. For this audit R=A and I=(epsilon).
L007 gives the actual ideal-parametrization formula; L009 gives the
specific local colon equations. Their application must be audited,
rather than replaced by a claim that arbitrary colons are flat.

### Hartogs on the correct reduced space

[Stacks Lemma 15.24.18, Tag 0AVB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AVB)
assumes a Noetherian normal domain and a finite module. It equates
reflexivity with torsion-freeness plus S_2, and with equality to the
intersection of height-one localizations. The proof's depth-at-least-two
extension step was read. This supports algebraic extension for locally
free modules on the smooth normalization; its normal-domain hypothesis
must not be imposed silently on the singular or nonreduced support.

Read Lebl's [Theorem 2.1.4 and Corollary 2.1.5, pp. 49--50](https://www.jirka.org/scv/scv.pdf#page=49),
version 4.4, 31 May 2026. The corollary extends a holomorphic function
across a single point in an open subset of C^n for n>=2. The theorem's
Cauchy-integral proof and the corollary's Hartogs-figure case were both
read. For sections of an existing holomorphic vector bundle, the
applicability argument is coefficientwise in a local frame, with
uniqueness from the identity theorem. No extension theorem for an
unknown bundle or a nonreduced family is imported.
