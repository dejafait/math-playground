# L034 — Prym determinant Lefschetz transfers miss the cubic tensor

## Hypotheses

Retain the very-general cubic-RM data of L031 and L032. Thus S is
a projective K3 surface, T=T(S) has rational dimension eighteen,
End_Hdg(T)=E=Q(zeta_7+zeta_7^(-1)) is the full endomorphism field,
dim_E T=6, and the full Kuga--Satake variety A has
V=H^1(A,Q)=C^+(T,q). Put Y=A^4. Fix the rational Hodge tensor
beta_U in H^4(Y,Q) defined in L031, including its actual embedding
kappa and polarization. Its Kunneth multidegree is (1,1,1,1).

Let C -> C_0 be a connected finite etale Galois cover of smooth
projective complex curves with finite abelian group Gamma and
g=g(C_0)>=4. Let e be the idempotent of a nontrivial irreducible
rational representation of Gamma, P=e J(C) its abelian isogeny
factor, K=e Q[Gamma], W=H^1(P,Q), and h=2g-2. No generality,
dimension or endomorphism-algebra restriction is imposed on P.

Write Delta(P) for the rational determinant subspace in H^h(P,Q).
Precisely, if Omega is the orbit of nontrivial complex characters
selected by e and W_chi the corresponding character space, then

\[
W_{\mathbb C}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\Omega}W_\chi,
\quad \dim W_\chi=h,\qquad
\Delta(P)_{\mathbb C}=
 \bigoplus_{\chi\in\Omega}\bigwedge^h W_\chi.             \tag{1}
\]

This is the cohomological realization of exterior_K^h W. Each
summand on the right is one-dimensional; the sum, rather than
an individual complex line, is the rational subspace.

Let D^c(B) denote the rational span of products of c divisor
classes on an abelian variety B, including every mixed divisor.
For d=dim P and c=d+2-h/2, consider any correspondence
alpha in D^c(P x Y). Its action is

\[
\alpha_*:H^h(P,\mathbb Q)(h/2)\longrightarrow
                  H^4(Y,\mathbb Q)(2),\qquad
\alpha_*(x)=(p_Y)_*(p_P^*x\smile\alpha).                \tag{2}
\]

Allow Lefschetz degree-lowering, cohomological projectors and
compositions of divisor correspondences. Tate twists are explicit
in (2); below the polarization-preserving groups act trivially
on every Tate line.

## Conclusion

Every such image is generated by target divisors:

\[
\alpha_*\Delta(P)\ \subseteq\ D^2(Y).                   \tag{3}
\]

Consequently beta_U is outside the rational span of all these
images, even allowing special Pryms, arbitrary cover orders and
all base genera g>=4, common isogeny factors, mixed divisors, the indicated
degree-lowering operations, and isogeny replacements. The same
conclusion holds for finite external products of these determinant
inputs and divisor classes followed by such correspondences.

This stops this determinant-transfer channel. It does not exclude
arbitrary algebraic correspondences or source classes with a
nontrivial action of the derived Lefschetz group. Algebraicity of
beta_U and the separate Kuga--Satake correspondence kappa remains
unresolved. The attained span remains 21 on the Dickson family,
with three attained RM directions against four required.

## Proof

**Imported results and the specialization.** The prior
[assessment](../drafts/literature/2026-09-27-higher-genus-prym-lefschetz-transfer.md)
is SPECIALIZE for this exact image test. Patel--Zhang,
*Algebraicity of Hodge classes on some generalized Prym Varieties*,
[arXiv:2506.13729v2, Theorem 1.2, Lemma 2.9, Corollary 2.10 and
section 2.6, PDF pp. 3, 6--7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13729v2#page=6),
supplies (1) and algebraicity of Delta(P), including rational
factors and special covers. The invariant Jacobian summand is
excluded. No reproof of their algebraicity theorem is needed.

We use Milne, *Lefschetz classes on abelian varieties*, Duke Math.
J. 96 (1999), [Propositions 1.1 and 1.5, PDF pp. 5--6](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1999aP.pdf#page=5),
for isogeny-factor behavior, [Theorem 3.2, p. 15](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1999aP.pdf#page=15),
for the divisor-invariant criterion, and [Proposition 5.7 and
Theorem 5.9, pp. 26--27](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1999aP.pdf#page=26),
for equivariance and lowering. The
[author's correction to the proof of Theorem 5.9](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1999a.html)
uses equivariant primitive projections; Lambda is not an inverse
of L on all cohomology. These general results are imported.
The calculation of their image here is REPRODUCTION of known
representation tools, with no originality claim.

**The full target polarization centralizer.** For an abelian
variety B let S_B denote its full polarization-preserving
centralizer of End_Hdg(H^1(B,Q)). This is Milne's group S(B) in
the cohomological, contragredient convention. It fixes Tate lines.
It is the Tate-trivial subgroup of the similitude group L(B);
no equality with the Hodge group is assumed.

L032 gives the entire complex Hodge-group representation

\[
V_{\mathbb C}=\bigoplus_{\epsilon\in\{+,-\}^3}
 R_\epsilon\otimes M_\epsilon,
\qquad \dim R_\epsilon=64,\quad\dim M_\epsilon=256,       \tag{4}
\]

where R_epsilon is the external tensor product of three
four-dimensional half-spin modules. Each is irreducible. The
central volume element in the ith six-dimensional spin factor
has eigenvalue i epsilon_i on R_epsilon, as in L031. Distinct
sign triples therefore give inequivalent representations.
The invariant polarization identifies V_C with its dual, so
each irreducible dual occurs in (4). Dualizing inverts the
central eigenvalues, forcing R_epsilon^*=R_(-epsilon).
No sign triple is its own opposite.

A rational endomorphism is a Hodge endomorphism precisely when
it commutes with the Hodge circle, equivalently its rational
algebraic closure, the Hodge group. Commutants commute with
extension of scalars: they are kernels of linear equations.
Schur's lemma applied to (4) thus gives

\[
D_{\mathbb C}:=\operatorname{End}_{\rm Hdg}(V)\otimes\mathbb C
 =\prod_\epsilon
       \bigl(1_{R_\epsilon}\otimes\operatorname{End}(M_\epsilon)\bigr).
                                                               \tag{5}
\]

The centralizer of (5) is the product of the algebras
End(R_epsilon) tensor 1_(M_epsilon). In particular, it cannot
permute sign spaces: the eight central idempotents belong to
D_C. This uses the full endomorphism algebra, not merely the
cover field or a Clifford subalgebra of it.

The polarization pairs only opposite sign spaces. On each pair
Schur's lemma expresses it as the evaluation between R_epsilon
and its dual times a perfect pairing of their multiplicity
spaces. An element centralizing (5) preserves this polarization
exactly when its actions on R_epsilon and R_(-epsilon) are
inverse transposes. Hence, choosing one sign from each of the
four opposite pairs gives an actual group isomorphism

\[
(S_A)_{\mathbb C}\simeq\prod_{j=1}^4\operatorname{GL}(R_j)
             \simeq\operatorname{GL}_{64}(\mathbb C)^4,
\qquad
(S_A)_{\mathbb C}^{\rm der}=\operatorname{SL}_{64}(\mathbb C)^4.
                                                               \tag{6}
\]

Each factor acts by its standard representation and its dual,
both with multiplicity 256. There is no finite permutation
component to discard. On H^1(Y,C)=V_C^(direct sum 4), both
multiplicities are 1024 and S_Y=S_A. Neither an individual R_j
nor an individual factor in (6) is asserted to descend to Q.

**The joint group retains common factors.** Set J=S_(P x Y).
Its restriction pi:J -> S_Y is surjective. To check this with
mixed divisors present, write P x Y up to isogeny as a product
of powers of pairwise nonisogenous simple abelian varieties B_i.
Milne's Proposition 1.5 identifies J with the product of their
S_(B_i), each taken once regardless of multiplicity. The same
description for Y keeps exactly the B_i that occur in Y.
Restriction is this product projection. A factor common to P
and Y uses the same group on both, rather than two independent
copies. This also follows from the algebras with involution in
Proposition 1.1, so the choice of polarization causes no loss.

Since S_Y over C is connected by (6), pi maps J_C^0 onto it:
the image of the identity component has finite index in the
connected target. Moreover

\[
\pi\bigl((J_{\mathbb C}^0)^{\rm der}\bigr)
                         =(S_Y)_{\mathbb C}^{\rm der}.   \tag{7}
\]

Indeed a surjective homomorphism maps commutators onto
commutators and hence the generated connected derived groups
onto one another. No product assertion for S_P x S_Y is used.
Possible disconnected factors of S_P do not alter (7).

**Determinant inputs are fixed by the joint derived group.**
The group J commutes with the endomorphisms of P extended by
zero on Y, including K. Its action on W_C therefore preserves
each W_chi in (1). On the line exterior^h W_chi it acts by the
determinant of this restricted action, an algebraic character
of J_C. Every character is trivial on (J_C^0)^der. Thus

\[
\Delta(P)_{\mathbb C}\subseteq
 H^h(P,\mathbb C)^{(J_{\mathbb C}^0)^{\rm der}}.          \tag{8}
\]

This argument does not require W_chi to be irreducible, P to
be general, or a transfer to commute with Gamma. Additional
endomorphisms of P do not remove the commutation with K.
Nor is the character on its top exterior line being confused
with a field element's scalar action on W_chi.

Milne's Proposition 5.7 makes (2) equivariant for the joint
Lefschetz group and hence for its subgroup J. Equations (7)--(8)
imply

\[
\alpha_*\Delta(P)_{\mathbb C}\subseteq
               H^4(Y,\mathbb C)^{\operatorname{SL}_{64}^4}.
                                                               \tag{9}
\]

In particular, source determinant classes need not themselves
be divisor-generated for (9) to hold.

**In degree four derived invariance is full invariance.** Put
G=GL_64(C)^4 and H=SL_64(C)^4. The abelian cohomology algebra
gives H^4(Y,C)=exterior^4 H^1(Y,C). Every factor of H^1 is a
standard or dual representation of one G factor by (6).
For the central scalar torus Z=(C^*)^4, any weight occurring
in the fourth tensor power, and therefore in its exterior
power, has the form

\[
(t_1,t_2,t_3,t_4)\longmapsto\prod_{j=1}^4t_j^{k_j},
\qquad k_j\in\mathbb Z,\quad |k_j|\le4.                 \tag{10}
\]

Multiplicities and all Kunneth summands are included. The space
of H-invariants is stable under Z, so it is the sum of its
Z-weight spaces. For each j, the scalar matrices zeta I in
the jth factor, with zeta^64=1, belong to H. A Z-weight in an
H-fixed vector must consequently satisfy 64 divides k_j.
The bound in (10) forces k_j=0 for every j. Thus Z fixes every
H-invariant vector. Over C every invertible matrix is a scalar
times a determinant-one matrix, so G=ZH. We have proved

\[
H^4(Y,\mathbb C)^{H}=H^4(Y,\mathbb C)^{G}.              \tag{11}
\]

The needed comparison is 4<64, not a bound on dim P or on g.
The argument does not claim equality of these invariant spaces
in every cohomological degree; determinant characters can first
have nonzero central weight at tensor degree at least 64.

Milne's Theorem 3.2 identifies the right side of (11) with
D^2(Y) tensor C. Combining (9)--(11) proves (3) after scalar
extension. Both alpha and Delta(P) are rational. The map from
H^4(Y,Q)/D^2(Y) to its complexification is injective, so this
containment descends to Q. Individual complex determinant lines,
spin blocks and group factors need not descend.

**Allowed operations and the whole tensor.** Theorem 5.9 with
its cited correction supplies Lefschetz lowering operators;
Corollary 5.8 supplies cohomological projectors. Compositions
remain divisor correspondences because cup products, pullbacks
and pushforwards along the abelian projections preserve the
divisor algebra (Milne, Corollary 5.5, PDF p. 25). Alternatively
apply (8)--(11) to the joint group of all intermediate abelian
varieties. These arguments require no primitivity assumption
for the source class and no nonzero value for a lowering map.

For finitely many source factors use their product with Y.
Each determinant line is again preserved by the full joint
group. A tensor product of their characters is a character,
and divisor classes are fixed by that group. Thus the same
derived-invariance proof applies to external products and any
appropriate final degree-four correspondence. Rational sums
of images from different sources stay in D^2(Y). Isogenies
preserve the rational cohomology, endomorphism centralizers
and divisor algebras, so the conclusion persists under them.

Finally L031 proves that the whole rational beta_U is outside
D^2(Y), for the fixed actual embedding and indeed for all of
its allowed auxiliary choices. Equation (3) therefore excludes
the whole tensor, not only a selected component or a selected
source. The threshold is an image outside D^2(Y); the achieved
image is zero in H^4(Y,Q)/D^2(Y). This is new negative evidence
for this notebook route, obtained by specializing known tools,
and is not a claim of a new general theorem or nonalgebraicity.
No complete candidate for the rational Hodge conjecture appears.
