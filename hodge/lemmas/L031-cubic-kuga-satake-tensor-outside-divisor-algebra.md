# L031 — The cubic Kuga--Satake tensor is outside the divisor algebra

## Hypotheses

Let S be a projective K3 surface at a very general point of the
four-dimensional cubic RM locus under consideration. Put T=T(S),
with its nondegenerate rational cup-product form q, and assume the
saved very-general data

\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}T=18,\qquad
\operatorname{End}_{\rm Hdg}(T)=E=
\mathbb Q(\zeta_7+\zeta_7^{-1}),\qquad \dim_E T=6.
\]

Let U be multiplication by the chosen generator
u=zeta_7+zeta_7^(-1), whose polynomial is
f(z)=z^3+z^2-2z-1. Let A be the full Kuga--Satake variety of
(T,q), and identify its weight-one Hodge structure V=H^1(A,Q)
with C^+(T,q). Fix a polarization psi on V and an element
v_0 in T with q(v_0) nonzero. Use the standard embedding

\[
M_v(w)=v w v_0,\qquad
\kappa:T\longrightarrow V\otimes V\subset H^2(A^2,\mathbb Q),
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

where the identification End(V)=V tensor V^* -> V tensor V
uses psi. Tate lines are implicit in this identification; every
group used below fixes them. The first tensor factor is the output
factor of End(V). Swapping the two factors changes none of the
conclusions after the same factor permutation.

Define u_U in T tensor T by the convention that x tensor y acts
on t as q(t,x)y. Thus u_U corresponds to U. Set

\[
\beta_U=(\kappa\otimes\kappa)(u_U)\in
V^{\otimes4}\subset H^4(A^4,\mathbb Q).
\]

All four copies of A are retained; the displayed inclusion is the
Kunneth summand of multidegree (1,1,1,1). The divisor algebra means
the algebra generated by **all** rational divisor classes on A^4,
including divisors mixing its factors.

## Conclusion

The class beta_U is a rational Hodge class outside the rational
span of products of two divisor classes on A^4. This holds for
every choice v_0 and psi in (1), and after transporting the whole
construction through an isogeny.

More precisely, after extending scalars to C there is an element
g in the full polarization centralizer which fixes every divisor
class, and a g-stable projection P of V tensor V, such that

\[
(P\otimes P)\beta_U\ne0,\qquad
(P\otimes P)(g\beta_U)=4(P\otimes P)\beta_U.                \tag{2}
\]

This stops the divisor-product supply for the transported tensor.
It does not show beta_U is nonalgebraic, provide an obstruction to
other cycles, or resolve algebraicity of kappa. It adds no surface
in the missing RM direction and no class to the attained
21-dimensional span on the Dickson family. Three attained
directions against four required, and the universal Hodge gap,
are unchanged.

## Proof

**Imported inputs and the specialization.** Milne,
*Lefschetz classes on abelian varieties*,
[Theorem 3.2 and Lemma 3.1, author PDF pp. 14--15](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1999aP.pdf#page=14),
identifies the divisor algebra on every power with invariants of
the full polarization centralizer and allows a scalar-extension
test. In the cohomology convention here that group is

\[
\mathcal S=\{g\in\operatorname{GL}(V):
 gd=dg\ (d\in D),\quad \psi(gx,gy)=\psi(x,y)\},\qquad
D=\operatorname{End}_{\rm Hdg}(V).                          \tag{3}
\]

Milne formulates it on homology; taking contragredients gives
(3). The Hodge endomorphism algebra agrees with the rational
abelian endomorphism algebra, with the harmless opposite-algebra
convention on cohomology. The theorem includes all components
and all mixed divisor classes. A single element of S(C) moving
beta_U is therefore sufficient; no assertion about invariance
under only the Hodge group is being substituted.

Schlickewei,
*The Hodge conjecture for self-products of certain K3 surfaces*,
[sections 2.2--2.4 and Theorem 3.3.1(i), arXiv:0907.2503v1,
pp. 4--6 and 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.2503v1#page=9),
gives the orthogonal E-eigenspace decomposition and identifies
the special Mumford--Tate group on V with the image of the
restricted spin group, acting by left Clifford multiplication.
Its hypotheses use the full endomorphism field, as assumed here.
Formula (1) is the standard construction in van Geemen,
[Proposition 6.3, arXiv:math/9903146v1, pp. 13--14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9903146v1#page=13),
also stated explicitly in Varesco,
[section 3 preceding Lemma 3.5, arXiv:2304.02519v3, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02519v3#page=13).

These general results are imported. The calculation below locates
this particular tensor under (3); it is a specialization of known
Clifford and invariant theory, classified as REPRODUCTION, with
no originality claim. The saved assessment did not identify a
theorem already deciding this exact beta_U.

**Commuting volume elements.** Work over C from now on. The field
E is self-adjoint for q, so the three eigenspaces give

\[
T_{\mathbb C}=T_1\perp T_2\perp T_3,\qquad
\dim T_i=6,\qquad U|_{T_i}=\lambda_i\,\mathrm{id}.           \tag{4}
\]

Each restricted form q_i is nondegenerate. All lambda_i are
nonzero because f(0)=-1. The imported group theorem puts the
left action of G=Spin(T_1) times Spin(T_2) times Spin(T_3)
inside the Hodge group of V_C. Its finite kernel is irrelevant
for this action. In particular D_C commutes with G, and psi is
G-invariant.

Choose q_i-orthonormal Clifford generators e_i1,...,e_i6,
with e_ij^2=1, and set z_i=e_i1...e_i6. The Clifford relations give

\[
z_i^2=-1,\quad z_i z_j=z_j z_i\ (i\ne j),\qquad
z_i v=\begin{cases}-v z_i&v\in T_i,\\v z_i&v\in T_j,\ j\ne i.
\end{cases}                                                \tag{5}
\]

The reversion of z_i is -z_i, hence its Clifford norm is one;
as an even product of six unit vectors it belongs to Spin(T_i).
Write Z_i=L_(z_i) on V_C. For a sign triple
epsilon=(epsilon_1,epsilon_2,epsilon_3), define

\[
V_\epsilon=\{w:Z_iw=\mathrm i\epsilon_iw\text{ for all }i\},
\qquad
p_\epsilon=\frac18\prod_{i=1}^3
       (1+\epsilon_i z_i/\mathrm i).                       \tag{6}
\]

The commuting semisimple Z_i give the direct sum of these eight
spaces, with projector L_(p_epsilon). Every space is nonzero:
p_epsilon is an even Clifford element with coefficient 1/8
on the identity. Its other seven terms are distinct nonempty
Clifford basis monomials, so it cannot vanish, and
L_(p_epsilon)(1)=p_epsilon. This argument works in the actual
full even Clifford algebra; it discards no spin multiplicities.

For x in V_epsilon and y in V_eta, invariance under each z_i gives

\[
\psi(x,y)=\psi(Z_ix,Z_iy)=-\epsilon_i\eta_i\psi(x,y).
\]

Consequently psi pairs only V_epsilon with V_(-epsilon), and
these pairings are perfect by nondegeneracy of psi. In particular
the eight sign spaces occur in four dual pairs. Every element
of D_C preserves each space because it commutes with the Z_i.

Set alpha=(+,+,+). For any t in C^*, let g_t act by t on V_alpha,
by t^(-1) on V_(-alpha), and by one on the other six spaces.
It commutes with D_C and preserves every pairing just described.
Thus

\[
g_t\in\mathcal S(\mathbb C).                               \tag{7}
\]

This proves membership in the full group directly; a classification
of that group or of the simple abelian factors is unnecessary for
this negative certificate. The elements g_t are linear symmetries
in the invariant criterion, not asserted to be rational maps of A.

**A nonzero block of the actual embedding.** Let
delta=(-,+,+), eta=-delta=(+,-,-). The map R_(v_0) from C^+
to C^- is invertible, since v_0^2=q(v_0) is nonzero, and commutes
with left multiplication. By (5), for v in T_i the operator
M_v=L_v R_(v_0) takes the sign space V_epsilon into the space
whose ith sign is flipped. Therefore the block

\[
F:T_1\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(V_\delta,V_\alpha),
\qquad F(v)=M_v|_{V_\delta},                               \tag{8}
\]

vanishes identically on the other two T_i when regarded as a
projection of the full embedding. It is nonzero: choose v in
T_1 with q(v) nonzero. Then M_v^2=q(v)q(v_0) id, so its
restriction in (8) is an isomorphism onto V_alpha.

The map F is G-equivariant. Indeed left multiplication by s in
G conjugates M_v to M_(s v s^(-1)), and the projectors (6)
commute with G because z_i is central in its own spin factor.
The natural six-dimensional representation T_1 is irreducible,
so F is injective. For an elementary verification of this familiar
irreducibility, in an orthonormal basis its Lie algebra contains
E_ab-E_ba. For distinct a,b,c, the product
(E_ac-E_ca)(E_cb-E_bc)=E_ab. Products also yield E_aa, so the
associative algebra generated by the Lie action is the full matrix
algebra. A nonzero invariant subspace is therefore the whole space.

Under the identification through psi, V_delta^* is identified
with V_(-delta)=V_eta. Thus for the ordered tensor projection

\[
P:V_{\mathbb C}\otimes V_{\mathbb C}
       \longrightarrow V_\alpha\otimes V_\eta,
\]

the map F'=P kappa is injective on T_1 and zero on T_2 and T_3.
This uses the actual polarization pairing, including all its
multiplicity spaces. No special choice of v_0 or replacement of
the embedding by an abstract copy of T has been made.

**Projecting the whole cubic tensor.** Let Omega_i in T_i tensor
T_i be the inverse-pairing tensor for q_i, corresponding to the
identity of T_i. Orthogonality and (4) give

\[
(u_U)_{\mathbb C}=\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_i\Omega_i,
\qquad
w:=(P\otimes P)\beta_U
   =\lambda_1(F'\otimes F')\Omega_1\ne0.                  \tag{9}
\]

Nonvanishing follows because Omega_1 is nonzero, lambda_1 is
nonzero, and the tensor square of an injective linear map is
injective. The other two summands have zero projection, which
excludes cancellation between the three cubic eigenvalues.

The projection P commutes with every g_t. On its image the first
factor V_alpha has weight t and the second V_eta has weight one.
Hence on the image of P tensor P the weight is t^2. Taking t=2,

\[
(P\otimes P)(g_2\beta_U-\beta_U)=3w\ne0.                  \tag{10}
\]

This proves (2). The Kunneth summand V^(tensor 4) injects into
H^4(A^4,C), so (10) is also a nonzero cohomological difference.
Milne's theorem now excludes beta_U from the full divisor algebra.
A complex group element suffices: a rational invariant remains
invariant after extending scalars, so no rational descent of the
projectors or of g_2 is needed.

Finally U is a Hodge endomorphism and kappa is a Hodge embedding,
so beta_U is rational of type (2,2). Changing v_0 retains the
invertibility used in (8); changing psi retains the perfect
opposite-sign pairings. The argument therefore applies to all
the specified auxiliary choices. Pullback by an isogeny gives
an isomorphism on rational cohomology carrying the divisor
algebra to the divisor algebra; transport the entire embedding
and tensor along it to obtain the same conclusion.

The exact membership threshold has failed, rather than merely
an estimate for a smaller divisor span. Classes outside this
algebra may still be algebraic. No counterexample to the rational
Hodge conjecture, and no complete candidate resolution, follows.
