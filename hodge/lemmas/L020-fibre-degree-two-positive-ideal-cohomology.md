# L020 — Positive ideal cohomology in fibre degree two

## Hypotheses

Use the very general cubic surface S, its elliptic fibre F, the reduced
correspondence C in X=S x S, and its normalization nu:W -> C from
L008. Use the section P=(x_0,y_0), the zero section O, and the exceptional
component E from L019; y_0 is nonzero. Fix the original ample line bundle
L and put H=L external tensor L. In this lemma assume L.F=2.
All cohomology is coherent cohomology over C.

## Conclusion

The exact positive-degree groups satisfy

\[
h^1(X,I_C(H))=L^2-4>0,\qquad
H^1(X,I_C(rH))=0\quad(r\ge2).                         \tag{1}
\]

Every such L is carried by a simultaneous translation preserving C to
O_S(O+P+bF), with b>=3 and L^2=4b-4. This is an isomorphism of the
original restriction problem, not a replacement by a power of L.

Thus the positive group relevant to L018's summand-recovery argument
can be nonzero: in this numerical range it occurs exactly when n-m=1.
Nonzero cohomology is not a nonzero obstruction for a particular
inclusion, and gives no transverse lift. Polarizations with L.F>=3
are not decided here. No additional surface or Hodge class is obtained,
and the universal rational Hodge conjecture remains unresolved.

## Proof

**Reduction of the original numerical class by an automorphism.**
L019 computes determinant -7 for the integral classes F,O,E,P and
proves that they span NS(S)_Q. If their span had index e in NS(S),
its determinant would be e^2 times the integral determinant of NS(S).
Hence e=1. There is no torsion or Picard variety on a K3, so these
classes give an integral basis for its line bundles as well.

Write L=aO+b_0F+cE+eP. The two components E and R=F-E have positive
integral L-degrees adding to two, so both degrees are one. Consequently

\[
a+e=2,\qquad -2c+e=1,
\]

and e is odd. On the generic elliptic curve, the degree-two divisor
class of L has group sum eP. Pullback by translation by jP subtracts
2jP from this sum. Set j=(e-1)/2. The translated class therefore
restricts to O+P on that curve, and its difference from O+P is vertical.
Vertical classes are generated by F and E, since all other fibres are
irreducible. Translation permutes the two components at infinity, so
their L-degrees remain one. Intersecting with E now removes the E
coefficient. Thus the translated line bundle is O_S(O+P+bF).
Its intersection with O is b-2, forcing b>=3, and its square is 4b-4.

Translation by a section extends over the singular fibres of a minimal
elliptic surface; import the standard statement in Schuett--Shioda,
[*Elliptic Surfaces*, arXiv:0907.0298v3, section 7.6, pp. 33--34](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.0298v3#page=33).
On W the pullback of P is fixed by the rotation sigma: its constant
coordinates x_0,y_0 do not change. Translation by jP therefore lifts
to W and commutes with sigma and with both projections. Its simultaneous
action on S x S preserves C. It consequently identifies the ideal
cohomology and restriction maps for L with those for its translated
bundle. This justifies doing the calculation with L=O+P+bF, while
retaining the original numerical class and all three singular points.

This numerical range is nonempty. The same integral basis shows that
every section is Q_j=jP: its generic-fibre class has group sum jP.
L019's intersection formula gives
(O+P+bF).Q_j=b-2+7j(j-1)/2, which is positive for every integer j
when b>=3. Its degrees on E and R are one. L019 shows these are all
the irreducible negative curves. The class has positive square and
positive intersection with F, placing it in the positive cone; Hodge
index gives positive intersection with every other irreducible curve.
The Nakai--Moishezon criterion therefore makes O+P+bF ample for b>=3.

**Two elliptic functions and their poles.** On the original Laurent
chart of the family set

\[
z=\frac{y+y_0}{x-x_0},\qquad w=2x+x_0-z^2.
\]

The function z has simple poles at O and P on a general elliptic fibre.
It is regular at -P: substituting x=x_0,y=-y_0 cancels the apparent
pole, and 2y_0 is nonzero. The lines through -P show that its degree-two
map has involution Q -> P-Q. Dividing the Weierstrass equation by
x-x_0 after substituting y=z(x-x_0)-y_0 gives

\[
x^2+(x_0-z^2)x+x_0^2+A(t)+x_0z^2+2y_0z=0,
\]

where A(t)=b_1P_a(t)+b_0. Thus w changes sign under that involution and

\[
w^2=z^4-6x_0z^2-8y_0z-3x_0^2-4A(t).               \tag{2}
\]

For every k>0 the functions

\[
z^i\ (0\le i\le k),\qquad
wz^{i-2}\ (2\le i\le k)                            \tag{3}
\]

are a basis of the generic-fibre sections of O(k(O+P)). There are 2k
of them, the genus-one Riemann--Roch dimension. Independence follows
from the two involution eigenspaces and independence of powers of z.
Their pole orders at O and P are at most k.

The vertical pole bounds must also be checked on the resolved surface.
At infinity use L008's minimal coordinates X=q^4x, Y=q^6y. At a general
point of R, q has order one and X,Y have order zero. Along E the three
functions q,X,Y all have order one. Therefore

\[
z=q^{-2}\frac{Y+y_0q^6}{X-x_0q^4}
\]

has pole order at most two on each component, and w has pole order
at most four. There are no other vertical poles. On the finite chart,
x=x_0 consists of the two smooth sections P and -P, and cancellation
at -P is valid also over the nodal fibres. Normality of S then checks
regularity in codimension two from these divisor bounds. In particular

\[
\operatorname{div}_\infty(z)\le O+P+2F_\infty,
\quad
\operatorname{div}_\infty(w)\le2O+2P+4F_\infty.      \tag{4}
\]

No smooth-fibre computation is substituted for the component bounds.

**Complete section spaces on S and W.** For r>=1 put a_i=rb-2i.
Equation (4) provides the following independent sections of rL:

\[
t^jz^i\quad(0\le i\le r,\ 0\le j\le a_i),
\qquad
t^jwz^{i-2}\quad(2\le i\le r,\ 0\le j\le a_i).
                                                               \tag{5}
\]

All a_i>=r(b-2)>=1. Their number is

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{r}(a_i+1)+\sum_{i=2}^{r}(a_i+1)
=2+2r^2(b-1)=2+\tfrac12r^2L^2.
\]

Kodaira vanishing and K3 Riemann--Roch give precisely this value for
h^0(S,rL). Thus (5) is the entire section space, not a selected subsystem.

Write T_0,T_infinity for W's fibres over v=0,infinity. Both projections
pull O back to O_W, P to P_W, and F_infinity to T_0+T_infinity. This
checks the actual equality of pullback line bundles in this numerical
range. With M=nu^*(H|_C),

\[
M=2O_W+2P_W+2b(T_0+T_\infty)=g_0^*(2L).             \tag{6}
\]

It is not necessary to assert equality of the two bundles on C itself.
For every i write N_i=2rb-2i, and let V_N be the space of Laurent
polynomials in v with exponents between -N and N. Equation (4) and
the unramified base change at 0 and infinity give independent sections

\[
z^iV_{N_i}\ (0\le i\le2r),\qquad
wz^{i-2}V_{N_i}\ (2\le i\le2r)                     \tag{7}
\]

of M^r. They too constitute the full section space. To check the count,
L008's trace splitting is g_{0*}O_W=O_S direct sum O_S(-F), whence

\[
h^0(W,M^r)=h^0(S,2rL)+h^0(S,2rL-F)
=4+4r^2L^2-4r.                                     \tag{8}
\]

For the second equality, 2rL-F=(L-F)+(2r-1)L is ample by L019's
nef-and-big assertion, so Kodaira vanishing and Riemann--Roch apply.
The sum of the dimensions in (7) equals (8). This avoids any assumption
about completeness of a chosen rational-function basis on a singular
fibre.

L019 further proves the exact normalization sequence with quotient
length three and surjective difference evaluation for every r>0.
Consequently

\[
h^0(C,H^r|_C)=h^0(W,M^r)-3.                         \tag{9}
\]

All three identifications at infinity are retained in (9), including
the point where the fibre components are tangent. Descent is not replaced
by arbitrary sections on W.

**The actual two-projection coefficient spaces.** On W put

\[
t=v+a/v,\qquad s=\zeta v+a\zeta^{-1}/v,
\qquad \zeta=\exp(2\pi i/7).
\]

For integers A,B>=1 let U_{A,B} be the span of t^j s^k with
0<=j<=A and 0<=k<=B, inside V_{A+B}. Then

\[
\dim U_{A,B}=2(A+B),\qquad
U_{A,B}=\{c\in V_{A+B}:
c_{-(A+B)}=a^{A+B}\zeta^{-2B}c_{A+B}\}.             \tag{10}
\]

Indeed the kernel of C[t,s] -> C[v,v^{-1}] is generated by the
irreducible conic

\[
t^2+s^2-(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})ts
     +a((\zeta+\zeta^{-1})^2-4).
\]

Its bidegree is (2,2), so the kernel in the (A,B) rectangle has dimension
(A-1)(B-1). Subtracting from (A+1)(B+1) gives the dimension in (10).
Only t^A s^B contributes either extreme Laurent coefficient; their
ratio gives the displayed equation. Thus it is the complete equation
of this hyperplane. Two such spaces with the same A+B span V_{A+B}
whenever their B values differ by an integer not divisible by seven.

Restrictions of the sections in (5) are exactly their expressions
with t in the first factor and s in the second. The elliptic functions
z and w agree under the two projections because x,y,x_0,y_0 agree.
Canonical rational sections for the divisor O+P+bF give the same
trivialization used in (6). Thus this computation concerns the actual
ambient map, without an unproved linearization or a normal-generation
substitution.

**The cokernel at r=1.** There are only the two types 1 and z in (5).
Their products give the following independent blocks in (7):

\[
U_{b,b},\qquad
z\bigl(U_{b,b-2}+U_{b-2,b}\bigr),\qquad
z^2U_{b-2,b-2}.                                    \tag{11}
\]

The middle sum fills V_{2b-2} by (10): its two B values differ by two.
The block dimensions are respectively 4b, 4b-3, and 4b-8. Their total
is 12b-11. No w block can occur in a product of these sections. On the
other hand (8)--(9) give h^0(C,H|_C)=16b-19. Thus the actual restriction
cokernel has dimension 4b-8=L^2-4, which is at least four.

**Surjectivity in every r>=2.** Use only products of two invariant
functions in (5), and products of one invariant and one anti-invariant
function. For the invariant block z^k, the coefficient space includes

\[
\sum_{i+j=k,\ 0\le i,j\le r}U_{a_i,a_j}.             \tag{12}
\]

For k=0 and k=2r there is one summand, of codimension one. For every
1<=k<=2r-1 there are two adjacent allocations of i,j. Their second
degrees differ by two, so (10) makes (12) all of V_{N_k}.

For the anti-invariant block wz^{k-2}, use the allocations with
0<=i<=r, 2<=j<=r, i+j=k, together with the reversed factors. For k=2
the allocations (0,2) and (2,0) have second degrees differing by four,
so they span all of V_{N_2}. For 3<=k<=2r-1 there are adjacent
allocations, again giving the full coefficient space. For k=2r only
(r,r) occurs; its coefficient space has codimension one. This covers
r=2 as well: the middle allocation is (1,2) and its reversal.

All degrees in these applications of (10) are at least r(b-2)>=1.
The independent blocks in (7) therefore have exactly three missing
dimensions in the span of the specified products: two invariant
endpoint dimensions and one anti-invariant endpoint dimension.
Every product is an ambient section and descends to C. By (9), a
subspace of this dimension already equals H^0(C,H^r|_C). Products of
two anti-invariant factors are not needed for surjectivity. This is
also why no omitted compatibility condition at infinity can be filled
by silently enlarging the target space.

Finally Kodaira vanishing on S and Kunneth give H^1(X,H^r)=0 for r>0.
The ideal sequence identifies H^1(X,I_C(rH)) with the restriction
cokernel just computed, proving (1).

**Scope and relevance.** The positive group in (1) is a real exception
to all-positive-degree ideal vanishing under ampleness alone. The
original class is retained, but the answer remains conditional on
its fibre degree being two. It supplies neither a value nor vanishing
for a fixed L of larger fibre degree. In L018's reversed ordering,
the sufficient group H^1(I_C((n-m)H)) is nonzero here precisely at
n-m=1. Computing the obstruction of the actual summand inclusion is
a separate problem. The prior m>n>0 exclusion, the three-versus-four
RM direction gap and the 21-dimensional attained span remain intact.

assessment. This is a partial local mathematical advance, potentially
beyond the checked statements, without certified originality. The
