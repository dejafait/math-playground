# L017 — Unfiltered rotation extensions retain the cubic obstruction

## Hypotheses

Use the very-general cubic Dickson surface S, X=S x S, the three reduced rotation supports C^(1)=C, C^(2), C^(3), and the generic smooth-critical-fibre and nonzero-j-derivative hypotheses of L014. Retain the NS-fixed tangent space V_N and its subspaces V_D contained in V_RM from L008. Their latter dimensions are three and four. Both ambient factors have the same deformation S_A over A=C[epsilon]/(epsilon^2).

Set K=O_(C^(2))^{oplus 2} and Q=O_C direct sum O_(C^(3)). For every global extension class e, consider

\[
0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow F_e\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1}
\]

A lift is an A-flat coherent sheaf on X_A with identified central fibre F_e. The maps, filtration and support in (1) need not lift. The superscript (2) labels a reduced rotation support; K is two copies of its structure sheaf, not a thickening or an ideal square.

## Conclusion

For i=1,3, let D_i be the disjoint union of the four finite elliptic intersection curves of C^(i) and C^(2), and let p_1,p_2,p_3 be their common infinity points. With inclusions understood, the mixed Ext sheaves are

\[
\mathcal Hom_X(O_{C^{(i)}},O_{C^{(2)}})=0,\qquad
\mathcal Ext^1_X(O_{C^{(i)}},O_{C^{(2)}})
\simeq \bigoplus_{D\subset D_i}O_D\ \oplus\
       \bigoplus_{a=1}^3 k(p_a).
\tag{2}
\]

The curve summands are intrinsically the lines N_(D/C^(2)); their displayed trivializations use the base equations. In particular

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(O_{C^{(i)}},O_{C^{(2)}})\simeq\mathbb C^7,
\qquad \operatorname{Ext}^1_X(Q,K)\simeq\mathbb C^{28}.
\tag{3}
\]

These vector-space identifications depend on choices of bases. For every e and every kappa in V_N,

\[
F_e\text{ lifts to }X_A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\kappa\in V_D.
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the Atiyah--Kodaira--Spencer obstruction of each middle sheaf has kernel V_D on V_N and rank one on V_RM. None has a transverse RM lift, including after forgetting the filtration and allowing arbitrary local extension data at infinity.

Its leading Chern character is

\[
\operatorname{ch}_2(F_e)=[C]+2[C^{(2)}]+[C^{(3)}],
\qquad T_{\operatorname{ch}_2(F_e)}|_{T(S)}=U^2-3\operatorname{id}.
\tag{5}
\]

This action is non-scalar. The result stops this class of representatives at first order; it does not obstruct every representative of (5), higher ramification tests, or algebraic Hodge classes on transverse surfaces.

## Proof

**Finite mixed Ext sheaves.** L014 supplies all finite intersections and their coordinates. For i=1 or 3, near any component D of D_i, write

\[
R=\mathbb C\{r,q,z,w\},\quad
M=R/(z,r),\quad N=R/(z,q),
\tag{6}
\]

where r=Q_i(t,s), q=Q_2(t,s), z is the common fibre-coordinate difference, and w is a coordinate along the elliptic curve. The ambient is smooth, both branches are smooth, and r,q are transverse base coordinates. Applying Hom(-,N) to the Koszul resolution of M gives

\[
N\xrightarrow{b\mapsto(0,rb)}N^2
 \xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto-ra}N.
\tag{7}
\]

Multiplication by r is injective on N. Therefore Hom(M,N)=0 and Ext^1_R(M,N)=N/rN. Intrinsically, Hom(I_(C^(i)),O_(C^(2))) near D is O_(C^(2))(D): the relation forces the image of z to vanish, leaving the dual of the Cartier ideal generated by r. Quotienting by O_(C^(2)) identifies the Ext sheaf with N_(D/C^(2)). On the normalization of C^(2), D is an entire smooth elliptic fibre over one finite base value. The function r has a simple zero there and trivializes this normal line along the whole D. Thus its global sections are constants. There are four such curves for each i, disjoint from infinity and from each other. This is the same Koszul framework used in L013, with the new rotation geometry supplied by L014.

**Mixed Ext at infinity: both arguments are singular.** At each p_a, L014 gives linear graph ideals in

\[
R=\mathbb C[[u_1,u_2,w_1,w_2]],\qquad
J_k=(w_1-\zeta^{kd_1}u_1,\ w_2-\zeta^{kd_2}u_2),
\tag{8}
\]

with (d_1,d_2) equal to (6,2), (3,5), or (6,2). All distinct graphs among k=+/-1,+/-2,+/-3 are pairwise transverse. Put

\[
P_\pm=R/J_{\pm i},\quad T_\pm=R/J_{\pm2},\quad
M=R/(J_i\cap J_{-i}),\quad N=R/(J_2\cap J_{-2}),
\]

and write k for the residue field. The exact normalization sequences are

\[
0\to M\to P_+\oplus P_-\to k\to0,\qquad
0\to N\to T_+\oplus T_-\to k\to0,
\tag{9}
\]

with the last maps differences of values at the origin. The following calculation retains the scheme structure of the two-branch supports; replacing the target by a smooth branch would give a different answer.

Transversality and the Koszul resolutions give

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(P_\pm,T_\eta)
=\operatorname{Ext}^1(P_\pm,T_\eta)=0,
\quad
\operatorname{Hom}(k,T_\eta)
=\operatorname{Ext}^1(k,T_\eta)=0,
\quad \operatorname{Ext}^2(k,T_\eta)=k.
\]

The second sequence in (9) consequently identifies Ext^1(k,N)=k and Ext^1(P_\pm,N)=k by their connecting homomorphisms from Hom(k,k) and Hom(P_\pm,k).

The natural map

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^2(k,T_+\oplus T_-)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Ext}^2(k,k)
\tag{10}
\]

is injective. Indeed Ext^2(k,k) is the second exterior power of the ambient tangent space. A Koszul calculation puts the two images on the bivector lines of the tangent planes of T_+ and T_-. In the coordinate order of (8), a generator of the line for J_k is

\[
(1,0,\zeta^{kd_1},0)\wedge(0,1,0,\zeta^{kd_2}).
\]

These two bivectors are linearly independent: their u_1 wedge u_2 coordinates both equal 1, whereas their u_1 wedge w_2 coordinates are distinct for k=2 and -2. This also verifies directly that (10) is not a zero map. The long exact sequence now makes the connecting map

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1(k,k)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Ext}^2(k,N)
\tag{11}
\]

an isomorphism. For P_+ and P_- separately, the analogous connecting map Ext^1(P_\pm,k) -> Ext^2(P_\pm,N) is injective.

Apply Hom(-,N) to the first sequence in (9). The relevant segment is

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1(k,N)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Ext}^1(P_+\oplus P_-,N)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Ext}^1(M,N)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Ext}^2(k,N)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Ext}^2(P_+\oplus P_-,N).
\tag{12}
\]

The first map is k -> k^2, with coordinates 1 and -1 up to the choice of sign in (9). Naturality of the connecting maps identifies the last map, on the subspace (11), with

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1(k,k)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Ext}^1(P_+,k)\oplus\operatorname{Ext}^1(P_-,k)
\tag{13}
\]

followed by the two injections just noted. Map (13) restricts an ambient tangent vector to the two conormal spaces. It is an isomorphism between four-dimensional spaces because P_+ and P_- are transverse: the four linear generators of J_i and J_(-i) form a basis of the ambient cotangent space. Hence the last map in (12) is injective, and

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(M,N)=\operatorname{coker}(k\to k^2)\simeq k.
\tag{14}
\]

The maximal ideal kills this module, so it is one reduced skyscraper contribution, not a larger unknown finite-length module. Completion is faithfully flat for these finite modules; the calculation determines the Ext sheaf at each p_a. It applies for both i=1 and i=3.

**Global extension classes.** The Hom sheaf is zero everywhere. Locally the target is reduced with its displayed smooth branches, none shared with the source. An element of the source ideal can be chosen nonzero on every target branch, and is therefore a nonzerodivisor on the target. It kills any putative Hom image, proving the assertion also at infinity. Off the intersections all mixed Ext sheaves vanish. The calculations (7) and (14), whose supports are disjoint, prove (2).

In the local-to-global Ext spectral sequence, total degree one then has only H^0 of the Ext^1 sheaf; the possible outgoing differential lands in H^2 of the zero Hom sheaf. Every connected complete elliptic curve has only constant global functions. There are four curve summands and three point summands, giving C^7. Additivity in the two quotient summands and the two kernel copies gives (3). The supporting general framework is [Stacks Project, section 20.43, tag 0BQP](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BQP); the values and the infinity maps have been computed here.

**The central modules along the finite curves.** Near a C^(i)--C^(2) curve, (1) restricts to an extension of M by N^2 in (6). Choose kernel generators a_1,a_2 and a lift b of 1 in M. The relations can be written

\[
za_j=qa_j=0,\qquad zb=0,\qquad
rb=c_1a_1+c_2a_2.
\tag{15}
\]

The Koszul relation forces zb=0; changing b removes terms in c_j divisible by r. By (2), the vector (c_1,c_2) on the entire curve is constant in the base trivialization. If it vanishes, the module is M direct sum N^2 on neighbourhoods of that curve. Otherwise one entry is a unit locally, a kernel basis change reduces the vector to (1,0), and elimination gives

\[
F_e\simeq U\oplus N,\qquad U=R/(z,rq).
\tag{16}
\]

At a C--C^(3) finite curve, C^(2) is absent by L014's no-triple-intersection result. There F_e is the direct sum of the two reduced branch sheaves. The punctual extension coordinates in (14) do not change any of these statements away from infinity.

**Graph centres for separated modules.** Suppose F_e has a lift F_A. Its support has finite first projection: the projection is proper, and its central topological support is a finite union of finite covers of S; a nilpotent thickening does not add positive-dimensional fibres. On a small disk in a dense open of the first elliptic base, avoid branch values, all intersection values, singular fibres and infinity. The central support separates into graph sheets. The module has rank one on the C and C^(3) sheets and rank two on the C^(2) sheets. These statements concern modules; F_A is not being made into a quotient algebra.

Restrict to each separated sheet and push forward by the first projection. A-flatness and the locally free central module make the pushforward a vector bundle E_A of rank m=1 or 2 on S_A over the disk, by the local flatness criterion. Every function from the second factor acts on E_A and reduces to a scalar, the function pulled back by the central graph. Its normalized trace defines a morphism of algebras to O_(S_A). To check multiplicativity, work in a local basis and write two such endomorphisms as

\[
f\,I+\epsilon B,\qquad g\,I+\epsilon D
\]

for arbitrary lifts f,g of their central scalars. Their product is fg I+epsilon(fD+gB), whose normalized trace equals the product of their normalized traces. The term involving BD has order epsilon^2 and vanishes. This also proves independence of scalar lifts. Trace is invariant under change of basis, so the maps glue and respect changes of second-factor coordinates. They define a graph map g_A from the complete elliptic surface over the chosen disk to the second S_A. For m=1 this is the usual support graph. For m=2 it is a centre obtained from trace; no splitting of the two copies in F_A is required.

Each centre sends complete smooth elliptic fibres to fibres. Its second base-coordinate function has central reduction coming from the first base. Subtract a lifted base function; its epsilon coefficient is a central function on the elliptic surface, hence again comes from the base because the proper connected fibres have only constant functions. Its fibre map reduces to an isomorphism, so is an isomorphism over A as well, by the finite rank-one argument for a map of smooth proper curves. The fibre j-invariants consequently agree.

Use the NS-fixed elliptic pencil from L008 and write its j-map as

\[
J_A(t)=J_0(t)+\epsilon\,dJ(t),\qquad
J_0=R\circ P_a.
\tag{17}
\]

We have proved J_A(t)=J_A(s) on each graph centre over the dense complete-fibre locus. The j-invariance input, also used in L014, is [Schuett--Shioda, *Elliptic Surfaces*, section 2.6 and Theorem 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.0298#page=5). Over A it follows from the same origin translation and short-Weierstrass scaling; equal j-invariants are used only as a necessary condition. We do not assert that their difference annihilates F_A or its entire scheme-theoretic support.

**An arbitrary unfiltered lift at a nonsplit crossing.** Use (16) in a trivialized smooth ambient chart. The local lift facts proved in L013 apply: an A-flat lift determines an element of Ext^1_R(U direct sum N,U direct sum N). Each diagonal block is a self-extension, hence itself an A-flat lift of that summand by letting epsilon be the composite of the quotient and inclusion. A lift of a cyclic central module is cyclic by Nakayama and thus has an A-flat annihilator quotient. Consequently the U,U and N,N blocks give respective ideals

\[
(z-\epsilon\alpha,\ rq-\epsilon\beta),\qquad
(z-\epsilon\gamma,\ q-\epsilon\delta),
\tag{18}
\]

where alpha,beta are regular modulo (z,rq) and gamma,delta are regular modulo (z,q). L007's ideal parametrization or the two regular sequences give these forms. The two off-diagonal blocks of the original lift are allowed and have not been discarded.

On the punctured C^(i) branch, q is invertible and N restricts to zero. The actual rank-one displacement from F_A is therefore that of the U,U block:

\[
\delta r=\frac{\beta|_M}{q},\qquad \delta z=\alpha|_M.
\tag{19}
\]

On the punctured C^(2) branch, r is invertible and U direct sum N restricts to two copies of the smooth graph. The trace centre of the actual lift is

\[
\delta q=\frac12\left(\frac{\beta|_N}{r}+\delta\right),\qquad
\delta z=\frac12(\alpha|_N+\gamma).
\tag{20}
\]

To see why arbitrary mixed blocks do not change (20), use the first projection as local coordinates on this unramified branch. A normal equation acts on the rank-two pushforward by epsilon times a two-by-two matrix. Its diagonal entries are the normal displacements of the two self-extension blocks in (18); mixed blocks supply only off-diagonal entries, whose trace is zero. A central change of basis conjugates this matrix, preserving its trace. Thus (20) is the centre of the given lift, not an assumed split lift. Normal displacement is independent of tangential reparametrization, so the formulas remain valid when r,q,w are not the first-projection coordinate system.

Put mu=beta|D. Equations (19)--(20) show that the two base-normal residues are mu and mu/2; their fibre-normal displacements are regular. These are first-order module trace identities, distinct from the rank-three algebra calculation for an ordinary square.

**The j-relation kills the residues.** At a mixed curve over an ordered critical pair (p_(e,k),p_(e,l)), L014's Dickson factorization gives

\[
J_0(t)-J_0(s)=H(t,s)\,r q,\qquad H|_D=H_D\ne0.
\tag{21}
\]

The two critical-point derivatives J_0'(p_(e,k)) and J_0'(p_(e,l)) vanish. Write d=dJ(p_(e,k))-dJ(p_(e,l)), a scalar along D. The first-order j-identities proved on the dense open extend as identities for the meromorphic normal displacements (19)--(20) on the branches. At D their products in (21) are regular because the poles are at most simple. Restricting gives

\[
d+H_D\mu=0,\qquad d+\tfrac12 H_D\mu=0.
\tag{22}
\]

Regular parts of (19)--(20) are multiplied by q or r and vanish on D. Changes of tangential coordinates have no effect, since the central function in (21) vanishes on each branch. Subtracting (22) forces mu=0 and d=0. In particular the rank-one branch in (19) extends regularly across the whole mixed curve.

If the central extension vector vanishes on a mixed curve, take the M,M block of the arbitrary lift of M direct sum N^2 instead. It already gives a regular lift of C^(i) across that curve. No trace comparison or lifting of the filtration is necessary there. At each C--C^(3) curve the corresponding self-extension block of their direct sum likewise extends C regularly. These arguments include every possible e by (15).

**Recovering C, including its three singular points.** Away from all other supports, F_e is O_C and the cyclic-support construction gives an embedded lift of C. At each mixed curve with nonzero extension vector, mu=0 makes beta|_M divisible by q; (19) is a regular normal section and defines an embedded lift of the smooth C branch. At split mixed curves and at C--C^(3) curves use the regular block lifts just described. All these lifts agree off the intersection curves. On any overlap, their difference is a regular section of the normal bundle of the smooth C branch, zero on a dense open, so it is zero everywhere. They therefore glue on C minus {p_1,p_2,p_3}.

No condition on the point coordinates of e is needed to fill these punctures. L008 proves N_C=nu_*N_j with the normalization W smooth and N_j locally free. In a local ambient trivialization at p_a, L007 identifies an embedded first-order lift on the punctured neighbourhood with a section of N_C there. Removing p_a removes just two points on W, and Hartogs extends that vector-bundle section uniquely. The ideal parametrization then supplies a flat embedded C lift on the entire neighbourhood. Uniqueness glues it to the previously recovered lift. Proper GAGA over A applies if the constructions use analytic charts.

This extends C, not an arbitrary annihilator or Fitting support of F_A at infinity. The nonzero point contributions in (14) were retained; any actual sheaf lift, with any of those coordinates, has already furnished the punctured C lift to which Hartogs applies. L008 now implies kappa in V_D. This proves the necessary direction of (4) for all global extensions, without lifting a quotient or a submodule of (1).

**Every extension class lifts in a Dickson-family direction.** For the converse, vary the Dickson parameters, producing flat reduced supports C^(i)_A for kappa in V_D. Their four finite mixed curves are smooth elliptic fibres, with the same relative Koszul model (6)--(7). At infinity the simultaneous finite-group linearization from L014 makes all six graph branches the fixed planes (8) over the parameter base. Hence the whole calculation (9)--(14) works after tensoring by A; its injective maps have unit minors and its cokernel is one copy of A on each point section.

The relative Hom sheaf is zero. Locally, a nonzerodivisor from the source ideal on the central target stays a nonzerodivisor on its A-flat lift, which proves this directly. Thus the relative Ext^1 sheaf is the sum of four relative normal lines and three copies of O_(p_a,A). The normal lines are trivial after choosing bases in their lifted base tangent lines. Each complete smooth elliptic D_A has H^0(O_(D_A))=A: reduce a global function to a constant, subtract a lifted constant, and identify the remaining epsilon coefficient with a central global function. The relative local-to-global spectral sequence therefore gives

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_{X_A}(O_{C^{(i)}_A},O_{C^{(2)}_A})\simeq A^7.
\tag{23}
\]

Its reduction onto the C^7 in (3) is surjective. This uses the displayed relative computations, not an automatic base-change claim for Ext. Additivity gives a surjection A^28 -> C^28 for (1). Lift any e; its extension has A-flat submodule and quotient, hence an A-flat middle sheaf with the required central fibre. This proves the sufficient direction of (4).

**Atiyah obstruction, represented action and scope.** On the smooth projective X, F_e is perfect. The criterion imported from [Huybrechts--Thomas, arXiv:0805.3527v2, Corollary 3.4, p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.3527v2#page=14) is

\[
o_{F_e}(\kappa)=(\operatorname{id}_{F_e}\otimes\kappa_X)
                   \circ\operatorname{At}(F_e)\in\operatorname{Ext}^2_X(F_e,F_e).
\tag{24}
\]

Its vanishing is equivalent to a perfect lift. Use the absolute-over-C formulation confirmed by their [2014 erratum, pp. 561--563](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00208-013-0999-x.pdf), as checked in the saved assessment. The NS-fixed ample class makes X_A projective and supplies the smooth ambient embedding required there. L013 proves that a perfect lift of a sheaf over these dual numbers is an A-flat sheaf lift, and conversely. Thus (24) detects exactly (4). Linearity in kappa and the dimensions in L008 give the claimed three-dimensional kernel and rank one on V_RM.

Additivity of the Chern character in (1) gives the first formula in (5). For each reduced rotation support, normalization has generic degree one and differs from its structure sheaf only at the three points; Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch gives leading term [C^(i)], while point corrections start in degree eight. This is the same leading-term argument used in L013. The rotation actions recorded in L015 are U, U^2-2 id, and -U^2-U+id. Their weighted sum with coefficients 1,2,1 is U^2-3 id. The irreducible cubic minimal polynomial of U excludes a rational quadratic relation, so this is non-scalar. The identity concerns action on T(S), not a Chow relation.

The attained kernel has dimension three for all 28 extension parameters, against four RM directions required. This is an informative negative result for the stated sheaves; the known 21-dimensional cycle span still covers only the Dickson family. Higher-order lifting and algebraization would remain necessary after a successful first-order test of another representative. Arbitrary primitive fourfold classes and the universal rational Hodge conjecture remain unresolved.

The supporting command

checks the two connecting-map rank conditions in (10)--(13) at all three infinity points by exact cyclotomic arithmetic, the independent residue coefficients, and the action in (5). It does not verify global Ext gluing, trace-centre geometry, support recovery, or the reverse kernel inclusion; those informal proofs are above.
