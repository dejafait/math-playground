# L012 — A sufficiently negative second syzygy retains the cubic obstruction

## Hypotheses

Let S, X=S x S, C, V_N, V_RM, and V_D satisfy L008, and let I=I_C. In particular C is the reduced cubic correspondence support, smooth except at its three transverse surface double points. Work over A=C[epsilon]/(epsilon^2). For kappa in V_N, let X_A=S_A x_A S_A be the corresponding product deformation.

Fix an ample line bundle L on S and put H=p_1^*L tensor p_2^*L, h=c_1(H). Write G(k)=G tensor H^k. Choose m>0 so I(m) is generated by global sections and H^i(X,I(m))=0 for every i>0. Fix a finite surjection F_0=H^(-m)^{a_0} -> I and denote its kernel by K. Next choose n>m so K(n) is generated by global sections and H^i(X,K(n))=0 for every i>0. Fix a finite surjection F_1=H^(-n)^{a_1} -> K and put E=ker(F_1 -> K). Thus

\[
0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow F_0\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
0\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow F_1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1}
\]

The two choices exist by Serre global generation and Serre vanishing. No stability of E or minimality of these presentations is assumed. A lift of E means a vector bundle on X_A with identified special fibre E; its presentation maps are not initially part of the lifted data.

## Conclusion

E is locally free, and its second Chern class satisfies

\[
c_2(E)=[C]+\frac{(a_0m-a_1n)^2-a_1n^2+a_0m^2}{2}\,h^2.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently c_2(E) has the same action U on T(S) as C. For every kappa in V_N,

\[
E\text{ lifts to }X_A
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C\text{ has a flat embedded lift to }X_A.
\tag{3}
\]

Let

\[
o_E(\kappa)
=(\mathrm{id}_E\otimes\kappa_X)\circ\operatorname{At}(E)
\in\operatorname{Ext}^2_X(E,E),
\tag{4}
\]

where kappa_X is the product Kodaira–Spencer class and
At(E) belongs to Ext^1_X(E,E tensor Omega_X^1). Then

\[
\ker(o_E|_{V_N})=V_D,\qquad
\dim\ker(o_E|_{V_{\rm RM}})=3,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(o_E|_{V_{\rm RM}})=1.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus forgetting the presentation maps does not remove the missing fourth-direction obstruction for any E constructed under these hypotheses. This is not an obstruction for all bundles with the same c_2, all other cycle representatives, or the rational Hodge class.

## Proof

**The second syzygy is a vector bundle.** At a smooth point of C its ideal is generated by a regular sequence of length two, so I has projective dimension one there; outside C it is free of rank one. At each remaining point, L007 and L008 give the completed ring R=C[[r_1,r_2,s_1,s_2]] and ideal I_p=(r_i s_j). The sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow R/I_p\longrightarrow
R/(r_1,r_2)\oplus R/(s_1,s_2)
\longrightarrow\mathbb C\longrightarrow0
\tag{6}
\]

is exact, with the last map the difference of constant terms. The middle module has depth two and the last module has depth zero. The depth lemma gives depth(R/I_p)=1; the ideal sequence then gives depth(I_p)=2. The Auslander–Buchsbaum formula over the regular local ring of dimension four gives pd_R(I_p)=2. Completion preserves the needed depth calculation and detects local freeness. A supporting precise reference for the formula is [Stacks Project, Proposition 10.111.1, tag 090U](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/090U).

It follows that I has local projective dimension at most two everywhere. In any two successive presentations by free modules, the second kernel is projective locally: dimension shifting identifies its Ext^1 against any module with Ext^3 of I, which is zero. A finite projective module over a local ring is free. Applied to (1), this proves that E is locally free, including at all three double points.

**The Chern class still represents the desired action.** The exact sequences give

\[
[E]=[F_1]-[F_0]+[I]
     =[F_1]-[F_0]+[\mathcal O_X]-[\mathcal O_C]
\tag{7}
\]

in K_0(X). Here ch_0(O_C)=ch_1(O_C)=0 and ch_2(O_C)=[C]. For completeness these leading terms also follow directly from the named Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem in the present geometry. L008's smooth normalization nu:W -> C has cokernel O_C -> nu_*O_W equal to one copy of C at each double point, by (6). With j:W -> X its finite composition, the theorem gives

\[
\operatorname{ch}(j_*\mathcal O_W)\operatorname{td}(X)
=j_*\operatorname{td}(W).
\]

The right side starts in cohomological degree four with j_*1=[C], since the generic degree is one by L007. Applying the same theorem to each point shows that the normalization cokernel contributes only in degree eight. This proves the stated leading terms without a smoothness assumption on C.

Expanding (7) gives

\[
c_1(E)=(a_0m-a_1n)h,\qquad
\operatorname{ch}_2(E)=-[C]+\frac{a_1n^2-a_0m^2}{2}h^2.
\]

The identity ch_2=(c_1^2-2c_2)/2 proves (2). If ell=c_1(L), then h^2=p_1^*(ell^2)+2p_1^*ell\,p_2^*ell+p_2^*(ell^2). Its first and last terms act trivially on H^2(S) by degree, and its middle term acts on t in T(S) by 2q(t,ell)ell=0. By L006 and L007 the action of [C] is U. This proves the assertion about c_2.

**The vanishings that recover the forgotten maps.** Kunneth and H^1(S,O_S)=0 give

\[
\omega_X\simeq\mathcal O_X,\qquad
H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=H^3(X,\mathcal O_X)=0.
\tag{8}
\]

Twisting the second sequence in (1) by H^n, its long exact sequence contains

\[
H^2(X,K(n))\longrightarrow H^3(X,E(n))
\longrightarrow H^3(X,\mathcal O_X)^{a_1}.
\]

Both outer groups vanish, so H^3(X,E(n))=0. Twisting the first sequence by H^m similarly gives H^3(X,K(m))=0, using H^2(X,I(m))=0.

Serre duality on the smooth projective fourfold, in its Ext form and using omega_X=O_X, now gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(E,F_1)
&\simeq\operatorname{Ext}^3_X(F_1,E)^*
 =H^3(X,E(n))^{*\,a_1}=0,\\
\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(K,F_0)
&\simeq\operatorname{Ext}^3_X(F_0,K)^*
 =H^3(X,K(m))^{*\,a_0}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Duality applies to K even though K need not be locally free: it is perfect by the local projective-dimension bound, and the argument uses global Ext rather than a sheaf-Hom shortcut. The duality for perfect complexes is supported by [Stacks Project, Lemma 57.4.1, tag 0FY7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FY7), with the smooth fourfold's dualizing complex O_X[4].

The NS-preserving condition extends L to L_A on S_A, hence H to H_A on X_A. This is the line-bundle obstruction kappa contracted with c_1(L), which vanishes in V_N, as used in L008. Fix the corresponding bundles F_{0,A} and F_{1,A}.

Suppose E_A is any lift of E. The first vanishing in (9) lifts E -> F_1 to a morphism E_A -> F_{1,A}. One way to see this precisely is to take the short exact sequence of Hom sheaves obtained by reduction modulo epsilon and its H^0-to-H^1 connecting map; the obstruction group is Ext^1_X(E,F_1). The reduction of the lifted map is injective. Since source and target are A-flat, the map is injective and its cokernel K_A is A-flat, with special fibre K.

The second vanishing in (9) then lifts K -> F_0 to K_A -> F_{0,A}. This assertion does not treat K_A as a bundle. It has the two-term locally free resolution [E_A -> F_{1,A}]. Apply Hom(-,F_{0,A}) to that complex and take a Cech complex for an affine cover. Reduction gives a short exact sequence of these total complexes whose kernel is the corresponding complex on X. The H^0-to-H^1 boundary lands in Ext^1_X(K,F_0)=0. Their H^0 groups are the actual Hom groups for K_A and K, so the required sheaf morphism lifts. Its reduction is injective, hence it too is injective with A-flat cokernel, denoted Q_A. We have constructed

\[
0\longrightarrow E_A\longrightarrow F_{1,A}
\longrightarrow F_{0,A}\longrightarrow Q_A\longrightarrow0,
\qquad Q_A\otimes_A\mathbb C\simeq I.
\tag{10}
\]

For clarity, the flatness assertion just used is elementary over A. If a map f:M -> N of A-flat modules has injective reduction, an element in its kernel is epsilon times another element; reducing the equation for the latter shows it is again epsilon times an element, so the original element is zero. If epsilon n=f(m), injectivity after reduction forces m=epsilon m_1; A-flatness of N then gives n-f(m_1) in epsilon N. Thus ker(epsilon) on the cokernel equals its image under epsilon, which is the flatness criterion over A.

**A sheaf lift of the ideal recovers an embedded lift.** This is a necessary separate step: (10) alone does not specify an inclusion Q_A -> O_{X_A}. Let U=X minus C, and let u:U_A -> X_A be the open subset with this underlying complement. On U_A, the A-flat sheaf Q_A is a line bundle because its reduction is O_U. Locally, lift a generator of O_U to obtain O_{U_A} -> Q_A; Nakayama gives surjectivity, and A-flatness makes its kernel zero.

Define the line bundle

\[
D_A=\det(F_{0,A})\otimes\det(F_{1,A})^{-1}\otimes\det(E_A).
\tag{11}
\]

On U_A all short exact sequences in (10) have locally free terms, so the elementary determinant identities identify D_A|_{U_A} with Q_A|_{U_A}. On X, its reduction D is trivial: its restriction to U is the canonical determinant of I|_U=O_U, and a line bundle on the smooth X trivialized off a codimension-two subset is trivial on X. Indeed that rational trivialization has neither zeros nor poles along any divisor, and regularity of X extends it and its inverse.

The exact sequence of unit sheaves

\[
1\longrightarrow1+\epsilon\mathcal O_X
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{X_A}^{\,*}
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_X^{\,*}\longrightarrow1
\]

identifies the kernel of Pic(X_A) -> Pic(X) with a quotient of H^1(X,O_X), which is zero by (8). Therefore D_A is trivial. Choose its trivialization to reduce to the one just specified for D. This identifies Q_A|_{U_A} with O_{U_A}, compatibly with the usual central identification.

Hartogs extension here gives

\[
u_*\mathcal O_{U_A}=\mathcal O_{X_A}.
\tag{12}
\]

To check its validity over the nonreduced base, work locally on a smooth affine chart of X. Its first-order ambient deformation is trivial, by formal smoothness, so the structure sheaf is O_X direct sum epsilon O_X as an O_X-module. Each summand has the ordinary Hartogs property across C, of codimension two in the smooth X. Extension on both summands gives (12), and the natural map makes these local identifications glue. The central normal-domain Hartogs property is supported by [Stacks Project, Lemma 15.24.18, tag 0AVB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AVB); the two-layer argument is what supplies the nilpotent version used here.

Adjunction now supplies

\[
Q_A\longrightarrow u_*(Q_A|_{U_A})
\simeq u_*\mathcal O_{U_A}=\mathcal O_{X_A}.
\tag{13}
\]

Its reduction is the usual inclusion I -> O_X: this holds on U by construction and hence everywhere, since a morphism into the torsion-free O_X is determined on that dense open. Both sheaves in (13) are A-flat, so the elementary argument following (10) proves injectivity and flatness of its cokernel. The image is an ideal J_A with flat quotient and reduction I. Thus it defines a flat embedded lift C_A. This proves the forward implication in (3) without assuming a support or a presentation in the initial E_A data.

**The converse lifts the chosen presentations.** Suppose C_A is an embedded lift. Its ideal I_A is A-flat, as the kernel of O_{X_A} -> O_{C_A}. The vanishing

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(F_0,I)=H^1(X,I(m))^{a_0}=0
\]

lifts the chosen quotient F_0 -> I to F_{0,A} -> I_A. Reduction is surjective, so Nakayama gives surjectivity over A. Its kernel K_A is A-flat with central fibre K. Similarly,

\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(F_1,K)=H^1(X,K(n))^{a_1}=0
\]

lifts F_1 -> K to a surjection F_{1,A} -> K_A. Its kernel E_A is A-flat and reduces to the locally free E. An A-flat coherent sheaf on the flat X_A with locally free central fibre is locally free: lift a local basis, use Nakayama for surjectivity, then use A-flatness and reduction to make the kernel zero. Hence E_A is a vector bundle lifting the original E. This proves (3).

**The Atiyah obstruction and the threshold.** The standard Atiyah–Kodaira–Spencer obstruction theorem says that (4) vanishes exactly when E lifts. A precise supporting citation is Huybrechts–Thomas, [Deformation-obstruction theory for complexes via Atiyah and Kodaira–Spencer classes, Corollary 3.4, arXiv version p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.3527#page=14). Its hypotheses hold here: X_A is projective over A because H_A is ample, hence has an embedding in a smooth complex ambient space; E is perfect and X is smooth. The classical Atiyah class is the relevant truncated class on this smooth central fibre. A perfect lift whose derived central restriction is a vector bundle is itself a vector bundle locally, by cancelling split terms in a local finite free complex and applying Nakayama. Thus the theorem's perfect-complex criterion gives exactly the bundle criterion in use, with no simplicity assumption needed.

Combining that criterion with (3) and L008 yields ker(o_E|V_N)=V_D. The map (4) is linear in kappa. Since L008 gives dim V_RM=4 and dim V_D=3, rank-nullity proves (5).

The achieved kernel dimension is therefore three, against the four needed to extend this representative in every RM tangent direction. The c_2 class retains its required Hodge type throughout V_RM, since (2) differs from [C] by an NS-fixed divisor product, but this does not annihilate its bundle obstruction. No higher-order lifting or algebraization conclusion is inferred from first-order cohomology. The 21-dimensional algebraic span already obtained on the known family has not been extended to a new surface. The construction's negative result uses the specified vanishing hypotheses; it does not cover an arbitrary vector-bundle representative or other sheaves carrying the same class.
