# Weak discriminant control does not force the distinct-zero count

The approved generic implication is refuted by
[L371](../lemmas/L371-weak-discriminant-unbounded-distinct-zero-deficit.md).
The pair is real entire and satisfies both required differential signs
everywhere, with exactly verified zeros and endpoints.

WHY IT FAILS: Common double zeros can supply two units of multiplicity
but only one distinct zero. The Wronskian vanishes at those shared zeros,
so a separation argument that assumes a nonzero Wronskian across them
loses an unbounded number of distinct zeros. L371 exhibits precisely
this behavior with an unbounded deficit after every finite increase of
onset. Stop the generic implication with its approved hypotheses;
neither a no-common-zero variant nor a multiplicity count proves it.
This is no obstruction to RH itself and no proof about actual-theta
common zeros. A future actual-family route needs independently justified
additional information. The separate arithmetic source blocker and
L365 fixed-regulator stop remain preserved.
