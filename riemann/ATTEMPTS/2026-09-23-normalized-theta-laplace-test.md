# Normalized actual-theta Laplace test — 2026-09-23

The gap is unconditional positivity of all actual reciprocal-zero mixed forms. Three mechanisms were considered: a positive Laplace representation in x=e^(2u); a sum-of-squares decomposition for the associated kernels; and a sign-preserving recurrence exploiting the exact theta differential identity. The first has an immediately discriminating derivative test. The second must improve on the previously screened unproved positive-definiteness premise. The third must overcome the signed logarithmic recurrence already audited. Neither of those other mechanisms was investigated in this step.

The chosen intermediate target was complete monotonicity of F(x)=x^(−5/4)K((log x)/2), which removes the common power from the exact theta summands. A positive measure could potentially support a new integral treatment of logarithmic forms, but that downstream transfer was explicitly unresolved. Success meant proving all signed derivatives nonnegative and then examining the transfer; a rigorous negative derivative meant abandoning this particular representation. Searches of the existing lemmas and attempts found no duplicate test for this F. The comparison-kernel obstruction and the source's coefficient complete-monotonicity claims concern different statements.

[L232](../lemmas/L232-normalized-theta-kernel-is-not-completely-monotone.md) proves eventual negative signed derivatives at x=k/π by isolating the n=1 term and exponentially bounding the entire n≥2 tail. This is evidence using the actual theta series, not another generic shape counterexample. It excludes a positive Laplace representation for this normalization even after restricting to arbitrarily large x.

**WHY IT FAILS.** The polynomial prefactor creates a negative high derivative near x=k/π, while the remaining theta modes are too small to compensate. Complete monotonicity requires every derivative sign, so eventual failure decisively stops this target. This does not exclude other normalizations or supply a negative actual Hankel form. No bound toward the all-degree RH criterion improves. A materially different next test is to derive the Fourier-side identity for the first associated kernel and determine exactly what signed term prevents an autocorrelation proof of positive definiteness.
