**WHY IT FAILS.** Transfer to A makes zero-order b factors into origin
mass 1/2. Spreading every such mass with the actual normalized profile
A(u)cosh(u/2), including the h-origin and all intersection masses,
preserves the entire numerator. But when one variable tends to infinity
the retained h-derivative subtraction has order -t exp(sqrt(h)t),
while its positive h zeroth factor has order exp(sqrt(h)t) and every
other placement is polynomial times exp(t/2). Full symmetrization
therefore leaves a strictly negative leading coefficient at both
orders. This defeats this specific pointwise payment rule, not the
integrated inequality or RH. Preserve the distant term; repeating
the same algebra or changing labels supplies no repair.

The negative region can have tiny actual weight. An integrated estimate
or a genuinely different payment profile needs a new scoped assessment.
