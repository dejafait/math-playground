# Endpoint functional-equation test — 2026-09-24

The gap is L302's positive arithmetic margin beyond (1+r)²exp(−r/256). The intermediate target was an exact contour shift for L303 followed by a quantitative dual-sum comparison at r=2n, X=exp(2r), a=sqrt(4π²X²−25). A smaller or sign-controlled dual term could support a residue-based lower bound; reproduction of the original signed sum would stop that shortcut. Existing L297–L306 tests did not include this transform. All existing uncommitted work was preserved.

[L307](../lemmas/L307-endpoint-functional-equation-self-reproduction.md) completes the test, including the three possible residues, absolutely convergent dual expansion, uniform gamma-factor comparison, tails and cutoff displacement. Its error O(sqrt(r)(2e)^(−r)) is smaller than the assembled endpoint error. The dual is −S_r(a_n) to that precision; the central residue is 2S_r(a_n) to the same precision. This is NEGATIVE evidence for residue dominance, not a negative value or proof that S_r is large.

WHY IT FAILS: the dual cutoff is Y=X−25/(4π²X), almost exactly the original cutoff. Even-order left-line inversion contributes a minus sign, so the functional equation returns the same unknown signed arithmetic sum rather than a lower-order correction. The remote residues are negligible and the central Taylor coefficient has no established sign. A new lower bound for the latter remains possible, but the transformation alone supplies no such estimate. The canonical proof is L307; no derivation is duplicated here.

The endpoint lower bound, smaller indices and bounded heights remain unresolved. After these distinct endpoint certificate failures, the next direction addresses the independent bounded-height gap: an upper variance estimate could reduce a compact-height all-index problem to a finite check.
