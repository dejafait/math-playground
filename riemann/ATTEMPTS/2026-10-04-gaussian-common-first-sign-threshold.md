# Common Gaussian global-first-sign threshold — stopped 2026-10-04

The proposed certificate was one epsilon_0>0 such that L357's
Gaussian-regulated approximants satisfy F_epsilon'^2-F_epsilon
F_epsilon''>=0 at every real frequency for every
0<epsilon<=epsilon_0. Together with L357's uniform absolute-error
limit, that certificate could have supplied the actual-theta first
sign, leaving higher low-index and mixed-form conditions unresolved.

**WHY IT FAILS.**
[L365](../lemmas/L365-matveev-forces-small-regulator-negative-signs.md)
imports the screened Matveev bound, specializes it to prime logarithms,
and meets L364's joint shrinking-window return inequalities. It gives
an actual negative sign in every sufficiently high dyadic interval
at each fixed sufficiently small regulator. The onset can depend on
the regulator, which suffices to contradict the proposed common
threshold. The earlier six finite examples alone did not settle this
quantifier. This stops the stated global certificate, not every use
of regulated approximations: a restricted-frequency comparison would
need its own quantitative error and height relation. The result
supplies neither a negative actual-theta sign nor an off-line zero;
the absolute approximation error has no controlled ratio to the
gamma-envelope sign margin at these escaping frequencies.
