## Inspected primary statements and applicability

1. J. Borcea, S. Friedland and B. Shapiro, *Parametric Poincare-Perron
   theorem with applications*, J. Analyse Math.113(2011),197–225,
   [DOI10.1007/s11854-011-0004-0, author PDF](https://staff.math.su.se/shapiro/Articles/Poincare-Perron.pdf#page=2).
   Read Theorems2–5, pp198–200, and the start of the proof discussion
   pp206–208. Theorem2 assumes convergent monic recurrence coefficients
   and characteristic roots of distinct moduli; it gives eventual zero
   or a root-valued ratio limit. Realization of each root additionally
   requires every last coefficient nonzero. Theorem3 still assumes
   a constant matrix plus a perturbation tending to zero. Theorems4–5
   allow a singular limiting matrix, but require invertible matrices
   at each step, convergence (locally uniform for parameters), and a
   unique simple dominant eigenvalue with positive spectral radius.
   Thus a singular limit is different from an arbitrary singular
   relation at individual times. No such normalized convergent system
   has been established for the exact c_n. These homogeneous results
   also do not supply the requested forced inverse bound. No theorem
   from this paper is imported or reproved for T.

2. L. Berezansky and E. Braverman, *On Bohl-Perron type theorems for
   linear difference equations*, Functional Differential Equations
   11(2004),19–28,
   [journal PDF, article starts at PDF page21](https://www.ariel.ac.il/wp/fde/wp-content/uploads/sites/97/2020/02/2004-1-2.pdf#page=21).
   Read equation(1)/preliminaries p20, Lemmas3–4 p22, Theorem1 and
   its complete proof pp23–25. The explicit system is
   x(n+1)-x(n)=sum_(k=0)^n B(n,k)x(k)+g(n).
   Assume sup_n sum_k ||B(n,k)||<infinity and bounded memory
   B(n,k)=0 when n-k>T. If every bounded g has a bounded solution
   with zero initial condition, then ||X(n,k)||<=M exp(-lambda(n-k))
   uniformly for n>=k, for some M,lambda>0. The proof obtains a
   bounded inverse on the zero-initial-data space, makes a small
   exponential-weight perturbation, and separately verifies uniformity
   in the starting index. This bounded-forcing hypothesis is essential
   to that argument; coefficient bounds alone are not the theorem.
   Here neither an explicit bounded recurrence nor that admissibility
   property is established; the target allows arbitrary initial data
   and assumes a given x has finite exponential growth. The source
   therefore does not decide review267's implication. Only Theorem1
   is assessed; later application claims in this volume are not used.

3. The same authors, *New Stability Conditions for Linear Difference
   Equations using Bohl-Perron Type Theorems*,
   [arXiv0906.3239v1,17June2009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.3239v1#page=2),
   assumptions(a1)–(a2), solution formula(4), Lemma1, pp2–3.
   Bounded coefficients and bounded delays are assumed throughout.
   Lemma1 additionally requires an exponentially stable comparison
   equation and a strict l-infinity operator-norm bound
   ||C_1(L_1-L)||<1. Formula(4) retains the homogeneous initial-data
   term as well as the forcing. No stable comparison or strict bound
   is available locally. Normalization ||c_n||_1=1 alone is not that
   condition. The source does not remove the need to treat homogeneous
   solutions, and no reproduction of its result is proposed.

4. S. Jitomirskaya and C. A. Marx, *Continuity of the Lyapunov Exponent
   for Analytic Quasi-Periodic Cocycles with Singularities*,
   [arXiv1106.6097v1,30June2011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.6097v1#page=2).
   Read definitions and Theorem1.2, equations(1.5)–(1.8), pp1–2;
   Lemma2.9 with proof and Theorem3.1, pp7–8, including the proof's
   initial singular-determinant cutoff case. The setting is a fixed
   one-frequency Diophantine rotation, analytic 2-by-2 matrix functions
   extending to a fixed complex strip, with determinant not identically
   zero; zeros at some phases are allowed. Theorem1.2 concerns
   continuity of Lyapunov exponents. Theorem3.1 controls measures of
   exceptional phase sets, not every prescribed phase. Lemma2.9 treats
   integrability/continuity of the logarithmic determinant through
   factorization at its zeros. Neither continuity nor such integrability
   is an all-index inverse estimate. L990 supplies neither this exact
   analytic representation nor its frequency hypotheses; its leading
   two-phase formula with additive error is not such a representation.
   No Lyapunov exponent or its sign for T is inferred.

5. A. Czornik, K. Kitzing and S. Siegmund, *The new notion of Bohl
   dichotomy for nonautonomous difference equations and its relation
   to exponential dichotomy*,
   [arXiv2305.16408v2,6February2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16408v2#page=2).
   Read section1, pp2–3, equations(1)–(5) and the standing definition
   of a Lyapunov sequence. Their framework assumes every coefficient
   matrix invertible and both the matrix sequence and its inverse
   bounded. Definitions distinguish uniform exponential dichotomy
   from vector-dependent Bohl constants. These hypotheses have not
   been supplied for a companion representation of T. The paper's
   main theorem and perturbation machinery were not assessed or used.
