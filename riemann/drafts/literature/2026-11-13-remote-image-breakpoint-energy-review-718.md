### Primary numerical-analysis inputs actually inspected

**Analytic Gauss error.** Lloyd N. Trefethen, *Is Gauss Quadrature Better
than Clenshaw-Curtis?*, SIAM Review50(1)(2008),67-87,
[author PDF](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/~trefethen/CC.pdf),
Theorem4.5/(4.14), p77. With the paper's n+1 nodes, a continuous
function on[-1,1] analytic and bounded by M in the Bernstein ellipse
whose semiaxes sum to rho>1 has error at most

    64 M / [15(1-rho^(-2)) rho^(2n+2)], n>=0.

Read(2.2)'s indexing, Theorem4.1/proof pp73-74, the analytic cases
(4.7)/(4.11) pp75-76, and the Gaussian proof completion pp79-80:
polynomial exactness eliminates the initial coefficients, odd terms
integrate to zero, and the even tail is bounded using(5.6). A screenshot
of p77 confirms the exponent/indexing. The partial proof's printed
negative denominator is not used. Later use may restrict to n>=1.
No target-specific ellipse, bound M or rescaling is supplied here.
This is not a theorem for a panel containing a jump or an unresolved
pole. It concerns exact quadrature; node/value rounding is additional.
