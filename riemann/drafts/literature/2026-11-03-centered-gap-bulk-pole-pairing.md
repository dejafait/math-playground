### Primary theorem statements and essential cases

All DLMF passages below are the live online text retrieved 2026-11-03;
no upstream book proof is claimed to have been independently audited.

[NIST DLMF 5.2(i),(iii)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.2), equations 5.2.4–5,
gives (a)_N=a(a+1)...(a+N-1)=Gamma(a+N)/Gamma(a), with the displayed
gamma quotient restricted to a not a nonpositive integer. Reciprocal gamma
is entire with simple zeros at the nonpositive integers; gamma has no
zeros and has simple poles there. These provide finite-product tools,
including the polynomial interpretation at exceptional arguments, not a
zero theorem for a sum of products. Keep exceptional factors explicit.

[DLMF 5.5.1 and 5.5.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.5) gives gamma recurrence
and reflection. The latter is stated for noninteger z. These are available
for exact product rearrangement and moving arguments across the negative
real direction. A later application must justify removable limits and
keep the sine factors and branches; reflection is not an estimate for
the summed numerator and does not justify division by a vanishing factor.

[DLMF 5.11(i)–(iii)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11), equations 5.11.1,
5.11.12–13 and the error paragraph before 5.11.10, supplies Stirling
expansions in |arg z|<=pi-delta, delta>0, with the stated logarithm branch.
The ratio subsection takes a,b,c to be constants. For a complex argument,
the log-gamma remainder after the terms through k=N-1 is bounded by
sec(arg z/2)^(2N) times the magnitude of the first omitted term; the
digamma bound uses exponent 2N+1. The real-positive case additionally has
the sign of the first omitted term. Thus complex estimates are available,
but the ratio statement alone is not a uniform theorem for n-dependent
shifts. A family application must verify sector separation and the size
of every argument, rather than declare uniformity in j or m. No such
verification is performed in this literature turn.

F. Johansson, *Arbitrary-precision computation of the gamma function*,
[arXiv:2109.08392v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.08392v1), 17 September 2021,
equations (21)–(27),(30)–(32), printed pp.11–14: write

    log Gamma(z)=(z-1/2)log z-z+log(2*pi)/2
                 +sum_(k=1)^(N-1) T_k(z)+R_N(z),
    T_k(z)=B_(2k)/[2k(2k-1) z^(2k-1)].

Theorem 3.2 states, for integers m>=0, N>=1 and z outside (-infinity,0],

    |R_N^(m)(z)|<=sec(arg z/2)^(2N+m) |T_N^(m)(z)|.
