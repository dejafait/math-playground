### Three inspected source mechanisms

1. **Actual functional limits.** E. Saksman and C. Webb, *The Riemann
   zeta function and Gaussian multiplicative chaos: statistics on the
   critical line*, [arXiv:1609.00027v3, 22 February 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.00027v3),
   Theorem 1.1(i), pp. 7--8: for omega uniform on [0,1], weighted random
   shifts converge in law in W^(-alpha,2), alpha>1/2, unconditionally.
   Theorem 1.2, p. 9, gives quadratic-Wasserstein mesoscopic approximation
   in that space for some deterministic delta_T->0. Its complete proof
   on p. 47 selects T_k then delta_T=1/k on [T_k,T_(k+1)); it does not
   prescribe delta_T=1/log T. Theorem 1.6, p. 11, says the limit is not
   a Borel measure on any open interval. Section 1.3 controls smoothed
   linear observables. **Comparison:** the topology/scale supplies no
   ordinary jet products or clipped expectations. Missing transfer must
   also track actual Hardy phase and dyadic sampling. W2 here concerns
   the Sobolev norm, not uniform integrability of our nonlinear statistic.
   This is a scope mismatch, not a new discontinuity proof.

2. **Weighted value limits.** A. Fazzari, *Weighted value distributions
   of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line*,
   [arXiv:2101.08036v1, 20 January 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.08036v1),
   Theorem 1, p. 3, assumes RH and Conjecture 1 for k. For fixed natural
   k,m, log|zeta| has a Gaussian limit with mean k log log T and variance
   (log log T)/2 under |zeta^(m)|^(2k)dt on [T,2T]. Conjecture 1,
   pp. 2--3, concerns small complex shifts and short twisted moments;
   its known k=1,2 cases do not remove RH. Proposition 2.1 and its proof
   opening, pp. 5--6, retain both assumptions in the prime-polynomial
   approximation. **Comparison:** this weighted scalar logarithmic law
   is not an unconditional joint ordinary Hardy-jet or tail theorem.
   Using it to prove the unconditional saving would assume the main
   goal. Its CUE analogue is not an actual-Xi theorem.

3. **Mollified sign information.** H. M. Bui and R. R. Hall,
   *On the derivatives of Hardy's function Z(t)*,
   [arXiv:2304.05178v1, 11 April 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.05178v1),
   Theorem 1 gives meas{ZZ''<0}>=(3/25+o(1))T. Equations (3)--(4)
   use signed mollified means and Cauchy--Schwarz for sign-set lower
   bounds. M is a one-piece Mobius polynomial of length T^vartheta.
   Proposition 1 requires P(0)=0, vartheta<4/7; (6)--(8) convert fixed
   ordinary Hardy derivatives. Proposition 2 requires P(0)=P'(0)=0,
   vartheta<1/8, four complex shifts O(1/log T), and has error
   O_epsilon(T(log T)^(-1+epsilon)). Its proof opening uses holomorphy
   and separated annuli before treating coincident shifts.
   **Comparison:** weighted polynomial moments and sign-density lower
   bounds do not give an unweighted clipped-quadratic upper bound.
   Removing the mollifier and controlling nonlinear exceptional tails
   remain obligations. No inverse-weight bound or growing-degree
   uniformity is supplied. The original fixed-moment assessment is
   reused for the remaining second/fourth arithmetic scope.
