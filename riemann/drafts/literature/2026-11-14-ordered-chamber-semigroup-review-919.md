### Primary theorem records

1. S. Karlin and J. McGregor, *Coincidence probabilities*, Pacific J.
   Math. **9** (1959), 1141-1164,
   [publisher text](https://msp.org/pjm/1959/9-4/pjm-v9-n4-p13-p.pdf).
   Section5 Theorem1, pp1148-1150: a time-homogeneous strong Markov
   process on a metric space has right-continuous paths; its independent
   n-fold product must also be strong Markov. For Borel endpoint sets,
   the transition determinant equals the sum, weighted by permutation
   parity, of endpoint-permutation probabilities with no coincidence
   through time t. Read the entire proof: the strong Markov decomposition
   at first coincidence cancels collided paths by transposition. It does
   not cancel order-changing paths without coincidence. Introduction
   case(C), pp1142-1143, assumes continuous paths on a real interval;
   case(D) retains the signed sum. Thus collision killing and chamber-exit
   killing are distinct issues for this review. Theorem4's strict
   positivity statement, p1153, was inspected for scope only; it retains
   continuity and an additional strict transition-support hypothesis.

2. R. L. Schilling, *An Introduction to Levy and Feller Processes*,
   [arXiv:1603.00251v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.00251v2),
   17 October 2016. Theorem4.12 (printed p29) supplies strong Markov
   for a Levy process in any dimension. Lemma6.3 (pp37-38) places
   compactly supported smooth functions in its generator domain.
   Theorem6.8 (p39), equation(6.8), gives
   A f(x)=l.grad f(x)+(1/2)grad.Q.grad f(x)
   + integral[f(x+y)-f(x)-grad f(x).y 1_(|y|<1)] nu(dy),
   with Q positive semidefinite and integral min(1,|y|^2) nu finite.
   Read the full statement and proof steps1-2, including its off-support
   positivity requirement. Remark5.10 (p34), with the hypotheses of a
   strongly continuous contraction semigroup on C_0, proves the
   sub-Markov/positive-maximum-principle link. It concerns the full-space
   positive cone. It does not certify positivity after antisymmetric
   extension. Compensation and drift must be matched together.

3. R. L. Schilling, R. Song and Z. Vondracek, *Bernstein Functions:
   Theory and Applications*, De Gruyter, **first edition, 2010**,
   ISBN978-3-11-021530-4,
   [primary book text in mirror](https://dokumen.pub/bernstein-functions-theory-and-applications-9783110215311-9783110215304.html).
   Theorem3.2 (p15): the Bernstein representation is
   f(s)=a+bs+integral(1-exp(-st)) nu(dt), uniquely, with a,b>=0 and
   integral min(1,t) nu(dt)<infinity. Read its statement and the
   derivative-measure identification in the proof (pp17-18).
   Theorem5.2 (p35), with Definition5.1 (p34), identifies these exponents
   with unique vaguely continuous convolution semigroups of

   Read the complete proof and Definition5.4 (p35) and the adjoining
   killed-subordinator convention (p36). Mass is exp(-tau f(0+)).
   These statements do not assert smooth densities at arbitrary tau.
