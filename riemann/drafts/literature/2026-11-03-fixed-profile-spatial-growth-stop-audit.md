### Essential contour and maximum-principle scope

Direct retrieval was limited to the new exterior/bridge interface and
its finite-residue prerequisite. Read Ash–Novinger, *Complex Variables*
(2004), [2.4.12(a)–(d) and complete proof, printed pp.23–24, PDF pp.37–38](https://people.math.sc.edu/girardi/m7034/book/AshComplexVariablesWithHyperlinks.pdf#page=37).
The local maximum principle assumes a connected open analytic domain;
the bounded-region boundary form also requires continuity on its closure.
Read [4.1.5(a), its finite-limit proof, p.4, and 4.1.8, p.5](https://people.math.sc.edu/girardi/m7034/book/AshComplexVariablesWithHyperlinks.pdf#page=65).
The former characterizes removable singularities; the latter treats
infinity through the reciprocal coordinate. The boundedness equivalence
left as an exercise is unnecessary for the stated finite-limit use.
These cover the tools in L711 provided its finite rational function has
all poles enclosed, a removable point at infinity and a valid boundary
bound. No new exterior principle is proved here. Its contour geometry,
bridge multiplier and estimates remain audit obligations.

Reopened Howell–Mathews, [Section 8.1, Theorems 8.1.5 and 8.1.7(1),
including both relevant proofs](https://complexanalysis.org/web/sec_residue-thm.html).
The first uses a positively oriented simple closed contour, finitely
many isolated interior singularities and none on the contour; the simple
pole formula is the limit of the pole factor times the function.
The proof separates small circles and uses Cauchy–Goursat. These cover
finite rectangles only after their pole census and orientation are
checked. Vanishing vertical edges, infinite-line limits and rates uniform
in parameters are separate local estimates, not consequences imported
from this theorem. Higher-pole formulas were visible but are not needed.
