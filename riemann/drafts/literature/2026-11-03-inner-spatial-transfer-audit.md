### Primary inputs, hypotheses and application boundary

**Complete spatial roots.** F. Brunault and W. Zudilin,
*Many Variations of Mahler Measures*, ISBN 9781108794459,
[publisher excerpt, Propositions 1.4--1.5, pp.6--7](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811087/94459/excerpt/9781108794459_excerpt.pdf).
For a nonzero complex polynomial of actual degree d, nonzero leading
coefficient b and roots alpha counted with multiplicity, the normalized
circle logarithmic mean is log|b|+sum log max(1,|alpha|). Constants
have an empty root sum. The saved primary reading covers interior,
exterior and boundary roots; the boundary case uses an indented
contour with vanishing error, rather than assuming a bounded logarithm.
This is the exact root input needed by L683. No arithmetic coefficient,
root separation or nonvanishing-on-the-circle hypothesis is required.
The source does not state L683's finite-family inequality: verifying
its inner-circle application, actual degree, leading factor, zero

**Angular quadrature.** V. I. Danchenko and L. A. Semin,
*Sharp quadrature formulas and Nikol'skii type inequalities for
rational functions*, [arXiv:1503.06476v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.06476v1),
22 March 2015, Section 1.1, Theorem 2, PDF pp.1--2; complete proof
pp.6--7, using Theorem 1. For a finite distinct set S inside a circle
of radius r_s, containing zero, complete it with r_s^2/conjugate(z),
including infinity. Rational f may have poles only in this completion,
ordinary orders at most the positive integer s and combined orders at
zero/infinity at most 2s-1. With the source Blaschke product B and
mu_s(u)=sum_(z in S)(r_s^2-|z|^2)/|u-z|^2, its formula is

    integral_(|u|=r_s) |f(u)|^2 |du|
      =(pi*r_s/s) sum_(B(u_l)^(2s)=exp(i*phi)) |f(u_l)|^2/mu_s(u_l).

The saved proof reading includes conjugate/reflected factors and the
zero/infinity polynomial parts; Theorem 1's underlying proof was not
independently audited and is imported as a cited theorem. There is no
new source blocker or reason to repeat that manuscript reading.
The source radius r_s=1 and s=1 are separate from L683's spatial r.
