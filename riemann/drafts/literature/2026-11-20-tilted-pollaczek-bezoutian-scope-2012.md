### General-basis theorem, with its own orientation

H. Wu, X. Wu and Z. Yang, *More on polynomial Bezoutians with respect
to a general basis*, Electronic Journal of Linear Algebra 21 (2010),
154–171, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.13001/1081-3810.1421),
[primary journal PDF mirror](https://emis.de/ft/34467).
Read definition(1.1), recurrence(1.6), matrix(1.8), Lemmas2.1–2.2
and their complete proofs, Corollary2.4, and Propositions2.5–2.6 with
their complete proofs: printed pp154–159, PDF pp1–6.

The assumptions are deg(p)=n, deg(q)<=n, and a degree-graded basis
Q=(Q_0,...,Q_(n-1)), with nonzero recurrence leading factors. The
kernel convention is (p(x)q(y)-p(y)q(x))/(x-y). For the row basis
Q(x)=pi(x)T, T is invertible and the source states

    T B_Q(p,q) T^t=B(p,q),
    C_Q=T^(-1) C_power T,
    B_Q(p,q)=S_Q(p) q(C_Q^t),
    B_Q(p,q) C_Q^t=C_Q B_Q(p,q).

These are source statements, not new local matrix identities. The last
is a right symmetrizer identity for the source's coefficient matrix
C_Q, equivalently a left-metric identity for its transpose. Lemma2.1's
proof fixes this convention through xQ=Q C_Q modulo p. One must not
identify C_Q itself with C029n's descending evaluation comrade.
Reversal of basis and the positive diagonal scaling must be checked
in the proposed finite application. No inverse Bezoutian is called for
merely by the source's word “symmetrizer.”

The congruence holds without simple roots or coprimality. Corollary2.4
requires coprimality exactly for invertibility. Hence the derivative
choice q=p' may be singular and cannot be presumed a nonsingular
Pontryagin metric. A different q changes the inertia problem; existence
of B_Q(p,1) alone supplies no theorem of negative index<=1.
Pollaczek's varying recurrence factors are allowed here, unlike the
constant-factor symmetrizer in comparison2008. Its unbounded
orthogonality support is irrelevant to this finite algebraic theorem.

For the classical Barnett identity underlying Proposition2.5, also
read G. Heinig and K. Rost, *Bezoutians*, Chemnitz preprint2008-09,
[author-hosted PDF](https://www.tu-chemnitz.de/mathematik/preprint/2008/PREPRINT_09.pdf),
Section6.3, Theorem6.2 and full proof, printed p38. It assumes a monic
degree-n denominator and degree<=n numerator. Its companion is the
evaluation orientation (superdiagonal ones), the transpose of Wu's
power-basis companion. The proof uses monomial numerators and a finite
recursion, with no root simplicity assumption. This proved modern
source replaces the uninspected original Barnett1972 paper; no reproof
of Barnett's theorem is proposed.

### Derivative inertia and the duplicate comparison

Heinig–Rost, same preprint: read Example7.2, printed p40; Section7.5,
Proposition7.15/(7.11) and proof, p45; Proposition7.18/proof, pp45–46;
and Section10.3, Theorem10.4 and its preceding justification, p59,
with the repeated-root discussion on p60. The square n-by-n identity
in(7.11) is sufficient: for deg(p)=n and deg(q)<=n, the coefficient
Bezoutian is a congruence of the Hankel form generated by q/p at
infinity through B(p,1). No reduced-fraction hypothesis is imposed
on this square identity. The proof compares coefficients after
multiplication by p in both variables. This is not an expansion of
1/p at the origin. We do not use the more general padded construction.

Example7.2 identifies H_n(p'/p) with the Newton-sum Hermite matrix,
counting multiplicities. Theorem10.4 gives the number of distinct real
roots as its signature and as the derivative Bezoutian's signature;
the preceding rank statement counts distinct complex roots. In
particular these are the **same inertia problem**, including singular
forms. The local interpretation (r+c,c,n-r-2c), for r distinct real
roots and c distinct nonreal conjugate pairs, is already covered by
Coste/Plaumann in review2001; no new root-count argument is needed.
Nonsingularity certifies simplicity. With nullity, negative index
counts distinct pairs, not nonreal algebraic multiplicity, and a
singular form does not meet the nonsingular metric theorem's premises.
The Hermite route of C029l accommodates singular forms independently.

Use the displayed square formula(7.11) and Example7.2 for orientation:
the indexed text of Corollary7.16 shows the reciprocal ratio in the
opposite order. No conclusion here depends on that corollary's ratio
rendering. Wu's kernel convention and Heinig–Rost's(7.11) agree in the
order p,q. Reversing it negates the form, so it cannot be ignored.

Thus changing from Newton sums to a Pollaczek coefficient Bezoutian
does not furnish a new sign mechanism or weaken the eventual premise
in C029m. Its possible benefit is an implementation avoiding divisions
by the top coefficient and Newton-sum growth. No complexity or accuracy
improvement for actual T_6 has been measured here.
