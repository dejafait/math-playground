## Inspected primary theorems and exact limits of their use

**Finite contours.** Friedrich Haslinger, *Complex Analysis*, author
[PDF](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~has/scriptneu.pdf), Section2.2,
Theorem2.5 and Corollary2.6, printed pp45-48; revision date unknown.
Reopened the statements and corollary proof; reuse1850's inspected
triangle proof, including exceptional vertex/interior cases. A
continuous function on a convex open set, holomorphic except possibly
at one point, has a primitive and zero closed-path integrals. Continuity
at that point is essential: this is no permission to cross a pole.
Use the ordinary holomorphic case for finite rectangles, retaining
all four oriented sides. It proves neither an infinite limit nor
cancellation of a finite connector.

**Absolute product integration.** John K. Hunter, *Measure Theory*,
Section5.6, [Theorem5.18 and Example5.19, printed p61](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/measure_theory/measure_notes_ch5.pdf).
Read both complete statements. For sigma-finite measure spaces and
complex measurable f, finiteness of an iterated integral of |f| is
equivalent to product integrability and permits both integration
orders. The example treats absolutely summable complex double series
using counting measure. No proof of Theorem5.18 is printed in this
chapter; no uninspected proof is claimed. This is the standard named
Fubini theorem, not an imported assertion of our integrability.

Applicability comparison: enumerate the divisor with multiplicity,
use counting measure, and parameterize each finite side by a real
interval. These are sigma-finite spaces. The specialization must
establish measurable kernels and finite absolute sums of side
integrals before using the theorem, including all independent and
diagonal divisor pairs. Separate conditionally convergent iterates
would not meet it. Member-dependent shifts must remain in the
integrands; no shared displacement or conjugate-only pairing is licensed.

**Limits.** Hunter, Section4.5,
[Theorem4.24, printed p49, and Section4.6, p50](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/measure_theory/measure_notes_ch4.pdf).
Read the full Fatou-based proof and the complex-valued/a.e. extension.
Measurable pointwise convergent functions dominated by one integrable
g have convergent integrals. For incomplete spaces the limit must
also be measurable. Source publication/revision date is unverified;
the associated Hunter course page identifies Spring2011 notes.
For the proposed limits a width-uniform common majorant must be
proved; an upper bound for each member separately is insufficient.
In particular a far vertical side cannot be discarded merely because
it vanishes for each fixed center before summing the divisor.

**Scalar Gaussian formulas.** Reopened
[NIST DLMF7.7.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/7.7#E2): the Gaussian Cauchy
integral for w(z) requires Im z>0. Reuse1846's recorded version1.2.8
and other inspected formulas, without asserting a newly checked version.
This is an optional scalar evaluation, not the requested cutoff or
divisor-pair theorem. No on-axis pole or endpoint-free substitution is

is unnecessary for the finite-rectangle route.
