## Primary sources actually inspected

1. [Gan–Röllin–Ross, arXiv:1602.07010v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.07010v3),
   8 December 2016, *Dirichlet approximation of equilibrium distributions
   in Cannings models with mutation*, subsequently Adv. Appl. Probab.
   49 (2017), 927–959. Read the positive-parameter density definition
   (pp.2–3), Lemma 3.1 and (3.1)–(3.3) (p.8), Theorem 3.2 (p.9),
   and its boundary-extension case (p.12). Lemma 3.1 permits all a_i>0
   and BC^(2,1) tests. Its mean-zero operator is
   sum x_i(delta_ij-x_j) f_ij + sum (a_i-s x_i) f_i, s=sum a_i,
   in K-1 independent coordinates; the diffusion generator has half
   this normalization. Theorem 3.2's boundary extension uses continuous
   extensions of the derivatives when m>=2. The short forward proof
   of Lemma 3.1 contains no detailed singular-face flux calculation.
   Applicability: a_i=1/2 is within the stated parameter range. The
   actual theta-weighted law is not Dir(a); its identity cannot be
   imported unchanged. No signed response estimate is furnished.

2. [Andres–von Renesse, arXiv:0903.5085v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.5085v1),
   29 March 2009, *Regularity Properties for a System of Interacting
   Bessel Processes*, (1.1) and Proposition 2.1/Remark 2.2 (pp.2,4).
   Read the corresponding full cutoff proof in Sebastian Andres,
   [*Diffusion Processes with Reflection*, TU Berlin dissertation,
   2009](https://d-nb.info/994899424/34), Proposition 3.7, Section 3.2,
   printed pp.54–55 (PDF pp.64–65). The measure on ordered positions
   has density proportional to the product of the N+1 successive
   gaps to power beta/(N+1)-1, beta>0. The integration-by-parts
   formula requires a C^1 vector field with zero normal component
   on each face. The proof truncates every gap at 1/k, splits the
   boundary into faces, uses the mean-value theorem to obtain one
   factor 1/k from the normal component, and retains the other
   integrable gap powers. It treats both endpoint faces and returns
   to the volume product rule. Thus singular weights are included;
   smooth extension and face conditions are essential. For the future
   specialization use C^1 extensions to the closed simplex, avoiding
   any reliance on unbounded derivatives at its boundary.

3. [Mijoule–Raič–Reinert–Swan, arXiv:2101.05079v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.05079v2),
   revised 26 April 2023, *Stein's density method for multivariate
   continuous distributions*, EJP 28 (2023), DOI
   [10.1214/22-EJP883](https://doi.org/10.1214/22-EJP883).
   Read Section 2.3's function spaces (pp.8–9), Definitions 3.1–3.2,
   Proposition 3.3 and its full slicing/Fubini proof, Remark 3.4,
   (10)–(11) (pp.9–11), Definition 3.11 (p.12).
   The canonical divergence is div(pX)/p. Mean zero is a Stein-class
   condition, not a consequence of interior differentiability.
   Proposition 3.3 gives a compact-support sufficient case. Their
   W_0^(1,1) notation imposes zero integrals of weak derivatives;
   do not substitute an unexamined convention for that definition.
   This provides a useful domain check, not automatic membership for
   a field reaching singular faces. Componentwise all-direction
   derivative conditions can be stronger than the needed combined
   tangential-divergence condition. Use the face-cutoff framework
   rather than claiming such membership without proof.

4. [Gorham–Duncan–Vollmer–Mackey, arXiv:1611.06972v6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.06972v6),
   13 November 2018, *Measuring Sample Quality with Diffusions*,
   Proposition 3 (p.7) and full Appendix A proof (p.24); also the
   constrained-domain qualification at pp.15–16. Proposition 3 assumes
   a C^1 density of full support R^d, finite first moment, C^1 matrices
   a,c, integrable a,c,b, and bounded tests with bounded Lipschitz
   derivatives. Appendix A uses balls, divergence and coarea to find
   vanishing boundary flux along a sequence. The later qualification
   discusses product domains with extra boundary constraints. Neither
   statement directly covers this singular simplex law. It must not
   be cited as already proving the required cutoff limit or a sign.

The thesis proof, rather than the Bessel paper's brief approximation
remark, supplies the inspected singular-face cases. The prospective

not complete the target and why a limited specialization is justified.

## Decision and test specification

The sources support a boundary framework, not actual upward crossings.
Prepare one later attempt with an explicit permutation-equivariant
polynomial field X in twelve coordinates, component degree <=13,
sum X_i=0 on the simplex, and X_i=0 on its i-th coordinate face.
These are face conditions in addition to tangency to sum t_i=1.
Use the fixed interval [1/4,4], the actual K and the full Pbar.
No field or derivative has been constructed here.

A concrete prospective sign test is a uniform positive residual after
adding a justified mean-zero weighted divergence and subtracting a
scalar multiple of Pbar. The accompanying nomination fixes that test;
its radial identity, normalization and crossing implication are
obligations for the later research turn. Strict positivity at actual
zeros, not a formal Stein identity or an absolute covariance bound,
is the required output. A negative finite combination annihilating
the allowed coefficient columns would instead be an informative
obstruction to this sufficient ansatz. An unsuccessful solver or a
grid without error bounds establishes neither result. Stop at the
fixed degree and interval; do not enlarge them after failure.
