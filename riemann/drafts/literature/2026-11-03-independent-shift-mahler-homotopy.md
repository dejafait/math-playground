### Primary statements and relevant proof cases

**Fixed-degree continuity.** D. W. Boyd, *Uniform Approximation to
Mahler's Measure in Several Variables*, Canadian Mathematical Bulletin
41(1) (1998), 125--128, [DOI 10.4153/CMB-1998-019-6](https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-1998-019-6),
[publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/7A3DB3EC60114F565C7972CACBDDA552/S0008439500004227a.pdf/div-class-title-uniform-approximation-to-mahler-s-measure-in-several-variables-div.pdf).
Read the bounded-degree setup p.126, construction (5)--(8), theorem and
proof p.127. It allows vanishing coefficients, degree drops and torus
zeros. Its approximants obey
`log M_l(f)-d*2^(-l)*log 2 <= log M(f) <= log M_l(f)`.
The bounded-degree continuity theorem supplies neither a parameter-path
derivative formula nor this family's absolute loss constant.

**Quantitative coefficient comparison.** V. Dimitrov and P. Habegger,
*Galois Orbits of Torsion Points near Atoral Sets*,
[arXiv:1909.06051v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.06051v2), 24 March 2022,
Appendix A, printed pp.45--48. Read A.3's normalization and statement,
A.4's full dyadic-tail proof, and A.5's full proof, including constant Q.
For nonzero complex polynomials P,Q in d variables, each having at most
k terms, k>=2, maximum-coefficient norm |.| and
`delta=|P-Q|/|Q|<=1/2`, Lemma A.5 gives
`m(P)<=m(Q)+C(d,k)*delta^(1/(8(k-1)))`.
It does not require algebraic coefficients or the main theorem's atoral
hypothesis. A.4 bounds the normalized small-value logarithmic tail by
`O_(d,k)(r^(1/(4(k-1))))`; its inherited assumption is nonconstant
P with |P|=1. A.5 splits at |Q|=sqrt(delta), so torus zeros need not be
excluded. For a lower comparison the order must be reversed and the new
relative-norm hypothesis checked. Relative coefficient control and
growing k remain unverified for the complete family, not assumptions.

**Smooth regularization with explicit tail cost.** F. Brunault,
A. Guilloux, M. Mehrabdollahei and R. Pengo, *Limits of Mahler Measures
in Multiple Variables*, [arXiv:2203.12259v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12259v2),
27 June 2022, Sections 2.2--2.5 (p.3), Theorem 4.6 (pp.9--10),
Proposition 4.10 and complete proof (p.11). Write d for their variable
count, k for the number of nonzero coefficients, and 0<alpha<1.
For L_infty(P)=1, their proposition bounds
`integral [log(|P|^2+epsilon)/2-log|P|] dmu_A`
between zero and
`C_2(d,k,alpha)*epsilon^((1-alpha)/(2(k-1)))`, where
`C_2=12*k^2*(18*d*k^2)^(d-1)*alpha^(1-d)/(1-alpha)`.
Its hypotheses include k>=2, A an integer m-by-d matrix, m<=d and
rho(A)>diam(P). For the ordinary torus A=I_d, their definitions give
rho(A)=infinity and mu_A=mu_d. Read also the monomial-slice cases in
Theorem 4.6 and the layer-cake proof of Proposition 4.10: singular
logs are controlled by small-value mass, not by deleting zeros.
A monomial is handled directly instead. Normalization and k-dependent
error must be retained; this result does not bound signed variation
along the notebook's path. No new specialization estimate is asserted.

**Divisor current including singularities.** J.-P. Demailly,
*Complex Analytic and Differential Geometry*, version 21 June 2012,
[author's text hosted at Harvard](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~demarco/Math274/Demailly_ComplexAnalyticDiffGeom.pdf),
Chapter III, (2.15), printed pp.143--144. Read the full statement and
proof: if f is meromorphic on a complex manifold and is not identically
zero on any connected component, log|f| is locally integrable and
`(i/pi)*partial*barpartial log|f|=sum_j m_j[Z_j]` for its full divisor.
The proof uses a local unit times a power at regular divisor points
and the support theorem to extend over singular points. Thus
multiplicities and negative pole orders are retained. This is a current
identity, not permission to restrict arbitrary currents to a real torus,
differentiate a singular torus integral, or bound real-directional loss.
Any such application needs its own justification. The author's document
index timed out; this readable primary-text copy resolved access.

**Boundary-root accounting.** Rechecked F. Brunault and W. Zudilin,
*Many Variations of Mahler Measures*, ISBN 9781108794459,
[Propositions 1.4--1.5, pp.6--7](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811087/94459/excerpt/9781108794459_excerpt.pdf).
Read the complex-analytic proof's inside, outside and boundary cases;
the boundary indentation error tends to zero as epsilon*|log epsilon|.
The polynomial formula sums log max(1,|root|) with multiplicity and the
actual leading coefficient. It is valid at boundary roots; it provides
endpoint accounting, not a bound on motion or a root-label derivative.
No reliance is placed on the alternate Riemann-sum proof at a boundary
zero. No reproof of these general source results is needed.
