### Primary theorems actually inspected

**1. Explicit logarithmic derivative bound.** D. S. Lubinsky,
*Orthogonal Dirichlet Polynomials with Arctangent Density*,
J. Approx. Theory 177 (2014), 43–56, DOI
[10.1016/j.jat.2013.10.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jat.2013.10.001).
Inspected [author manuscript, 14 October 2013](https://lubinsky.math.gatech.edu/Research%20papers/DirichletOPOct2013RohrsVn.pdf):
hypotheses/(1.1)/(1.3), pp. 1–2; Theorem 1.5/(1.23)–(1.24),
p. 6; complete proof pp. 14–15.
For 1=lambda_1<...<lambda_m and arbitrary complex coefficients in
P(t)=sum_(j<=m) a_j lambda_j^(-it), it gives

    ||P'||_nu <= [log lambda_m
       + (sum_(j=2)^m (lambda_j-lambda_(j-1))
                           /(lambda_j+lambda_(j-1)))^(1/2)] ||P||_nu
       <= [log lambda_m+sqrt(log lambda_m)] ||P||_nu,
    dnu(t)=dt/[pi(1+t^2)].

Read both proof components: the residual after adding
i(log lambda_j)phi_j uses Cauchy–Schwarz; the diagonal part uses
orthonormality. The remaining sum is bounded by log lambda_m.
This theorem concerns that real-line measure. No isometry from
our full divisor Gram norm to it is supplied or asserted.
Its explicit constant is comparison evidence, not a K_c budget.

**2. Multiplicative Hardy-space norm and multipliers.** H. Hedenmalm,
P. Lindqvist and K. Seip, *A Hilbert space of Dirichlet series and
systems of dilated functions in L2(0,1)*, Duke Math. J. 86 (1997),
1–37; inspected [arXiv:math/9512211v1, 1 December 1995](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9512211).
Section 2.2/(2-7)/Lemma 2.3, pp. 7–9, identifies square-summable
Dirichlet coefficients isometrically with analytic H2 on the character
torus, with normalized product Haar measure. Theorem 3.1, pp. 9–11,
states that multipliers are precisely functions representable by
a Dirichlet series in some half-plane which extend boundedly and
holomorphically to Re s>0; their operator norm equals that supremum
norm. Read both proof directions: lifting products and powers to the
torus, then Carlson's theorem and finite-series approximation.

This supports a possible full-space formulation, but supplies neither
a bound for a proposed shifted multiplier nor a Bernstein estimate
in L623's weighted image. A later use must construct the operator
and prove its hypotheses and quantitative norm; finite coefficient
length alone does not check them. General complex-shift bilinear
forms and signed extraction are outside this theorem.

**3. More general weighted Bernstein inequalities.** D. S. Lubinsky,
*Weighted Markov–Bernstein Inequalities for Entire Functions of
Exponential Type*, Publ. Inst. Math. (N.S.) 96(110) (2014), 181–192,
[DOI 10.2298/PIM1410181L; published PDF](https://emis.de/ft/22950).
Theorem 1.1, pp. 182–183, assumes sigma,p>0, 0<r<=1, measurable
w:R->[0,infinity), and D>1, B,beta>=1 satisfying

    |integral_a^(2a) w| <= D |integral_(a/2)^a w|, |a|>=r,
    integral_(jr)^((j+1)r) w
       <= B(1+r|j-k|)^beta integral_(kr)^((k+1)r) w,
    k>=0, -1<=j<=max{2k+1,1/r}, j,k integers,

and the analogous reflected condition for k<0. For entire f of type
<=sigma and finite right-hand side, with
w_r(t)=(2r)^(-1)integral_(t-r)^(t+r)w,

    integral |f'|^p w_r <= C(B,beta,p,D)(sigma+1)^p integral |f|^p w_r.

Corollary 1.2 replaces w_r by w if these conditions hold for every
0<r<r0<1 with uniform D,B,beta. Read the weight-tail and
sinc-cutoff/limit cases, pp. 189–190. Those parameter hypotheses
are unverified here; no signed budget or numerical C is supplied.
The [author draft](https://lubinsky.math.gatech.edu/Research%20papers/WtdBnExptypeDec82013.pdf)
was also read; numbering above follows the published version.
