### Precise primary input and application boundary

Use M. Vanlessen, *Strong asymptotics of Laguerre-type orthogonal
polynomials and applications in random matrix theory*,
[math/0504604v2, 29 August 2005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0504604v2).
The weight assumptions are alpha>-1 and polynomial Q with positive
leading coefficient, (1.1)-(1.2), p1. Theorem2.4(b)/(2.13), pp5-6,
has uniform bulk errors; Remark2.5, pp6-7, specifies branch and upper
boundary conventions. Proposition3.31, pp32-33, removes the real bulk
jump. The reduced contour (3.101)/Figure6 is independent of degree.
Theorem3.32/(3.110), p34, controls R uniformly on its complement
(also uniformly in the stated delta/nu ranges). Section5.2, p39,
gives the exact (5.5)-(5.6), retaining R and both oscillatory branches;
(5.1), p38, supplies the scalar normalization. This is stronger
usable input than an unstructured real-axis asymptotic remainder.

[DLMF Table18.3.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.3#T1), Laguerre row,
specifies weight x^alpha*exp(-x), alpha>-1, norm squared
Gamma(N+alpha+1)/N! and leading coefficient (-1)^N/N!.
Thus the source's positive-leading orthonormal convention is the one
needed for the conversion already written in C997e. Q(x)=x and
alpha=2 satisfy the cited hypotheses. Remark2.3 covers its monomial
scaling. The argument to check is c*(n+u)/(4N), N=n+1, 0<=u<=2,
near the compact real bulk interval J/4. The audit must establish
one fixed admissible neighborhood and the exact analytic continuation,
not assume that the theorem's upper boundary convention already proves
every claimed amplitude formula. No general asymptotic theorem needs
reproof; the degree shift and short-interval conversion require the
