### Primary contracts actually inspected

1. A. Bostan and P. Dumas, *Wronskians and linear independence*,
   American Mathematical Monthly117(2010), 722-727;
   [author manuscript](https://mathexp.eu/dumas/publications/Bostan_Dumas_2010_WLI.pdf),
   Theorems1-2, pp1-2; Lemmas1-3 and proof of Theorem2, pp2-4
   (also [arXiv:1301.6598v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.6598v1)).
   For a finite independent family of real or complex analytic functions
   on a connected domain, its Wronskian is not identically zero.
   The formal-series statement requires characteristic zero and
   independence over the constant field. Read the proof's constant
   invertible column reduction to distinct vanishing orders, monomial
   Vandermonde determinant and leading-term argument. Neither theorem
   guarantees pointwise nonvanishing, a quantitative lower bound or a
   bound uniform in the number of functions. Smoothness alone is
   insufficient, as the introduction explicitly explains. This is the
   appropriate degeneracy contract; independence of our band columns
   remains to be verified, not assumed.

2. A. Kasman, *Spectral difference equations satisfied by KP soliton
   wavefunctions*, Inverse Problems14(1998), 1481-1487,
   DOI10.1088/0266-5611/14/6/008,
   [author published PDF](https://kasmana.people.charleston.edu/spec-solitons.pdf),
   p1481 finite point-derivative distributions, p1483 coefficient ring,
   p1484 equation(3), following paragraph, Lemma3.1 and its proof.
   A finite-dimensional distribution space with a basis produces
   polynomial-exponential functions. Equation(3) expresses the monic
   annihilator as their augmented Wronskian divided by their Wronskian.
   The following paragraph explicitly identifies the unnormalized
   operator as having polynomial-exponential coefficients. The monic
   quotient is not a globally regular operator across Wronskian zeros;
   its numerator is the relevant division-free object. The lemma's
   constant-coefficient factorization uses rational-exponential factors
   and cleared denominators. It supplies no quantitative bounds on
   those factors or on compact adjoint densities. Read the support and
   maximal-derivative construction, operator commutation, right division
   and denominator clearing in the proof. No quantitative norm transfer
   from that factorization is imported.

3. S. Dyatlov, *Lecture notes for 18.155: distributions, elliptic
   regularity, and applications to PDEs*, version **2 November2026**,
   [author PDF](https://math.mit.edu/~dyatlov/18.155/155-notes.pdf),
   Definition7.12, Remark7.13 and Proposition7.14 with proof, pp84-85;
   Definition9.1 and (9.1)-(9.4), pp97-98. For an open U and a finite
   operator P=sum a_alpha partial^alpha with smooth complex
   coefficients, the **bilinear transpose** is explicitly

       P^t u=sum (-1)^|alpha| partial^alpha(a_alpha u).     [source (9.3)]

   The source uses ordinary derivatives in (9.1)-(9.3); no i-convention
   conversion is needed there. Its sesquilinear adjoint is distinguished

   conjugating a_alpha. The source also gives support preservation.

   None of these statements supplies growing-order norm constants.
   This explicit variable-coefficient formula fills the coverage gap
