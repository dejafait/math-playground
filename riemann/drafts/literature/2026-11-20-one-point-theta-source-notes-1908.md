## Primary formula and read proof cases

A. M. Delgado, L. Fernandez, T. E. Perez and M. A. Pinar,
*Multivariate Orthogonal Polynomials and Modified Moment Functionals*,
SIGMA 12 (2016), 090, 25 pages, DOI 10.3842/SIGMA.2016.090,
[arXiv:1601.07194v2, 10 September 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.07194v2).
Inspected Section2 p7, definition of quasi-definiteness and (2.3);
Section3 pp8-10, (3.1)-(3.14), Theorem3.1 and its complete proof.
It permits arbitrary distinct real sites and nonzero real masses;
invertibility conditions characterize the modified functional. For one
variable, one site c and positive mass t, its monic formula is

    q_n(x)=p_n(x)-t*p_n(c)*K_(n-1)(c,x)/(1+t*K_(n-1)(c,c)),
    K_(n-1)(c,x)=sum_(j=0)^(n-1) p_j(c)*p_j(x)/h_j,
    h_j=integral p_j^2 dmu.

The proof expands q_n-p_n in lower degrees, obtains coefficients by
orthogonality, evaluates at the sites and solves the finite system.
It also checks invertibility, the norm formula and the converse.
No outside-support premise enters this theorem. This known formula is
the imported input; the proposed moving-site skewness limit is absent.

Applicability uses the already established L019/L021/C028q hypotheses:
mu_0 is the full even theta probability, with all absolute moments and
strictly positive density on R. Its finite polynomial Gram matrices
are positive definite. Adding a positive atom preserves that property.
Take t=1, c=L, and use mu_0+delta_L for the formula. Multiplication by
1/2 gives nu_L and changes no monic orthogonal polynomial. In particular
h_0=1 refers to mu_0, not an unnormalized half-line theta measure.
The displayed kernel is a finite sum of squared real evaluations on its
diagonal with positive h_j; the theorem's scalar denominator is positive.
There is no division by p_n(L); its possible vanishing is allowed.
These are applicability checks, not a new deformation theorem or a
claim about the actual Q_d's zeros.

NIST [DLMF18.2(i), Orthogonality on General Sets,

infinite support and finite moments. [DLMF18.2(vi), opening zero

## A stronger-looking zero theorem does not apply

E. J. Huertas, F. Marcellan and F. R. Rafaeli,
*Zeros of Orthogonal Polynomials Generated by Canonical Perturbations
on Standard Measure*, [arXiv:1005.0336v1, 3 May 2010](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0336v1).
Inspected Section1.1 pp1-2; Section2.3 p5 (15);
Theorem2 pp6-7, its orthogonality proof and connection to the iterated
Christoffel measure; Section3 p7, Theorems3-4, (18)-(23).
The latter require the site at an exterior side of the convex hull
of the original support and take mass N to infinity at a fixed site.
Their proof invokes the stated interlacing and Lemma1 for combinations
of interlacing polynomials. Here the convex hull is all R, mass is 1,
and the location L varies. Neither hypothesis nor limiting variable
matches. Do not cite these theorems as an escaping-zero assertion for
nu_L. The unrestricted formula above avoids that source mismatch;
any large-L conclusion still needs its own subsequent calculation.
