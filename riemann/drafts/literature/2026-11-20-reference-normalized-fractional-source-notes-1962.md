## Primary sources inspected and limitations

1. Andreas Defant and Mieczyslaw Mastylo, *Lp-norms and Mahler's measure
   of polynomials on the n-dimensional torus*,
   [arXiv:1508.05556v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.05556v1),
   23 August2015, (4)-(5) printed p2, Theorem3.1 and full induction,
   limiting and sharpness cases pp7-8. For complex polynomials and
   p>0 the theorem compares torus Lp norms with Mahler measure using
   explicit degree-dependent factors Lambda(p,d_j). It imposes no
   real-root condition. Its domain is the normalized torus; it does
   not state an interval estimate for a rational function with a pole
   at the reference origin. No measure-changing transfer, vanishing
   degree-dependent factor, or actual-family upper bound is imported.
   The underlying univariate Arestov theorem is cited by that paper;
   its original proof was not independently inspected in this step.

2. Thomas M. Cover and Joy A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*,
   first edition (1991), Section2.6, Theorems2.6.1-2.6.2, printed
   pp24-26, [author-text chapter scan](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/liam/teaching/neurostat-spr11/papers/EM/Cover%26Thomas-Ch2.pdf).
   Read the convexity/Taylor argument, Jensen statement, finite-mass
   induction and stated continuous extension. Strict convexity gives
   equality only for an almost surely constant variable; the text
