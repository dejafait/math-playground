## Formulation 1: terminal-anchored Riemann-Liouville differentiation

Torre Caparatta, Sebastian Pauli and Filip Saidak, *Zeros of fractional
derivatives of polynomials*, [arXiv:2301.08149v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08149v3),
revision19July2024, PDF dated22July2024. The version matters: v1 was
initially retrieved; the completed comparison uses v3. The published
article is *Experimental Mathematics*34(3)(2025),541-554,
[DOI10.1080/10586458.2024.2382362](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2024.2382362);
no identity with every published passage is asserted.

Inspected Definition2.1/Remark2.2 and Lemma2.5 with its proof, pp3-5;
Theorem4.1 and its full proof, pp10-11; Section5's opening p12.
The definition starts with analytic f on a convex open set containing a:
I_a^beta f(t)=Gamma(beta)^(-1) integral_a^t f(tau)(t-tau)^(beta-1)dtau,
D_a^alpha=(d/dt)^m I_a^(m-alpha), m=ceil(alpha).
The paper chooses the principal logarithm. Its zero-path results are
for finite polynomials. Theorem4.1 requires absence of double zeros
throughout 0<=alpha<n and concludes paths terminating at a at integer
orders. Its proof uses vanishing low coefficients and symmetric root
functions. The following section explicitly disclaims general
Gauss-Lucas containment for fractional derivatives.

**Applicability decision.** The polynomial normalization in Lemma2.5
retains a terminal power (x-a)^(-alpha); its zeros away from a are read
from a polynomial factor. At integer orders terminal zeros in that
factor and zeros of the ordinary derivative must be distinguished.
No theorem inspected extends this finite path statement to the actual
transcendental A or asserts a monotone angular motion back to order zero.
No actual no-double-zero hypothesis is available here. The convex
analytic domain of the definition alone is not a zero-location theorem.
We neither specialize the operator to A nor claim such specialization
impossible. No new fractional coefficient identity is derived.

The Section5 preserver classification is cited there from Genchev-Sendov.
Its original proof was not inspected; it is not imported as a separate
obstruction theorem. The comparison needs only the direction and scope
of the paper's own stated zero-path result.

complete proof, pp6-7. Reverse differences use f(s+kh) with (-1)^alpha.

abstract's broader order wording does not enlarge Theorem4.2's range.

**Applicability decision.** These operations act on zeta in s. Neither

not been equated. No semigroup, composition or Leibniz identity is used
