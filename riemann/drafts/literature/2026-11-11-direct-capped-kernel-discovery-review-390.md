### Primary source contracts actually inspected

**Superoscillatory interpolation comparator.** P. J. S. G. Ferreira and
A. Kempf, *Superoscillations: Faster Than the Nyquist Rate*, IEEE Trans.
Signal Processing 54(10) (2006), 3732–3740, DOI
[10.1109/TSP.2006.877642](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSP.2006.877642),
[author PDF](https://sweet.ua.pt/pjf/PDF/Ferreira2006a.pdf),
Theorem2 and SectionsIII-IV, pp3736–3738. The contract uses finite-energy
bandlimited signals interpolating prescribed real values at distinct
real times. The minimum-energy solution is a sinc combination determined
by its nonsingular Gram matrix. Inspected the variational construction,
energy/eigenvector case and the following warning: the extreme-eigenvalue
energy bound is attained only for the corresponding data direction;
it is not a universal lower bound for arbitrary data. The small-spacing
asymptotic has separate parameter hypotheses. Our atomic Fourier sums
are generally not global L2 signals, our cap is coefficient l1, and our
data are complete moving integrals. Thus neither this theorem nor its
conditioning discussion imports a cap obstruction or a construction.

**Bounded analytic propagation.** L. N. Trefethen, *Quantifying the
ill-conditioning of analytic continuation*, BIT 60 (2020), 901–915,
[published author PDF](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/trefethen/trefethen_ac.pdf),
Lemma2.1 p904 and Theorem5.2/proof p908, with Theorem5.1's hypotheses.
The domain is connected and open; the data set is a bounded nonempty
continuum enclosing no remaining domain points, with piecewise smooth
boundary. The function is analytic through the data and bounded by one
globally. Data bound epsilon yields a pointwise power epsilon^alpha,
0<alpha<1. Read the bounded-strip Phragmen–Lindelof proof and the
connecting-arc/conformal reduction. The source supplies no effective
uniform exponent on our shrinking contour or paid original-cap norm.
Review322 already assessed this theorem in the frequency variable;
its scope is reused, with the changed slope-variable purpose explicit.

**Exact germ tools.** NIST DLMF, live pages retrieved 11 November2026,
release identifier not independently verified:
[1.9.30–31](https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.9#E31) give Cauchy value/derivative
formulas for an analytic function inside a simple closed contour,
continuous on it, and an interior evaluation point. Read the full
contour hypotheses. [1.10.18–19](https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.10#viii)
allow differentiation of an integral over a fixed finite real segment
when the integrand is jointly continuous and analytic in the parameter;
the improper version additionally needs compact-uniform convergence.
[18.5.12](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5#E12) gives the finite Laguerre
power expansion, here with alpha=2 and degree n+1. It can supply exact
germ coefficients without a bulk asymptotic. Moving endpoints must be
converted to a fixed parameter interval before invoking 1.10.18.
The prospective inverse and its growing-order norm remain new work.

**Contour comparison only.** Read
[DLMF2.4(iii)-(iv)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/2.4#iii): analytic phase/amplitude
on the deformation domain, endpoint behavior, convergence and decay
along the contour are explicit hypotheses. Ordinary saddle asymptotics
do not by themselves control an n-dependent amplitude exponentially.
No saddle theorem is an input to the nominated origin test.

These standard results should be cited, not reproved. Only the
