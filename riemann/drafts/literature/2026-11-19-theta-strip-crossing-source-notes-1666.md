Gerald Teschl, *Ordinary Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems*,
AMS GSM140(2012), [author preliminary edition](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~gerald/ftp/book-ode/ode.pdf),
preface April2012. Read Section4.1, printed pp111-114 (PDF pp121-124),
(4.1)-(4.9), Theorem4.1 and connected-overlap uniqueness; Section4.2,
printed p116, ordinary analytic linear equations. For analytic bounded
f on the rectangle |z-z0|<epsilon, |w-w0|<delta, Theorem4.1 gives the
unique analytic IVP solution on radius min(epsilon,delta/M),
M=sup|f|. The preceding integral proof uses path independence on a disk
and uniform convergence of analytic Picard iterates. The next page
warns that maximal domains need not be unique in general. This local
theorem alone must not be called continuation over an arbitrary disk;
the future constant-coefficient application must justify reconstruction
over the chosen disk, for example by analytic primitives, and match
interior initial data. No assumption of finite boundary values of the
old solution is licensed.

J.-C. Puchta, *On Fabry's gap theorem*, Arch. Math. (Brno)38(2002),
307-309, [primary PDF](https://emis.de/ft/36329): read the opening Fabry
statement, Theorem2 and its proof through p309. Theorem2 assumes radius
one, a subsequence k_l with limsup |a_(k_l)|^(1/k_l)=1, and a fixed
theta>0 for which the count of nonzero coefficients in
[(1-theta)k_l,(1+theta)k_l] is o(k_l). It concludes a natural boundary.
The proof rotates a hypothetical regular boundary point to1 and uses
Cauchy bounds, a power-sum inequality and Parseval. This confirms the
standard terminology and a stronger general source; no gap-theorem
application to the actual differential kernel is derived or needed
here. Its references to Landau/Turan/Szusz were not independently read.
