### Zero handling: a precise known input, not a new lemma

I. Anapolitanos and M. Hott, *Asymptotic behavior of the ground state
energy of a Fermionic Fröhlich multipolaron in the strong coupling
limit*, CRC Preprint 2016/2, 21 January 2016,
[Lemma 2.11, printed p. 10 (PDF p. 12)](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2016-2.pdf#page=12),
states the diamagnetic inequality for a real magnetic vector field
with components in L2_loc and a vector-valued f in its magnetic H1
space, in arbitrary dimension d. The componentwise modulus is in H1
and its gradient norm is bounded almost everywhere by the covariant
gradient norm. It cites Lieb–Loss, *Analysis*, Theorem 7.21 for proof;
that book proof was not independently inspected. Read the full lemma
and this proof-reference qualification, not just the search snippet.

For applicability use d=1, one complex component and magnetic field
zero. A smooth compact localization equal to one on a neighborhood of
[X,X+T] places the finite smooth M in the stated global H1 class. On
that neighborhood the theorem concerns the unchanged M and |M|. No
global integrability of the original Dirichlet polynomial is presumed.
The derivative is weak/a.e.; its value on a null set does not define
a different energy. No classical differentiability at every zero,
positive lower bound for |M|, or division by M is required.

D. Bonheure, M. Nys and J. Van Schaftingen, *Properties of groundstates
of nonlinear Schrödinger equations under a weak constant magnetic
field*, [arXiv:1607.00170v1, Section 2.1.2/(8)–(9), printed p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.00170v1#page=7),
explicitly uses the real scalar product on complex vectors and defines
sign(0)=0 in the weak modulus-chain formula. Read that entire short
subsection. This corroborates the zero-safe interpretation; its PDE
groundstate hypotheses and later conclusions are not imported. The
paper's introduction works in dimension N>=2, so the arbitrary-dimension
Lemma 2.11 above supplies the direct one-dimensional reference.
The arXiv version label is v1 (submitted 1 July 2016); the retrieved
PDF itself carries a 20 October 2018 compilation date. Both are recorded
rather than silently substituting a claimed later arXiv version.

The known a.e. modulus-gradient bound, when weighted by nonnegative
v Z^4, supplies only domination by the existing D. It contains no
strict saving and no comparison with P. This is the necessary scope
comparison of an imported theorem, not a new radial-energy estimate.

### The amplified Wirtinger lead and the missing quantitative input

H. M. Bui, R. R. Hall and M. Subira Jorge,
[*Amplified fourth moment of the Riemann zeta-function and applications*,
arXiv:2511.14415v1, Theorem 4.1, printed p. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14415v1#page=14),
assumes endpoint-zero differentiable complex f and a nonnegative
parameter nu. With ell=(b-a)/pi it states

    3 lambda_0(nu) integral |f|^4
        <= ell^4 integral |f'|^4
                      +6nu ell^2 integral |f f'|^2,
    lambda_0(nu)=(1+4nu+sqrt(1+8nu))/8.

Read its complete proof/(16)–(18), through the concluding comparison,
and the consecutive-zero/gap assumption in Section 4.5, printed p. 15.
The proof first uses g=|f|, then bounds |g'| by |f'|. It offers no
quantitative strict radial saving. The generic isolated-zero assertion
does not follow from mere differentiability; the independent H1 source
above avoids relying on it. The endpoint/gap inequality is not a
weighted upper bound for the present radial moment. Theorem 1.1 uses
a specified divisor-polynomial amplifier to the fourth power; this
differs from the unchanged raw-Mobius square here. No Hall extension,
endpoint partition, taper limit or inverse-amplifier estimate is proved
or assumed in this review.
