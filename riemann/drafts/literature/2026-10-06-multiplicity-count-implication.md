Inspected sources (comparisons, not mathematical imports):

1. MIT 18.034 Spring 2009 Lecture 9, Theorem 9.1, printed pp. 1–2,
   with complete proof, reopened at the URL above. It requires independent
   solutions of one homogeneous second-order equation with continuous
   coefficients. Its endpoint sign argument uses a nowhere-zero Wronskian;
   it contains no weak arbitrary-pair or common-zero extension. Neither
   the common equation nor that nonvanishing premise is available here.

2. J. Borcea, P. Brändén and B. Shapiro, *Pólya–Schur master theorems
   for circular domains and their boundaries*, author Master4 PDF, §3.1,
   printed p. 9, definitions and Theorems 9–10 reopened. The weak polynomial
   formulations allow common roots; their strict versions exclude them.
   Obreschkoff requires every real linear combination to be hyperbolic
   or zero. Hermite–Biehler requires stability of the complex combination.
   A first differential inequality cannot be substituted for these
   hypotheses by citation. Manuscript revision date remains unverified.

3. G. Csordas and A. Escassut, *The Laguerre inequality and the
   distribution of zeros of entire functions*, Annales mathématiques
   Blaise Pascal **12** (2005), 331–345, publisher PDF. Read §4, pp. 338–340:
   Theorem 4.1, Definition 4.2, Theorem 4.3, and Theorem 4.5 with its proof.
   The entire-function Hermite–Biehler characterization requires all real
   combinations to have only real zeros and a strictly positive Wronskian;
   class HB also has a global half-plane zero exclusion and modulus
   inequality. The proof of Theorem 4.5 obtains simplicity from an actual
   imaginary translate in HB. An arbitrary analytic pair on a ray has
   none of these premises. This extends the polynomial comparison without
   supplying the requested count. No inference from weak first Laguerre
   signs to full pencil real-rootedness is licensed.

4. M. Tyaglov, *On the number of real critical points of logarithmic
   derivatives and the Hawaii conjecture*, arXiv:0902.0413v2, 3 February
   2009. Read Definitions 1–2/count conventions, printed p. 2, and Theorem 8
   with its terminating induction, pp. 36–37. The class L-P* consists of
   a Laguerre–Pólya function times a real polynomial without real roots.
   If such a function has 2m nonreal zeros with multiplicity, its
   logarithmic-derivative derivative has at most 2m real zeros with
   multiplicity. This counts a differential expression of one global
   entire function, rather than the zeros of two unrestricted functions.
   The induction uses finitely many nonreal zeros. Neither that class nor
   the derivative relationship is assumed in TARGET. This version is
   used only for comparison, not as a verified input to a new proof.

5. D. Gnatyuk and M. Tyaglov, *Hawaii conjecture through the lens of
   Cauchy indices*, arXiv:2506.22330v1 (27 June 2025; manuscript dated
   30 June), PDF and HTML. Read the p_sigma=p′+sigma p definition,
   Q[p]=(p′/p)′ convention, Theorem 4.5 and its complete proof,
   printed pp. 6–9. Under Q[p_sigma]<0 wherever defined on the whole
   real line it gives Z_R(Q[p])=Z_C(p)−Z_C(p_sigma), with multiplicities.
   The proof explicitly separates shared roots of p,p_sigma, extra
   multiple roots of p_sigma, the p′=0 and p′≠0 pole cases, and sigma=0
   with its changed index at infinity. Thus multiplicities are accounted
   for, but via a fixed derivative relation and rational behavior at
   infinity. The theorem supplies no finite-interval bound for arbitrary
   real analytic F,G. Its strict inequality is imposed only away from
   zeros of p_sigma, unlike TARGET's physical inequality at every point.
   No specialization to TARGET is established.
