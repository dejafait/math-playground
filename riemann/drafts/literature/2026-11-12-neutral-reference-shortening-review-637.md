### Primary statements and relevant proof cases

[Corach, Maestripieri and Stojanoff, *Oblique projections and Schur
complements*, arXiv:math/0006120v1,16June2000](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0006120v1):
read Definition3.1, Lemma3.2/proof pp5-6, Proposition3.3/proof p6,
Theorem4.1 pp9-10, and Proposition4.2/full proof p10 (PDF printed pages).
For bounded positive B and a closed subspace S, Theorem4.1(3) gives
the short to S-perp as B^(1/2) P_M B^(1/2), where
M={v:B^(1/2)v belongs to S-perp}. This is a preimage notation,
not a bounded inverse square root. Theorem4.1(2) gives its block
Schur formula using a reduced square-root solution. Compatibility is
the range condition in Proposition3.3. Proposition4.2 proves attainment
as B(I-E), with E a B-selfadjoint projection onto S. Its proof verifies
positivity, support and maximality directly. Ambient invertibility of B
is unnecessary. Theorem4.1 attributes earlier results to Anderson--Trapp
and Pekarev; their original proofs were not retrieved or used separately.

[Pappas, *Minimization of constrained quadratic forms in Hilbert spaces*,
Ann.Funct.Anal.2(1)(2011),1-12](https://ftp.gwdg.de/pub/misc/EMIS/journals/AFA/AFA-tex_v2_n1_a1.pdf):
read the closed-range convention p2 and Theorem3.4/full proof p6.
It treats a bounded positive invertible cost T and a bounded closed-range
constraint A, assuming feasibility. Writing T=Q^2, its optimizer is
Q^-1(AQ^-1)^dagger b; the proof reduces to a least-norm solution.
It cannot be applied directly with cost R, which is unbounded, or with
cost R^-1, whose bounded inverse is unavailable. No such application is
imported. It is a useful domain warning, not the essential theorem.
