### 1. Localized heat contraction

D.H.J. Polymath, *Effective approximation of heat flow evolution of the
Riemann xi function, and a new upper bound for the de Bruijn--Newman
constant*, arXiv:1904.12438v2, 4 August2019,
https://arxiv.org/html/1904.12438v2 , Proposition3.1(i)--(ii),
Theorem3.2 and Proposition3.3 with the complete Section3 proofs read.
Theorem3.2 cites de Bruijn's Theorem13 rather than reproving it; the
original 1950 proof was not retrieved here. The actual-family localized
criterion is proved in the inspected Polymath source.

The normalization is H_0(z)=xi(1/2+iz/2)/8, as in C019j. For a simple
zero, Proposition3.1 gives dz_j/dt=2 sum'_(k!=j)1/(z_j-z_k), with
k,-k paired, and the exceptional -j term separate. At a multiplicity-m
zero it gives the local splitting
z_j+sqrt(2)*(t-t_0)^(1/2)*lambda_l+O(|t-t_0|), where lambda_l are the
m distinct real probabilists' Hermite roots. Constants in that local
error may depend on the zero, time and multiplicity. The proof pays
paired Hadamard convergence, and uses the repeated-root splitting before
the simple-root velocity argument; there is no uniform collision-free
assumption available for the whole family.

Proposition3.3 requires t_0,X>0 and 0<y_0<=1, and all three exclusions:

1. H_0 has no zero with 0<=x<=X and
   sqrt(y_0^2+2*t_0)<=y<=1.
2. H_(t_0) has no zero with x>=X+sqrt(1-y_0^2) and
   y_0<=y<=sqrt(1-2*t_0).
3. For 0<=t<=t_0, H_t has no zero with
   X<=x<=X+sqrt(1-y_0^2) and
   sqrt(y_0^2+2*(t_0-t))<=y<=sqrt(1-2*t).

The conclusion excludes all zeros of H_(t_0) with y>=y_0. The proof
uses (3) to prevent entrance through the side boundary, handles repeated
first contact by Proposition3.1(ii), and at simple contact groups exterior
zeros with their conjugates. Its sufficient pair inequality is
y_k^2<=(x-x_k)^2+Y(t)^2, with Y(t)=sqrt(y_0^2+2*(t_0-t)). This inequality
is from that proof, not a newly established estimate. Geometry and the
assumed exclusions pay it; ignoring unknown exterior zeros does not.

Theorem1.5(i)--(iv) and Proposition9.1(i)'s entire Dirichlet-tail proof
were also retrieved. The real-zero conclusions require x or index at
least exp(C/t), for a sufficiently large absolute C, whereas (iv) counts
all zeros by real part. Assessments1367/1370 already record this limit;
no repeat threshold comparison is claimed as new evidence. C019j/t's
real-axis value/norm comparison pays none of the three complex exclusions
above, and is not a complex root-count comparison. This candidate fails
the source-import test at these explicit unpaid inputs. No new zero-free
region, strip bound, heat estimate or contraction specialization is
proposed, so the October zero-free-region baseline is not reopened.

### 3. Complex-zero-decreasing coefficient transformations

Thomas Craven and George Csordas, *Complex zero decreasing sequences*,
Methods Appl.Anal.2 (1995),420--441; inspected author manuscript
https://www.math.hawaii.edu/~tom/mathfiles/zds.pdf , manuscript pp.2--5,
Definitions1.2--1.5, (1.4)--(1.6), Theorem1.4, Theorem1.7 and Example1.8.
The publisher PDF returned403. The author publication page supplied the
working manuscript link, so this source blocker was resolved. The
manuscript has 28 pages; its page numbers are not journal pagination.
Theorem1.4 imports Laguerre/Pólya and is not reproved in this manuscript;
no independent audit of that classical theorem is claimed.

CZDS means Z_c(T[p])<=Z_c(p) for every real polynomial p, counting
nonreal roots with multiplicity and allowing degree loss. Theorem1.4
assumes that the interpolating polynomial has only real roots outside
(0,deg p), or that its entire interpolant is LP with that exclusion.
The same source's (1.4)--(1.5) gives Pólya--Schur's multiplier/Jensen
equivalence. Thus taking the actual gamma sequence as an unverified
CZDS introduces a stronger premise than the unpaid multiplier property;
it is not an independent small-deficit estimate. Using another sequence
would require its exact coefficient map to the actual J_d, its certified
CZDS property and a useful input count. None has been supplied.

Example1.8 explicitly distinguishes preservation from decreasing the
number of complex roots. Its multiplier 1+k+k^2 has exponential symbol
(1+x)^2 exp(x), but the source gives an input p with Z_c(p)=2 and
Z_c(T[p])=4. The paper explicitly withdraws earlier general claims that
all multiplier sequences are CZDS. We import this as a cited source
obstruction, not a new locally certified counterexample; no reproof or
arithmetic replay was performed. Bare preservation therefore cannot
justify a deficit reduction on nonhyperbolic inputs.

The author's later *Composition theorems, multiplier sequences and
complex zero decreasing sequences*, in *Value Distribution Theory and
Related Topics*, Kluwer2004, manuscript
https://www.math.hawaii.edu/~tom/mathfiles/czdssurvey.pdf , pp.24--26,
Definition4.3, the qualification immediately before Theorem4.4,
Example4.5 and Problems4.6/4.8 were inspected. It corrects the strictness
in 1995 Theorem1.7: general lambda-sequences are semidefinite; the
strict determinant equivalences concern positive definite sequences.
Do not reject a possible CZDS merely because a reciprocal determinant
vanishes. Example4.5 reproduces the counterexample; Problem4.8 is a
question, not a sufficient theorem. No claim about its present open
status is made from a 2004 source.
