### Inspected primary sources

1. Mika Mattila, *On the eigenvalues of combined meet and join matrices*,
   [arXiv:1403.5907v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.5907v2),
   Linear Algebra Appl. 466 (2015), 1-20. Section2 permits complex f on
   a locally finite lattice. Proposition2.5, pp5-6, states the
   meet-closed incidence factorization E D E^T, with its specified
   Mobius-convolution diagonal; Proposition2.3, pp4-5, uses A A^T.
   The transpose is not an adjoint. The integer divisor lattice has
   bottom 1 and [1,N] is factor-closed, so these algebraic hypotheses
   fit. Theorem3.1, pp7-10, instead requires real nonzero
   semimultiplicative f, real exponents and positive convolution values;
   its proof uses positive squares. Theorem4.1, pp11-13, allows complex f
   with its modulus-ratio hypothesis but gives eigenvalue enclosures,
   not singular-value contraction. Read both relevant proof cases.
   The PDF header identifies v2, 5 November2015; its cover also displays
   5 July2021. We identify the served version and numbered passages.
   No complex lower-bound extension is imported.

2. Pentti Haukkanen and Laszlo Toth, *Inertia, positive definiteness
   and ell_p norm of GCD and LCM matrices and their unitary analogs*,
   [arXiv:1707.09473v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09473v1),
   served PDF cover dated 24 September2018; header dates v1 to
   29 July2017. Equations(2.1)-(2.2), p3, and (3.1), p5, give the
   arithmetic incidence/convolution form for GCD-closed sets.
   Theorem4.1, p8, assumes real m>0; its prime-power Jordan values
   are positive and its proof uses that positivity. Those hypotheses
   do not authorize replacing L928's complex weights by positive ones.
   This corroborates factorization coverage, without a joint bound.

3. Antal Bege, *Generalized GCD matrices*, Acta Univ. Sapientiae,
   Mathematica 2(2) (2010), 160-167,
   [arXiv:1012.5445v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.5445v1).
   Equations(2)-(3), p161, record the Smith/Carlitz divisor-incidence
   identity for f(n)=sum_(d|n)g(d). Theorem1 and its complete

   Its Carlitz1960 reference was identified but not retrieved; the

4. Rajendra Bhatia, *Positive Definite Matrices*, Princeton University
   Press (2007), [Chapter1, pp13-14](https://assets.press.princeton.edu/chapters/s8445.pdf).
   For finite complex matrices and A,B>=0, Proposition1.3.2 says
   [[A,V],[V*,B]]>=0 iff V=A^(1/2) K B^(1/2), ||K||<=1.
   For A,B>0, Theorem1.3.3 gives A>=V B^(-1)V*, equivalently
