## Degree-changing comparison (imported, not reproved)

Rajesh Pereira, *Differentiators and the geometry of polynomials*,
J. Math. Anal. Appl. **285** (2003), 336-348,
DOI10.1016/S0022-247X(03)00465-7,
[primary-paper text](https://www.academia.edu/20926761/Differentiators_and_the_geometry_of_polynomials).
Read Definitions5.1-5.2, Proposition5.3, Theorem5.4 and its entire proof,
Corollary5.5, pp345-347; also Definitions2.3/2.6, Theorem2.5,
Corollaries2.7-2.8 and multiplicity convention, pp337-341.
The publisher endpoint returned403; the complete relevant paper text
was available through the linked mirror. No inaccessible theorem is used.

For any degree-n complex polynomial, n>=2, with roots z_j and critical
points w_i, Corollary5.5 says for every convex Phi:C->R,

    (1/(n-1)) sum_i Phi(w_i) <= (1/n) sum_j Phi(z_j).

All roots count multiplicities. Theorem5.4 realizes w=S z with
nonnegative S, row sums1 and column sums(n-1)/n. Its proof compresses a
normal diagonal root matrix using a trace vector, then triangularizes
the compression. Repeated eigenvalues are permitted; neither simplicity
nor root reality is required. This is a theorem about derivative roots,
not roots of an averaged characteristic polynomial. It controls the
lower degree by the higher degree. It cannot upper-bound parent heights
from daughter heights. No reproof or new hinge specialization is made.

The 2005 Pereira Theorem10 already inspected in assessment1493 concerns
p-alpha p' at unchanged degree. It is not the degree-changing statement.
The actual reciprocal identity matches the latter's derivative interface,
but supplies no inverse cost. Also inversion preserves real-rootedness
while changing imaginary height: a contraction in reciprocal coordinates
is not automatically the desired ordinary-Jensen payment.
