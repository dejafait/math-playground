### New primary source coverage

Norbert Hungerbühler and Micha Wasem, *Non-Integer Valued Winding
Numbers and a Generalized Residue Theorem*, Journal of Mathematics
2019, Article6130464, [Definition2 p2, Proposition3/proof p3,
Theorem8/proof p7](https://www.math.ch/norbert.hungerbuehler/publications/Non-Integer_Valued_Winding_Numbers_and_a_Generalized_Residue_Theorem.pdf),
DOI10.1155/2019/6130464. Read the spatial-distance principal-value
definition, sector comparison and simple-pole decomposition. Theorem8
assumes open U, discrete S, holomorphic f on U minus S, and an immersed
piecewise-C1 cycle null-homologous in U. With only simple poles on the
cycle it gives the principal-value residue sum weighted by generalized
winding numbers. Proposition3 tracks the oriented sector contributions
when contour points meet the singularity. Its proof replaces small
pieces by clockwise detours; the weighted count is not automatically
an integer. Higher-order boundary poles require the theorem's extra
flatness and angle/Laurent conditions, not assumed here. This provides
a conditional boundary-zero tool, not an index of an arbitrary smooth
coefficient. The next attempt must verify its own contour hypotheses
and relate its quantities to this principal-value convention.

Jiří Lebl, *Guide to Cultivating Complex Analysis*, v1.10,
5 November 2026, [Theorem5.4.1 and proof, pp136-137](https://www.jirka.org/ca/ca.pdf#page=136).
The usual argument principle assumes a meromorphic function on an
open U and a cycle homologous to zero there, with no zeros or poles
on the cycle. The integral of f'/f is the multiplicity-weighted
divisor count; the proof factors finite-order zeros/poles and computes
their logarithmic-derivative residues. Read both the hypotheses and
this residue computation. The zero-avoiding theorem cannot silently
count a boundary zero. The cited factorization applies to G since
G is entire and G(0)>0. Its one-variable holomorphic statement is not
a theorem that the smooth parameter density Q is meromorphic.
Any avoidance/limit argument must be justified separately; this
review makes no such argument or count.

University of Toronto, course-hosted *DiffGeomNotes*, Chapter4,
[Lemma4.2 (Hadamard Lemma), statement/proof/footnote, printed p72](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/mgualt/courses/18-367/docs/DiffGeomNotes-8.pdf#page=4),
retrieved 6 November 2026; excerpt has no checked version date or
author attribution. It states smooth coordinate division on an open
ball and proves it by coordinate telescoping and Taylor remainder.
The smooth one-variable divided difference is explicitly included.
This is elementary support for extracting normal coefficients once
the actual vanishing is verified, not a statement that an arbitrary
smooth pullback has finite order. Any use with the other angle as
parameter must retain smooth parameter dependence and periodic
identifications. Import Taylor's theorem with integral remainder as
the standard named calculus result; no general smooth-division
theorem needs reproving. This review extracts no target coefficient.
