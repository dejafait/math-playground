### Exact theorem, normalization and form qualifications

Primary citation: **Li Hongmin**, *Cutoff-free positivity of localized
Weil forms through the (log 11)/2 threshold*, rStation paper 45, **v1**,
23 October 2026, [Theorem 1.1 and Sections 2–8](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/45/v1).
The theorem asserts inf sigma(A_a)>0 for 0<a<=a_*=(log 11)/2, for the
Friedrichs realization of (2.1), including both complex parity spaces.
The closure is that of the smooth compact core, with the logarithmic
Fourier energy; this is not a claim that every L2 test has finite energy.
The additional assertion a_deg>a_* uses eigenvalue continuity from
Suzuki [1]; no quantitative interval to the right is supplied.

The Fourier transform is integral f(x) exp(-itx) dx, the Fourier
quadratic measure is dt/(2pi), and h(t)=Re psi(1/4+it/2)-log pi.
Equation (2.1) retains the product
2 Re(F(i/2) conjugate(F(-i/2))) and every prime-power correlation
-2 Lambda(n)/sqrt(n) Re C_f(log n). These conventions match the
displayed complete smooth energy of L744 (6) after the interval
translation, whose applicability must still be stated at any import.
L741 (3) imports only Zhu's smaller certificate and is not strengthened.

The unitary parity map is f(x)=v(x)/sqrt(2) for x>0 and
f(-x)=epsilon v(x)/sqrt(2), epsilon=+/-1. Equations (2.2)–(2.3)
give the signed rank-one pole epsilon p_epsilon tensor p_epsilon,
p_epsilon(x)=exp(x/2)+epsilon exp(-x/2), and the partial shifts and
reflection, each with its support indicator. At a_* the active atoms
are n=2,3,4,5,7,8,9; n=11 has zero-measure overlap. Thus the endpoint
is included without an extra positive-measure n=11 contribution.
The source uses real matrices for these real kernels but states the
Hermitian form result for complex coefficients in both parity spaces.
