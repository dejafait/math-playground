# Strict PF256 reciprocal-polynomial source comparison — working record

This is a literature-only step. No density, determinant or polynomial sign
has been calculated, and no new mathematical conclusion is asserted.

Main gap: actual-Xi all-degree positivity, with the complete first-sign
signed remainder still uncontrolled. The reviewed intermediate implication
is exactly the startup strict-PF256 reciprocal-polynomial question. Its
downstream use is to distinguish a possible finite-order sign bridge from
a generic-density obstruction; Xi realization and higher signs are separate.

Sources located/read so far:

- Sra, riem-xi v2, Theorem 1.2: the ordered minors are strictly positive
  for every size 1 through 256 and all strictly increasing real tuples.
  Parent Proposition 3.3/Section 3.2 assessment is reused with its limits.
- Khare, arXiv:2008.05121v2 (12 April 2021), Theorem D: compact support,
  positivity on its interior, a specified TN_p rectangle, and zero exclusion
  for the bilateral transform in an imaginary strip. This is not a theorem
  locating poles of a rational reciprocal transform of a positive density
  on the whole real line. Its relevant proof uses sampled Toeplitz matrices,
  a polynomial sector theorem, compact Riemann sums and Hurwitz.
- Khare Theorem E: fixed-order determinant/decay characterization; it is
  not the all-order reciprocal-entire characterization.
- Gröchenig, arXiv:2007.12889v1, Theorem 1: Schoenberg's characterization
  assumes nonnegative ordered minors of every size. No finite-order
  substitution is licensed.
- Sra v2 Appendix C.4, Lemma C.6 and its complete short proof: strict signed
  derivative Hankel determinants through m, with C^(2m-2) regularity,
  suffice for all strict ordered minors through m by two Wronskian
  transfers. Lemma C.7 is specific to a separately certified Xi convolution;
  that certificate is not audited or transferred here.

Classical DOI access failed for Schoenberg 1951 and 1955. Khare's own
theorem/proof is available; the originals are not silently claimed read.
Shell download also failed (DNS); browser primary text/PDF remains available.

Continuation test: a covering theorem with all hypotheses, an applicable
source obstruction, or one fixed separately assessed diagnostic. No search
failure proves that the universal implication is false. Candidate direction
under consideration: a high-rank real-pole convolution plus one fixed
nonreal pair, using C.6 as a sufficient strictness interface. This would
require new global determinant estimates and is not yet approved or proved.
