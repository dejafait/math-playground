# Lemma 586: periodic second-cone interiority and carrier absorption

## Hypotheses

Use the six ordinary coordinates a=(r,j), r=0,1 and j=0,1,2, in
the order of L473. For a real smooth periodic function h with period
P>0, put

    J_h(s)=(h(s),h'(s),h''(s),h(s+1),h'(s+1),h''(s+1)),
    C(h)=P^(-1) integral_0^P J_h(s) J_h(s)^T ds.

Let C_per be the cone of finite nonnegative sums of these matrices,
including the zero sum. Periods may differ between summands. There is
no prescribed total weight or frequency cutoff. Requiring each path
to satisfy sup_s |J_h(s)|<=1 defines the same cone: divide h by
M=max(1,sup_s |J_h(s)|) and multiply its weight by M^2.

K is exactly the real symmetric matrix in L473 (6):

    K_((r,j),(s,k))
      =integral_(-1/2)^(1/2) (iu)^j(-iu)^k exp(i(r-s)u) du.   (1)

Fix any one finite representation in L585 (1), with paths h_i and
nonnegative weights a_i, and set

    R=sum_i a_i C(h_i),       W_4=sum_i a_i<infinity.

This is a fixed matrix, independent of the parameter ell below. No
numerical paths, weights, R, W_4 or onset are supplied. The theorem is
an analytic statement about the displayed K and periodic paths. Its
Hardy-moment interpretation retains L473's arithmetic-source
qualifications; it does not audit those source proofs or reprove L585.

## Conclusion

K lies in the relative interior of C_per, in its actual linear span.
More strongly, it lies in the interior relative to that same span of
the cone generated by single-frequency periodic paths with angular
frequencies |u|<=1/2, including the constant path at zero.

There is a fixed finite ell_0>=1 such that for every real ell>=ell_0,

    ell K-R in C_per.                                      (2)

Each matrix in (2) has an exact finite representation by the stated
single-frequency paths. The paths can individually be normalized to
sup |J_h|<=1 by changing their nonnegative weights. No common period,
bound on total normalized weight, probability normalization or
uniform choice of nodes as ell varies is claimed.

This supplies derivative-consistent positive second completion at the
cone level. It is a local specialization of the cited moment-cone,
finite-representation and Fourier theorems, not a new general theorem
about those subjects. It does not construct a probability law meeting
the simultaneous second/fourth tolerances, prove support at the final
ell-dependent scale, or supply positive clipped mass or actual-Xi
signs. The separate critical application audit in Section 6 passes;
L473's arithmetic-source qualifications remain unchanged.

## Proof

### 1. Spectral curve and its exact periodic generators

For real u define the complex column v(u) and real symmetric matrix A(u)
by

    v_(r,j)(u)=(iu)^j exp(iru),
    A_((r,j),(s,k))(u)=Re((iu)^j(-iu)^k exp(i(r-s)u)).          (3)

Thus A(u)=Re(v(u)v(u)^*) and A(-u)=A(u). For real c,
c^T A(u)c=|sum_a c_a v_a(u)|^2>=0; this ambient positivity is not
the interior argument below. In particular A_((0,0),(0,0))(u)=1.

For u!=0, take g_u(t)=sqrt(2) cos(ut), of period 2pi/|u|.
Its a-th lagged derivative is sqrt(2) Re(v_a(u) exp(iut)).
The average of exp(2iut) over that period is zero. Expanding the
product of the two real parts therefore gives

    C(g_u)_ab=Re(v_a(u) conjugate(v_b(u)))=A_ab(u).           (4)

At u=0 use g_0(t)=1, with any positive period: its derivative
coordinates vanish, so C(g_0)=A(0). The exceptional normalization
at zero is necessary; sqrt(2) cos(0t) would give 2A(0).
All these paths are real and smooth. Normalizing their jets as in
the hypotheses preserves membership in C_per.

### 2. The actual periodic cone equals the finite spectral cone

Let C_spec=cone{A(u):u in R}, where cone always means finite
nonnegative combinations. Equation (4) gives C_spec subset C_per.
For the converse, let h be a real smooth P-periodic path, put
u_n=2pi n/P, and let

    c_n=P^(-1) integral_0^P h(t) exp(-iu_n t) dt.

We use the Fourier orthonormal basis and bilinear Parseval theorem on
the circle with probability Haar measure, specifically Terence Tao,
*245C, Notes 2: The Fourier transform* (6 April 2009), Section 2,
[Theorem 21, Corollary 22 and translation formula (3)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2009/04/06/the-fourier-transform/).
The circle here is R/PZ; its characters are exp(2pi int/P).
Integration by parts j times, with cancelling periodic boundary terms,
and translation of the integral show that h^(j)(t+r) has coefficient
(iu_n)^j exp(iru_n)c_n. Parseval for these two real functions gives

    C(h)_ab=sum_(n in Z) |c_n|^2
                       v_a(u_n) conjugate(v_b(u_n)).

Every entry sum is absolutely convergent by Cauchy--Schwarz applied
to the two L2 coefficient sequences. Taking real parts yields

    C(h)=sum_(n in Z) |c_n|^2 A(u_n).                       (5)

This retains the zero mode and both real conjugate modes, with no
extra factor two in the all-integer sum. Equivalently the nonzero
positive-frequency terms have weight 2|c_n|^2 because h is real.
In particular the same-height entries (j,k)=(0,1),(1,2) are zero,
while (0,2) is minus (1,1), as required for ordinary derivatives.

To deduce finite membership from (5), not just closure membership,
consider the positive measure mu=sum_n |c_n|^2 delta_(u_n).
Parseval for h and h'' gives

    integral_R (1+u^4) dmu(u)<infinity.

Also |A(u)|_F<=|v(u)|^2=2(1+u^2+u^4), where |.|_F is the
Frobenius norm. Thus the 21 distinct entries of A define an
integrable measurable vector map. Apply Christian Bayer and Josef
Teichmann, *The proof of Tchakaloff's theorem*, Proc. Amer. Math.
Soc. 134 (2006), 3035--3040,
[arXiv:math/0502473v2, Corollary 2 and proof, pp.3--4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0502473v2#page=3),
to this map and mu. Its hypotheses are a positive measure, a
measurable finite-dimensional vector map and finite first norm of
the push-forward, all verified above. It supplies finitely many
frequencies and nonnegative weights reproducing integral A dmu.
If mu=0, use the zero sum instead. Consequently C(h) belongs to
C_spec, and finite sums preserve this inclusion. Hence

    C_per=C_spec.                                          (6)

No closedness of either cone or common period for the quadrature
frequencies was used. The cited finite-representation theorem is
imported, not reproved.

### 3. Restricted and unrestricted frequency spans coincide

Let I=[-1/2,1/2],

    V=span_R{A(u):u in R},
    V_I=span_R{A(u):u in I},     C_I=cone{A(u):u in I}.

Identify symmetric matrices with their 21 unordered entries for
these finite-dimensional statements. Each entry of A has the entire
extension

    A_ab(z)=( (iz)^j(-iz)^k exp(i(r-s)z)
             +(-iz)^j(iz)^k exp(-i(r-s)z) )/2.              (7)

It agrees with (3) for real z. If a real linear functional lambda
on symmetric matrices annihilates V_I, lambda(A(z)) is entire
and zero on I. The holomorphic identity theorem makes it identically
zero, so lambda annihilates V. Conversely every annihilator of V
annihilates V_I. Equality of annihilators in a finite-dimensional
space proves

    V_I=V.                                                (8)

This is an exact relation test, not a sampled rank or an assumption
that V is the whole space of symmetric matrices. From (6), V is the
linear span of C_per. Since the cones contain zero, their affine
hulls equal their linear spans. In particular C_I and C_per have
the same affine hull V.

### 4. Strict positivity in the actual function space

Let E be the finite-dimensional real space of functions on I spanned
by the entries of A|_I. These are continuous real analytic functions;
the constant 1 is among them. Use the actual function space, so
identically zero coordinate combinations are already identified.
The map

    V^* -> E,     lambda -> (u -> lambda(A(u)))

is an isomorphism: it is onto by the definition of E, and injective
because A(I) spans V by (8). Dually, for M in V there is a
well-defined linear functional L_M on E satisfying

    L_M(lambda(A(.)))=lambda(M).

This identifies V with E^*. Under this identification A(u) is the
evaluation functional at u, and C_I is precisely the finite
nonnegative evaluation cone.

The imaginary part of the integrand in (1) is odd in u, by
conjugation under u -> -u. Therefore

    K=integral_I A(u) du,
    L_K(f)=integral_I f(u) du  for every f in E.             (9)

In particular K belongs to V, a closed finite-dimensional subspace.
If f is nonnegative on I and is a nonzero element of E, there is
u_0 in I with f(u_0)>0. Continuity gives a relatively open interval
on which f stays positive. That interval has positive Lebesgue
measure even when u_0 is an endpoint. Thus

    L_K(f)>0 for every nonzero f in E with f>=0 on I.       (10)

Apply Grigoriy Blekherman and Lawrence Fialkow, *The core variety
and representing measures in the truncated moment problem*,
[arXiv:1804.04276v1, Proposition 2.1 and Corollary 2.2, pp.5--6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.04276v1#page=6).
Their setup is a nonempty T1 space and a finite-dimensional space of
real Borel functions; here I with its usual topology and E satisfy
these hypotheses. Corollary 2.2 identifies strict positivity on
the nonzero nonnegative functions with interior membership in the
finite evaluation cone, and provides finite atomic representation.
Condition (10) supplies the exact strict-positivity hypothesis.
Transporting this statement through V=E^* proves

    K in int_V C_I.                                       (11)

This application uses the function-space quotient, not merely
positive definiteness of the 6 by 6 matrix K. It asserts actual
finite-cone interior, so it does not substitute membership in the
closure. Since C_I subset C_per and both have affine hull V,
(11) also proves K in relint C_per. No additional convexity
theorem or reproof of the imported interior criterion is needed.

### 5. One eventual ray for the fixed carrier covariance

By definition and (6), R belongs to C_per subset V. Its size is
finite; more specifically L585's phasewise jet bound gives

    |R|_F<=trace R=sum_i a_i average |J_(h_i)|^2<=W_4,       (12)

where the first inequality follows from nonnegative eigenvalues
of the positive semidefinite matrix R. Choose epsilon>0 with

    {K+D:D in V, |D|_F<epsilon} subset C_I,

as permitted by (11). Set

    ell_0=max(1, 2|R|_F/epsilon).

For every real ell>=ell_0, |R/ell|_F<=epsilon/2 if R!=0;
for R=0 the norm is zero. Hence K-R/ell is in C_I. Multiplying
its finite nonnegative representation by ell proves
ell K-R in C_I subset C_per. Applying (4) to its finitely many
frequencies proves the finite periodic representation in (2),
and the normalization in the hypotheses supplies bounded individual
jets if desired. The interior radius and R are fixed, so the
quantifier holds on one eventual ray of real ell, with no
integer restriction or height-by-height choice of onset.

The argument uses only the fixed carrier covariance, not its fourth
entries or any presumed sign of the clipped observable. Mixing and
scaling these cone representations into a probability law with
simultaneous moment tolerances is a further problem. Neither (2)
nor ambient matrix positivity proves an RH-equivalent sign condition.

**Spectral normalization and finite membership.** For u nonzero the
period average of exp(2iut) is zero, so the two surviving terms in
the product of sqrt(2) real parts give exactly A(u), not A(u)/2
or 2A(u). For u=0, the constant 1 gives the two value coordinates
equal to 1 and all derivative coordinates zero, hence exactly A(0).
For a P-periodic path, integration by parts gives (iu_n)^j and
translation by +r gives exp(iru_n). Bilinear Parseval therefore has
the second factor (-iu_n)^k exp(-isu_n), in agreement with (1).
Reality implies c_(-n)=conjugate(c_n); the all-integer sum (5)
already counts both nonzero conjugate modes. Its zero coefficient
has weight |c_0|^2 with no conjugate-pair factor.

Smoothness supplies all the L2 functions used here; for each entry
Cauchy--Schwarz gives

    sum_n |c_n|^2 |u_n|^(j+k)
      <= (sum_n |c_n|^2 |u_n|^(2j))^(1/2)
         (sum_n |c_n|^2 |u_n|^(2k))^(1/2)<infinity,

with the zeroth power interpreted as 1, including at n=0.
In particular mu is finite and has finite fourth moment. The estimate
on |A(u)|_F follows from |Re(vv^*)|_F<=|vv^*|_F=|v|^2;
u^2<=1+u^4 then gives integrability. The 21-entry map is continuous,
so measurable, and its Euclidean norm is at most the Frobenius norm.
Bayer--Teichmann Corollary 2 applies with the measurable full-measure
node set R and this push-forward; the zero measure is handled by the
empty sum. No mass-normalization inference is needed. In fact the
value-value coordinate A_((0,0),(0,0))=1 is already among the
observables. The conclusion is a finite sum, and every returned
frequency has the exact generator above. This proves equality of
the actual cones, not merely their closures, including constant paths.

**Common span and the precise interior.** In (7) the second term
is the conjugate of the first on the real line, and both terms are
entire in z. Every real coefficient combination has an entire
extension. Vanishing on the interval I supplies an accumulation point
in the complex domain, so the identity theorem applies. Finite-
dimensional annihilator equality gives V_I=V without asserting
dimension 21 or independence of all entries. Thus R, obtained from
arbitrary smooth periodic paths, belongs to the same V as C_I.

The map V^* to E is onto because a real combination of entries is
the restriction of a matrix functional, and injective because A(I)
spans V. Consequently the dual map M to L_M is a linear isomorphism
of finite-dimensional spaces and preserves relative-open sets.
Identically zero functions are the zero element of E, so strict
positivity is checked on exactly the cone required by Blekherman--
Fialkow Corollary 2.2. The interval is nonempty and T1, its functions
are Borel, and its Lebesgue measure charges every nonempty relatively
open interval. A continuous nonnegative nonzero f therefore has
strictly positive integral, including when its positive value is
initially found at an endpoint. Oddness of the imaginary part in
(1) verifies that this integral functional is exactly L_K.
The corollary gives interior of the finite evaluation cone in E^*;
the isomorphism gives int_V C_I. No ambient PSD interior, closure
membership or unverified measure-realization premise substitutes for
this conclusion. Inclusion C_I subset C_per then preserves an open
neighborhood of K in their common affine hull V.

**Uniform absorption.** Each C(h_i) is positive semidefinite, and
the unit-jet assumption gives trace R<=W_4. Thus |R|_F<=W_4 is
finite. Choose one open-ball radius epsilon from the established
interiority. The choice ell_0=max(1,2|R|_F/epsilon) puts K-R/ell
strictly inside that ball for every real ell>=ell_0, also when
R=0. Multiplication of its finite conic representation by ell and
normalization of each whole generator prove the stated conclusion.
This neither fixes the nodes uniformly in ell nor bounds their
normalized total weight; those stronger assertions are not used
here and require the separate construction in L587.

This validates L586 as a standard-theorem specialization. L585 (1)
is the audited carrier input, and L473 (6) identifies K. Arithmetic
proofs, numerical dictionaries/onsets, clipped mass and actual-Xi
transfer are not verified by this audit. No new mathematical input
was added to the existing direct dependencies.
