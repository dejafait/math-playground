# L520 — First-prime-image cusp from two localized inverse substitutions

## Hypotheses

Fix 0<a<A, ell=2a and I=(0,ell). Translate the retained model of
L516 to I, with zero extension E and Fourier convention
hat f(xi)=integral_R f(x)exp(-i xi x)dx. Thus

    Psi(xi)=(1/2)log(1+xi^2), n(x)=exp(-|x|)/(2|x|),
    A_I=G_I-cI-R_I K_(mu_A) E, Dom(A_I)=Dom(G_I),
    c=log(2pi), d_n=log(n), w_n=Lambda(n)/sqrt(n),
    mu_A=nu_rho+sum_(n>=2,d_n<=2A)w_n(delta_(d_n)+delta_(-d_n))
                         -2cosh(x/2)1_(|x|<=2A)dx,
    rho(x)=exp(-x/2)/(1-exp(-2x))-exp(-x)/(2x), x>0,

where nu_rho has the even density rho. G_I is the smooth-core part
operator for Psi, as specified in L513 and L515. Retain all atoms,
including equality at the cutoff, and the full signed pole. The
identification with the full Weil realization and its manuscript proof
retain L516's unaudited external-input qualification.

Assume a real u in Dom(A_I), A_Iu=0, ||u||_2=1. Use L515's bounded
continuous zero extension f=Eu and L513's normalized renewal function:

    V(t)sqrt(log(1/t))->1,
    u(t)=tau_L V(t)+o(V(t)) as t->0+, tau_L!=0.

Existence of such a nullvector and nonvanishing of its trace are test
hypotheses, not conclusions. Put d=log(2), assume ell>d, and require

    d != ell-d_n whenever w_n>0 and d_n<=2A.                 (1)

This excludes a direct image of the right endpoint at the same point.
It imposes no smoothness at other translated points or restriction on
coincidences generated by further translations.

## Conclusion

With w_2=log(2)/sqrt(2), as t decreases to zero,

    u(d+t)-u(d)=(2/3)w_2 tau_L V(t)/log(1/t)
                              +o(V(t)/log(1/t)),             (2)
    u(d-t)-u(d)=-(1/3)w_2 tau_L V(t)/log(1/t)
                              +o(V(t)/log(1/t)).             (3)

The proof uses two localized inverse substitutions. The remainder from
all forcing except the direct left-endpoint image has increment
O(log^(-2)(1/t)); the endpoint trace approximation supplies the remaining
little-o. Constants depend on the fixed model and vector; no uniform
bound as ell, A, u or the isolation distance varies is asserted.

These profiles are compatible local consequences of the assumed
equation. They give neither trace vanishing nor a contradiction, and
do not prove nullvector existence, L516's eigenvalue-relative signed
bound, a branch rate, collars, full-Weil identification or Xi positivity.
The collision case excluded by (1) is not covered.

## Proof

### A high-frequency inverse and its kernel

Write G for the whole-line multiplier Psi. Choose an even eta in
C_c^infinity(R), equal to one on [-1,1] and zero outside [-2,2]. Set

    P=(1+G)^(-1), Q=(1-eta(D))P, K=p-s,
    s=F^(-1)[eta/(1+Psi)],                                   (4)

where p is the convolution kernel of P and K that of Q. The function
s is Schwartz. This is a positive resolvent followed by a smooth
high-frequency cutoff, not a zero-energy Green potential.

Here is a direct kernel computation, including its low-frequency part.
Let b=sqrt(1-exp(-2)). The principal logarithm gives, for z>=0,

    1/[1+(1/2)log(1+z)]
      =2exp(-2)/(z+b^2)
        +integral_1^infinity 2/[(log(r-1)+2)^2+pi^2]
                                      *dr/(z+r).            (5)

To justify (5), apply the Cauchy integral formula to
2/(log(1+w)+2) on the plane cut along (-infinity,-1]. Its only pole
off the cut is w=-b^2, of residue 2exp(-2). On the upper bank at -r
its imaginary part is -2pi/[(log(r-1)+2)^2+pi^2]; the lower bank is
its conjugate. The outer-circle contribution to the Cauchy formula
is O(1/log R). The small circle at -1 tends to zero because the
function is O(1/|log|w+1||). The cut integral converges at infinity
by 1/(r log^2 r) and at r=1. Taking these limits proves (5), with
the residue and the stated sign of the cut term.

The elementary Fourier identity
F{exp(-q|x|)/(2q)}(xi)=1/(q^2+xi^2), q>0, follows by integrating
the two exponentials. Use it in (5), with z=xi^2, to obtain

    p(x)=exp(-2)exp(-b|x|)/b
       +2 integral_1^infinity exp(-y|x|)
                  /[(log(y^2-1)+2)^2+pi^2]dy, x!=0.        (6)

Tonelli and (5) at z=0 give integral_R p=1. Thus (6) is integrable;
Fubini verifies its Fourier transform and hence its identity as the
resolvent kernel, without an unjustified inverse-transform interchange.
It is positive, even, decreasing on (0,infinity), and smooth there.
Put L_x=log(1/x). As x->0+,

    p(x)~1/(2x L_x^2), -p'(x)~1/(2x^2 L_x^2),
    M(x):=integral_(-x)^x p(y)dy ~1/L_x.                    (7)

For completeness, the density in the y integral of (6) is bounded
and is asymptotic to 1/(2log^2 y). Its contribution for
1<=y<=x^(-1/2) is O(x^(-1/2)), and its first derivative contribution
is O(x^(-1)). Both are negligible at their stated scales. On the
remaining range put z=xy. There log y>=L_x/2; after multiplying the
density by L_x^2 it is bounded by an absolute constant and converges
to 1/2 for every fixed z>0. Dominated convergence with exp(-z), or
z exp(-z) for the derivative, proves the first two assertions.
Integrating the first, with upper and lower asymptotic bounds, gives
the third. Formula (6) also gives exponential decay of p and its
first derivative away from zero. In particular

    integral_R [p(x)+|x p'(x)|]dx < infinity,
    x p(x)->0 at both positive endpoints 0 and infinity.    (8)

### Two inverses control arbitrary bounded forcing

The convolution q=p*p is nonnegative, even and nonincreasing on the
positive half-line. Indeed, express each decreasing p by its nonnegative
layer-cake superposition of centered interval indicators. The convolution
of two such indicators is even and nonincreasing; Tonelli passes this
property to q. For any even decreasing integrable q and t>0, splitting
at x=-t/2 gives the exact formula

    ||q(.+t)-q||_1=4 integral_0^(t/2) q(x)dx.               (9)

For small t, estimate integral_(-t)^t(p*p) by splitting its y integral
at |y|=2t. The part |y|<=2t is at most M(2t)M(3t)=O(L_t^(-2)).
For |y|>2t, monotonicity bounds the inner interval integral by
2t p(|y|-t). On 2t<|y|<r_0, for one fixed small r_0, (7) bounds
the resulting integrand by

    C t/[y^2 log^4(1/|y|)].

Its integral on (2t,sqrt(t)) is O(L_t^(-4)); on (sqrt(t),r_0)
it is O(sqrt(t)), using the weaker bound C t/y^2. The remaining
integral is O(t), since p(|y|-t) is bounded there and p is integrable.
Together with (9), this proves ||q(.+t)-q||_1=O(L_t^(-2)).

Now K*K=q-2p*s+s*s. The last two terms have integrable first
derivatives, since s is Schwartz and p is integrable. Consequently,
for every bounded g on R,

    ||(Q^2g)(.+t)-Q^2g||_infinity
                          <=C||g||_infinity L_t^(-2).       (10)

No regularity or sign of g is used. The same argument applied to p
in (9), together with (7), gives ||K(.+t)-K||_1=O(L_t^(-1));
in particular Q sends bounded functions to continuous functions.

### Localization errors are smaller than the requested scale

For any smooth compactly supported cutoff chi and bounded f, define

    C_chi f(x)=integral_R [chi(x)-chi(y)]n(x-y)f(y)dy.

The integral is absolutely convergent and uniformly bounded. Near
the diagonal the Lipschitz difference cancels n's 1/|x-y| bound;
away from it n is integrable. For 0<t<1/4, split the difference
at |x-y|<=2t. The contribution there is O(t)||f||_infinity,
because both integrands are bounded on a set of length O(t).
On 2t<|x-y|<1 the derivative in x of the kernel is bounded by
C/|x-y|: use |chi(x)-chi(y)|<=C|x-y| and
|n'(x-y)|<=C/|x-y|^2. The remaining tail has an integrable
derivative bound. The mean value theorem therefore gives

    ||C_chi f(.+t)-C_chi f||_infinity
                         <=C||f||_infinity t(1+L_t).        (11)

Also [chi,Q]g has a Lipschitz bound C||g||_infinity. Its kernel
is [chi(x)-chi(y)]K(x-y). Off the diagonal its x derivative is
bounded in absolute integral by
C integral_R (|K(r)|+|r K'(r)|)dr, finite by (8) and the Schwartz
property of s. The kernel tends to zero at the diagonal because
r p(r)->0; hence its distributional derivative has no diagonal
atom. Integrating this derivative and using Fubini proves the
Lipschitz bound. This argument also bounds the commutator itself.
Convolution by K preserves any uniform modulus up to ||K||_1.
Multiplication by a fixed smooth cutoff preserves (11), with an
additional O(t) term for a bounded function.

### Retaining the complete equation in two substitutions

Set nu=nu_rho-2cosh(x/2)1_(|x|<=2A)dx and define globally

    H=(c+1)f+sum_(n>=2,d_n<=2A)w_n[T_(d_n)f+T_(-d_n)f]+nu*f,
    T_s f(x)=f(x+s).                                        (12)

H is bounded. The density of nu is of bounded variation on R.
Indeed rho extends to rho(0)=3/4 with a bounded one-sided derivative,
by Taylor expansion of its displayed expression; rho and rho' are
integrable at infinity by exponential decay. Its even extension is
continuous at zero. The truncated pole is smooth between its two
finite jumps. Thus

    ||(nu*f)(.+t)-nu*f||_infinity <=Var(nu)||f||_infinity |t|, (13)

where Var(nu) here means the total variation of the distributional
derivative of its density. This retains both signed densities.

The weak equation is (1+G)f=H on I. For chi in C_c^infinity(I),
the full-line distributional localization identity is

    (1+G)(chi f)=chi H+C_chi f.

To justify it at this regularity, multiplication by chi preserves
the logarithmic form domain: expand chi(x)f(x)-chi(y)f(y), and
use the finite bound integral min(1,r^2)n(r)dr for the cutoff
difference. Pair with a compactly supported smooth test in the
bilinear form; the difference giving C_chi is absolutely integrable
by its preceding kernel bound. This proves the identity without
assuming f belongs to the whole-line operator domain. To justify inversion,
set h_chi(r)=min(||chi'||_infinity |r|,2||chi||_infinity)n(r).
This is integrable, and |C_chi f|<=h_chi*|f|, so Young's inequality
gives C_chi f in L2(R). Also chi H is in L2, since H is bounded and
chi has compact support. Taking the Fourier transform of the
distributional identity now gives

    (1+Psi) F(chi f)=F(chi H+C_chi f) in L2(R).

Thus the localized chi f belongs to the whole-line multiplier domain
and equals P(chi H+C_chi f). This does not assert that the unlocalized
f belongs to that domain. Applying 1-eta(D) proves

    chi f=Q(chi H)+R_chi,
    R_chi=eta(D)(chi f)+Q C_chi f.                           (14)

The low-frequency term is Lipschitz, and (11) gives R_chi a uniform
O(t(1+L_t)) modulus. All terms in (14) are continuous, so its
distributional equality is also an equality of representatives.

Choose chi_0 supported in a sufficiently small neighborhood of d,
equal to one in a smaller neighborhood. In (12) the direct term
T_(-d)f samples the left endpoint. Every other sampled center d+s,
including s=0 for the scalar term, is strictly inside I or strictly
outside [0,ell]. Indeed the negative prime shifts for n>2 are below
zero, d-d_n=0 occurs only at n=2, and (1) excludes d+d_n=ell.
There are finitely many shifts. We can therefore choose chi_0 so
that each remaining sampled support is entirely inside or entirely
outside. Outside terms vanish on supp chi_0 by zero extension;
their coefficients remain in (12).

Write S for the remaining interior shifts, with coefficient b_0=c+1
at s=0 and the corresponding w_n otherwise. A single chi_1 in
C_c^infinity(I) can equal one on the finite union
union_(s in S)(supp chi_0+s). Apply (14) first with chi_0 and then
once with chi_1. The latter single identity treats all retained
interior translates simultaneously. Since Q commutes with translations,
for every s in S it gives

    Q[chi_0 T_s f]
      =Q^2[chi_0 T_s(chi_1 H)]
        +Q[chi_0,Q]T_s(chi_1 H)+Q[chi_0 T_s R_(chi_1)].      (15)

This follows from chi_0 T_s f=chi_0 T_s(chi_1 f) and
chi_0 Q=Q chi_0+[chi_0,Q]. It neither deletes the new shifts in H
nor assumes their regularity. H still contains every atom, nu_rho,
the scalar and the signed pole, including any endpoint collisions
generated at this second stage.

The first term of (15) has the bound (10), because chi_1 H is
bounded. The second is Lipschitz by the inverse commutator estimate.
The third has modulus O(t(1+L_t)) by (11) and (14). The remaining
term Q(chi_0 nu*f) from the first equation is Lipschitz by (13).
Finally R_(chi_0) has modulus (11). Summing this finite collection
therefore gives, near d,

    f(x)=w_2 (Q[chi_0 T_(-d)f])(x)+E_0(x),
    |E_0(d+t)-E_0(d)|+|E_0(d-t)-E_0(d)|=O(L_t^(-2)).       (16)

Only the two substitutions (14) with chi_0 and chi_1 were used.
No infinite inverse series, contraction, spectral gap or smoothness
of any unknown translated copy has entered the estimate. In particular
an atom equal to ell or to the external cutoff is retained in H;
its zero first-stage overlap is a consequence of support, not a
change to the operator.

### The endpoint profile and the constants

Translate the direct forcing in (16): F(y)=chi_0(d+y)f(y). It is
bounded, compactly supported in [0,infinity), and
F(y)=tau_L V(y)+o(V(y)) as y->0+. Its Q convolution at d+t is
QF(t). The subtraction s*F in (4) is Lipschitz, so it suffices to
compute the increments of p*F. Write L=L_t.

First use F(y)=V(y) near zero, with any bounded compact cutoff away
from zero. On 0<y<2t,

    integral_0^(2t) p(t-y)V(y)dy ~ L^(-3/2),
    integral_0^(2t) p(y)V(y)dy ~ (1/3)L^(-3/2).             (17)

For the first assertion, on t/2<y<3t/2 one has
V(y)/V(t)->1 uniformly, by L513's asymptotic and
log(1/y)=L+O(1). The kernel mass there is M(t/2)~1/L.
The two complementary intervals inside (0,2t) contribute at most
C t p(t/2)V(2t)=O(L^(-5/2)), using monotonicity of V and p.
For the second assertion, (7) and V(y)~log^(-1/2)(1/y) reduce
the integral, with asymptotic upper and lower bounds, to
(1/2) integral_(log(1/(2t)))^infinity z^(-5/2)dz.
Its constant is 1/3, as stated.

For 2t<y<r_0 the difference |p(y-t)-p(y)| is bounded by
C t/[y^2 log^2(1/y)], from (7) and the mean value theorem.
Multiplying by V(y), the integral over (2t,sqrt(t)) is
O(L^(-5/2)); over (sqrt(t),r_0) it is O(sqrt(t)) using the
weaker bound C t/y^2 and bounded V. The part bounded away from
zero is O(t). Thus (17), with its subtraction, proves the right
increment constant 1-1/3=2/3.

For the left increment replace p(t-y) by p(t+y). On 0<y<2t
the positive integral is at most 2t p(t)V(2t)=O(L^(-5/2)),
whereas the subtracted integral is again the second one in (17).
The tail has the same derivative bound. The left constant is -1/3.

These constants persist for the actual little-o trace error without
any modulus assumption on that error. In fact the preceding near-zero
and tail estimates also give

    integral_0^(r_0) |p(t-y)-p(y)|V(y)dy=O(L^(-3/2)),
    integral_0^(r_0) |p(t+y)-p(y)|V(y)dy=O(L^(-3/2)).        (18)

Given epsilon>0, the trace error has absolute value <=epsilon V(y)
on a sufficiently small fixed interval (0,delta). Apply (18) there;
on the complement its convolution increment is O_delta(t), since
the kernel is smooth at a fixed distance from zero and the error
is integrable. The resulting limsup after division by L^(-3/2)
is at most C epsilon. Let epsilon decrease to zero. This proves
the required little-o, rather than assuming smooth endpoint error.

Finally V(t)/log(1/t)~L^(-3/2), whereas the full translated remainder
in (16) is O(L^(-2))=o(L^(-3/2)). Multiplying the two computed
constants by w_2 tau_L proves (2) and (3).

### Independent conceptual audit, 2026-10-30

The separate, pre-existing EXPLORE scope is
`drafts/literature/2026-10-30-weil-first-prime-image-cusp-audit-coverage.md`.
All of (5)–(18) were checked under the unchanged hypotheses. The audit
passes (2)–(3); it adds no stronger regularity or uniform conclusion.
The explicit L2 inversion justification above records a domain detail
that was implicit in (14), not an extra hypothesis. The decisive checks
are as follows.

**Resolvent and normalization.** At w=-b^2, the derivative of
log(1+w)+2 is exp(2), so its reciprocal's numerator 2 gives residue
2exp(-2). The upper-bank imaginary part in (5) is negative; taking
minus that part divided by pi gives exactly the positive cut density
2/[(log(r-1)+2)^2+pi^2]. At z=0 its Stieltjes integral plus the pole
term equals one. Under the stated Fourier convention, replacing
r by y^2 introduces dr=2y dy and cancels the exponential Fourier
kernel's 1/(2y), giving exactly (6), with no missing 2pi factor.
For x small, the y<=x^(-1/2) pieces contribute O(x^(-1/2)) to p
and O(x^(-1)) to -p'; these are negligible relative to
x^(-1)L_x^(-2) and x^(-2)L_x^(-2). On the complement the bounded
rescaled density and exp(-z), z exp(-z) dominate. This verifies
both asymptotics and the integrability in (8), including the kernel
mass 1/L_x rather than 1/(2L_x).

**Translation and localization.** Formula (9) is valid for the
integrable decreasing representative, even if its value at zero is
infinite: on x>=-t/2 the nearer-to-zero translate is q(x), and the
two half-line integrals give 4 integral_0^(t/2)q. In the far part
of the q estimate, |y|-t is comparable to |y| for |y|>2t. On
(2t,sqrt(t)) this gives the claimed O(L_t^(-4)) contribution;
on (sqrt(t),r_0) the weaker integrable bound gives O(sqrt(t)).
The near part is O(L_t^(-2)), establishing (10). The commutator
derivative is bounded by

    ||chi'||_infinity |K(r)|
      +||chi'||_infinity |r K'(r)|.

Its integral is finite by (8). The kernel tends to zero on both
sides of the diagonal, so there is no distributional jump term.
For C_chi the moving near-diagonal sets have total length O(t)
and bounded integrands; outside them the derivative bound integrates
to O(t(1+L_t)). These checks require no derivative of f. Together
with the L2 argument in (14), they justify the inverse and all
continuous representatives used in (15).

**Full signed forcing.** Taylor expansion gives
rho(x)=3/4-(13/48)x+O(x^2) at zero. Its even extension is continuous
there with integrable one-sided derivative; the pole cutoff contributes
only its two finite jumps. This verifies (13) without deleting either
density. The finite set of first-stage centers has positive distance
from both endpoints except for s=-d: negative shifts n>2 are outside,
and (1) excludes the positive-shift collision with ell. This proves
the existence of the single chi_1, without any second-stage isolation
assumption. Equation (15) retains the complete H inside chi_1 H.
Its first term has (10), its commutator term is Lipschitz, and its
last term has the modulus (11). Hence even further endpoint collisions
cost only O(L_t^(-2)) in (16). Equality atoms and the signed pole
remain part of the equation. No positivity or smoothness of H is used.

**Constants and arbitrary trace error.** In (17), the central y
interval contributes V(t)M(t/2)~L^(-3/2). The subtracted integral
has constant (1/2)*(2/3)=1/3. The right outer intervals and the
left positive integral are O(L^(-5/2)). For either sign, the
weighted difference on 2t<y<r_0 is
O(L^(-5/2))+O(sqrt(t)); the portion away from zero is O(t).
The triangle inequality on 0<y<2t consequently gives (18) with
one constant for the entire interval (0,r_0), also bounding any
smaller (0,delta). This independence from delta is essential.
For an endpoint error e=o(V), fix epsilon and then delta before
letting t tend to zero. The error on (0,delta) is bounded by
C epsilon L^(-3/2); the rest is O_delta(t), since its distance
from the kernel singularity is at least delta/2 for t<delta/2.
The limsup is at most C epsilon, and epsilon can then tend to zero.
Thus the unquantified trace error genuinely remains little-o at the
cusp scale. Finally L^(-2)/L^(-3/2)=L^(-1/2)->0. The checked
constants are therefore 2/3 and -1/3 with the full smaller remainder.

This is verification of the existing local argument, not a new
discovery beyond the sources checked. It uses L513's normalized trace,
L515's bounded continuous representative and form domain, and L516's
complete retained model; L518/L519 are not additional premises.
Nullvector existence, nonzero trace, full-Weil identification, trace
vanishing, signed eigenvalue-relative cancellation, collars and Xi
transfer remain unresolved. The audit neither checks the external
