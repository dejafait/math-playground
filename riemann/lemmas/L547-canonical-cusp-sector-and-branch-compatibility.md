# L547 — Canonical cusp sectors and simultaneous branch compatibility

## Hypotheses

Let O=Z[omega], omega=exp(2pi i/3), lambda=1+2omega, and
q_z=|z|^2. Fix a finite set S containing the primes over 6 and a
nonzero L supported on S with (18)|(L). Put M=lambda^12 L^4.
For each h_0 modulo L choose once a representative and a reduced
fraction lambda^2 h_0/L=a_F/c_F. Normalize by a common unit so that
a_F=1 modulo 3 when lambda divides c_F, and c_F=1 modulo 3 otherwise.
For h_0=0 the fraction 0/1 is permitted. The prime support of c_F
is contained in S and c_F divides L up to a unit.

Let r be a squarefree product of primary primes p outside S, with
p=1 modulo 3; r=1 is permitted. At each p|r choose any nonzero
frequency h_p modulo p. Inactive frequencies are zero. All local
prime sets under comparison have this same fixed L and fixed base
Fourier function phi on O/(L). Retain its finite Fourier expansion
and the one-prime Fourier values, in particular C_(p,0)(0)=1-q_p^(-1).
No new coefficient-size or finite Gauss bound is assumed proved here.

Import the cubic theta function, its Gamma_2 automorphy and full cusp
expansions from Dunn–Radziwill at the precise locators below. Import
cubic reciprocity and its supplementary laws on their primary,
coprime domains. Write check-e(z)=exp(2pi i(z+bar z)) and
e(z)=check-e(z/lambda). For a good prime, chi_p(x)^2=(x/p)_3 on
units. Only this cubic character identity is needed for the multiplier.

## Conclusion

There are simultaneous choices of cusp matrices for all these data
such that c=c_F r and, in each fixed class of h_0 and r modulo M^2:

* the conjugate cusp coefficient function d is one fixed member of
  the triple from F_1,F_19,F_10;
* a unit scalar kappa_F and an additive character vartheta of O,
  with a modulus depending only on L, are fixed;
* for the chosen reduced numerator a, matrix g=G H with
  G in Gamma_1(3), and delta its lower-right entry,

\[
 \kappa(G)=\kappa_F\prod_{p\mid r}\chi_p(a)^2,              \tag{1}
\]

and, for every mu in lambda^(-4) O, writing x=lambda^4 mu,

\[
 \check e(-\delta\mu/c)
 =\vartheta(x)\prod_{p\mid r}e(\epsilon_p h_p^{-1}x/p),
 \quad \sigma_p=\lambda^2c/p,\quad
 \epsilon_p=-((\lambda^3c/p)\sigma_p)^{-1}\pmod p.          \tag{2}
\]

All inverses in (2) are of units; x itself need not be a unit.
For fixed active frequencies, adding or deleting an inactive
exponent-zero prime changes none of c_F,c,d,vartheta,kappa_F, the
other active local data or q_c^2. Its only finite Fourier scalar is
1-q_p^(-1). Thus an absent-minus-inactive mark has scalar q_p^(-1)
and the same conductor and kernel scale in those two branches.

This discharges the canonical construction portion of L546 Hypothesis 1
and its use in L541. It does not prove a Mellin reflection theorem,
the marked expansion's analytic justification, coefficient or Gauss
bounds, a norm estimate, global closure, or any actual-Xi sign.

### Reduced fractions and fixed residues

A lambda-unit of O has a unique unit multiple equal to 1 modulo 3:
the six units map bijectively to the six units of O/(3). This gives
the stated normalization. When lambda divides c_F, reducedness makes
a_F a lambda-unit; otherwise c_F is a lambda-unit. This includes c_F=1.

Lift each nonzero h_p so h_p=0 modulo M^2. CRT applies since p is
outside S. Set

\[
 a=a_F r+\lambda^2c_F\sum_{p\mid r}(r/p)h_p,
 \qquad c=c_F r.                                         \tag{3}
\]

At every prime of c_F, a is a unit because it is a_F r modulo
that prime. At p|r it equals lambda^2 c_F(r/p)h_p modulo p,
also a unit. Thus (a,c)=1, including nonunit h_0 and r=1.
Changing any frequency lift changes a/c by lambda^2 O=3O,
which is a period of theta: the upper translation is in
Gamma_1(3) with lower-left entry zero and multiplier one.

Fix r modulo M^2. Because c_F divides M up to a unit, M c_F
divides M^2. Formula (3) fixes a modulo M c_F: its moving sum
is divisible by M^2 c_F, and a_F r is fixed modulo M^2.
It also fixes c modulo M. If lambda|c, a=1 modulo 3;
otherwise c=1 modulo 3. None of these claims requires h_0 to
be a unit or any upper bound on the active frequencies.

At primes of c_F choose delta=a^(-1) modulo the corresponding
prime-power parts of M c_F. At the other primes of M choose
delta=0 modulo M. At each p|r choose delta=a^(-1) modulo p.
Every inverse is justified by (a,c)=1. These conditions have
pairwise disjoint prime supports after the indicated partition,
so CRT supplies a solution. Put b=(a delta-1)/c. Then

\[
 g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&\delta\end{pmatrix}\in SL_2(O),
 \quad
 b\equiv\begin{cases}0&\text{at the parts of M over }c_F,\\
                    -c^{-1}&\text{at the other parts of M}.
       \end{cases}                                        \tag{4}
\]

For the first assertion divide a delta-1 by c_F r locally;
r is a unit there. For the second c is locally a unit and
delta=0 modulo M. Therefore a,b,c,delta have fixed residues
modulo M in the sector. The stronger residue of a at M c_F
fixes the first set of delta conditions as well. In particular
their residues modulo 9 are fixed: 9 divides M.

Here and below zero entries can be avoided simultaneously without
changing these facts. Translate a/c by lambda^2 M^2 t, t in O,
to make a nonzero. This changes a by lambda^2 M^2 c t, hence
preserves its residues modulo M c_F and every p|r and preserves
the entire translated theta function. The delta solutions then
form one residue class modulo M c_F r. Varying delta in that
class varies b by aM times an arbitrary element. Since a is
nonzero, avoid b=0, delta=0, and, in the middle case below,
a-u_0b=0 and c-u_0delta=0. Each excludes at most one parameter
value: their slopes in the delta parameter are respectively aM,
M c_F r, -u_0 aM and -u_0 M c_F r, all nonzero in the cases
where they are used. This is valid even for a_F=0,c_F=r=1. These choices can
be made by a fixed enumeration of O using only the active data;
no inactive prime enters them. The subsequent symbol calculations
thus have nonzero denominators and, where required, nonzero
lower-left entries. Symbols with unit denominator equal one.

### The three matrix cases and their multipliers

If v_lambda(c)>=2, (4) gives g=I modulo 3. Take H=I and G=g.
Both a and every prime of r are primary and coprime. Thus

\[
 \kappa(G)=(c/a)_3=(c_F/a)_3(r/a)_3
                    =(c_F/a)_3(a/r)_3.                   \tag{5}
\]

If v_lambda(c)=1, let u_0 be the unique member of {lambda,-lambda}
equal to c modulo 3. Existence follows by dividing by lambda
and reducing the remaining unit modulo lambda, whose residue
field is F_3. Take H=[[1,0],[u_0,1]]. Here a=delta=1 and b=0
modulo 3, so

\[
 G=gH^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}a-u_0b&b\\c-u_0\delta&\delta\end{pmatrix}
 \equiv I\pmod3.
\]

Write A=a-u_0b and C=c-u_0delta. The primary elements a,A are
coprime: a common prime divides u_0b, while (a,b)=1 and
(a,u_0)=1. Also A is coprime to u_0. From a delta-bc=1,

\[
 aC=cA-u_0,\qquad bc=-1\pmod a.
\]

All symbols in the following computation are therefore units:

\[
 \begin{split}
 \kappa(G)&=(C/A)_3=(-u_0/A)_3(a/A)_3^{-1},\\
 (a/A)_3&=(A/a)_3=(-u_0b/a)_3
                 =(u_0/a)_3(c/a)_3^{-1},\\
 \kappa(G)&=(-u_0/A)_3((c_F/u_0)/a)_3(a/r)_3.             \tag{6}
 \end{split}
\]

The second line uses bc=-1 modulo a and (-1/a)_3=1,
since -1 is itself a cube. Here c_F/u_0 is integral and prime
to a; reciprocity was applied only to a,A and to primary
primes of r, not to a ramified denominator.

If v_lambda(c)=0, c_F and c are primary. Choose
u_0=s+t omega equal to a modulo 3, with 0<=s,t<3, and put
H=[[u_0,-1],[1,0]]. At lambda, (4) gives delta=0 and b=-1
modulo 3. Direct multiplication gives

\[
 G=gH^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-b&a+b u_0\\-\delta&c+\delta u_0\end{pmatrix}
 \equiv I\pmod3.                                        \tag{7}
\]

Both -b and c are primary. Moreover delta=0 modulo 9, so
-bc=1-a delta=1 modulo 9. Factor delta=u lambda^k delta_0,
with u a unit and delta_0 primary. The supplementary laws
(1.5) make (u lambda^k/(1-a delta))_3=1. Reciprocity gives
(delta_0/(1-a delta))_3=1 since 1-a delta=1 modulo delta_0.
Their coprimality follows from delta_0|delta. Consequently

\[
 \begin{split}
 1&=(\delta/(-bc))_3=(\delta/(-b))_3(\delta/c)_3,\\
 \kappa(G)&=(-\delta/(-b))_3=(\delta/(-b))_3
       =(\delta/c)_3^{-1}=(a/c)_3
       =(a/c_F)_3(a/r)_3.                               \tag{8}
 \end{split}
\]

The inverse uses a delta=1 modulo c. This also covers unit c
and hence h_0=0,r=1 after the harmless zero-entry choices.

Define kappa_F by the first factor in (5), the two fixed factors
in (6), or (a/c_F)_3 in (8), respectively. All have modulus one.
To check sector constancy in the first two cases, factor their
fixed numerators into units, lambda powers and fixed primary
primes. The unit and lambda symbols depend only on the primary
denominator modulo 9 by (1.5); primary-prime factors depend only
on that denominator modulo those primes by (1.4). These residues
of a and A=a-u_0b are fixed by (4). In (8) the fixed denominator
c_F directly makes the factor depend only on a modulo c_F.
The choice of u_0 is fixed by c or a modulo 3 as appropriate.
Thus kappa_F is fixed by h_0 and r modulo M^2, and (5)–(8)
prove (1) because (a/r)_3=product_(p|r) chi_p(a)^2.

### Cusp identification, including conjugation

Write Lw(v)=[[1,0],[v,1]], E=[[0,-1],[1,0]] and
(t_0,t_1,t_2)=(0,omega^2,omega). A lower translation by an
element of Z+3O has multiplier one: factor it into an integral
matrix and Lw(3z), whose Kubota symbol has denominator 1.
The same assertion for upper translations follows from the
zero lower-left entry. E also has multiplier one.

Define Theta_j=theta composed with Lw(t_j). Then
Theta_0=F_1, Theta_1=F_19 and Theta_2=F_10 by DR (5.9).
In the first matrix case H=I. In the middle case
lambda-omega^2=2+3omega and -lambda-omega=-1-3omega,
so H selects Theta_1 or Theta_2 without another multiplier.
In the last case H=E Lw(-u_0), and -u_0-t_t lies in Z+3O
for each t=0,1,2. Explicitly those differences are -s, 1-s,
and -s-3omega. Thus H selects Theta_t. In every case this
choice is fixed by the sector residues already proved.

For specificity the three conjugate coefficient functions, valid
on the entire raw lattice, are

\[
 \begin{split}
 d^{(0)}(\mu)&=\overline{\tau(-\mu)},\\
 d^{(1)}(\mu)&=\overline{\omega^2\tau_1(-\omega^2\mu)\check e(-\mu)},\\
 d^{(2)}(\mu)&=\overline{\omega\tau_2(-\omega\mu)\check e(-\mu)}.
                                                               \tag{9}
 \end{split}
\]

These are exactly Appendix A rows 1,19,10 combined with (5.16).
The raw cases (5.7), (5.13), (5.14), including their zero cases,
are used as coefficient definitions; no further support restriction
is imposed. The only constant term is that of Theta_0, equal
to (3^(5/2)/2)v^(2/3), by (5.15). The requested coefficient-size
bound remains outside this audit.

Finally, DR (5.1) applied to g^(-1) at (a/c,v) gives precisely
(-delta/c,1/(q_c v)). Since g=G H, automorphy gives the cusp
expansion there with multiplier bar(kappa(G)), coefficient (9),
and additive phase check-e(-delta mu/c). This confirms the
orientation of H and the conjugation. No inverse on a Fourier
index has entered, and no Mellin interchange is used.

### Additive CRT and simultaneous choices

Set D_F=lambda^3 c_F. It has only fixed-prime support and is
coprime to r. The delta congruences fix its class delta_F:
at a prime of c_F the modulus M c_F has enough valuation;
at lambda when lambda does not divide c_F, M contains lambda^3.
Also D_F divides M^2, so the sector fixes r^(-1) modulo D_F.
Define

\[
 \vartheta(x)=e(-\delta_F r^{-1}x/D_F).                    \tag{10}
\]

This is a character modulo D_F because e(y)=1 for y in O.
Indeed if y=m+n omega, Tr(y/lambda)=n is integral.
A common multiple of the finitely many D_F gives a modulus
depending only on L. No inverse of a at a prime outside c_F
in S was required: delta was set to zero there.

For every integral x, additive CRT for the pairwise coprime
factors D_F and p|r yields

\[
 \frac{-\delta x}{D_F r}\equiv
 \frac{-\delta_F r^{-1}x}{D_F}
 +\sum_{p\mid r}\frac{-\delta(D_F r/p)^{-1}x}{p}
 \pmod O.                                                \tag{11}
\]

This identity follows by multiplying by D_F r and checking
each factor separately, so it is valid even if x is a nonunit
at any or all of them. Formula (3), unchanged by the permitted
translations, gives a=sigma_p h_p modulo p. Both factors are
units, and hence delta=sigma_p^(-1)h_p^(-1) modulo p. The
local numerator in (11) is therefore epsilon_p h_p^(-1)x,
with epsilon_p as in (2). Since x=lambda^4 mu,

\[
 e(-\delta x/(D_F r))=\check e(-\delta\mu/c),
\]

which proves (2), with its sign and all powers of lambda.
The only inverses were r modulo D_F and, modulo p, h_p,
sigma_p and D_F r/p; all their coprimalities were checked.
For r=1 the product is empty and (11) reduces to (10).

Choose the representatives of h_0 once, and the lifts of each
h_p as fixed functions of (p,h_p,M), with the inactive lift
equal to zero. Make the zero-entry choices using the fixed
enumeration described above. These prescriptions refer only to
h_0 and the active frequencies. An absent prime and an inactive
prime therefore give literally identical a,c,g,H and raw
theta expansions before summing active frequencies. The resulting
d,vartheta,kappa_F are already independent of those frequencies
within each sector. This proves simultaneous compatibility across
arbitrary local prime sets, not merely separate existence of a
choice for each set. The fixed base phi and its h_0 coefficient
are also unchanged.

With the retained local Fourier value, an inactive exponent-zero
prime contributes 1-q_p^(-1); an absent prime contributes 1.
All other factors, including sigma, epsilon at active primes,
the conductor and its scale q_c^2, agree. Subtraction gives
q_p^(-1). Products of these identities hold for several distinct
inactive marks by finite expansion. This does not equate an active
branch with an inactive one: an active prime belongs to r and
changes c. The full raw support (9), including all allowed common
prime factors and all nonunit x, is preserved throughout.

### Independent critical review, 2026-10-31

The finite implication above passes a separate review against the raw
DR inputs and the pinned source construction. This verdict covers the
three matrix cases and all frequencies in the stated fixed-data scope;
it does not independently prove the imported theta theory.

The residue and symbol domains are essential. At a prime of c_F,
division in (4) loses precisely v_p(c_F), so the imposed modulus
has the required surplus v_p(c_F). Elsewhere over M, c is a unit.
Thus the claimed residues of b, as well as a,c,delta, really are fixed.
In (6), any common prime of a and A would divide both a and u_0b,
contradicting (a,b)=(a,u_0)=1. In (8), 1-a delta=-bc is nonzero
because zero b was excluded and c is nonzero. It is primary, equals
1 modulo 9, and is coprime to delta. Reciprocity and the supplementary
laws therefore apply exactly as written, including unit denominators.
No primary or coprimality condition has been replaced by a size bound.

The simultaneous-choice check uses equality of active data, not only
equality of sectors. Fix a single enumeration of O for all CRT choices.
Equal h_0 and active frequencies give the same a before the optional
translation, the same first permitted translation and the same first
permitted delta. They consequently give identical matrices. Sector
constancy of d,kappa_F,vartheta then follows from the separately proved
residue calculation. It does not require matrices themselves to be
constant across all active frequencies in a sector. The inverses in
(11) concern only the pairwise coprime moduli, never x; in particular
the identity remains valid when x shares every active prime.

As a direct check of the most degenerate branch, take h_0=0,r=1,
a_F=0,c_F=1. The permitted period translation allows

\[
 a=\lambda^2M^2,\qquad \delta=M,\qquad b=aM-1,\qquad c=1.
\]

Here a,b,delta are nonzero, a delta-bc=1, and the CRT prescriptions
hold. The unramified case has u_0=0, H=E and
G=[[1-aM,a],[-M,1]] in Gamma_1(3). Formula (8) gives kappa(G)=1,
and H selects Theta_0. Since lambda^3 divides M, (10) is identically
one on O. The reflected point is (-M,1/v), whose first coordinate
differs from zero by a theta period. Thus the empty branch satisfies
the same cusp and phase formulas, with q_c=1, without deleting a
zero frequency or dividing by a_F. Nonzero nonunit h_0 is already
covered by the reduced-fraction prime-by-prime argument above.

The raw support, conjugation, unit and ramified factors, and the
absent-minus-inactive scalar all survive the review. The two wording
clarifications above make explicit that the lattice contains zero
coefficients and that a lambda-unit need not equal 1. They do not
change the theorem. Coefficient/Gauss bounds, the analytic reflection
audit, uniform estimates, global closure and Xi signs remain separate.
