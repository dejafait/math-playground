(10) below. Its density is common to every actual triple, row and label.

### Extraction and zeros before separation

Write \(H_o=CJ\), \(r_0=\operatorname{rad}(q_0)t'\), \(d_0=d_k\),
and \(a_0(n)=\bar\alpha(n)\gamma_2(n)\). The contracted formal
component has outer factor
\(w_o^+\psi_g\psi_d\psi_k\mu(d_2)\mathcal B_2|B_o(g')|^2\),
and inner summand, for squarefree \(z_1,z_2\) prime to \(g'\),

\[
\begin{split}
 &1_{(z_1,z_2)=1}L(z_1)\overline{L(z_2)}
 G([z_2][z_1]^{-1})\mathfrak d_{I_i}(g'z_1)
       \overline{\mathfrak d_{I_i}(g'z_2)}\\
 &\quad\cdot W_i(q_{g'}q_{z_1}/Z^{s_i})
       \overline{W_i(q_{g'}q_{z_2}/Z^{s_i})}
 \frac{K}{q_{d_2}\sqrt{q_{z_1}q_{z_2}}}
 \mathcal K_2\left(\frac{Kq_{k''}}{q_{d_2}q_{z_1}q_{z_2}}\right),
                                                               \tag{5}
\end{split}
\]
where `eq:inverse-second-outer-coefficients` specifies
\[
\begin{split}
 B_o(g')&=\mu(g')\nu_i(g')\rho(g')1_{(g',r_0)=1}
              \chi_{g'}(H_o)^4\chi_{g'}(d_0),\\
 L(z)&=a_0(z)\nu_i(z)\rho(z)1_{(z,r_0)=1}
              \chi_z(H_o)^4\chi_z(d_0)\overline{\chi_z(d_2)}\chi_z(k'').
\end{split}
\]
The original principal pair \(z_1=z_2=1\) and the masked restoration
have already been treated by contract 1; (5) does not assert their
estimates. Inserting the complete identity
\(1_{(z_1,z_2)=1}=\sum_{v'\mid(z_1,z_2)}\mu(v')\) into its
formal extension gives \(z_a=v'n_a\). The new \(n_a\) need not be
mutually coprime. By the contracted extraction, the common factor is
\[
 D_o(v')=a_0(v')\nu_i(v')\rho(v')1_{(v',r_0)=1}
  \chi_{v'}(H_o)^4\chi_{v'}(d_0)
  \overline{\chi_{v'}(d_2)}\chi_{v'}(k''),
\]
and the residual coefficient is
\(a_0(n)\nu_i(n)\rho(n)1_{(n,r_0)=1}
\chi_n(H_o)^4\chi_n(d_0)\overline{\chi_n(d_2)}
\chi_n(k'')\chi_n(v')^4\).

Thus \(|B_o|^2\) keeps the fixed \(\rho,r_0,CJ,d_0\) zeros on
\(g'\), and \(|D_o|^2\) keeps those on \(v'\) and also its
\(d_2,k''\) zeros. Neither is cancelled or divided out. The residual
\(\chi_n(v')^4\) makes an overlap with \(v'\) zero without any
nonsquarefree Gauss sum. The old gcd condition supplies
\(1_{(v',g')=1}\); adding the already present \(CJ\) zero gives
\(1_{(v',g'CJ)=1}\).

For a zero-extended sixth root \(u\), \(\bar u=u^5=u\,u^4\)
also when \(u=0\). Consequently the moving factors become exactly
\(\chi_n(k)\chi_n(f)^4\), by (2). The residual exclusion at
\(d_2\mid g'\) is repeated by these factors, and the remaining fixed
puncture is
\(\rho_\gamma(n)=\rho(n)1_{(n,\operatorname{rad}(q_0)t'r_g)=1}\).
The contracted ray identity is
\(G([n_2][n_1]^{-1})=\sum_\vartheta\widehat G(\vartheta)
\overline{\vartheta(n_1)}\vartheta(n_2)\).
Both columns therefore use \(\nu_i\bar\vartheta\), with one outer
coefficient \(\widehat G(\vartheta)\). The slot identity assigns
original coefficients to \((d_2,r_g,v';n_a)\); denote the surviving
marks by \(\mathfrak d'_a\) and the outer product, conjugated on
side two, by \(\mathcal A_{2,\sigma}\). No slot is deleted here.

On nonzero outer support, \(J,C,d_2,v'\) are pairwise coprime and
squarefree: \((C,J)=1\) is retained, \(B_o\) excludes primes of
\(CJ\) from \(g'\), \(d_2\mid g'\), and the explicit last mask
excludes primes of \(g'CJ\) from \(v'\). This proves squarefreeness
of the derived \(f\) on that support, before an upper bound removes zeros.

### Six coordinates, roots and clipping

Define independent positive coordinates before actual ideals vary:
\[
 (y_g,y_d,y_v,y_k,y_1,y_2)=
 (q_{g'}/Z^g,q_{d_2}/Z^\theta,q_{v'}/Z^v,q_{k''}/Z^{h_2},
                  q_{n_1}/Z^{N_c},q_{n_2}/Z^{N_c}).
\]
With \(A=Z^{H_{\rm use}+h_2-\theta-2v-2N_c}>0\), set
\[
\begin{split}
 F_{2,\boldsymbol t}(\boldsymbol y)={}&Z^{-6\eta}
 \prod_\alpha\Omega_{2,\alpha}(y_\alpha)
 y_d^{-1}y_v^{-1}(y_1y_2)^{-1/2}\\
 &\cdot W_i(y_gy_vy_1)\overline{W_i(y_gy_vy_2)}
 \mathcal K_2\left(A\frac{y_k}{y_dy_v^2y_1y_2}\right).
                                                               \tag{6}
\end{split}
\]
The scalar exponent in the real root is
\(H_{\rm use}-\theta-v-N_c
=H_c-\theta-(s_i-g)+12\eta+\tau\).
It follows that
\[
 \frac{K}{q_{d_2}q_{v'}\sqrt{q_{n_1}q_{n_2}}}
 =Z^{\lambda_c+12\eta+\tau}y_d^{-1}y_v^{-1}(y_1y_2)^{-1/2}.
                                                               \tag{7}
\]
Substitution also restores the kernel argument in (5) and both old
window arguments, since \(g+v+N_c=s_i\). Multiplying (6) by
\(Z^{\lambda_c+18\eta+\tau}\) thus restores the full real root.
Its inverse powers must not be appended to a child test a second time.

If \(N_c\ge0\), then \(Z^{\delta_N}=1\). If \(N_c<0\), a
nonempty fresh column contains a norm \(q_n\ge1\) with
\(q_n/Z^{N_c}\le b\); hence \(Z^{-N_c}\le b\). Also its
full-support logarithmic bound gives \(-N_c\le6\eta\). Thus in
both retained cases
\(0\le\delta_N\le6\eta\) and \(1\le Z^{\delta_N}\le b\).
The clipped test has support inside \([a/b,b]\). Euler differentiation
of \(\omega_{n,a}(Z^{\delta_N}z)\) evaluates the same Euler
derivative at \(Z^{\delta_N}z\), so its seminorms are uniformly
bounded. All these scales depend on centers only. No \(Z^\eta\)-wide
support is used. If the row ball or a column is empty, the component
vanishes and no child estimate is invoked.

Define, without any real root in its test,
\[
\begin{split}
 Q_{a,\gamma,\boldsymbol\zeta}(k,f)=
 \sum_{n\ {\rm sf}}{}&a_0(n)(\nu_i\bar\vartheta)(n)\rho_\gamma(n)
 \chi_n(k)\chi_n(f)^4\mathfrak d'_a(n)\\
 &\cdot\omega_{n,a}(Z^{\delta_N}z_n)
                    z_n^{\varepsilon_a i\zeta_a},
 \qquad z_n=q_n/Z^{N_{\rm ch}}.                          \tag{8}
\end{split}
\]
The complete outer weight is
\[
\begin{split}
 V_\sigma(\xi;\boldsymbol\zeta)={}&w_o^+\mu(d_2)\mu(v')\widehat G(\vartheta)
  |B_o(g')|^2|D_o(v')|^2 1_{(v',g'CJ)=1}\mathcal B_2(k'')\\
 &\cdot\mathcal A_{2,\sigma}(\xi)
   \psi_g(y_g)\psi_d(y_d)\psi_v(y_v)\psi_k(y_k)
   \omega_f(q_f/Z^{V_c})\\
 &\cdot\prod_{\alpha=g,d,v,k}y_\alpha^{i\zeta_\alpha}
      Z^{i\delta_N(\zeta_1+\zeta_2)}.                    \tag{9}
\end{split}
\]
Using the logarithmic Fourier convention of L535, Fourier inversion gives
\[
 \mathcal T_{\sigma,i}=\frac{Z^{\lambda_c+18\eta+\tau}}{(2\pi)^6}
  \int_{\mathbb R^6}\widehat F_{2,\boldsymbol t}(\boldsymbol\zeta)
   \sum_{\xi\in\Xi_\sigma}V_\sigma(\xi;\boldsymbol\zeta)
    Q_{1,\gamma,\boldsymbol\zeta}(k,f)
       \overline{Q_{2,\gamma,\boldsymbol\zeta}(k,f)}\,d\boldsymbol\zeta.
                                                               \tag{10}
\]
Indeed the two column modes in the expanded product are
\(z_{n_1}^{i\zeta_1}z_{n_2}^{i\zeta_2}\), because side two has
the opposite sign before conjugation. With the last factor of (9), they
are \(y_1^{i\zeta_1}y_2^{i\zeta_2}\). The four other modes restore
the remaining coordinates. Larger cutoffs equal one on retained supports;
fresh column cutoffs equal one wherever old windows are nonzero, and
\(\omega_f=1\) on original label products. Equations (6)–(7) and
the contracted extraction therefore restore each original term. Added
combinations in the fresh column supports give zero when the old window
vanishes after inversion. They are not assumed to have parent witnesses.

For fixed \(Z\) the pre-enlargement sums are finite, and the estimate
below gives absolute Fourier integrability. Thus the termwise inversion
and interchange used here are justified. The profile depends on fixed
centers, tests, \(Z\) and \(\boldsymbol t\), never on current
\(\gamma,k,f\). In particular its kernel coordinate is the bare
\(k''\), not \(k\); the derived row and label enter only characters,
outer masks and cutoffs. Relations such as \(g'=d_2r_g\) restrict
evaluation points in one ambient profile, not the Fourier density.

### Every finite height order with zero Fourier scale loss

Let \(D_\alpha=y_\alpha\partial_{y_\alpha}\). For the kernel
monomial in (6), its exponent vector in the displayed coordinate order is
\(e=(0,-1,-2,1,-1,-1)\). The chain rule gives
\[
 D^{\boldsymbol m}\mathcal K_2(A\boldsymbol y^e)
  =\left(\prod_\alpha e_\alpha^{m_\alpha}\right)
   (u\partial_u)^{|\boldsymbol m|}\mathcal K_2(u)
                  \big|_{u=A\boldsymbol y^e}.            \tag{11}
\]
A derivative in an absent coordinate makes that term zero; an exponent
with derivative order zero contributes one. No power of \(A\) is
left outside the Euler derivative. The radial Fourier conclusion of
`lem:kernel-seminorms` applies to the fixed \(\Phi_+\). At small
argument its Euler derivatives are polynomial combinations of smooth
Fourier derivatives, bounded even at zero; at large argument the
polynomial factors are bounded by Schwartz decay. Hence (11) is uniform
over all \(A>0\), including arbitrarily small and large scalars.

The inverse powers in (6) have bounded derivatives on the fixed box.
Each logarithmic derivative of \(W_i\) costs at most a polynomial in
\(|t_{x_i}|\). By Leibniz's rule, for any integer \(m\ge0\),
\[
 \max_{|\boldsymbol a|\le m}\|D^{\boldsymbol a}F_{2,\boldsymbol t}\|_\infty
 \le C_m Z^{-6\eta}(1+|\boldsymbol t|)^{m}\mathfrak S_m,
\]
where \(\mathfrak S_m\) collects finitely many seminorms of the fixed
cutoffs and radial test and is independent of actual ideals, \(Z,A\).
For \(2m_0>J_0+6\), integration by parts with \((1-\Delta)^{m_0}\)
on the compact logarithmic box, followed by the integrability of
\((1+|\zeta|)^{J_0}(1+|\zeta|^2)^{-m_0}\) in six dimensions, gives
\[
 \int|\widehat F_{2,\boldsymbol t}(\zeta)|(1+|\zeta|)^{J_0}\,d\zeta
 \le C_{J_0}Z^{-6\eta}(1+|\boldsymbol t|)^{2m_0}\mathfrak S_{2m_0}.
                                                               \tag{12}
\]
This is exactly the applicable fixed-box Fourier fact; the calculation
identifies the required order and its independence from scale.

Let \(\widehat F_1\) denote the first density in L535. Apply its
weighted bound at finite order \(B_0+2m_0\). Tonelli's theorem for
the nonnegative integrand and (12) yield
\[
 \iint |\widehat F_1(t)|\,|\widehat F_{2,t}(\zeta)|
       (1+|t|)^{B_0}(1+|\zeta|)^{J_0}\,d\zeta\,dt
 \le C_{B_0,J_0}\mathfrak S,                             \tag{13}
\]
for a finite product \(\mathfrak S\) of input seminorms. Dropping
\(Z^{-6\eta}\le1\) suffices. That scalar occurs once in this
quadratic profile, not once per column; no squared gain is claimed.
The constants and finite seminorm orders may increase with the requested
height orders, but their scale exponent remains zero. This is the
conditional specialization of `eq:inverse-joint-fourier-moments`.

### Whole-set Cauchy and one normalized triple measure

For fixed modes apply weighted Cauchy on the whole \(\Xi_\sigma\):
\[
 \left|\sum_\xi V_\sigma Q_1\overline{Q_2}\right|
 \le\prod_{a=1}^2\left(\sum_\xi|V_\sigma||Q_a|^2\right)^{1/2}.
                                                               \tag{14}
\]
All zeros of (9), including the frequency-dependent zero of \(D_o\),
are still present. Only now use contract 5 and squarefreeness established
above to bound \(|V_\sigma|\le C\mathcal B_2\mu^2(f)\).
Grouping nonzero terms by (2) and then applying the contracted fibre
bound gives
\[
 \sum_\xi|V_\sigma||Q_a|^2
 \le C\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_\sigma}C_{K_s}(\gamma)H_{a,\gamma},\quad
 H_{a,\gamma}=\sum_{\substack{f\ {\rm sf}\\q_f/Z^{V_c}\in I_f}}
 D_{K_s}(f)\sum_{0<q_k\le Z^{M_{\rm ch}}}|Q_{a,\gamma,\zeta}(k,f)|^2.
                                                               \tag{15}
\]
The removal of further eligibility restrictions is a nonnegative upper
bound. It does not change the child puncture, surviving marks or test.
Additional rows and labels need not arise from a parent factorization;
their supports are the independent full windows already in the contract.
The mask becomes exactly the displayed nonzero row ball. If desired,
\(H_{a,\gamma}=Z^{F_{\rm ch}}E_{a,\gamma}\) defines the energy with
this same ball; a larger canonical ball gives only the upper inequality.

Set
\[
 \nu_\sigma=Z^{-c_\sigma}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_\sigma}
       C_{K_s}(\gamma)\delta_\gamma.
 \qquad \|\nu_\sigma\|\le C Z^{\pi_{\rm count}+\pi_{\rm fib}}.
                                                               \tag{16}
\]
The mass estimate follows directly from the two separate contracts (4).
It does not prove them. Equations (10), (14) and (15) give
\[
 |\mathcal T_{\sigma,i}|
 \le C Z^{\lambda_c+18\eta+\tau+c_\sigma}
   \int|\widehat F_{2,t}(\zeta)|
     \prod_{a=1}^2\left(\int_{\Gamma_\sigma}H_{a,\gamma}
                                      \,d\nu_\sigma\right)^{1/2}d\zeta.
                                                               \tag{17}
\]
Each square root contributes \(Z^{c_\sigma/2}\), giving one
exterior \(Z^{c_\sigma}\). If uniform upper bounds on the children
are later supplied, their two square roots contribute one total
\(\|\nu_\sigma\|\), not its square.

For explicit coefficient-measure bookkeeping, the positive joint measure
on \(\mathbb R^9\times\mathbb R^6\times\Gamma_\sigma\) is
\[
 d\mu_\sigma=(2\pi)^{-15}|\widehat F_1(t)|
       |\widehat F_{2,t}(\zeta)|\,dt\,d\zeta\,d\nu_\sigma(\gamma).
                                                               \tag{18}
\]
It is measurable (the compact smooth profile depends smoothly on the
first modes), common to all current rows and labels, and has every fixed
\((B_0,J_0)\) weighted moment bounded by
\(C_{B_0,J_0}\mathfrak S Z^{\pi_{\rm count}+\pi_{\rm fib}}\),
by (13) and (16). No claim of zero *discrete* mass exponent is made.

The distinct triples stay in \(\Gamma_\sigma\); the larger balls
used to obtain (4) supply no new children. The divisor weight in \(f\)
and the row sum in \(k\) remain inside (15). The choices of
\(J,C,d_2,v'\) and source witnesses are already covered by the
contracted fibre factor, with the \(f\) part inside the child and
the \(\gamma\) part in (16). The old \(f,h\) were removed in
L535 and do not reappear. Neither frequencies nor assigned primes are
counted a second time. No recursive child exponent has been used.

The bounded audit passes with exactly the stated contracts. It removes
