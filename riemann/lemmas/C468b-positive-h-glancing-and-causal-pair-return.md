1. At every positive-t,h glancing endpoint in the open x,h box,

   \[

   \[

   0<h\le H_*-2\epsilon,\quad 0<|s|<1

   Smoothness is local; no uniform derivative bound is claimed.

   pair segment starts inside the pair and has a first earlier
   endpoint hit (r_b,x_b,h_b) with D<0 and r_b<t. This return map

   \[
   T(t,x,h)=T(r_b,x_b,h_b)\qquad(D(t,x,h)>0).

### A strict turning inequality at every glancing point

C467 proves that D is smooth on a neighborhood of the compact
(t,x,h) box, D(0,x,h)=-2h, D_h=-2+O(t), and
D(t,x,0)=d(x)t^2+O(t^3), uniformly in x. Consequently, after
one common reduction,

\[
D_h\le-1,\qquad D_h-\tfrac13D_x\le-1,\qquad
|D_t|+|D_x|\le C_D,\qquad |D(t,x,0)|\le A t^2.
\tag{6}
\]

For the second inequality use D_x(0,x,h)=0 and compact smoothness;
no differentiation of a merely bounded Taylor remainder is needed.
Take A>0, increasing a finite bound if necessary. If D(t,x,h)=0
and h>0, the fundamental theorem of calculus and D_h<=-1 give
0=D(t,x,0)+integral_0^h D_h(t,x,y)dy<=A t^2-h. Hence

\[
0<h\le A t^2\quad\hbox{at every positive-h glancing point}.
\tag{7}
\]

This uniform upper bound needs no positive lower bound for d(x),
and makes no assertion that a glancing point exists for each x.

For the pair field C467p(29)–(30) supplies constants C_c,delta,d_*>0
and a common time such that

\[
|\dot c|\le C_c,\qquad
\dot h\le-\frac{d_*}{2h}\quad(0<h\le\delta).
\tag{8}
\]

Dots are forward derivatives along the auxiliary physical pair
characteristic. These are actual inverse-chart estimates; they do
not differentiate h while incorrectly holding initial parameters
fixed. Since x=c-h/3, the chain rule gives exactly

\[
\mathcal X_pD=D_t+D_x\dot c+
                    (D_h-\tfrac13D_x)\dot h.
\tag{9}
\]

Let C0=C_D(1+C_c). Equations (6),(8) imply
X_p D>=-C0+d_*/(2h). Set
delta1=min(delta,d_*/[4(C0+1)]). Reduce time so A tau^2<=delta1.
Then (7) puts every glancing point within that collar, and (9)
is at least d_*/(4h). This proves the first inequality of (3).

For the second one, work in a neighborhood of any fixed glancing
endpoint. Here t,h are positive, v is nonzero, and all physical

### Continuity of the first hit at a positive-h glancing endpoint

Fix a glancing point (t0,x0,h0) with t0,h0>0 and strict core margins.
It suffices to use one signed endpoint. The auxiliary characteristic
through it has rho>0 at time t0-eta for every sufficiently small
eta>0, by (10). Fix such an eta<t0 and a smooth compact path
neighborhood with h>0. Continuous ODE dependence shows that the
backward paths of all nearby terminal points also have rho>0 at
that same earlier time.

For nearby interior terminal points, rho<0 at their terminal time.
They therefore have an endpoint root between t0-eta and that time.
Their first backward hit is no earlier than this root, so
t0-eta<r_b<t_terminal. For nearby endpoint terminal points with
D>0, the path is inside the pair immediately before the terminal
time, giving the identical bracket for the first earlier root.
These comparisons take place in the fixed local chart and strict
outer margins. For arbitrary sufficiently small eta they prove
r_b -> t0 as the terminal point tends to the glancing point.
Smooth ODE dependence then gives Q(r_b) -> Q0, and the positive-h
inverse chart gives (x_b,h_b) -> (x0,h0).

At D<0 a terminal seam point has rho>0 immediately before contact,
and the same bracketing argument proves continuity to its identity
value from the interior; alternatively it follows from its transverse
implicit hit chart. Thus no distant first-hit branch survives in the
limit to a fixed positive-h glancing point. This is a local continuity
proof, not a uniform estimate as t0 or h0 tends to zero.

composition T(r_b,x_b,h_b) through these fixed positive-h glancing
