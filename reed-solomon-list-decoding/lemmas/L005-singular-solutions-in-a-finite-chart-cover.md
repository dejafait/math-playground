# L005 — Singular solutions in a finite chart cover

## Hypotheses

Let K be algebraically closed of characteristic zero or characteristic
p>max(d,B). Let 0<=r<=d, D>=0, and B>=1 be integers, and let

\[
 0\ne Q\in K[X,Y_0,\ldots,Y_r],\qquad
 \deg_XQ\le D,\quad \deg_Y^{\mathrm{tot}}Q\le B.
\]

For P in K[X], write P^[i] for its ith Hasse derivative, specified by
\(P(X+U)=\sum_i P^{[i]}(X)U^i\). Let S_d(Q) be the set of coefficient vectors
of degree-at-most-d polynomials satisfying

\[
 Q(X,P,P^{[1]},\ldots,P^{[r]})=0
 \quad\text{identically in }X.
\]

All algebraic sets are reduced. Cumulative degree is the sum of the
projective-closure degrees of their irreducible components, as in the
[geometric foundation](../foundations/03-proper-hyperplane-sections.md).
Put

\[
 \begin{split}
 N&=D+(B-1)d+1, & A_0&=D+B,\\
 L_*&=\max(0,2d-1), & E_*&=A_0(1+B L_*),\qquad T_*=A_0L_*+1.
 \end{split} \tag{1}
\]

## Conclusion

There are at most B N closed affine algebraic sets V_i in K^(d+1) such
that

\[
 S_d(Q)\subseteq\bigcup_i V_i,\qquad
 \dim V_i\le r,\qquad
 \sum_i\operatorname{cdeg}(V_i)
 \le \Delta_*:=B^2N(E_*T_*)^r. \tag{2}
\]

Empty sets can be omitted. Every V_i is the closure of a nonsingular
Taylor chart for Q or one of its successive highest-variable partials,
converted to monomial coefficient coordinates. Those partials can have
solutions outside S_d(Q); only the displayed containment is asserted.
Anchors and covering sets are allowed over K, even if Q is defined over a
finite subfield.

For fixed r,B, the chart count and degree bound are polynomial in D,d.
If D,d=O(n), with fixed implied constants, then Delta_*=O_(r,B)(n^(4r+1)),
where the bound also depends on those implied constants. This supplies a
solution cover for a given differential polynomial, not the existence of
an interpolant containing a Reed–Solomon list.

This is a direct scalar proof of the cover mechanism reviewed in
[TR26-169, proof of Theorem 5.5 and Corollary 6.1, printed pp. 24–26](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/169/download#page=24),
September 5, 2026 version, read September 25. The explicit constants in
(1)–(2) use L004. No parametric exceptional-set theorem or novelty is
claimed.

## Proof

**A single finite derivative chain.** Set Q_0=Q. If Q_j still depends
on at least one Y variable, let r_j be its highest such index and set

\[
 Q_{j+1}=\frac{\partial Q_j}{\partial Y_{r_j}}. \tag{3}
\]

Every constructed Q_j is nonzero. To check this, expand Q_j as a
polynomial in Y_(r_j) over the integral domain generated by X and the
other variables. Its positive highest exponent e is at most B. In
characteristic zero, or p>B, the coefficient e multiplying its leading
term upon differentiation is nonzero. Thus (3) cannot be zero. Moreover
its total Y-degree drops by at least one, and its X-degree does not
increase. This argument applies at each stage, since every later
Y-degree is still at most B.

The process consequently stops after h<=B differentiations with

\[
 0\ne Q_h\in K[X]. \tag{4}
\]

There is one chain, not a branching choice depending on the solution.
If h=0, the original Q depends only on X and S_d(Q) is empty. The empty
cover proves (2). Henceforth suppose h>=1. Every r_j for j<h is at most
r and therefore at most d.

**A nonsingular equation for every original solution.** Fix P in S_d(Q).
Evaluate every Q_j on its full Hasse jet, using the same P throughout:

\[
 g_j(X)=Q_j(X,P(X),P^{[1]}(X),\ldots,P^{[r]}(X)).
\]

Unused variables do not affect this expression. We have g_0=0 and, by
(4), g_h=Q_h!=0. Let j+1 be the least index with g_(j+1)!=0. Then

\[
 g_j=0,\qquad
 \frac{\partial Q_j}{\partial Y_{r_j}}
       (X,P,\ldots,P^{[r_j]})=g_{j+1}\ne0. \tag{5}
\]

Thus P is a solution of Q_j with a nonzero substituted highest-variable
partial. This step does not differentiate the identity Q_j(P)=0 with
respect to X: it selects the first nonzero evaluation along (3).

The polynomial Q_(j+1) has X-degree at most D and total Y-degree at most
B-1. Every substituted Hasse derivative has degree at most d, including
the possibility that it is zero. Monomial substitution therefore gives

\[
 \deg_X g_{j+1}\le D+(B-1)d=N-1. \tag{6}
\]

**One fixed anchor set suffices.** Choose any N distinct elements Omega
of K. An algebraically closed field is infinite, so this is possible.
A nonzero polynomial of degree at most N-1 has at most N-1 distinct
roots, by the factor theorem. Equations (5)–(6) imply that some
alpha in Omega satisfies g_(j+1)(alpha)!=0.

For each pair (j,alpha), j<h, define the initial-coefficient polynomial

\[
 H_{j,\alpha}(c_0,\ldots,c_{r_j})
   =Q_{j+1}(\alpha,c_0,\ldots,c_{r_j}). \tag{7}
\]

If (7) is the zero polynomial, omit this pair. Otherwise apply the
fixed-parameter part of L004 to Q_j, considered independent of its dummy
parameter Z, with order s=r_j, message degree d, and degree bounds D,B.
Form its base equation and all residual equations, retain H_(j,alpha)!=0,
and only then close the lifted graph. Call the result Gamma_(j,alpha).
This uses the separately closed fixed-parameter chart in L004, not a
fiber of a total graph closure.

For the P and anchor selected above, \(c_i=P^{[i]}(\alpha)\) makes (7) equal
g_(j+1)(alpha)!=0. Also P solves Q_j. L004's identification of the open
graph with the nonsingular solution locus therefore places P's Taylor
vector in that open graph. In particular this pair was not omitted.
The invertible linear conversion from Taylor coefficients at alpha to
monomial coefficients places P in the resulting set V_(j,alpha).
This proves coverage of every original solution, including those whose
highest-variable partial for Q vanishes identically.

**Counting and degree.** For a pair with order r_j set

\[
 L_j=\max(0,2(d-r_j)-1),\quad
 E_j=A_0(1+B L_j),\quad T_j=A_0L_j+1.
\]

L004 supplies

\[
 \dim V_{j,\alpha}\le r_j,\qquad
 \operatorname{cdeg}(V_{j,\alpha})\le B(E_jT_j)^{r_j}.
\]

All numbers A_0,E_*,T_* are at least one. Since L_j<=L_* and r_j<=r,
each degree is at most B(E_*T_*)^r and each dimension is at most r.
There are at most hN<=BN pairs. Summing, with any empty charts omitted,
proves (2). Overlaps only increase this upper bound on the sum. The
coordinate conversions preserve degree and dimension, as established
in L004. For fixed B and D,d=O(n), we have N,A_0,L_*=O(n) and
E_*,T_*=O(n^2), giving the asserted exponent 4r+1.

**Exact checks of the delicate cases.** If Q=Y_r^b with 2<=b<=B, every
solution has P^[r]=0. The first b-1 evaluated partials are zero, while
Q_b=b! is nonzero. The last chart equation is a nonzero scalar times
Y_r and its partial is constant and nonzero. Under the characteristic
assumption, P^[r]=0 is exactly the degree-less-than-r subspace, of
dimension r (the singleton zero for r=0). This checks the deepest
singular descent and shows why a dimension-r cover is appropriate.

Dropping the differential order is also allowed. For Q=Y_0Y_1,
the chain is Q_1=Y_0, Q_2=1. The zero solution is singular for Q and
is captured by the order-zero chart of Q_1. Nonzero constant solutions
are already nonsingular for Q.

The covering sets need not lie in S_d(Q). For Q=Y_1^2-Y_0, the chain
contains Q_1=2Y_1. In characteristic zero, its nonsingular chart contains
every constant polynomial, but P=1 does not solve Q. This is harmless
for the containment in (2); no equation of the original Q has silently
been imposed on such derivative charts.

Anchors cannot in general be restricted to the finite field of
definition. Over K=algebraic closure of F_p take d<p and
Q=(X^p-X)Y_0, so B=1 and D=p. Its only solution is zero, and its
substituted partial X^p-X vanishes at every anchor in F_p. A set of
N=p+1 distinct anchors in K detects that partial. Hence the proof does
not assume the field size exceeds N.

If B>=p, Q=Y_0^p has an identically zero highest-variable partial,
so the chain argument itself fails. This is not a counterexample to
some different cover construction. If d>=p, a genuine obstruction to
the dimension claim appears: Q=Y_1 has solution space spanned by
1,X^p,...,X^(p floor(d/p)), of dimension floor(d/p)+1. For r=1 and
d>=p this exceeds r and cannot be covered by finitely many closed sets
of dimension at most r. Neither characteristic restriction has been
removed from the present mechanism.

The cases d=0, r=0, no lifted coefficients, zero residual equations,
and an empty solution set are all included in the construction. The
assumption r<=d is explicit; if r>d, the whole ambient K^(d+1) already
has dimension at most r and degree one, but that separate trivial case
is not needed for (2).
